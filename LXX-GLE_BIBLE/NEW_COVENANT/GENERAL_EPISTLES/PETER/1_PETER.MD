\p
\v 1 Peter — an apostle of Jesus the Anointed — to the elect exiles of the Dispersion in Pontus — Galatia — Cappadocia — Asia — and Bithynia —
\v 2 according to the foreknowledge of God the Father — in the sanctification of the breath-of-life (*pneumatos*) — for obedience to Jesus the Anointed and for sprinkling with his blood: May grace and peace be multiplied to you.
\p
\v 3 Blessed be the God and Father of our Lord Jesus the Anointed! According to his great mercy he has caused us to be born again (*anagennēsas*) to a living hope through the resurrection (*anastaseōs*) of Jesus the Anointed from the dead —

\v 5 who by God's power are being guarded through faith for a salvation (*sōtērian*) ready to be revealed in the last time.
\v 6 In this you rejoice though now for a little while if necessary you have been grieved by various trials —
\v 7 so that the tested genuineness of your faith — more precious than gold that perishes though it is tested by fire — may be found to result in praise and glory and honor at the revelation (*apokalypsei*) of Jesus the Anointed.
\v 8 Though you have not seen him you love (*agapate*) him. Though you do not now see him you believe in him and rejoice with joy that is inexpressible and filled with glory —
\v 9 obtaining the outcome of your faith — the salvation (*sōtērian*) of your souls (*psychōn*).

\v 12 It was revealed to them that they were serving not themselves but you in the things that have now been announced to you through those who preached the glad tidings (*euangelisamenōn*) to you by the Holy Breath-of-life (*pneumati hagiō*) sent from heaven — things into which angels long to look.

\v 14 As obedient children do not be conformed to the passions of your former ignorance —
\v 15 but as he who called you is holy — you also be holy in all your conduct —
\v 16 since it is written: You shall be holy for I am holy.
\v 17 And if you call on him as Father who judges impartially according to each one's deeds — conduct yourselves with fear throughout the time of your exile —
\v 18 knowing that you were ransomed (*elytrōthēte*) from the futile ways inherited from your forefathers — not with perishable things such as silver or gold —
\v 19 but with the precious blood of the Anointed like that of a lamb (*amnou*) without blemish or spot.
\v 20 He was foreknown before the foundation of the world but was made manifest in the last times for the sake of you —

**1 PETER — GLE v2.2 CAE NOTES**

---

**TEXT BASE:** RP2026 Byzantine Majority Text

**VERSE COUNT:** 105 verses / 5 chapters

---

**1 PETER IN THE CORPUS ARCHITECTURE**

1 Peter is the most LXX-saturated letter in the Catholic Epistles and one of the most LXX-dependent documents in the entire NT canon — scholarly counts identify between 30 and 46 LXX allusions and citations across 105 verses, a density rivaled only by Hebrews and Revelation. The letter is addressed to *parepidēmoi* — sojourners / resident aliens — scattered across five provinces of Asia Minor, and its governing framework is the identity-in-exile theology of the LXX: the community is the new Israel in dispersion, constituted as *basileion hierateuma / ethnos hagion / laos periousiou* (2:9 — royal priesthood / holy nation / people for his own possession) through the blood of the Anointed. Every major theological category in the letter is constructed from LXX covenantal vocabulary applied to the new covenant community.

The letter's CAE function is concentrated in six nodes of exceptional density: the trinitarian election formula of 1:2 — the fullest NT statement of the three-person election-sequence; the *anagennēsas* / living hope / imperishable inheritance arc of 1:3–5 — the new birth through resurrection as the ground of eschatological hope; the *amnos* / *timion haima* ransom declaration of 1:18–19 — the most explicit Passover-lamb ransom statement in the NT outside John 1:29; the *oikos pneumatikos / basileion hierateuma* declaration of 2:4–9 — the most sustained *naos*-chain community-identity statement in the Catholic Epistles, drawing on five LXX texts simultaneously; the Isaiah 53 LXX citation-cluster of 2:22–25 — the most extensive Isaiah 53 LXX application to the Anointed's atonement in the NT outside John 12; and the *hapax peri hamartiōn* — once for sins — declaration of 3:18 — the *ephapax* atonement vocabulary applied to the once-for-all sufficiency of the Anointed's death.

---

**KEY LEXICAL DECISIONS**

*pneuma* — "breath-of-life": 1:2 (*hagiasmō pneumatos* — sanctification of the breath-of-life), 1:11 (*pneuma Christou* — breath-of-life of the Anointed in the prophets), 1:12 (*pneumati hagiō* — Holy Breath-of-life), 3:4 (*pneumatos* — gentle and quiet breath-of-life), 3:18 (*pneumati* — made alive in the breath-of-life), 3:19 (*pneumasin* — breaths-of-life in prison), 4:6 (*pneumati* — live in the breath-of-life), 4:14 (*pneuma tēs doxēs* — breath-of-life of glory). Eight occurrences — the *pneuma* vocabulary lock across the full theological range from trinitarian election to prophetic inspiration to personal adornment to resurrection-vindication to eschatological glory.

*1:11 CAE NOTE:* *ereunōntes eis tina ē poion kairon edēlou to en autois pneuma Christou — promartyromenon ta eis Christon pathēmata kai tas meta tauta doxas* — "inquiring what person or time the breath-of-life of the Anointed in them was indicating when it predicted the sufferings of the Anointed and the subsequent glories." The *pneuma Christou* — breath-of-life of the Anointed — operating in the OT prophets is the most explicit pre-incarnate Christological pneumatology in the NT. The prophets who wrote Isaiah 53 / Psalm 22 / Daniel 9 were inspired by the *pneuma Christou* — the Anointed's own Breath-of-life was the agent of their prophetic utterance concerning him. **CAE trinitarian pneumatology node:** the Breath-of-life of the Anointed who inspires the prophets (1:11) / sent from heaven to inspire the glad-tidings proclaimers (1:12 — *pneumati hagiō apostathentos ap' ouranou*) is the same Holy Breath-of-life whose sanctification constitutes the election (1:2). The prophetic-inspiration and the community-sanctification are both works of the same *pneuma Christou / pneuma hagion*. **LXX-primacy argument: the prophets who searched and inquired were searching the LXX prophetic corpus — the *pneuma Christou* was operating through the LXX text-tradition.** The prophetic sufferings-and-glories sequence (*pathēmata / doxas*) is the LXX Isaiah 52:13–53:12 arc — servant humiliation (*pathēmata*) followed by servant exaltation (*doxas*).

*3:18 CAE NOTE:* *hoti kai Christos hapax peri hamartiōn epathen — dikaios hyper adikōn — hina hēmas prosagagē tō theō — thanatōtheis men sarki — zōopoiētheis de pneumati* — "for the Anointed also suffered once for sins — the righteous for the unrighteous — that he might bring us to God — being put to death in the flesh but made alive in the breath-of-life." The *hapax peri hamartiōn* — "once for sins" — is the *ephapax* atonement vocabulary of Hebrews (7:27 / 9:12 / 10:10) stated in the verbal form: the single unrepeatable sufficiency of the Anointed's death for sins. The *dikaios hyper adikōn* — "righteous for the unrighteous" — is the substitution formula in its starkest contrast: the righteous one (*dikaios*) in place of the unrighteous ones (*adikōn*). **Chain 1 (*hilastērion*) once-for-all substitution node:**

**Hebrews 7:27 (*ephapax* — once for all) + Hebrews 10:10 (*ephapax*) → 1 Peter 3:18 (*hapax peri hamartiōn*) = Romans 6:10 (*hapax apethanen*)**

The *thanatōtheis sarki — zōopoiētheis pneumati* — "put to death in the flesh — made alive in the breath-of-life" — is the 1 Peter form of 1 Timothy 3:16's *ephanerōthē en sarki / edikaiōthē en pneumati* — the *sarx / pneuma* antithesis expressing the humiliation-and-vindication arc of the Anointed's death and resurrection. The *pneuma* as the resurrection-power is consistent with Romans 1:4 (*kata pneuma hagiōsynēs ex anastaseōs nekrōn* — declared Son of God in power according to the breath-of-holiness by resurrection from the dead).

*4:14 CAE NOTE:* *hoti to tēs doxēs kai to tou theou pneuma eph' hymas anapauetai* — "because the breath-of-life of glory and of God rests upon you." The *pneuma tēs doxēs* — "breath-of-life of glory" — is a unique formulation in the NT — the Breath-of-life identified as the *doxa* (glory) of God resting on the suffering community. **LXX-primacy argument:** the *anapauetai* — "rests upon" — echoes Isaiah 11:2 LXX (*anapausetai ep' auton pneuma tou theou* — the breath-of-life of God will rest upon him — the Branch passage describing the Messiah). The community in suffering shares the pneumatic resting that characterizes the Anointed himself in Isaiah 11:2 LXX. **CAE chain: Isaiah 11:2 LXX (*pneuma... anapausetai ep' auton*) → 1 Peter 4:14 (*pneuma... anapauetai*)** — the messianic *pneuma*-resting extended to the suffering community.

*anagennēsas / anagegennēmenoi* — "born again / having been born again": 1:3 (*anagennēsas hēmas eis elpida zōsan* — caused us to be born again to a living hope), 1:23 (*anagegennēmenoi ouk ek sporās phthartēs alla aphthartou dia logou zōntos theou* — having been born again not of perishable seed but of imperishable through the living and abiding word of God). The *anagennāō* vocabulary is a 1 Peter distinctive — appearing only here in the NT (two occurrences both in 1 Peter). The double new-birth declaration frames the letter's opening: the new birth to living hope through resurrection (1:3) / the new birth through the imperishable word of God (1:23). The two grounds of the new birth are complementary: the resurrection of the Anointed is the event that accomplishes the new birth / the living word of God is the agent through which it is received.

*1:3 CAE NOTE:* *anagennēsas hēmas... eis elpida zōsan di' anastaseōs Iēsou Christou ek nekrōn* — "caused us to be born again to a living hope through the resurrection of Jesus the Anointed from the dead." The *elpis zōsa* — "living hope" — is the most eschatologically vital phrase in 1 Peter — the hope is *zōsa* (living / alive / not static) because it is grounded in the *anastasis* (resurrection) of the one who is himself the Living Stone (*lithon zōnta* — 2:4). The resurrection is not merely a past event that grounds a future hope — it is the present living reality from which the community's hope derives its *zōsa* (living) character. **CAE Chain 15 (Eden-to-new-creation) — new-birth-through-resurrection node:**

**Ezekiel 37:1–14 LXX (the valley of dry bones — the breath-of-life (*pneuma*) breathed into the dead → *elpida* / hope of new life) → 1 Peter 1:3 (*anagennēsas... eis elpida zōsan di' anastaseōs*)**

The new-birth-to-living-hope-through-resurrection is the NT form of the Ezekiel 37 LXX hope — the community of the dead made alive by the divine breath-of-life. Chain 15 receives its resurrection-hope node.

*klēronomia* — "inheritance": 1:4 (*klēronomian aphtharton kai amianton kai amaranton — tetērēmenēn en ouranois eis hymas* — an inheritance imperishable and undefiled and unfading — kept in heaven for you). The *klēronomia* vocabulary lock — the three alpha-privative adjectives (*a-phtharton / a-mianton / a-maranton*) — constitute the most complete negative-attribute description of the eschatological inheritance in the NT. Each attribute refutes the Folly register's characteristic decay: *aphtharton* (imperishable — against the *phthora* / corruption of the Folly register) / *amianton* (undefiled — against the moral defilement of the Folly register) / *amaranton* (unfading — against the temporal transience of earthly glory). **Chain 4 (*huiothesia*) eschatological inheritance node:**

**Romans 8:17 (*klēronomoi theou*) → Titus 3:7 (*klēronomoi zōēs aiōniou*) → 1 Peter 1:4 (*klēronomian aphtharton / amianton / amaranton*)**

The three negative-attribute description of the inheritance adds the imperishability / undefiledness / unfadingness dimensions to the *klēronomia* chain — confirming that the eschatological inheritance is the complete inversion of all Folly-register characteristics.

*amnos* — "lamb": 1:19 (*hōs amnou amōmou kai aspilou Christou* — like a lamb without blemish or spot — the Anointed). The *amnos* vocabulary lock — the Passover / sacrifice lamb applied to the Anointed. **Chain 13 (*arnion / amnos*) node:**

**Exodus 12:5 LXX (*probaton teleion arsen amōmon* — a lamb without blemish / male) → Isaiah 53:7 LXX (*hōs probaton epi sphagēn ēchthē* — like a sheep led to slaughter) → John 1:29/36 (*amnos tou theou* — Lamb of God) → 1 Corinthians 5:7 (*to pascha hēmōn etythē Christos* — our Passover was sacrificed) → 1 Peter 1:18–19 (*hōs amnou amōmou kai aspilou*)**

The *amōmos kai aspilos* — "without blemish or spot" — is the LXX sacrificial-purity formula: Exodus 12:5 LXX (*amōmon*) / Leviticus 1:3/10 LXX (*amōmon*) / Numbers 6:14 LXX (*amōmon*). **LXX-primacy argument: the Passover-lamb purity formula of 1 Peter 1:19 is constructed from Exodus 12:5 LXX + Leviticus sacrificial vocabulary LXX — the sacrificial purity-language is entirely LXX-dependent.** Chain 13 receives its ransom-lamb node with the fullest sacrificial-purity vocabulary in the NT.

*elytrōthēte* — "were ransomed": 1:18 (*elytrōthēte ek tēs mataias hymeōn anastrophēs* — you were ransomed from the futile ways inherited from your forefathers — not with silver or gold but with the precious blood of the Anointed). The *lytroō* — ransom-release — vocabulary lock in its passive aorist form: the redemption is accomplished (*aorist*) and received (*passive*). The contrast: *phthartois argyriō ē chrysiō* (perishable silver or gold) versus *timiō haimati* (precious blood). **LXX-primacy argument:** The silver-and-gold / blood contrast echoes Isaiah 52:3 LXX (*dōrean eprasthēte kai ou meta argyriou lytrōthēsesthe* — you were sold for nothing and without silver you will be ransomed) — the LXX Isaiah 52 ransoming-without-money pattern is the background for the 1 Peter ransoming by blood rather than silver. **CAE Chain 1 (*hilastērion*) ransom-by-blood node:** the most explicit ransom-with-blood statement in the NT outside Hebrews — the blood of the Anointed as the *antilytron* (1 Timothy 2:6) that effects the *apolytrōsis* (Ephesians 1:7).

*dikaiosynē* — "righteousness": 2:24 (*zōsōmen tē dikaiosynē* — live to righteousness), 3:14 (*peri dikaiosynēs paschoi* — suffer for righteousness' sake). Two occurrences — the *dikaiosynē* vocabulary lock in its behavioral (2:24) and suffering (3:14) forms. The 2:24 *tē dikaiosynē zōsōmen* — "live to righteousness" — is grounded in the atonement: "he himself bore our sins in his body on the tree *that* we might die to sin and live to righteousness." The *dikaiosynē*-life is not self-generated moral achievement but the fruit of the Anointed's sin-bearing.

*2:24 CAE NOTE:* *hos tas hamartias hēmōn autos anēnenken en tō sōmati autou epi to xylon — hina tais hamartiais apogenomenoi tē dikaiosynē zēsōmen — hou tō mōlōpi iathēte* — "he himself bore our sins in his body on the tree that we might die to sin and live to righteousness. By his wounds you have been healed." **STRUCTURAL CAE NODES**

**1:1–2 — The trinitarian election formula:**
*kata prognōsin theou patros — en hagiasmō pneumatos — eis hypakoēn kai rantismon haimatos Iēsou Christou* — "according to the foreknowledge of God the Father — in the sanctification of the breath-of-life — for obedience to Jesus the Anointed and for sprinkling with his blood." The most complete trinitarian election formula in the NT — three persons / three roles / one act of election:

**God the Father** — *kata prognōsin* — according to foreknowledge — the eternal sovereign ground of election
**The Holy Breath-of-life** — *en hagiasmō* — in sanctification — the present operational agent of election
**Jesus the Anointed** — *eis hypakoēn kai rantismon haimatos* — for obedience and sprinkling of his blood — the historical accomplishment of election

**CAE cross-confirmation: 2 Thessalonians 2:13 (*heilato... en hagiasmō pneumatos kai pistei alētheias*) = 1 Peter 1:2 (*prognōsin theou patros / hagiasmō pneumatos / haimatos Iēsou Christou*)** — two independent trinitarian election formulas with identical three-agent structure. **LXX-primacy argument:** The *rantismos haimatos* — "sprinkling of blood" — is drawn from Exodus 24:8 LXX (*rantisas ton laon* — Moses sprinkled the people / covenant-ratification blood-sprinkling) and Leviticus 8:30 LXX (Aaron and sons sprinkled with blood at ordination). The Sinai covenant-ratification sprinkling becomes the new covenant election-sprinkling — the blood of the Anointed replacing the Sinai blood.

**1:13–21 — Holy living / ransomed by blood / foreknown lamb:**
The letter's foundational ethical-grounding unit — the holiness command (1:15–16 — LXX citation of Leviticus 11:44/19:2) grounded in the ransom by blood (1:18–19) grounded in the pre-temporal foreknowledge (1:20). The structure: (a) *hagioi genēthēte* — be holy (1:15–16 — Leviticus 19:2 LXX direct citation); (b) conduct yourselves with fear knowing you were ransomed (1:17–18); (c) with the precious blood of the Anointed like a lamb (*amnos*) without blemish (1:19); (d) foreknown before the foundation of the world / manifested in the last times (1:20). The holiness command is grounded not in human moral capacity but in the ransom that has already been accomplished by the foreknown lamb.

**LXX-primacy argument at 1:15–16:** *kata ton kalesanta hymas hagion kai autoi hagioi en pasē anastrophē genēthēte — dioti gegraptai: hagioi esesthe hoti egō hagios eimi* — "as he who called you is holy / you also be holy / for it is written: You shall be holy for I am holy." The citation is Leviticus 19:2 LXX (*hagioi esesthe hoti egō kyrios ho theos hymōn hagios* — you shall be holy because I the Lord your God am holy) — the *imitatio Dei* holiness command applied to the new covenant community. The Leviticus 19:2 LXX citation makes the new covenant community the heir of the Sinai holiness-call — the same divine character (*hagios*) that grounded Israel's holiness call grounds the community's holiness call.

**2:4–9 — The living stone / spiritual house / royal priesthood:**
The most sustained *naos*-chain community-identity statement in the Catholic Epistles — drawing on five LXX texts in six verses:

(1) *lithon zōnta* (living stone — 2:4) — the Anointed as the living foundation stone — unique combination of *zōn* (living) with the stone-imagery
(2) *oikos pneumatikos / hierateuma hagion* (spiritual house / holy priesthood — 2:5) — the community built on the living stone as the new temple and new priesthood
(3) Isaiah 28:16 LXX (*idou tithēmi en Siōn lithon akrogōniaion eklekton entimon — kai ho pisteuōn ep' autō ou mē kataischynthē* — 2:6 — Zion cornerstone citation)
(4) Psalm 117:22 LXX (*lithon hon apedokimasan hoi oikodomenountes — houtos egenēthē eis kephalēn gōnias* — stone the builders rejected became the cornerstone — 2:7)
(5) Isaiah 8:14 LXX (*kai estai... eis lithon proskommatos kai eis petran skandalou* — stone of stumbling and rock of offense — 2:8)
(6) Exodus 19:5–6 LXX + Isaiah 43:20–21 LXX + Deuteronomy 7:6 LXX (*genos eklekton / basileion hierateuma / ethnos hagion / laos eis peripoiēsin* — chosen race / royal priesthood / holy nation / people for possession — 2:9)

**LXX-primacy argument: 1 Peter 2:4–9 is the most sustained LXX-composite passage in the Catholic Epistles — six distinct LXX texts woven into a single community-identity declaration. The entire passage is impossible without the LXX.** The *basileion hierateuma* of 2:9 is the direct LXX Exodus 19:6 citation (*hymeis de esesthe moi basileion hierateuma kai ethnos hagion*) — the Sinai-covenant community identity (Exodus 19 LXX) transferred to the new covenant community. **Chain 9 (*skēnē / naos*) community-identity completion node:**

**Exodus 25:8 LXX (God's dwelling among his people) → Exodus 19:6 LXX (*basileion hierateuma*) → 1 Peter 2:5 (*oikos pneumatikos / hierateuma hagion*) → 1 Peter 2:9 (*basileion hierateuma / laos periousiou*) → Revelation 1:6 (*basileian / hiereis* — kingdom and priests)**

The *naos*-chain receives its fullest community-priesthood node — the community is simultaneously the *naos* (spiritual house / 2:5) and the *hierateuma* (priesthood / 2:5/9) — the building and its ministers unified in the same community.

**2:21–25 — The Isaiah 53 LXX atonement-cluster:**
The most extensive Isaiah 53 LXX application in the NT outside Hebrews and John 12 — five Isaiah 53 LXX echoes in five verses:

v.22 — Isaiah 53:9 LXX (*hamartian ouk epoiēsen* — he committed no sin / *oude heurethē dolos en tō stomati autou* — nor was deceit found in his mouth)
v.23 — Isaiah 53:7 LXX (*kai autos dia to kekakōsthai ouk anoigei to stoma* — he did not open his mouth when mistreated — sheep before shearers)
v.24a — Isaiah 53:12 LXX (*kai autos hamartias pollōn anēnenken* — he bore the sins of many)
v.24b — Isaiah 53:5 LXX (*tō mōlōpi autou hēmeis iathēmen* — by his wound we were healed)
v.25 — Isaiah 53:6 LXX (*pantes hōs probata eplannēthēmen* — all like sheep have gone astray)

**LXX-primacy argument: 1 Peter 2:21–25 is a five-verse Isaiah 53 LXX catena — the most sustained application of the Servant Song to the Anointed's atonement in the Catholic Epistles. Every significant atonement-claim in the passage is drawn from Isaiah 53 LXX, not the MT.** The MT of Isaiah 53 provides the same narrative but the specific verbal correspondences (*hamartian ouk epoiēsen / anēnenken / mōlōpi / probata eplannēthēmen*) confirm LXX dependence. Chain 1 (*hilastērion*) Isaiah 53 LXX primary node confirmed with five-point triangulation.

**3:18–22 — Once for sins / made alive / subjection of powers:**
The most compact cosmic-Christological statement in 1 Peter — moving from the once-for-all atonement (3:18) through the proclamation to the imprisoned spirits (3:19–20) through the Noah-ark / baptism typology (3:20–21) to the ascension and cosmic subjection (3:22). The cosmic scope: the Anointed's atoning death reaches back to the generation of Noah / his resurrection-vindication reaches forward to the subjection of all cosmic powers / his ascension establishes him at the right hand of God over all *angeloi / archai / exousiai* (angels / rulers / authorities). **Chain 9 (*skēnē / naos*) cosmic-sovereignty node:** the Anointed who is the *Archipoimenos* (5:4) and the *lithos akrogōniaios* (cornerstone — 2:6) is also the one at whose right hand all cosmic powers are subjected (3:22) — the full sovereignty arc from creation-corner to cosmic-throne.

**The Noah-baptism typology (3:20–21)** is the most explicit OT-typology for baptism in the NT outside 1 Corinthians 10:1–4. The ark (*kibōtos*) carried eight persons safely through (*diesōthēsan di' hydatos* — were brought safely through water) / baptism (*baptisma*) now saves through the resurrection of the Anointed (*di' anastaseōs Iēsou Christou*). **LXX-primacy argument:** The Noah narrative is from Genesis 6–8 LXX — the *diasōzō* vocabulary (*diesōthēsan*) and the *oligoi* (few) / *oktō psychai* (eight souls) details are drawn from Genesis 7:7/23 LXX. The typological argument depends on the LXX narrative.

**4:7–11 — The end is near / charisma / speaking as oracles of God:**
The most concentrated eschatological-community instruction in 1 Peter — four imperatives governed by the *telos pantōn ēggiken* (the end of all things has drawn near): be sober-minded / keep loving earnestly / show hospitality / serve with the *charisma* received. The *charisma* — gift of grace — vocabulary lock: every member's *charisma* is an expression of *oikonomoi poikilēs charitos theou* — "stewards of God's varied grace." The *poikilēs charis* — "varied grace" — is the 1 Peter form of the *poikilē sophia* (manifold wisdom — Ephesians 3:10) — the divine grace/wisdom expressing itself in multiple forms through the community's multiple charismata.

The *logion theou* — "oracle of God" — applied to speaking (4:11) is the highest possible description of community speech: speaking as though uttering *logia theou* — the divine oracles of the LXX tradition (Romans 3:2 — *ta logia tou theou* — the oracles of God entrusted to Israel). **LXX-primacy argument:** *logia theou* is the LXX term for the divine utterances — Psalm 118:11 LXX (*en tē kardia mou ekrypsā ta logia sou* — I have hidden your *logia* in my heart) / Numbers 24:4/16 LXX (Balaam's *logia theou*). The community's Spirit-empowered speech carries the weight of LXX *logia theou* — the highest claim made for community speech in the NT.

**5:1–11 — Shepherd the flock / humble / the Archipoimenos:**
The letter's pastoral conclusion — the elders as undershepherds / the community in humility / the adversary as prowling lion / the God of all grace who will restore. The *Archipoimenos phanentos* — "when the Chief Shepherd appears" — is the *parousia* / *epiphaneia* as the moment of pastoral vindication — the undershepherds receive the *amarantinon tēs doxēs stephanon* — "unfading crown of glory" — at the appearing of the Chief Shepherd. **LXX-primacy argument:** The *amarantinos* — "unfading / amaranth-crowned" — echoes Wisdom of Solomon 4:2 LXX (*en aiōni nikēphoros adikias... stephanos amarantinos* — a crown that never fades) — the deuterocanonical unfading-crown vocabulary applied to the eschatological pastoral reward.

The closing humility-exaltation pattern (5:6 — *tapeinōthēte... hypsōsei hymas* — humble yourselves / he will exalt you) echoes James 4:10 and Matthew 23:12 / Luke 14:11 / 18:14 — the Wisdom-register inversion principle stated as a divine promise: the Folly register grasps at exaltation (Philippians 2:6 — *harpagmos*) / the Wisdom register embraces humility and receives exaltation from God.

**JAMES IN THE CORPUS ARCHITECTURE**

James opens the Catholic Epistles — seven letters (James / 1 Peter / 2 Peter / 1 John / 2 John / 3 John / Jude) addressed to the broader community of believers rather than to specific congregations or individuals. James is the most wisdom-oriented letter in the NT canon — its closest literary parallel is not Paul but the LXX wisdom books (Proverbs / Sirach / Wisdom of Solomon) and the prophetic tradition (Amos / Isaiah / Jeremiah). The letter's sustained engagement with *sophia* — wisdom — as the governing category of the Christian life makes it the NT's most direct heir to the LXX wisdom tradition that underlies the entire CAE framework.

The letter's position in the CAE is often misread as tension with Paul — the *dikaioutai ex ergōn kai ouk ek pisteōs monon* of 2:24 (declared righteous by works and not by faith alone) appears to contradict Romans 3:28 (*dikaiousthai pistei anthrōpon chōris ergōn nomou* — a person is declared righteous by faith apart from works of the Law). The CAE resolves this through the two-*dikaioō* analysis: Paul's *dikaioō* is forensic — the divine verdict of righteousness rendered at the moment of faith / James's *dikaioō* is demonstrative — the visible vindication of genuine faith through its works. Abraham is *edikaiōthē* before God at Genesis 15:6 (Paul's text — the faith-reckoning) and *edikaiōthē* before witnesses at Genesis 22 (James's text — the faith-completed-by-works). The two *dikaioō* uses are not contradictory but complementary — they address different questions: Paul asks *what makes a person righteous before God?* / James asks *what demonstrates that a person's faith is genuine?*

The letter's CAE function is concentrated in five nodes: the *sophia anōthen* — wisdom from above — taxonomy of 3:17 which is the most complete Wisdom-register positive-content catalog in the NT outside Philippians 4:8; the *dipsychos* — double-minded — analysis of 1:8 and 4:8 which is the most precise CAE Wisdom/Folly register-mixture description in the corpus; the *aparchēn tōn ktismatōn* — firstfruits of his creatures — declaration of 1:18 which adds a creation-firstfruits node to Chain 14; the *logos tēs alētheias* — word of truth — as the regenerative agent of 1:18; and the *eleos* — mercy / steadfast love — triumph over judgment of 2:13 which is the most compact *eleos*-supremacy statement in the NT.

---

**KEY LEXICAL DECISIONS**

*sophia* — "wisdom": 1:5 (ask God for *sophia*), 3:13 (who is *sophos*?), 3:15 (*sophia* earthly / unspiritual / demonic), 3:17 (*sophia* from above). Four occurrences — the *sophia* vocabulary lock at its most taxonomically precise. James is the only NT writer who explicitly distinguishes two *sophiai* — the earthly/unspiritual/demonic wisdom versus the wisdom from above — and provides a complete behavioral taxonomy of each.

*3:15 CAE NOTE:* *ouk estin autē hē sophia anōthen katerchomonē alla epigeios — psychikē — daimoniōdēs* — "this is not the wisdom that comes down from above but is earthly — unspiritual (*psychikē*) — demonic." The three-term taxonomy of counterfeit wisdom: (1) *epigeios* — earthly — oriented toward the present age / the horizontal plane / material advantage; (2) *psychikē* — unspiritual / soul-level — the same term as 1 Corinthians 2:14 (*psychikos anthrōpos* — the natural / soul-person who cannot receive the things of the Breath-of-life). The *psychikē sophia* is wisdom operating at the *psychē*-level — the volitional / emotional faculty — without the *pneuma*'s orientation toward God; (3) *daimoniōdēs* — demonic — at its deepest level the counterfeit wisdom is not merely human limitation but spiritual opposition. **CAE Wisdom/Folly register taxonomy confirmed:** the counterfeit wisdom is: earthly (horizontal orientation) / *psychikē* (unspiritual — operating below the *pneuma* level) / demonic (spiritually sourced in the Folly register's ultimate power). The three terms map the descending depth of the Folly register: surface (earthly) / structural (soul-level without *pneuma*) / ultimate (demonic sourcing).

*3:17 CAE NOTE:* *hē de anōthen sophia prōton men hagnē estin — epeita eirēnikē — epieikēs — eupeithēs — mesē eleous kai karpōn agathōn — adiakritos — anypokritos* — "but the wisdom that is from above is first pure — then peaceable — gentle — open to reason — full of mercy and good fruits — impartial and sincere." The most complete Wisdom-register positive-content taxonomy in the NT — eight characteristics in ascending depth:

*hagnē* (pure) — the Wisdom register's foundational purity / undivided orientation / absence of the double-mindedness of 1:8 and 4:8
*eirēnikē* (peaceable) — the Wisdom register's relational orientation / the *eirēnē* that the God-of-peace gives (Philippians 4:7 / 1 Thessalonians 5:23)
*epieikēs* (gentle / reasonable) — the Wisdom register's character texture / Philippians 4:5 (*to epieikes hymōn gnōsthētō pasin*)
*eupeithēs* (open to reason / compliant with good) — the Wisdom register's cognitive posture / teachable / non-resistant to truth
*mesē eleous kai karpōn agathōn* (full of mercy and good fruits) — the Wisdom register's relational and practical expression / *eleos* as the covenantal-love category / good fruits as the behavioral output
*adiakritos* (impartial / without discrimination) — the Wisdom register's social impartiality / no favoritism (the letter's anti-partiality concern of chapter 2)
*anypokritos* (sincere / without hypocrisy) — the Wisdom register's integrity / the absence of the double-mouth of 3:10

**CAE Wisdom-register cross-reference:** James 3:17's eight-characteristic taxonomy complements Philippians 4:8's eight-item thought-catalog — together they constitute the most complete Wisdom-register behavioral description in the NT. The Philippians catalog describes the *content* of the Wisdom-register mind's meditation / the James catalog describes the *character* of the Wisdom-register person's conduct. Both catalogs begin with purity/truth as the foundational category.

**LXX-primacy argument:** The *sophia anōthen* of James 3:17 is constructed on the LXX Wisdom tradition: Proverbs 8:1–9 LXX (*hē sophia ekēryxen* — wisdom cried out / Wisdom as divine-origin gift) / Wisdom of Solomon 7:22–26 LXX (*pneuma noeron — hagion — monogenes — polymerēs — leptotaton — eukinēton — tranotaton — amōmoton — philanthrōpon — bebaion — asphales — amerimos* — the spirit of Wisdom as intelligent / holy / singular / many-faceted / subtle / mobile / clear / unblemished / loving / steadfast / secure / tranquil) — the most extensive Wisdom-attribute list in the LXX. **The James 3:17 taxonomy is an adaptation and compression of the Wisdom of Solomon 7:22–26 LXX Wisdom-attribute list** — the deuterocanonical LXX text provides the catalog-form that James employs. **LXX-primacy argument confirmed: James 3:17 is compositionally dependent on Wisdom of Solomon 7:22–26 LXX — the *sophia anōthen* taxonomy cannot be fully understood without the deuterocanonical LXX background.**

*dipsychos* — "double-minded": 1:8 (*anēr dipsychos — akatastatos en pasais tais hodois autou* — a double-minded man — unstable in all his ways), 4:8 (*katharisate kardias hoi dipsychoi* — purify your hearts you double-minded). Hapax in the NT outside James — the *dipsychos* vocabulary is unique to James in the NT canon. The *di-psychos* — "two-souled / double-souled" — is the most precise CAE description of the Wisdom/Folly register-mixture: the person who has not committed fully to either register / who oscillates between the Wisdom and Folly orientations / whose *kardia* is divided. **CAE register-mixture analysis:**

The *dipsychos* is not the person fully in the Folly register (who is consistently oriented away from God) but the person whose register is unstable — committed to neither. The *akatastatos* — "unstable / without fixed position" — characterizes the *dipsychos* person's whole life: unstable prayer (1:6–8) / unstable conduct (4:8). The *dipsychos* is the James form of the Lukewarm community of Revelation 3:16 (*oute psychros oute zestos* — neither cold nor hot) — the middle position that is not neutral but self-defeating. CAE Wisdom/Folly axis: the *dipsychos* condition is the register-mixture's personal form — simultaneously drawn toward the Wisdom register (asks God for wisdom / 1:5) and toward the Folly register (doubts / draws back / loves the world / 4:4). The 4:8 cure: *katharisate kardias* — purify your hearts — the *kardia* purification is the resolution of the double-mind by the full orientation of the *kardia* toward God.

**LXX-primacy argument:** *dipsychos* may derive from Psalm 11:2 LXX (*en kardia kai en kardia elalēsan* — they spoke with heart and heart / with a divided heart) — the double-heart condition of the Psalm expressed as double-soul in James. The two-*kardia* condition of the LXX Psalm becomes the two-*psychē* (*dipsychos*) of James — a direct LXX-shaped neologism.

*eleos* — "mercy / steadfast love": 2:13 (*eleos katakauchātai kriseōs* — mercy triumphs over judgment), 3:17 (*mesē eleous* — full of mercy). Two structurally significant occurrences. The *eleos* vocabulary lock — the LXX *hesed* / steadfast covenantal love — appears in James as both the highest attribute of the wisdom-from-above (3:17) and the ultimate triumph over judgment (2:13).

*2:13 CAE NOTE:* *hē gar krisis aneleos tō mē poiēsanti eleos — katakauchātai eleos kriseōs* — "for judgment is without mercy to one who has shown no mercy. Mercy triumphs over judgment." The *katakauchātai* — "triumphs over / exults over / boasts against" — is the strongest possible statement of *eleos* supremacy. **CAE Chain 10 (*alētheia* / *eleos*) node — the most compact *eleos*-supremacy statement in the NT:**

**Exodus 34:6–7 LXX (*kyrios ho theos oiktirmōn kai elēmōn — makrothymos kai polyeleos* — the Lord God merciful and compassionate — slow to anger and abounding in *eleos*) → Micah 6:8 LXX (*agapan eleon* — to love *eleos*) → James 2:13 (*katakauchātai eleos kriseōs*)** — the *eleos* that defines the divine character (Exodus 34:6 LXX) is the *eleos* that the wisdom-from-above community embodies (3:17) and that ultimately triumphs over the judgment that operates without it (2:13). Cross-link: Matthew 5:7 (*makarioi hoi eleēmones hoti autoi eleēthēsontai* — blessed are the merciful for they shall receive mercy) — the reciprocal *eleos* principle of the Beatitudes stated as the eschatological principle in James.

*logos tēs alētheias* — "word of truth": 1:18 (*boulētheis apekuēsen hēmas logō alētheias* — of his own will he brought us forth by the word of truth). The *logos tēs alētheias* as the regenerative agent — the word of truth that produces the new birth. **CAE Chain 10 (*alētheia*) regeneration node:**

**John 1:12–13 (*ek theou egennēthēsan* — born of God / born not of the will of man) → James 1:18 (*logō alētheias apekuēsen* — brought forth by the word of truth) → 1 Peter 1:23 (*anagegennēmenoi... dia logou zōntos theou* — born again through the living word of God)**

The three new-birth witnesses use different vocabulary but the same logic: new birth is by divine initiative (*boulētheis* — of his own will / 1:18) through the divine word (*logos tēs alētheias* / *logos zōntos theou*). Chain 10 receives its James regeneration node. The *logos tēs alētheias* is simultaneously the CAE v2.2 *Davar* — the spoken word as enacted reality — applied to the new birth: the truth-word of God creates the new life it declares, consistent with the Hebrew phenomenology that reality and speech share the root *davar*.

*aparchē* — "firstfruits": 1:18 (*aparchēn tina tōn autou ktismatōn* — a kind of firstfruits of his creatures). The *aparchē* vocabulary lock — firstfruits offering — applied to the redeemed community as the firstfruits of God's creation. **Chain 14 (*prōtotokos / aparchē*) node:**

**Leviticus 23:10–11 LXX (*aparchēn* — the firstfruits offering) → 1 Corinthians 15:20 (*aparchē tōn kekoimēmenōn* — firstfruits of those who have fallen asleep) → 2 Thessalonians 2:13 (*aparchēn eis sōtērian* — firstfruits for salvation) → James 1:18 (*aparchēn tōn ktismatōn* — firstfruits of his creatures)**

The Anointed is the *aparchē* of the resurrection (1 Corinthians 15:20) / the elect are the *aparchē* of salvation (2 Thessalonians 2:13) / the redeemed community is the *aparchē* of God's new creation (James 1:18). The three *aparchē* applications are concentric: the Anointed's resurrection as the first-installment of the general resurrection / the elect community as the first-installment of the eschatological salvation / the redeemed as the first-installment of the new creation. Chain 14 receives its creation-firstfruits node.

*kardia* — "heart": 1:26 (*kardian autou* — deceives his heart), 3:14 (*kardiais* — bitter jealousy in your hearts), 4:8 (*kardias* — purify your hearts), 5:5 (*kardias* — fattened your hearts), 5:8 (*kardias* — establish your hearts). Five occurrences — the *kardia* vocabulary lock at high density. The five uses span the full register range: the deceived heart (1:26 — Folly register) / the jealousy-filled heart (3:14 — Folly register) / the double-minded heart requiring purification (4:8 — register-mixture) / the fattened heart of the self-indulgent rich (5:5 — Folly register) / the established heart awaiting the *parousia* (5:8 — Wisdom register). The *kardia* is the register-orientation faculty throughout — the site of deception / jealousy / double-mindedness / self-indulgence (Folly) or purification / establishment (Wisdom).

*5:8 CAE NOTE:* *stērixate tas kardias hymōn — hoti hē parousia tou kyriou ēggiken* — "establish your hearts for the coming of the Lord is at hand." The *stērixate tas kardias* — establish your hearts — is the James form of 1 Thessalonians 3:13 (*stērixai hymas tas kardias amemptous* — establish your hearts blameless) and 2 Thessalonians 2:17 (*stērixai en panti ergō* — establish in every good work). The *parousia* as the ground and horizon of the heart-establishment is consistent across the Pauline and Catholic Epistles — the *kardia* is established *toward* the *parousia* / the *parousia* is the event that validates or exposes the *kardia*'s orientation.

*psychē* — "soul / life": 1:21 (*psychas* — your souls), 5:20 (*psychēn* — his soul from death). Two occurrences — the *psychē* vocabulary lock at its most soteriologically significant. The *psychē* of 1:21 is the recipient of the *logos tēs alētheias* — the implanted word saves the *psychē* / the *psychē* is the level of the person that the truth-word reaches and transforms. The *psychē* of 5:20 is the soul of the wanderer brought back — rescued from death. **Chain 1 (*hilastērion*) / *psychē*-rescue node:** the covering of sins (5:20 — *kalypsei plēthos hamartiōn*) and the saving of the *psychē* from death — the pastoral version of the atonement enacted through brotherly restoration.

*pneuma* — "breath-of-life": 2:26 (*sōma chōris pneumatos nekron estin* — the body apart from the breath-of-life is dead), 4:5 (*to pneuma ho katōkisen en hēmin* — the breath-of-life that he has made to dwell in us). Two occurrences — structurally opposed and complementary.

*2:26 CAE NOTE:* *hōsper gar to sōma chōris pneumatos nekron estin — houtōs kai hē pistis chōris ergōn nekra estin* — "for as the body apart from the breath-of-life is dead — so also faith apart from works is dead." The faith/works relationship is here described through the *pneuma / sōma* relationship — the same structural logic: the *pneuma* animates the *sōma* and makes it a living person / genuine faith animates works and makes them the expression of a living faith. Dead faith — faith without works — is to genuine faith as the corpse (*sōma chōris pneumatos*) is to the living body. CAE anthropological note: the *pneuma / sōma* analogy for faith/works is the most precise structural argument for the faith-works relationship in the NT — it establishes that works are to faith what the *pneuma* is to the body: not an addition or supplement but the animating principle that makes the visible form alive. The *psychē* (1 Thessalonians 5:23 anthropological triad) and *pneuma* here function as the animating life-principle — consistent with Genesis 2:7 LXX (*pnoēn zōēs* / *psychēn zōsan*).

*4:5 CAE NOTE:* *ē dokeite hoti kenōs hē graphē legei — pros phthonon epipothei to pneuma ho katōkisen en hēmin* — "or do you suppose it is to no purpose that the Scripture says: He yearns jealously over the breath-of-life that he has made to dwell in us?" The divine *epipothia* — yearning / longing / intense desire — over the *pneuma* that dwells in believers is the most intimate divine-love statement in James. The *katōkisen* — "made to dwell / caused to inhabit" — connects to Chain 9: the divine *katoikētērion* (Ephesians 2:22 — the called-out assembly as the divine dwelling) and the *pneuma enoikountos* (2 Timothy 1:14 — the indwelling Breath-of-life). **CAE Chain 9 (*skēnē / naos*) divine-jealousy node:** God's jealous yearning over the indwelling *pneuma* is the personal-affective expression of the *naos*-theology — the God who dwells in his people through the *pneuma* is jealously protective of that indwelling against the *philía tou kosmou* (friendship with the world / 4:4) that threatens it.

**LXX-primacy argument at 4:5:** The *pros phthonon epipothei* formula is a difficult text with no exact LXX citation identified. The closest parallels are: Exodus 20:5 LXX (*egō gar eimi kyrios ho theos sou — theos zēlōtēs* — I am the Lord your God — a jealous God) and Deuteronomy 32:10–11 LXX (the Lord's protective care over Israel as his portion — *epipothei* vocabulary). The divine jealousy over the *pneuma* is the NT personalized form of the LXX divine jealousy over Israel — the *pneuma*-indwelling community is the new covenant form of Israel as YHWH's portion.

*dikaiosynē* — "righteousness": 1:20 (*dikaiosynēn theou* — righteousness of God), 2:21/23/24/25 (*edikaiōthē* / *elogisthē eis dikaiosynēn* / *dikaioutai* / *edikaiōthē* — declared righteous / counted as righteousness / declared righteous), 3:18 (*karpos... dikaiosynēs* — harvest of righteousness), 5:6/16 (*dikaion* / *dikaiou* — the righteous person / the righteous person's prayer).

*2:21–24 CAE NOTE — The Two-dikaioō Analysis:*
The James faith/works passage requires the CAE two-*dikaioō* framework for correct reading. Two distinct questions / two distinct uses of *dikaioō*:

**Paul's *dikaioō* (Romans 3:28):** *How is a person made righteous before God?* → by faith alone (*pistei... chōris ergōn nomou*). Paul's proof-text: Genesis 15:6 (*elogisthē autō eis dikaiosynēn* — it was reckoned to him as righteousness) — Abraham was *dikaios* before God at the moment of faith / before any works.

**James's *dikaioō* (2:21/24):** *How is genuine faith demonstrated / vindicated before witnesses?* → by works that complete the faith (*hē pistis synērgei tois ergois autou kai ek tōn ergōn hē pistis eteleiōthē* — faith was active alongside his works and faith was completed by his works / v.22). James's proof-text: Genesis 22 — Abraham's offering of Isaac — which is the visible completion and demonstration of the faith of Genesis 15:6. The Scripture of Genesis 15:6 (*elogisthē auto eis dikaiosynēn* — 2:23) was *fulfilled* (*eplērōthē* — 2:23) by the works of Genesis 22 — the earlier faith-reckoning was demonstrated and completed by the later faith-works.

**CAE structural note:** The two passages are reading two different moments in the same Abraham narrative — Genesis 15:6 (forensic *dikaioō* — declared righteous before God by faith) and Genesis 22 (demonstrative *dikaioō* — vindicated/shown to be righteous before witnesses by works). The tension is not contradiction but complementarity — forensic righteousness (Paul) and demonstrative righteousness (James) are the two dimensions of the single *dikaiosynē* reality: you are declared righteous before God by faith / that righteousness is demonstrated before the world by the works that faith produces.

*parousia* — "coming": 5:7 (*heōs tēs parousias tou kyriou* — until the coming of the Lord), 5:8 (*hē parousia tou kyriou ēggiken* — the coming of the Lord is at hand). Two occurrences — the *parousia* vocabulary lock as the eschatological horizon of patient endurance. The farmer waits for the early and late rains (5:7) — the community waits for the *parousia* with the same agricultural patience. The *ēggiken* — "has drawn near / is at hand" — is the realized-eschatological formula of the NT (*ēggiken hē basileia* — the kingdom has drawn near / Matthew 4:17). The *parousia* is near — established hearts (5:8) and non-grumbling conduct (5:9) are the community's eschatological posture.

*Kyrios Sabaōth* — "Lord of hosts": 5:4 — *eis ta ōta kyriou Sabaōth eiselēlythan* — "have reached the ears of the Lord of hosts." The *Kyrios Sabaōth* — the LXX's rendering of YHWH *ṣĕbāʾôt* — is unique in the NT outside Romans 9:29 (also a LXX citation — Isaiah 1:9 LXX). **LXX-primacy argument:** the *Kyrios Sabaōth* is an untranslated LXX transliteration of the Hebrew divine title — it appears in the NT only in direct LXX citations (Romans 9:29 / James 5:4) — confirming the LXX as the text-base. The James 5:4 usage echoes the prophetic cry-of-the-oppressed passages: Isaiah 5:9 LXX (*ēkousthē gar eis ta ōta kyriou Sabaōth* — it was heard in the ears of the Lord of hosts) and Isaiah 5:7 LXX (the Lord expected *dikaiosynē* but heard a cry of injustice). **LXX composite: James 5:4 cites or echoes Isaiah 5:7/9 LXX** — the rich who withhold wages are the Isaiah 5 vineyard-owners whose injustice reaches the Lord of hosts' ears.

*teleios* — "complete / perfect / mature": 1:4 (*teleioi* — complete and whole), 1:17 (*teleion dōrēma* — every perfect gift), 1:25 (*nomon teleion* — the perfect law), 3:2 (*teleios anēr* — a perfect / complete man). Four occurrences — the *teleios* vocabulary lock at its James-distinctive concentration. The *teleios* in James is not moral perfection (sinlessness) but *wholeness / completeness / maturity* — the fully developed single-register person without double-mindedness. The *teleios anēr* of 3:2 is the one who has mastered the tongue — the smallest and most powerful member — and therefore controls the whole body. CAE Wisdom-register anthropological completion: the *teleios* / *holoklēros* (*whole* — 1:4 / from the same root as 1 Thessalonians 5:23 *holoteleis*) person is the James form of the fully sanctified *pneuma / psychē / sōma* of 1 Thessalonians 5:23 — complete / whole / lacking nothing.

**STRUCTURAL CAE NODES**

**1:2–18 — Trial-patience-wisdom-birth sequence:**
The letter's opening unit constitutes a complete Wisdom-register formation arc in seventeen verses: (a) trials produce *hypomonē* (1:2–3) — the same *hypomonē tēs elpidos* of 1 Thessalonians 1:3; (b) *hypomonē* produces *teleioi kai holoklēroi* — complete and whole (1:4); (c) the *teleios* person needs *sophia* — ask God (1:5–8); (d) the *dipsychos* cannot receive — single-mindedness required (1:6–8); (e) the lowly exalted / the rich humbled (1:9–11) — the Wisdom/Folly social inversion; (f) steadfastness under trial receives the *stephanos tēs zōēs* — crown of life (1:12); (g) temptation comes from *epithymia* — desire — not God (1:13–15); (h) every good gift from above / God the Father of lights (1:16–17); (i) brought forth by the *logos tēs alētheias* as *aparchēn ktismatōn* — firstfruits of creatures (1:18). The arc: trial → patience → completeness → wisdom-asking → single-mindedness → crown of life → divine gift-source → new birth. The Wisdom-register formation sequence complete.

**1:19–27 — The implanted word / doers not hearers:**
The most important *logos* passage in James — the *logos* received with meekness (1:21) / the mirror-analogy (1:23–24) / the perfect law of freedom (1:25). The *emphytos logos* — "implanted word" — is the NT form of Jeremiah 31:33 LXX (*epi kardias autōn grapsō autous* — I will write them on their hearts) — the new covenant word written into the person rather than imposed from without. **CAE Chain 8 (*diathēkē kainē*) implanted-word node:** Jeremiah 31:33 LXX → 2 Corinthians 3:3 (written on hearts by the Breath-of-life) → James 1:21 (*emphytos logos*) — three witnesses to the new covenant heart-writing. The *nomon teleion ton tēs eleutherias* — "perfect law of freedom" — (1:25) is the new covenant's law: perfect (not partial / not temporary) and liberating (not enslaving) — the Wisdom-register's relationship to the divine word is characterized by *eleuthereia* (freedom) because the *logos* is now internal and *emphytos* rather than external and coercive.

**2:1–13 — Partiality / the royal law / mercy over judgment:**
The letter's most socially concentrated unit — the called-out assembly's treatment of the poor as the test case of Wisdom-register conduct. The *basilikon nomon* — "royal law" — of 2:8 is Leviticus 19:18 LXX (*agapēseis ton plēsion sou hōs seauton* — you shall love your neighbor as yourself) — the *agapē*-command as the sovereign law governing the community. **LXX-primacy argument:** James 2:8's use of *basilikon nomon* for Leviticus 19:18 LXX establishes the LXX *agapē*-command as the *basileus* — king / sovereign — over all other conduct regulations. The *eleos katakauchātai kriseōs* (2:13) is the *eleos* of Leviticus 19:18 — the neighbor-love — operating as the eschatological principle that overrides judgment. Those who have shown *eleos* (embodied Leviticus 19:18 LXX) receive *eleos* at judgment / those who have not are judged without it.

**2:14–26 — Faith and works / the two-dikaioō:**
The most debated passage in James — now resolved by the two-*dikaioō* framework. The key structural observation: James uses two different proof-texts for Abraham's *dikaioō* — Genesis 15:6 LXX (faith-reckoning / cited in 2:23 — *elogisthē autō eis dikaiosynēn*) and the implied Genesis 22 (works-demonstration / the offering of Isaac / 2:21). The Genesis 15:6 LXX citation is the same text Paul uses in Romans 4:3 and Galatians 3:6 — but James uses it to establish that the faith of Genesis 15:6 was *fulfilled* (*eplērōthē* — 2:23) by the works of Genesis 22. **LXX-primacy argument:** both James and Paul work from Genesis 15:6 LXX (*elogisthē autō eis dikaiosynēn* — reckoned to him as righteousness) — the imputation vocabulary is LXX-dependent in both. The difference is the question each asks of the text: Paul asks what the reckoning establishes / James asks what completes and demonstrates it. The LXX text is the common ground of both arguments.

**3:1–12 — The tongue as register-indicator:**
The tongue passage is the CAE's most concentrated *logos*-as-power analysis in the NT. The tongue is simultaneously: a fire setting the whole course of life ablaze (3:6) / a rudder guiding the whole body (3:4) / a source of both blessing and cursing from the same mouth (3:10). The structural problem: the same mouth blesses the Lord and Father and curses people *made in the likeness of God* (*kath' homoiōsin theou gegonotas* — 3:9). **CAE Chain 15 (Eden-to-new-creation) / *eikōn* — cursing the image:**

**Genesis 1:26–27 LXX (*kath' homoiōsin hēmeteran* — in our likeness — humanity as image-bearer) → James 3:9 (*kath' homoiōsin theou gegonotas* — made in the likeness of God)**

The tongue that curses human beings curses the *eikōn* / *homoiōsis* of God — every human being is a *kath' homoiōsin theou* — made in the divine likeness — and cursing them is a Folly-register assault on the divine image. Chain 15 receives its image-protection ethical node: the *kat' eikona / kath' homoiōsin* creation mandate has direct ethical implications — every *homoiōsis theou* bearer deserves the Wisdom register's *eleos* and *agapē* not the Folly register's cursing.

**5:1–6 — The prophetic cry against the rich:**
The most prophetically intense passage in the NT outside Revelation — modeled on the LXX prophetic tradition (Amos 5–6 / Isaiah 5 / Jeremiah 22 / Ezekiel 34). The *Kyrios Sabaōth* of 5:4 places the passage explicitly in the LXX prophetic register. The rich who withhold wages / live in luxury / condemn and murder the righteous — all three charges echo the prophetic indictment tradition: **Amos 5:11–12 LXX (wages withheld / righteous afflicted) / Isaiah 5:7–9 LXX (*Kyrios Sabaōth* hears the cry) / Jeremiah 22:13 LXX (woe to the one who builds by injustice / withholds wages)**. **LXX-primacy argument confirmed: James 5:1–6 is a sustained LXX prophetic-indictment echo — the three charges (wages withheld / self-indulgence / murder of the righteous) map directly onto the LXX prophetic tradition and cannot be fully read without it.**

**5:13–20 — Prayer / anointing / confession / restoration:**
The letter's closing pastoral unit — the most comprehensive community-prayer instruction in the Catholic Epistles. The *euchē tēs pisteōs* — "prayer of faith" — will save the sick / the Lord will raise him up (*egerei* — the same *egeirō* as resurrection). The *dikaiou deēsis poluischyei energoumenē* — "the prayer of a righteous person has great power as it is working" — is the most explicit prayer-power statement in the NT. The Elijah example (5:17–18) is drawn from 1 Kings 17–18 LXX — **LXX-primacy argument: the James 5:17 description of Elijah as *anthrōpos homoiopathēs hēmin* — a man with a nature like ours — who prayed for drought and rain is shaped by 1 Kings 17:1/18:1/18:41–45 LXX**. The prayer-power demonstration through Elijah is LXX-dependent — the Hebrew text provides the narrative but the LXX vocabulary (*homoiopathēs* does not appear in the Hebrew) shapes the argument.

The letter closes with the most important pastoral statement in James — 5:19–20: the one who brings back a wanderer saves a *psychē* from death and covers a *plēthos hamartiōn* — multitude of sins. **LXX-primacy argument:** *kalypsei plēthos hamartiōn* — "will cover a multitude of sins" — echoes Proverbs 10:12 LXX (*panta de tous mē philoneikounta kalyptei philia* — love covers all those who do not quarrel / love covers all) and Psalm 31:1 LXX (*makarioi hōn aphethēsan hai anomiai kai hōn epekalyPhthēsan hai hamartiai* — blessed are those whose lawlessnesses are forgiven and whose sins are covered). The covering of sins through pastoral restoration is the community-level enactment of the divine *eleos* that covers sins in the atonement — Chain 1 (*hilastērion*) pastoral node.

\h 2 Corinthians
\toc1 The Second Letter of Paul to the Corinthians
\mt The Second Letter of Paul to the Corinthians

\c 1
\p
\v 1 Paul — an apostle of the Anointed Jesus by the will of God — and Timothy our brother — to the called-out assembly (*ekklēsia*) of God that is in Corinth — with all the holy ones who are throughout Achaia:
\v 2 Grace to you and peace from God our Father and the Lord Jesus the Anointed.
\p
\v 3 Blessed be the God and Father of our Lord Jesus the Anointed — the Father of mercies (*oiktirmōn*) and God of all comfort —
\v 4 who comforts us in all our affliction so that we may be able to comfort those who are in any affliction with the comfort with which we ourselves are comforted by God.
\v 5 For as we share abundantly in the sufferings of the Anointed so through the Anointed we share abundantly in comfort too.
\v 6 If we are afflicted it is for your comfort and salvation — and if we are comforted it is for your comfort — which you experience when you patiently endure the same sufferings that we suffer.
\v 7 Our hope for you is unshaken — for we know that as you share in our sufferings you will also share in our comfort.
\p
\v 8 For we do not want you to be unaware brothers of the affliction we experienced in Asia. For we were so utterly burdened beyond our strength that we despaired of life itself.
\v 9 Indeed we felt that we had received the sentence of death. But that was to make us rely not on ourselves but on God who raises the dead —
\v 10 who delivered us from such a deadly peril and will deliver us. On him we have set our hope that he will deliver us again —
\v 11 as you also join in helping us by prayer — so that many will give thanks on our behalf for the blessing granted us through the prayers of many.
\p
\v 12 For our boast is this: the testimony of our conscience that we behaved in the world with simplicity and godly sincerity — not by earthly wisdom (*sophia*) but by the grace of God — and supremely so toward you.
\v 13 For we are not writing to you anything other than what you read and understand and I hope you will fully understand —
\v 14 just as you did partially understand us — that on the day of our Lord Jesus you will boast of us as we will boast of you.
\p
\v 15 Because I was sure of this I wanted to come to you first so that you might have a second experience of grace —
\v 16 and I wanted to visit you on my way to Macedonia and to come back to you from Macedonia and have you send me on my way to Judea.
\v 17 Was I vacillating when I wanted to do this? Do I make my plans according to the flesh (*kata sarka*) — ready to say Yes and No at the same time?
\v 18 As surely as God is faithful our word (*logos*) to you has not been Yes and No.
\v 19 For the Son of God — Jesus the Anointed — whom we proclaimed among you — Silvanus and Timothy and I — was not Yes and No but in him it is always Yes.
\v 20 For all the promises of God find their Yes in him. That is why it is through him that we utter our Amen to God for his glory.
\v 21 And it is God who establishes us with you in the Anointed and has anointed us —
\v 22 and who has also sealed us and given us the breath-of-life (*pneuma*) in our hearts (*kardiais*) as a guarantee (*arrabōna*).
\p
\v 23 But I call God to witness against me — it was to spare you that I refrained from coming again to Corinth.
\v 24 Not that we lord it over your faith but we work with you for your joy — for you stand firm in your faith.

\c 2
\p
\v 1 For I made up my mind not to make another painful visit to you.
\v 2 For if I cause you pain who is there to make me glad but the one whom I have pained?
\v 3 And I wrote as I did so that when I came I might not suffer pain from those who should have made me rejoice — for I felt sure of all of you that my joy would be the joy of you all.
\v 4 For I wrote to you out of much affliction and anguish of heart (*kardias*) and with many tears — not to cause you pain but to let you know the abundant covenant-love (*agapēn*) that I have for you.
\p
\v 5 Now if anyone has caused pain he has caused it not to me but in some measure — not to put it too severely — to all of you.
\v 6 For such a one this punishment by the majority is enough —
\v 7 so you should rather turn to forgive and comfort him or he may be overwhelmed by excessive sorrow.
\v 8 So I beg you to reaffirm your covenant-love (*agapēn*) for him.
\v 9 For this is why I wrote — that I might test you and know whether you are obedient in everything.
\v 10 Anyone whom you forgive I also forgive. Indeed what I have forgiven — if I have forgiven anything — has been for your sake in the presence of the Anointed —
\v 11 so that we would not be outwitted by Satan (*Satana*) — for we are not ignorant of his schemes.
\p
\v 12 When I came to Troas to proclaim the glad tidings (*euangelion*) of the Anointed — even though a door was opened for me in the Lord —
\v 13 my breath-of-life (*pneuma*) was not at rest because I did not find my brother Titus there. So I took leave of them and went on to Macedonia.
\p
\v 14 But thanks be to God who in the Anointed always leads us in triumphal procession and through us spreads the fragrance of the knowledge of him everywhere.
\v 15 For we are the aroma of the Anointed to God among those who are being saved and among those who are perishing —
\v 16 to one a fragrance from death to death — to the other a fragrance from life to life. Who is sufficient for these things?
\v 17 For we are not like so many peddlers of the word (*logon*) of God but as men of sincerity — as commissioned by God — in the sight of God we speak in the Anointed.

\c 3
\p
\v 1 Are we beginning to commend ourselves again? Or do we need — as some do — letters of recommendation to you or from you?
\v 2 You yourselves are our letter of recommendation — written on our hearts (*kardiais*) — to be known and read by all.
\v 3 And you show that you are a letter from the Anointed delivered by us — written not with ink but with the breath-of-life (*pneumati*) of the living God — not on tablets of stone but on tablets of human hearts (*kardiais*).
\p
\v 4 Such is the confidence that we have through the Anointed toward God.
\v 5 Not that we are sufficient in ourselves to claim anything as coming from us — but our sufficiency is from God —
\v 6 who has made us sufficient to be ministers of a new covenant (*diathēkēs kainēs*) — not of the letter but of the breath-of-life (*pneumatos*). For the letter kills but the breath-of-life (*pneuma*) gives life (*zōopoiei*).
\p
\v 7 Now if the ministry of death — carved in letters on stone — came with such glory that the Israelites could not gaze at Moses' face because of its glory which was being brought to an end —
\v 8 will not the ministry of the breath-of-life (*pneumatos*) have even more glory?
\v 9 For if there was glory in the ministry of condemnation the ministry of righteousness (*dikaiosynē*) must far exceed it in glory.
\v 10 Indeed in this case what once had glory has come to have no glory at all because of the glory that surpasses it.
\v 11 For if what was being brought to an end came with glory much more will what is permanent have glory.
\p
\v 12 Since we have such a hope we are very bold —
\v 13 not like Moses who would put a veil over his face so that the Israelites might not gaze at the outcome of what was being brought to an end.
\v 14 But their minds were hardened. For to this day when they read the old covenant (*palaia diathēkē*) that same veil remains unlifted because only through the Anointed is it taken away.
\v 15 Yes to this day whenever Moses is read a veil lies over their hearts (*kardian*).
\v 16 But when one turns to the Lord the veil is removed.
\v 17 Now the Lord is the breath-of-life (*pneuma*) and where the breath-of-life (*pneuma*) of the Lord is there is freedom.
\v 18 And we all with unveiled face beholding the glory of the Lord are being transformed into the same image (*eikona*) from one degree of glory to another. For this comes from the Lord who is the breath-of-life (*pneuma*).

\c 4
\p
\v 1 Therefore having this ministry by the mercy of God we do not lose heart.
\v 2 But we have renounced disgraceful underhanded ways. We refuse to practice cunning or to tamper with the word (*logon*) of God but by the open statement of the truth (*alētheias*) we commend ourselves to everyone's conscience in the sight of God.
\v 3 And even if our glad tidings (*euangelion*) is veiled it is veiled to those who are perishing.
\v 4 In their case the god of this age has blinded the minds of the unbelievers to keep them from seeing the light of the glad tidings (*euangeliou*) of the glory of the Anointed who is the image (*eikōn*) of God.
\v 5 For what we proclaim is not ourselves but Jesus the Anointed as Lord — with ourselves as your servants for Jesus' sake.
\v 6 For God who said: Let light shine out of darkness — has shone in our hearts (*kardiais*) to give the light of the knowledge of the glory of God in the face of Jesus the Anointed.
\p
\v 7 But we have this treasure in jars of clay to show that the surpassing power belongs to God and not to us.
\v 8 We are afflicted in every way but not crushed — perplexed but not driven to despair —
\v 9 persecuted but not forsaken — struck down but not destroyed —
\v 10 always carrying in the body the dying of Jesus so that the life (*zōē*) of Jesus may also be manifested in our bodies.
\v 11 For we who live are always being given over to death for Jesus' sake so that the life (*zōē*) of Jesus also may be manifested in our mortal flesh (*sarki*).
\v 12 So death is at work in us but life (*zōē*) in you.
\v 13 Since we have the same breath-of-life (*pneuma*) of faith as he had who wrote: I believed and so I spoke — we also believe and so we also speak —
\v 14 knowing that he who raised the Lord Jesus will raise us also with Jesus and bring us with you into his presence.
\v 15 For it is all for your sake so that as grace extends to more and more people it may increase thanksgiving to the glory of God.
\p
\v 16 So we do not lose heart. Though our outer self is wasting away our inner self is being renewed day by day.
\v 17 For this light momentary affliction is preparing for us an eternal weight of glory beyond all comparison —
\v 18 as we look not to the things that are seen but to the things that are unseen. For the things that are seen are transient but the things that are unseen are everlasting (*aiōnia*).

\c 5
\p
\v 1 For we know that if the tent that is our earthly home is destroyed we have a building from God — a house not made with hands (*acheiropoiēton*) — everlasting (*aiōnion*) in the heavens.
\v 2 For in this tent we groan longing to put on our heavenly dwelling —
\v 3 if indeed by putting it on we may not be found naked.
\v 4 For while we are still in this tent we groan being burdened — not that we would be unclothed but that we would be further clothed so that what is mortal may be swallowed up by life (*zōē*).
\v 5 He who has prepared us for this very thing is God who has given us the breath-of-life (*pneuma*) as a guarantee (*arrabōna*).
\p
\v 6 So we are always of good courage. We know that while we are at home in the body we are away from the Lord —
\v 7 for we walk by faith not by sight.
\v 8 Yes we are of good courage and we would rather be away from the body and at home with the Lord.
\v 9 So whether we are at home or away we make it our aim to please him.
\v 10 For we must all appear before the judgment seat of the Anointed so that each one may receive what is due for what he has done in the body — whether good or evil.
\p
\v 11 Therefore knowing the fear of the Lord we persuade others. What we are is known to God and I hope it is known also to your conscience.
\v 12 We are not commending ourselves to you again but giving you cause to boast about us so that you may be able to answer those who boast about outward appearance and not about what is in the heart (*kardia*).
\v 13 For if we are beside ourselves it is for God — if we are in our right mind it is for you.
\v 14 For the covenant-love (*agapē*) of the Anointed controls us — because we have concluded this: that one has died for all — therefore all have died —
\v 15 and he died for all that those who live might no longer live for themselves but for him who for their sake died and was raised.
\p
\v 16 From now on therefore we regard no one according to the flesh (*kata sarka*). Even though we once regarded the Anointed according to the flesh (*kata sarka*) we regard him thus no longer.
\v 17 Therefore if anyone is in the Anointed he is a new creation. The old has passed away — behold the new has come.
\v 18 All this is from God who through the Anointed reconciled us to himself and gave us the ministry of reconciliation —
\v 19 that is — in the Anointed God was reconciling the world to himself not counting their trespasses against them and entrusting to us the message of reconciliation.
\v 20 Therefore we are ambassadors for the Anointed — God making his appeal through us. We implore you on behalf of the Anointed: be reconciled to God.
\v 21 For our sake he made him to be sin who knew no sin so that in him we might become the righteousness (*dikaiosynē*) of God.

\c 6
\p
\v 1 Working together with him then we appeal to you not to receive the grace of God in vain.
\v 2 For he says: In a favorable time I listened to you and in a day of salvation (*sōtērias*) I have helped you. Behold now is the favorable time — behold now is the day of salvation (*sōtērias*).
\p
\v 3 We put no obstacle in anyone's way so that no fault may be found with our ministry —
\v 4 but as servants of God we commend ourselves in every way: by great endurance — in afflictions — hardships — calamities —
\v 5 beatings — imprisonments — riots — labors — sleepless nights — hunger —
\v 6 by purity — knowledge — patience — kindness — the Holy Breath-of-life (*pneumati hagiō*) — genuine covenant-love (*agapē*) —
\v 7 by truthful speech (*logō alētheias*) and the power of God — with the weapons of righteousness (*dikaiosynēs*) for the right hand and for the left —
\v 8 through honor and dishonor — through slander and praise. We are treated as impostors and yet are true (*alētheis*) —
\v 9 as unknown and yet well known — as dying and behold we live — as punished and yet not killed —
\v 10 as sorrowful yet always rejoicing — as poor yet making many rich — as having nothing yet possessing everything.
\p
\v 11 We have spoken freely to you Corinthians — our heart (*kardia*) is wide open.
\v 12 You are not restricted by us but you are restricted in your own affections.
\v 13 In return — I speak as to children — widen your hearts (*kardian*) also.
\p
\v 14 Do not be unequally yoked with unbelievers. For what partnership has righteousness (*dikaiosynē*) with lawlessness? Or what fellowship has light with darkness?
\v 15 What accord has the Anointed with Belial? Or what portion does a believer share with an unbeliever?
\v 16 What agreement has God's sanctuary (*naos*) with idols? For we are the sanctuary (*naos*) of the living God — as God said: I will make my dwelling among them and walk among them and I will be their God and they shall be my people.
\v 17 Therefore go out from their midst and be separate from them says the Lord — and touch no unclean thing — then I will welcome you —
\v 18 and I will be a father to you and you shall be sons and daughters to me says the Lord Almighty.

\c 7
\p
\v 1 Since we have these promises beloved let us cleanse ourselves from every defilement of body and breath-of-life (*pneumatos*) bringing holiness to completion in the fear of God.
\p
\v 2 Make room for us. We have wronged no one — we have corrupted no one — we have taken advantage of no one.
\v 3 I do not say this to condemn you — for I said before that you are in our hearts (*kardiais*) to die together and to live together.
\v 4 I am acting with great boldness toward you — I have great pride in you. I am filled with comfort. In all our affliction I am overflowing with joy.
\p
\v 5 For even when we came into Macedonia our bodies had no rest but we were afflicted at every turn — fighting without and fear within.
\v 6 But God who comforts the downcast comforted us by the coming of Titus —
\v 7 and not only by his coming but also by the comfort with which he was comforted by you — as he told us of your longing — your mourning — your zeal for me — so that I rejoiced still more.
\v 8 For even if I made you grieve with my letter I do not regret it — though I did regret it. For I see that that letter grieved you though only for a while.
\v 9 As it is I rejoice not because you were grieved but because you were grieved into repenting. For you felt a godly grief so that you suffered no loss through us.
\v 10 For godly grief produces a repentance that leads to salvation (*sōtērian*) without regret whereas worldly grief produces death.
\v 11 For see what earnestness this godly grief has produced in you — what eagerness to clear yourselves — what indignation — what fear — what longing — what zeal — what punishment! At every point you have proved yourselves innocent in the matter.
\v 12 So although I wrote to you it was not for the sake of the one who did the wrong nor for the sake of the one who suffered the wrong but in order that your earnestness for us might be revealed to you in the sight of God.
\v 13 Therefore we are comforted. And besides our own comfort we rejoiced still more at the joy of Titus because his breath-of-life (*pneuma*) has been refreshed by you all.
\v 14 For whatever boasts I made to him about you I was not put to shame. But just as everything we said to you was true (*alētheia*) so also our boasting before Titus has proved true (*alētheia*).
\v 15 And his affection for you is even greater as he remembers the obedience of you all — how you received him with fear and trembling.
\v 16 I rejoice because I have complete confidence in you.

\c 8
\p
\v 1 We want you to know brothers about the grace of God that has been given among the called-out assemblies (*ekklēsiais*) of Macedonia —
\v 2 for in a severe test of affliction their abundance of joy and their extreme poverty have overflowed in a wealth of generosity on their part.
\v 3 For they gave according to their means — as I can testify — and beyond their means of their own accord —
\v 4 begging us earnestly for the favor of taking part in the relief of the holy ones —
\v 5 and this not as we expected but they gave themselves first to the Lord and then by the will of God to us.
\v 6 Accordingly we urged Titus that as he had started so he should also complete among you this act of grace.
\v 7 But as you excel in everything — in faith — in speech — in knowledge — in all earnestness — and in our covenant-love (*agapē*) that is in you — see that you excel in this act of grace also.
\v 8 I say this not as a command but to prove by the earnestness of others that your covenant-love (*agapē*) also is genuine.
\v 9 For you know the grace of our Lord Jesus the Anointed — that though he was rich yet for your sake he became poor so that you by his poverty might become rich.
\v 10 And in this matter I give my judgment: this benefits you who a year ago started not only to do this work but also to desire to do it.
\v 11 So now finish doing it as well so that your readiness in desiring it may be matched by your completing it out of what you have.
\v 12 For if the readiness is there it is acceptable according to what a person has — not according to what he does not have.
\v 13 For I do not mean that others should be eased and you burdened but that as a matter of fairness —
\v 14 your abundance at the present time should supply their need so that their abundance may supply your need — that there may be fairness.
\v 15 As it is written: Whoever gathered much had nothing left over and whoever gathered little had no lack.
\p
\v 16 But thanks be to God who put into the heart (*kardia*) of Titus the same earnest care I have for you.
\v 17 For he not only accepted our appeal but being himself very earnest he is going to you of his own accord.
\v 18 With him we are sending the brother who is famous among all the called-out assemblies (*ekklēsiais*) for his preaching of the glad tidings (*euangeliou*) —
\v 19 and not only that but he has been appointed by the called-out assemblies (*ekklēsiōn*) to travel with us as we carry out this act of grace that is being ministered by us for the glory of the Lord himself and to show our good will.
\v 20 We take this course so that no one should blame us about this generous gift that is being administered by us —
\v 21 for we aim at what is honorable not only in the Lord's sight but also in the sight of man.
\v 22 And with them we are sending our brother whom we have often tested and found earnest in many matters but who is now more earnest than ever because of his great confidence in you.
\v 23 As for Titus he is my partner and fellow worker for your benefit. And as for our brothers they are messengers of the called-out assemblies (*ekklēsiōn*) — the glory of the Anointed.
\v 24 So give proof before the called-out assemblies (*ekklēsiōn*) of your covenant-love (*agapēs*) and of our boasting about you to these men.

\c 9
\p
\v 1 Now it is superfluous for me to write to you about the ministry for the holy ones —
\v 2 for I know your readiness — of which I boast about you to the people of Macedonia saying that Achaia has been ready since last year. And your zeal has stirred up most of them.
\v 3 But I am sending the brothers so that our boasting about you may not prove empty in this matter — so that you may be ready as I said you would be —
\v 4 lest if some Macedonians come with me and find that you are not ready we be humiliated — to say nothing of you — for being so confident.
\v 5 So I thought it necessary to urge the brothers to go on ahead to you and arrange in advance for the gift you have promised so that it may be ready as a willing gift — not as an exaction.
\p
\v 6 The point is this: whoever sows sparingly will also reap sparingly and whoever sows bountifully will also reap bountifully.
\v 7 Each one must give as he has decided in his heart (*kardia*) — not reluctantly or under compulsion — for God loves a cheerful giver.
\v 8 And God is able to make all grace abound to you so that having all sufficiency in all things at all times you may abound in every good work.
\v 9 As it is written: He has distributed freely — he has given to the poor — his righteousness (*dikaiosynē*) endures forever.
\v 10 He who supplies seed to the sower and bread for food will supply and multiply your seed for sowing and increase the harvest of your righteousness (*dikaiosynēs*).
\v 11 You will be enriched in every way to be generous in every way — which through us will produce thanksgiving to God.
\v 12 For the ministry of this service is not only supplying the needs of the holy ones but is also overflowing in many thanksgivings to God.
\v 13 By their approval of this service they will glorify God because of your submission flowing from your confession of the glad tidings (*euangeliou*) of the Anointed and the generosity of your contribution for them and for all others —
\v 14 while they long for you and pray for you because of the surpassing grace of God upon you.
\v 15 Thanks be to God for his inexpressible gift!

\c 10
\p
\v 1 I Paul myself urge you by the meekness and gentleness of the Anointed — I who am humble when face to face with you but bold toward you when I am away —
\v 2 I beg of you that when I am present I may not have to show boldness with such confidence as I count on showing against some who suspect us of walking according to the flesh (*kata sarka*).
\v 3 For though we walk in the flesh (*sarki*) we are not waging war according to the flesh (*kata sarka*) —
\v 4 for the weapons of our warfare are not of the flesh (*sarkika*) but have divine power to destroy strongholds.
\v 5 We destroy arguments and every lofty opinion raised against the knowledge of God and take every thought captive to obey the Anointed —
\v 6 being ready to punish every disobedience when your obedience is complete.
\p
\v 7 Look at what is before your eyes. If anyone is confident that he is of the Anointed let him remind himself that just as he is of the Anointed so also are we.
\v 8 For even if I boast a little too much of our authority which the Lord gave for building you up and not for destroying you I will not be ashamed.
\v 9 I do not want to appear to be frightening you with my letters.
\v 10 For they say: His letters are weighty and strong but his bodily presence is weak and his speech of no account.
\v 11 Let such a person understand that what we say by letter when absent we do when present.
\p
\v 12 Not that we dare to classify or compare ourselves with some of those who are commending themselves. But when they measure themselves by one another and compare themselves with one another they are without understanding.
\v 13 But we will not boast beyond limits but will boast only with regard to the area of influence God assigned to us — to reach even to you.
\v 14 For we are not overextending ourselves as though we did not reach you. For we were the first to come all the way to you with the glad tidings (*euangeliō*) of the Anointed.
\v 15 We do not boast beyond limit in the labors of others. But our hope is that as your faith increases our area of influence among you may be greatly enlarged —
\v 16 so that we may preach the glad tidings (*euangelisasthai*) in lands beyond you without boasting of work already done in another's area of influence.
\v 17 Let the one who boasts boast in the Lord.
\v 18 For it is not the one who commends himself who is approved but the one whom the Lord commends.

\c 11
\p
\v 1 I wish you would bear with me in a little foolishness. Do bear with me!
\v 2 For I feel a divine jealousy for you — since I betrothed you to one husband to present you as a pure virgin to the Anointed.
\v 3 But I am afraid that as the serpent deceived Eve by his cunning your thoughts will be led astray from a sincere and pure devotion to the Anointed.
\v 4 For if someone comes and proclaims another Jesus than the one we proclaimed — or if you receive a different breath-of-life (*pneuma*) from the one you received — or a different glad tidings (*euangelion*) from the one you accepted — you put up with it readily enough.
\v 5 Indeed I consider that I am not in the least inferior to these super-apostles.
\v 6 Even if I am unskilled in speaking I am not so in knowledge — indeed in every way we have made this plain to you in all things.
\p
\v 7 Or did I commit a sin in humbling myself so that you might be exalted — because I preached God's glad tidings (*euangelion*) to you free of charge?
\v 8 I robbed other called-out assemblies (*ekklēsias*) by accepting support from them in order to serve you.
\v 9 And when I was with you and was in need I did not burden anyone — for the brothers who came from Macedonia supplied my need. So I refrained and will refrain from burdening you in any way.
\v 10 As the truth (*alētheia*) of the Anointed is in me this boasting of mine will not be silenced in the regions of Achaia.
\v 11 And why? Because I do not love (*agapō*) you? God knows I do!
\p
\v 12 And what I am doing I will continue to do in order to undermine the claim of those who would like to claim that in their boasted mission they work on the same terms as we do.
\v 13 For such men are false apostles — deceitful workmen — disguising themselves as apostles of the Anointed.
\v 14 And no wonder — for even Satan (*Satanas*) disguises himself as an angel of light.
\v 15 So it is no surprise if his servants also disguise themselves as servants of righteousness (*dikaiosynēs*). Their end will correspond to their deeds.
\p
\v 16 I repeat — let no one think me foolish. But even if you do accept me as a fool so that I too may boast a little.
\v 17 What I am saying with this boastful confidence I say not as the Lord would but as a fool.
\v 18 Since many boast according to the flesh (*kata sarka*) I too will boast.
\v 19 For you gladly bear with fools being wise yourselves!
\v 20 For you bear it if someone makes slaves of you — or devours you — or takes advantage of you — or puts on airs — or strikes you in the face.
\v 21 To my shame I must say we were too weak for that! But whatever anyone else dares to boast of — I am speaking as a fool — I also dare to boast of that.
\v 22 Are they Hebrews? So am I. Are they Israelites? So am I. Are they offspring of Abraham? So am I.
\v 23 Are they servants of the Anointed? I am a better one — I am talking like a madman — with far greater labors — far more imprisonments — with countless beatings — and often near death.
\v 24 Five times I received at the hands of the Jews the forty lashes less one.
\v 25 Three times I was beaten with rods. Once I was stoned. Three times I was shipwrecked — a night and a day I was adrift at sea —
\v 26 on frequent journeys — in danger from rivers — danger from robbers — danger from my own people — danger from Gentiles — danger in the city — danger in the wilderness — danger at sea — danger from false brothers —
\v 27 in toil and hardship — through many a sleepless night — in hunger and thirst — often without food — in cold and exposure.
\v 28 And apart from other things there is the daily pressure on me of my anxiety for all the called-out assemblies (*ekklēsiōn*).
\v 29 Who is weak and I am not weak? Who is made to fall and I am not indignant?
\v 30 If I must boast I will boast of the things that show my weakness.
\v 31 The God and Father of the Lord Jesus — he who is blessed forever — knows that I am not lying.
\v 32 At Damascus the governor under King Aretas was guarding the city of Damascus in order to seize me —
\v 33 but I was let down in a basket through a window in the wall and escaped his hands.

\c 12
\p
\v 1 I must go on boasting. Though there is nothing to be gained by it I will go on to visions and revelations of the Lord.

**2 CORINTHIANS IN THE CORPUS ARCHITECTURE**

2 Corinthians is the most autobiographical and rhetorically volatile Pauline letter — a sustained defense of apostolic ministry under conditions of personal attack, theological distortion, and community fracture. Where 1 Corinthians addresses congregational pathologies systematically, 2 Corinthians enacts apostolic theology through the texture of Paul's own suffering, weakness, and vindication. The letter's CAE function is concentrated in four nodes: the *diathēkē kainē / pneuma / gramma* contrast of chapter 3 — the fullest covenant-hermeneutic statement in the corpus; the *eikōn* transformation passage of 3:18 — the most developed image-restoration statement in the NT; the *acheiropoiēton* heavenly dwelling of 5:1 — an additional *naos*-chain node; and the *dikaiosynē theou* declaration of 5:21 — the most compressed atonement-and-imputation statement in the Pauline corpus. The letter also contains the fullest Trinitarian benediction in the NT at 13:14.

The letter's structural dynamic is the *weakness / power* inversion — the Anointed was crucified in weakness but lives by the power of God (13:4); Paul boasts in weakness so that the power of the Anointed may rest upon him (12:9). This is the 2 Corinthians form of the cross-as-wisdom-inversion of 1 Corinthians 1:18 — the Folly register reads weakness as defeat while the Wisdom register reads weakness as the vessel of divine power.

---

**KEY LEXICAL DECISIONS**

*pneuma* — "breath-of-life" throughout. Key structural uses: 1:22 (breath-of-life given in hearts as guarantee), 2:13 (my breath-of-life had no rest), 3:3 (written with the breath-of-life of the living God on hearts), 3:6 (ministers of the new covenant — not of the letter but of the breath-of-life — the letter kills / the breath-of-life gives life), 3:8 (ministry of the breath-of-life), 3:17/18 (the Lord is the breath-of-life / where the breath-of-life of the Lord is there is freedom / transformed by the Lord who is the breath-of-life), 4:13 (same breath-of-life of faith), 5:5 (breath-of-life as guarantee), 6:6 (Holy Breath-of-life), 7:1 (cleanse from defilement of body and breath-of-life), 7:13 (breath-of-life of Titus refreshed), 11:4 (different breath-of-life), 12:18 (same breath-of-life).

*3:6 CAE NOTE:* *ho de pneuma zōopoiei* — "the breath-of-life gives life (*zōopoiei*)." The same *zōopoieō* verb as 1 Corinthians 15:45 (*pneuma zōopoioun* — the last Adam became a life-giving breath-of-life). The *gramma* — letter / written code — kills because it pronounces the curse without providing the power to fulfill the covenant. The *pneuma* gives life because it is the power of the new covenant — the Jeremiah 31:31–34 promise fulfilled: the Law written on hearts by the Breath-of-life rather than on stone tablets. **CAE chain: Jeremiah 31:33 LXX (*dōsō nomous mou eis tēn dianoian autōn kai epi kardias autōn grapsō autous* — I will put my laws into their minds and write them on their hearts) → 2 Corinthians 3:3/6 → Hebrews 8:10** — Chain 8 (*diathēkē*) receives its pneumatological fulfillment node.

*3:17 CAE NOTE:* *ho de kyrios to pneuma estin* — "the Lord is the breath-of-life." This is the most direct identification of the Lord (the Anointed) with the *pneuma* in the NT. It does not collapse the persons of the Son and the Breath-of-life into identity — rather it establishes that the mode of the risen Lord's present availability to believers is *pneumatic*. The Anointed who was present in the flesh (*kata sarka* — 5:16) is now present as *pneuma* — accessible through the Breath-of-life. CAE trinitarian map: the Lord-who-is-the-Breath-of-life is the one in whose presence the veil is removed (3:16) and by whom believers are transformed (3:18). The Trinitarian grammar: the Father predestines (Ephesians 1:5) / the Son accomplishes (Galatians 4:4–5) / the Breath-of-life applies and transforms (2 Corinthians 3:17–18).

*diathēkē* — "covenant": 3:6 (*diathēkēs kainēs* — ministers of a new covenant), 3:14 (*palaia diathēkē* — old covenant). The *palaia diathēkē* / *diathēkē kainē* contrast is the only place in the NT where the phrase "old covenant" (*palaia diathēkē*) appears. The contrast is not between bad and good but between the covenant-of-the-letter (Sinai / written code / condemnation / fading glory) and the covenant-of-the-Breath-of-life (new covenant / Breath-of-life / righteousness / surpassing glory). **Chain 8 (*diathēkē aiōnios*) node:** The *diathēkē kainē* here is the ministerial application — Paul and co-workers are *ministers* of the new covenant, not merely recipients. The new covenant's content: Jeremiah 31:31–34 LXX implemented through the Breath-of-life writing on hearts. CAE anchor: **Jeremiah 31:31–34 LXX → 2 Corinthians 3:6/14 → Hebrews 8:6–13** — Chain 8 confirmed with the *palaia / kainē* contrast making the covenantal hermeneutic explicit.

*eikōn* — "image": 3:18 (*tēn autēn eikona metamorphoumetha* — being transformed into the same image), 4:4 (*eikōn tou theou* — the Anointed as image of God). **PRIMARY CHAIN NODE — Chain 15 (Eden-to-new-creation):**

*3:18* — *hēmeis de pantes anakekalymmenō prosōpō tēn doxan kyriou katoptrizomenoi tēn autēn eikona metamorphoumetha apo doxēs eis doxan kathaper apo kyriou pneumatos* — "and we all with unveiled face beholding the glory of the Lord are being transformed into the same image from one degree of glory to another — for this comes from the Lord who is the breath-of-life." The transformation is *progressive* (*metamorphoumetha* — present passive — ongoing), *communal* (*hēmeis de pantes* — we all), *pneumatic* (from the Lord who is the Breath-of-life), and *directional* (from glory to glory — not completed but moving). The *autēn eikona* — "same image" — is the *eikōn tou theou* of 4:4 — the image that the Anointed *is*, into which believers are being conformed. **CAE image-restoration chain completed:**

**Genesis 1:26–27 LXX (*kat' eikona*) → Colossians 1:15 (the Anointed as the *eikōn*) → Colossians 3:10 (renewed after the *eikōn* of creator) → 1 Corinthians 15:49 (bearing the *eikōn* of the man of heaven) → 2 Corinthians 3:18 (being transformed into the same *eikōn* from glory to glory)**

The chain moves: humanity created in the image → the image damaged (Fall) → the Anointed as the original Image incarnate → believers renewed toward that Image → progressive transformation into that Image by the Breath-of-life → eschatological bearing of the heavenly Image (1 Corinthians 15:49). Chain 15 receives its most developed present-tense transformation node.

*4:4 CAE NOTE:* *ho theos tou aiōnos toutou etyphlōsen ta noēmata tōn apistōn eis to mē augasai ton phōtismon tou euangeliou tēs doxēs tou Christou hos estin eikōn tou theou* — "the god of this age has blinded the minds of the unbelievers to keep them from seeing the light of the glad tidings of the glory of the Anointed who is the image of God." The *eikōn tou theou* of 4:4 identifies the Anointed as the visible form of the invisible God (Colossians 1:15) — the *eikōn* that the glad tidings proclaims and the god of this age blinds people from seeing. CAE Wisdom/Folly axis: the Folly register is here identified with the *god of this age* — the counter-pneuma of Ephesians 2:2 — whose operation is the blinding of minds to the *eikōn*. The Wisdom register is the unveiled face beholding the *eikōn* (3:18). The two registers are locked in pneumatic combat across 2 Corinthians 3–4.

*dikaiosynē* — "righteousness": 3:9 (ministry of *dikaiosynē* surpassing in glory), 5:21 (*dikaiosynē theou* — righteousness of God), 6:7 (weapons of *dikaiosynē*), 6:14 (partnership of *dikaiosynē* with lawlessness), 9:9/10 (his *dikaiosynē* endures forever / harvest of *dikaiosynē*), 11:15 (servants of *dikaiosynē*). The 5:21 *dikaiosynē theou* is the letter's Christological-soteriological climax.

*5:21 CAE NOTE:* *ton gar mē gnonta hamartian hyper hēmōn hamartian epoiēsen hina hēmeis genōmetha dikaiosynē theou en autō* — "for our sake he made him to be sin who knew no sin so that in him we might become the righteousness of God." This is the most compressed double-imputation statement in the NT: (a) the sinless one made *sin* — the sin of the many imputed to him; (b) those in him become *dikaiosynē theou* — the righteousness of God imputed to them. The exchange is total and asymmetric: he receives what is ours (sin) / we receive what is his (righteousness). **PRIMARY CHAIN NODE — Chain 1 (*hilastērion*):**

**Leviticus 16 LXX → Isaiah 53:5–6/10–12 LXX → 4 Maccabees 6:29/17:21 → John 1:29 → 2 Corinthians 5:21 → Romans 3:25 → Hebrews 2:17**

The Isaiah 53 LXX background is explicit: *kyrios paredōken auton tais hamartiais hēmōn* (53:6 LXX — the Lord delivered him over for our sins) / *tēn hamartian autōn autos oisei* (53:11 LXX — he will bear their sins). The *hyper hēmōn hamartian epoiēsen* of 5:21 is the Pauline summary of the Isaiah 53 LXX servant-bearing. **LXX-primacy argument: 2 Corinthians 5:21 is compositionally dependent on Isaiah 53:6/10–12 LXX — the sin-bearing language (*hamartian epoiēsen*) translates directly from the LXX servant-song.** Chain 1 receives its most compressed single-verse atonement statement.

*acheiropoiēton* — "not made by hands": 5:1 — *oikian acheiropoiēton aiōnion en tois ouranois* — "a building from God — a house not made with hands — everlasting in the heavens." The *acheiropoiēton* vocabulary lock applied to the resurrection body as heavenly dwelling. The tent (*skēnē*) / building (*oikodomē*) contrast: the present body is a *skēnē* (tent — 5:1/4) subject to dissolution; the resurrection body is an *acheiropoiēton oikodomē* (building not made with hands) — permanent and heavenly. **Chain 9 (*skēnē / naos / acheiropoiētos*) node:**

**Mark 14:58 (*acheiropoiēton*) → Colossians 2:11 (*acheiropoiētō*) → 2 Corinthians 5:1 (*acheiropoiēton* — heavenly dwelling/resurrection body) → Hebrews 9:11 (*naou ou cheiropoiētou*)**

The *acheiropoiētos* term now covers three domains: the true sanctuary (Mark 14:58), the circumcision of the heart (Colossians 2:11), and the resurrection body as heavenly dwelling (2 Corinthians 5:1). All three are the Breath-of-life's work — the divine *acheiropoiēton* construction across sanctuary / anthropology / eschatology.

*agapē* — "covenant-love": 2:4/8 (covenant-love for the Corinthians / reaffirm covenant-love for the offender), 5:14 (*agapē* of the Anointed controls us), 6:6 (genuine *agapē* in ministry list), 8:7/8/24 (*agapē* in the collection appeal), 11:11 (do I not love you?), 12:15 (I love you more), 13:11/14 (God of *agapē* / *agapē* of God in Trinitarian benediction).

*5:14 CAE NOTE:* *hē agapē tou Christou synechei hēmas* — "the covenant-love of the Anointed controls/constrains us." The *synechei* — "holds together / compels / constrains" — is a pressure verb — the *agapē* of the Anointed is not a sentiment Paul feels but a force that holds him in its grip. The ground: "one has died for all — therefore all have died." The substitutionary death logic (5:14–15) is the basis of the *agapē*-constraint — the *hyper pantōn* (for all) death of the one has changed the existential condition of all who belong to him. CAE Wisdom/Folly axis: the *agapē* of the Anointed operating as a compelling constraint is the Wisdom-register alternative to the *sarx*-driven self-determination of 5:16.

*alētheia* — "truth": 4:2 (open statement of the *alētheias*), 6:7 (*logō alētheias* — truthful speech), 6:8 (treated as impostors yet true / *alētheis*), 7:14 (*alētheia* — our boasting about you proved true), 11:10 (*alētheia* of the Anointed is in me), 12:6 (I would be speaking *alētheian*), 13:8 (we cannot do anything against the *alētheias* but only for it). The 13:8 *alētheia* statement is the letter's most absolute — apostolic ministry has no power against truth because its entire orientation is toward truth. CAE Wisdom/Folly axis: the false apostles (11:13–15) operate in the Folly register — disguising themselves as servants of *dikaiosynē* while serving the god of this age. The true apostle's ministry is grounded in *alētheia* precisely because it is grounded in the *eikōn tou theou* (4:4) which is the Wisdom register's ultimate reference point.

*kardia* — "heart": 1:22 (breath-of-life in hearts as guarantee), 2:4 (anguish of heart), 3:2/3 (letter written on hearts / on tablets of human hearts), 3:15 (veil over hearts), 4:6 (shone in our hearts), 5:12 (boast about outward appearance not about what is in the heart), 6:11/13 (wide open heart / widen your hearts), 7:3 (in our hearts to die and live together), 8:16 (God put earnest care into Titus' heart), 9:7 (decided in his heart). The *kardia* as the locus of the Breath-of-life's indwelling (1:22 / 3:3) and the site of the new covenant's implementation (3:3 — Jeremiah 31:33 LXX) is the Corinthian form of the Ephesian *pneuma sophias* operating through the eyes of the heart (Ephesians 1:17–18). The unveiled face (3:18) is the *kardia* with the veil removed — the heart open to the transforming *eikōn*-vision.

*sarx* — "flesh": 1:17 (*kata sarka* — plans according to the flesh), 4:11 (life of Jesus manifested in our mortal flesh), 5:16 (no longer regard anyone *kata sarka*), 7:1 (defilement of body and breath-of-life), 7:5 (bodies had no rest), 10:2/3/4 (*kata sarka* / walking in the flesh / weapons not *sarkika*), 11:18 (*kata sarka* boasting), 12:7 (thorn in the flesh). The *kata sarka / kata pneuma* opposition is operative throughout — the super-apostles boast *kata sarka* (11:18) / Paul boasts in weakness so that the power of the Anointed may rest upon him (12:9). The *sarx*-boasting is the Folly register in its most religious form — religious achievement, credentials, eloquence — which the *pneuma*-ministry deliberately refuses.

*euangelion* — "glad tidings": 2:12 (glad tidings of the Anointed), 4:3/4 (glad tidings veiled / light of the glad tidings of the glory of the Anointed), 8:18 (famous for preaching the glad tidings), 9:13 (confession of the glad tidings of the Anointed), 10:14 (first to come with the glad tidings), 11:4 (different glad tidings), 11:7 (God's glad tidings free of charge). The 4:3–4 cluster is the most theologically loaded *euangelion* occurrence — the glad tidings is *veiled* to those who are perishing because the god of this age has blinded them to the *eikōn tou theou* it proclaims. The glad tidings and the *eikōn* are identified — to receive the glad tidings is to see the *eikōn* / to be blinded to the *eikōn* is to be blinded to the glad tidings.

**STRUCTURAL CAE NODES**

**1:20–22 — All promises Yes in him / breath-of-life as guarantee:**
*hosai gar epangeliai theou en autō to nai* — "for all the promises of God find their Yes in him." The Anointed is the *nai* — the Yes — of every divine promise. This is the most comprehensive promise-fulfillment statement in the NT — not specific promises but *all promises* without exception find their affirmation in him. The consequent: God has "sealed us and given us the breath-of-life in our hearts as a guarantee (*arrabōna*)." The *arrabōn* — down-payment / pledge — is the Breath-of-life's present indwelling as the first installment of the full inheritance. CAE chain: **Ephesians 1:13–14 (*arrabōn*) = 2 Corinthians 1:22 = 2 Corinthians 5:5** — the three *arrabōn* occurrences in the NT all refer to the Breath-of-life as guarantee of the eschatological inheritance.

**3:1–18 — New covenant hermeneutic / letter and breath-of-life / veil / transformation:**
The most theologically dense single passage in 2 Corinthians — a sustained meditation on the contrast between the old covenant (Sinai / stone / letter / condemnation / fading glory / veil) and the new covenant (Jeremiah 31:31 / hearts / breath-of-life / righteousness / surpassing glory / unveiled face). The structural movement: letters of recommendation → the Corinthians themselves as living letters written by the Breath-of-life on hearts (3:2–3) → this is the new covenant ministry (3:6) → contrast with Sinai / Moses / fading glory / veil (3:7–15) → the Lord is the Breath-of-life / veil removed in him (3:16–17) → transformation into the same *eikōn* from glory to glory (3:18).

**LXX-primacy argument at 3:3:** *engrammena... ou en plaxin lithinais alla en plaxin kardiais sarkinais* — "written not on stone tablets but on tablets of human hearts of flesh." Direct citation of Ezekiel 36:26 LXX (*dōsō hymin kardian kainēn kai pneuma kainon dōsō en hymin — kai afelō tēn kardian tēn lithēn ek tēs sarkos hymōn kai dōsō hymin kardian sarkēn* — "I will give you a new heart and put a new breath-of-life in you — I will remove the heart of stone and give you a heart of flesh"). The Ezekiel 36:26 LXX combines with Jeremiah 31:33 LXX to form the new covenant pneumatology of 2 Corinthians 3. **LXX-primacy argument: 2 Corinthians 3:3 depends on Ezekiel 36:26 LXX (*kardian sarkēn* — heart of flesh) and Jeremiah 31:33 LXX (*epi kardias autōn grapsō* — write on their hearts) — both of which provide the new covenant pneumatological framework.** CAE anchor: **Ezekiel 36:26 LXX + Jeremiah 31:33 LXX → 2 Corinthians 3:3/6 → Hebrews 8:10** — Chain 8 pneumatological fulfillment node confirmed.

**LXX-primacy argument at 3:7–11:** The *doxa* argument comparing Moses' fading glory with the surpassing glory of the new covenant ministry depends on Exodus 34:29–35 LXX — the Moses-veil narrative. The LXX of Exodus 34:33 (*epethēken epi to prosōpon autou kalymma* — he put a veil on his face) is the textual ground for Paul's veil theology. The *katargoumenēn* — "being brought to an end / fading" — applied to the old covenant glory requires reading the Moses narrative as a type of the covenant's temporary status — a reading available from LXX Exodus 34.

**4:6 — God said let light shine out of darkness:**
*hoti ho theos ho eipōn ek skotous phōs lampsei hos elampsen en tais kardiais hēmōn* — "God who said: Let light shine out of darkness — has shone in our hearts." The creation-light of Genesis 1:3 LXX (*genēthētō phōs*) is identified with the light of the knowledge of the glory of God in the face of the Anointed. The new creation in 5:17 (*kainē ktisis* — new creation) is anticipated here — the same God who spoke light into the original creation speaks the light of the *euangelion* into the heart. **CAE chain — Chain 15 (Eden-to-new-creation):**

**Genesis 1:3 LXX (*genēthētō phōs*) → 2 Corinthians 4:6 (God shone in our hearts) → 2 Corinthians 5:17 (*kainē ktisis*) → Revelation 21:23/22:5 (no sun needed — the Lamb is the light)**

The creation-light → new creation arc receives its Pauline form in 4:6 / 5:17. Chain 15 confirmed.

**5:1 — The acheiropoiēton heavenly dwelling:**
*oikian acheiropoiēton aiōnion en tois ouranois* — "a house not made with hands — everlasting in the heavens." The *acheiropoiēton* applied to the resurrection body-as-dwelling. The *skēnē* — tent — of the mortal body contrasts with the permanent *oikodomē ek theou* — "building from God." The *aiōnion* — everlasting — qualification makes this the only place in the NT where the *acheiropoiēton* is explicitly identified as everlasting. CAE chain reinforcement: Chain 9 / *acheiropoiētos* cluster confirmed across four domains — true sanctuary (Mark 14:58) / circumcision of the heart (Colossians 2:11) / heavenly dwelling / resurrection body (2 Corinthians 5:1) / true tabernacle (Hebrews 9:11).

**5:14–21 — agapē constrains / new creation / reconciliation / dikaiosynē theou:**
The most theologically compressed passage in 2 Corinthians — five nodes in eight verses: (a) the *agapē* of the Anointed as constraining force (5:14); (b) one died for all — therefore all died — substitutionary identification (5:14–15); (c) no longer *kata sarka* — new creation (5:16–17); (d) God reconciling the world to himself in the Anointed — ministry of reconciliation entrusted to Paul (5:18–20); (e) he made him sin so that we might become the *dikaiosynē theou* — double-imputation (5:21).

*5:17 CAE NOTE:* *ei tis en Christō kainē ktisis* — "if anyone is in the Anointed — new creation." The *kainē ktisis* is not a new creature (individual renovation) but a new creation (new-order reality). The one in the Anointed participates in the eschatological new creation that has broken into the present age through the resurrection. *ta archaia parēlthen — idou gegonen kaina* — "the old has passed away — behold the new has come." The two-age structure (present evil age / age to come) is here collapsed into the present experience of those in the Anointed. **Chain 15 (Eden-to-new-creation) primary node:**

**Genesis 1–3 LXX (first creation) → Isaiah 65:17 LXX (*kainēn... ktizō* — I create new) → 2 Corinthians 5:17 (*kainē ktisis*) → Galatians 6:15 (*kainē ktisis*) → Revelation 21:1/5 (*kainēn ktisis / kaina panta*)**

The *kainē ktisis* of 5:17 is the present-tense instantiation of the Isaiah 65:17 new-creation promise — the Anointed's resurrection has inaugurated the new creation within the old age. Chain 15 receives its most important Pauline new-creation node.

**6:16–18 — We are the sanctuary of the living God / LXX catena:**
*naos gar theou esmen zōntos* — "we are the sanctuary of the living God." The *naos* vocabulary lock applied to the community as in 1 Corinthians 3:16. But here the declaration is followed by a sustained LXX catena — five OT citations woven together: (a) *enoikēsō en autois kai emperipatēsō* (Leviticus 26:12 LXX / Ezekiel 37:27 LXX — I will dwell among them and walk among them); (b) *esomar autōn theos kai autoi esontai mou laos* (Leviticus 26:12 LXX — I will be their God and they shall be my people); (c) *exelthate ek mesou autōn* (Isaiah 52:11 LXX — go out from their midst); (d) *akathartou mē haptesthe* (Isaiah 52:11 LXX — touch no unclean thing); (e) *kai esomai hymin eis patera kai hymeis esesthe moi eis huious kai thygateras* (2 Samuel 7:14 LXX / Isaiah 43:6 LXX — I will be a father to you / you shall be sons and daughters). **LXX-primacy argument: the most sustained OT catena in 2 Corinthians — six citations from five LXX texts establishing the called-out assembly as the new-covenant fulfillment of the dwelling-of-God promise.** CAE chain: **Leviticus 26:12 LXX + Ezekiel 37:27 LXX → 2 Corinthians 6:16 → Revelation 21:3** — Chain 9 (*skēnē*) receives direct citation of its two primary OT anchor texts within the *naos* declaration.

**12:9 — Power made perfect in weakness:**
*hē dynamis en astheneia teleitai* — "my power is made perfect in weakness." This is the theological principle underlying the entire letter's *weakness / power* dynamic. The *astheneia* — weakness — is not a defect to be overcome but the condition in which divine power is perfected. The Anointed was crucified in weakness but lives by the power of God (13:4) — the apostle participates in the same pattern. CAE Wisdom/Folly axis: the Folly register evaluates power by *sarx* metrics (eloquence / letters of commendation / visions / credentials — all the super-apostles' claims). The Wisdom register locates power in the *pneuma*-filled weakness that carries the treasure of the *eikōn*-knowledge in jars of clay (4:7).

**13:14 — The Trinitarian Benediction:**
*hē charis tou kyriou Iēsou Christou kai hē agapē tou theou kai hē koinōnia tou hagiou pneumatos meta pantōn hymōn* — "the grace of the Lord Jesus the Anointed and the covenant-love of God and the fellowship of the Holy Breath-of-life be with you all." The fullest Trinitarian benediction in the NT — three persons / three attributes / one blessing. The structural distribution: grace (*charis*) attributed to the Lord Jesus / covenant-love (*agapē*) attributed to God the Father / fellowship (*koinōnia*) attributed to the Holy Breath-of-life. CAE trinitarian map:

The Lord Jesus the Anointed → grace (*charis*) — the channel of undeserved covenantal favor
God the Father → covenant-love (*agapē*) — the eternal source of the covenantal relationship
The Holy Breath-of-life → fellowship (*koinōnia*) — the medium of participation in the divine life

The benediction is the summation of the letter's Trinitarian pneumatology: the Father's *agapē* is the ground (5:18 — all this is from God) / the Lord's *charis* is the channel (8:9 — you know the grace of our Lord Jesus the Anointed) / the Breath-of-life's *koinōnia* is the medium (3:17–18 — the Lord who is the Breath-of-life transforms). **CAE trinitarian governance node: 2 Corinthians 13:14** — the fullest single-verse Trinitarian statement in the NT corpus. Cross-link: Ephesians 2:18 (access through the Son / in one Breath-of-life / to the Father) — the two fullest Trinitarian access statements in Paul.

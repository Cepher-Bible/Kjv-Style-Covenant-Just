**1 TIMOTHY — GLE v2.2 CAE NOTES**

---

**TEXT BASE:** RP2026 Byzantine Majority Text

**VERSE COUNT:** 113 verses / 6 chapters

---

**1 TIMOTHY IN THE CORPUS ARCHITECTURE**

1 Timothy opens the Pastoral Epistles — three letters (1 Timothy / 2 Timothy / Titus) addressed not to communities but to individual co-workers charged with ordering the called-out assembly's life, teaching, and leadership. The Pastorals shift the CAE's register from doctrinal argument and eschatological exhortation to institutional and ecclesiological consolidation — the deposit of faith (*parathēkē*) must be guarded, the household of God (*oikos theou*) ordered, sound doctrine (*hygiainousa didaskalia*) maintained against the encroachments of false teaching. The Pastorals' vocabulary reflects this function: *hygiainousa didaskalia* (sound doctrine — five occurrences across the three letters) / *eusebeia* (godliness — ten occurrences in 1 Timothy alone) / *parathēkē* (deposit / 1 Timothy 6:20 / 2 Timothy 1:12/14) / *pistos ho logos* (trustworthy is the saying — five occurrences) are Pastoral-distinctive vocabulary.

1 Timothy's CAE function is concentrated in four nodes: the *antilytron hyper pantōn* — "ransom for all" — declaration of 2:6 which is the most universal formulation of the ransom-vocabulary in the NT; the *christological hymn* of 3:16 — six balanced clauses constituting the most compressed incarnation-vindication-proclamation sequence in the corpus; the *epiphaneia* — "appearing" — eschatological vocabulary of 6:14 which introduces the Pastoral-distinctive term for the *parousia*; and the doxology of 6:15–16 — the most exalted description of the divine transcendence in the Pauline corpus outside Romans 11:33–36.

The letter's sustained concern with *alētheia* — truth — as the content of the deposit and the criterion of sound doctrine makes it a key *alētheia*-chain witness: the called-out assembly is *stēlos kai hedraiōma tēs alētheias* — "pillar and buttress of the truth" (3:15) — the most institutionally-grounded *alētheia* statement in the NT.

---

**KEY LEXICAL DECISIONS**

*antilytron* — "ransom": 2:6 — *ho dous heauton antilytron hyper pantōn* — "who gave himself as a ransom for all." The *antilytron* is a compound of *anti* (in exchange for / in place of) and *lytron* (ransom price). It appears only here in the NT — the most lexically precise ransom-substitution term in the corpus. The *lytron* vocabulary lock runs across the NT: *lytron anti pollōn* (ransom for many — Matthew 20:28 / Mark 10:45) / *apolytrōsis* (ransom-release — Romans 3:24 / Ephesians 1:7 / Colossians 1:14) / *antilytron hyper pantōn* (ransom for all — 1 Timothy 2:6). The movement from *anti pollōn* (for many) to *hyper pantōn* (for all) is not a contradiction but a scope-extension: the *many* of the Synoptic logion is the Semitic inclusive-many (the full number) / the *all* of 1 Timothy 2:6 is the universal-scope declaration consistent with 2:4 (God desires all people to be saved). **Chain 1 (*hilastērion / antipsychos / antilytron*) primary node:**

**4 Maccabees 6:29 (*antipsychon*) → 4 Maccabees 17:21 (*antipsychon*) → Mark 10:45 / Matthew 20:28 (*lytron anti pollōn*) → 1 Timothy 2:6 (*antilytron hyper pantōn*) → Romans 3:25 (*hilastērion*) → Hebrews 2:17 (*hilaskomai*)**

The *antilytron* of 1 Timothy 2:6 is the most universal and lexically precise ransom-substitution statement in the chain. The *hyper pantōn* — "for all" — grounds the universal prayer-scope of 2:1–4 (prayers for all people / God desires all to be saved / one mediator between God and all men) in the universal scope of the ransom itself. CAE structural note: the universal prayer (2:1) → universal divine will (2:4) → one mediator (2:5) → universal ransom (2:6) is the tightest universal-scope soteriological argument in the Pauline corpus.

*2:5 CAE NOTE:* *heis gar theos heis kai mesitēs theou kai anthrōpōn anthrōpos Christos Iēsous* — "for there is one God and there is one mediator between God and men — the man the Anointed Jesus." The *heis theos / heis mesitēs* — one God / one mediator — formula is the most explicit monotheistic-mediatorial statement in the NT. The *anthrōpos* — "man" — emphasis: the mediator is specifically identified as a human being (*anthrōpos*) — the incarnation is the necessary condition of mediation. The one who is in the form of God (Philippians 2:6) and the image of God (Colossians 1:15 / 2 Corinthians 4:4) is the mediator precisely as *anthrōpos* — the two natures in one person as the structural requirement of the mediatorial office. **CAE cross-link: Hebrews 8:6 (*kreittonos diathēkēs mesitēs* — mediator of a better covenant) + Hebrews 9:15 (*diathēkēs kainēs mesitēs* — mediator of the new covenant) + Hebrews 12:24 (*mesitēs diathēkēs neas* — mediator of a new covenant)**. The *mesitēs* vocabulary lock across 1 Timothy 2:5 and Hebrews confirms the one-mediator formula as a primary CAE Christological node — Chain 8 (*diathēkē*) receives the mediatorial-office witness.

*pneuma* — "breath-of-life": 3:16 (*edikaiōthē en pneumati* — vindicated by the breath-of-life), 4:1 (*to pneuma rhētōs legei* — the breath-of-life expressly says / *pneumasin planois* — deceitful breaths-of-life). Three structurally opposed uses: the Holy Breath-of-life vindicating the Anointed (3:16) / the Holy Breath-of-life warning of apostasy (4:1) / the deceitful breaths-of-life leading people astray (4:1). The *pneumasin planois* — "deceitful / wandering breaths-of-life" — are the 1 Timothy form of the 2 Thessalonians 2:2 counterfeit *pneuma* — the Folly-register spiritual agency that produces false teaching.

*4:1 CAE NOTE:* *to de pneuma rhētōs legei hoti en hysterois kairois apostēsontai tines tēs pisteōs prosechontes pneumasin planois kai didaskaliais daimoniōn* — "now the breath-of-life expressly says that in later times some will depart from the faith by devoting themselves to deceitful breaths-of-life and teachings of demons." The *rhētōs* — "expressly / in explicit words" — is unique in the NT — the Breath-of-life's warning is not implicit but stated explicitly in prophetic utterance. The *pneumasin planois* — "wandering / deceiving breaths-of-life" — are distinguished from the *pneuma hagion* by their direction: the Holy Breath-of-life leads toward truth / the *pneumata plana* lead away from truth into apostasy (*apostēsontai tēs pisteōs*). CAE pneumatic discernment: the Breath-of-life warns about breath-of-life counterfeits — the discernment of breaths-of-life (*diakrisis pneumatōn* — 1 Corinthians 12:10) is necessary precisely because the Folly-register spiritual agency mimics the vocabulary and channels of the Wisdom-register Breath-of-life.

*alētheia* — "truth": 2:4 (God desires all to come to the knowledge of the *alētheias*), 2:7 (teacher of the Gentiles in faith and *alētheia*), 3:15 (the called-out assembly as pillar and buttress of the *alētheias*), 4:3 (those who know the *alētheian*), 6:5 (depraved and deprived of the *alētheias*). Five occurrences — the highest *alētheia* density in the Pastoral Epistles. The *alētheia* functions as the doctrinal-deposit content: the called-out assembly is the institutional guardian of the *alētheia* (3:15) / the false teachers are deprived of it (6:5) / all people are called to come to its knowledge (2:4).

*3:15 CAE NOTE:* *hē ekklēsia theou zōntos — stēlos kai hedraiōma tēs alētheias* — "the called-out assembly of the living God — a pillar and buttress of the truth." The most institutionally grounded *alētheia* statement in the NT — the called-out assembly is not merely where truth is proclaimed but the structural support system (*stēlos* — pillar / *hedraiōma* — foundation/buttress) of the truth in the world. The *theou zōntos* — "living God" — connects to the *naos* chain: the called-out assembly is both the *naos* of the living God (2 Corinthians 6:16 / 1 Corinthians 3:16) and the pillar-and-buttress of the truth — the structural and sacral dimensions of the community's identity are unified. CAE Chain 10 (*alētheia*) institutional node: **1 Esdras 4:41 LXX (*megalē hē alētheia kai ischyrotera* — great is the truth and strongest) → 1 Timothy 3:15 (*stēlos kai hedraiōma tēs alētheias*)**. The *alētheia* that triumphs in 1 Esdras 4:41 LXX is now the *alētheia* whose institutional support-structure is the called-out assembly.

*eusebeia* — "godliness": 2:2 (peaceful life godly and dignified), 3:16 (mystery of *eusebeia*), 4:7/8 (train yourself for *eusebeia* / *eusebeia* holds promise for present and future life), 6:3 (teaching according to *eusebeia*), 6:5/6 (*eusebeia* as means of gain / *eusebeia* with contentment is great gain), 6:11 (pursue *eusebeia*). Ten occurrences — *eusebeia* is the Pastoral Epistles' signature virtue-term, occurring more in 1 Timothy than in all other NT books combined. It denotes the covenantal orientation of the whole person toward God — not merely piety in a cultic sense but the *Wisdom-register life-pattern* expressed institutionally. CAE Wisdom-register behavioral node: *eusebeia* is the Pastoral Epistles' equivalent of *pneumatikos* (spirit-person — 1 Corinthians 2:15) — the person whose whole orientation is toward God expressed in ordered community life.

*3:16* — the *eusebeia* mystery: *homologoumenōs mega estin to tēs eusebeias mystērion* — "great indeed we confess is the mystery of godliness." The *mystērion tēs eusebeias* — mystery of godliness — is the doctrinal content of the *eusebeia* — the Anointed himself as the ground and pattern of godly life. The six-clause hymn that follows is the *mystērion's* content.

*3:16 — The Christological Hymn:*

*ephanerōthē en sarki* — "manifested in the flesh"
*edikaiōthē en pneumati* — "vindicated by the breath-of-life"
*ōphthē angelois* — "seen by angels"
*ekērychthē en ethnesin* — "proclaimed among the nations"
*episteuthē en kosmō* — "believed on in the world"
*anelēmphthē en doxē* — "taken up in glory"

The six clauses are structured as three antithetical pairs:

**Pair 1:** *sarx* (flesh — earthly / human / visible) / *pneuma* (breath-of-life — divine / vindicating / invisible) — the incarnation-and-vindication axis
**Pair 2:** *angeloi* (heavenly witnesses) / *ethnē* (earthly proclamation) — the cosmic-witness and historical-mission axis
**Pair 3:** *kosmos* (world reception through faith) / *doxa* (heavenly destination through ascension) — the present-faith and eschatological-glory axis

*3:16 CAE NOTE — ephanerōthē en sarki:* The *ephanerōthē* — "was manifested / appeared" — is the *epiphaneia* vocabulary root (appearing / manifestation) that becomes the Pastoral Epistles' distinctive eschatological term. The incarnation is the first *epiphaneia* (2 Timothy 1:10 — *dia tēs epiphaneias tou Sōtēros hēmōn Christou Iēsou* — through the appearing of our Savior the Anointed Jesus) / the *parousia* is the second *epiphaneia* (6:14 / Titus 2:13). The *en sarki* — "in the flesh" — is the most compressed incarnation statement in the NT — the one who is in the form of God (Philippians 2:6) and the image of God (Colossians 1:15) was manifested *en sarki* — in the full reality of human embodied existence. CAE Chain 15 (Eden-to-new-creation) incarnation node: **Genesis 1:26–27 LXX (humanity created in the image) → Colossians 1:15 (the Anointed as the image) → 1 Timothy 3:16 (*ephanerōthē en sarki* — the image manifested in flesh)**. The image-bearer became flesh so that flesh-bearers might be restored to the image.

*3:16 CAE NOTE — edikaiōthē en pneumati:* The *edikaiōthē* — "was declared righteous / vindicated" — is the *dikaioō* vocabulary lock applied to the Anointed himself. The Anointed was vindicated — declared righteous — by the Breath-of-life in his resurrection. The resurrection is the Breath-of-life's verdict on the crucifixion — where the cross appeared to be the Folly register's condemnation of the Anointed (*morphē doulou* / *thanatos staurou* — Philippians 2:7–8), the resurrection is the Wisdom register's vindication. **CAE cross-link: Romans 1:4 (*horisthentos huiou theou en dynamei kata pneuma hagiōsynēs ex anastaseōs nekrōn* — declared Son of God in power according to the breath-of-holiness by resurrection from the dead)**. The *edikaiōthē en pneumati* of 3:16 and the *horisthentos... kata pneuma* of Romans 1:4 are the two primary formulations of resurrection-as-pneumatic-vindication in the NT. Chain 1 (*hilastērion*) pneumatic-vindication node confirmed.

*epiphaneia* — "appearing / manifestation": 6:14 (*mechri tēs epiphaneias tou kyriou hēmōn Iēsou Christou* — until the appearing of our Lord Jesus the Anointed). The *epiphaneia* is the Pastoral Epistles' distinctive term for the *parousia* — five occurrences across the three letters (1 Timothy 6:14 / 2 Timothy 1:10 / 2 Timothy 4:1/8 / Titus 2:13). The term emphasizes the *visible manifestation* dimension of the Lord's return — the *parousia* as the public *epiphaneia* of the one who is presently hidden (*hyparchōn en morphē theou* — Philippians 2:6). CAE eschatological node: the two *epiphaneiai* — incarnation (3:16 / 2 Timothy 1:10) and *parousia* (6:14 / Titus 2:13) — frame the entire redemptive arc. The first *epiphaneia* is *en sarki* (in humility) / the second *epiphaneia* is *en doxē* (in glory — the final clause of the 3:16 hymn anticipating the second appearing).

*6:14–16 CAE NOTE — The doxology:* *ho makarios kai monos dynastēs — ho basileus tōn basileuontōn kai kyrios tōn kyrieuontōn — ho monos echōn athanasian — phōs oikōn aprositon — hon eiden oudeis anthrōpōn oude idein dynatai — hō timē kai kratos aiōnion* — "he who is the blessed and only Sovereign — the King of kings and Lord of lords — who alone has immortality — who dwells in unapproachable light — whom no one has ever seen or can see — to him be honor and everlasting dominion." The most exalted divine-transcendence doxology in the Pauline corpus — five attributes of absolute divine uniqueness: (1) *monos dynastēs* — only Sovereign; (2) *basileus basileuontōn / kyrios kyrieuontōn* — King of kings / Lord of lords; (3) *monos echōn athanasian* — alone having immortality; (4) *phōs oikōn aprositon* — dwelling in unapproachable light; (5) *hon eiden oudeis* — whom no one has seen or can see.

**LXX-primacy argument: the doxology draws on multiple LXX traditions:** (a) *basileus basileuontōn / kyrios kyrieuontōn* — Deuteronomy 10:17 LXX (*ho theos tōn theōn kai kyrios tōn kyriōn* — God of gods and Lord of lords) + Daniel 2:47 LXX (*ho theos hymōn autos estin theos theōn kai kyrios tōn basilēōn* — your God is God of gods and Lord of kings); (b) *phōs oikōn aprositon* — Psalm 103:2 LXX (*anaballon phōs hōs himation* — wrapping himself in light as a garment) + Exodus 33:20 LXX (no one can see the Lord's face and live); (c) *monos echōn athanasian* — Wisdom of Solomon 12:1 LXX / Wisdom of Solomon 15:3 LXX (divine immortality). **The doxology's five attributes are all LXX-derived — demonstrating that the Pastoral Epistles' highest divine-transcendence language is constructed from LXX tradition.** CAE Chain 10 (*alētheia*) / divine-transcendence node: the *phōs aprositon* — unapproachable light — is the highest transcendence statement in the NT, in tension with the *phōs* of 2 Corinthians 4:6 (God who shone in our hearts) — the same light that is unapproachable in itself is mediated through the Anointed's face. Cross-link: Revelation 21:23 (*ho lychnos autēs to arnion* — the Lamb is the lamp of the new Jerusalem) — the unapproachable light of the transcendent God becomes the accessible light of the new creation through the Lamb.

*sōtēr* — "Savior": 1:1 (God our Savior), 2:3 (God our Savior), 4:10 (the living God who is the Savior of all). The *Sōtēr* title — "Savior" — appears six times in the Pastoral Epistles (three in 1 Timothy / two in Titus / one in 2 Timothy) — more than in all other Pauline letters combined. The title is applied to both God the Father (1:1 / 2:3 / Titus 1:3 / 2:10 / 3:4) and to the Lord Jesus the Anointed (2 Timothy 1:10 / Titus 1:4 / 2:13 / 3:6). The divine *Sōtēr* title — applied to both Father and Son — is the Pastoral Epistles' contribution to the divine-identity argument: the one who is called *Sōtēr* in the LXX (Isaiah 45:21 LXX — *theos dikaios kai sōzōn* — a righteous God and Savior / Isaiah 43:11 LXX — *egō eimi ho theos — kai ouk estin parex emou sōzōn* — I am God and there is no Savior beside me) is applied to both the Father and the Son. **LXX-primacy argument: the *Sōtēr* title applied to both Father and Son in the Pastorals depends on the LXX's *Sōtēr* vocabulary for YHWH (Isaiah 43:11 / 45:21 LXX) — the same YHWH-*Sōtēr* title is distributed across both divine persons, making the divine-identity claim through the *Sōtēr* vocabulary lock.**

*dikaiosynē* — "righteousness": 6:11 (*dikaiosynēn* — pursue righteousness). Single occurrence — consistent with the Pastorals' shift from forensic to virtue-ethical register. The *dikaiosynē* of 6:11 is not the forensic *dikaiosynē theou* of Romans/Galatians but the Pastoral virtue-list item — righteousness as the practical behavioral expression of the *eusebeia* orientation.

*zōē aiōnios* — "everlasting life": 1:16 (*zōēn aiōnion* — everlasting life), 6:12 (*zōēs aiōniou* — everlasting life), 6:19 (*tēs ontōs zōēs* — that which is truly life). Three life-references — the *zōē aiōnios* vocabulary lock holds. The 6:19 *ontōs zōē* — "truly life / life that is life indeed" — is the most emphatic *zōē* statement in the Pauline corpus: the present material life is not the *ontōs zōē* — real/genuine life — but the eschatological life that those who are rich in good works lay hold of.

*kardia* — "heart": 1:5 (covenant-love from a pure *kardias*). Single occurrence — the *kardia* as the origin-point of the whole Christian ethical life: pure heart (*kardia*) + good conscience (*syneidēsis*) + sincere faith (*pistis*) = the three-source structure of the covenant-love that is the aim of the apostolic charge. CAE Wisdom-register three-source analysis: the Wisdom-register life (*agapē* / covenant-love) originates in a purified *kardia* (the register-orientation faculty), operates through a clear *syneidēsis* (the moral-discernment faculty), and is sustained by genuine *pistis* (the faith-faculty). The Folly register's counterfeit: seared conscience (4:2) + shipwrecked faith (1:19) + impure heart = the three-source structure of Folly-register religious performance.

*hygiainousa didaskalia* — "sound doctrine": 1:10 (*hygiainousē didaskalia*), 6:3 (*hygiainousin logois*). The *hygiainō* — "to be healthy / sound" — medical vocabulary applied to doctrine is a Pastoral-distinctive: doctrine can be healthy or diseased. The false teachers produce *logomachias* — word-battles — and *nosōn peri zētēseis* — sick cravings for controversies (6:4 — *nosōn* is the disease verb). CAE Wisdom/Folly medical metaphor: Wisdom-register doctrine produces *hygieia* — health — in the community / Folly-register teaching produces *nosos* — disease. The called-out assembly as the *stēlos kai hedraiōma tēs alētheias* (3:15) is also the community of *hygiainousa didaskalia* — the truth-structure and the health-structure are the same thing.

**STRUCTURAL CAE NODES**

**2:1–7 — Universal prayer / one God / one mediator / universal ransom:**
The most tightly structured universal-scope soteriological argument in the Pauline corpus — four steps in seven verses: (1) pray for all people (2:1) → (2) God desires all to be saved and know the truth (2:4) → (3) one God and one mediator — the man the Anointed Jesus (2:5) → (4) who gave himself as *antilytron hyper pantōn* — ransom for all (2:6). The argument moves from the imperative (pray for all) through the theological ground (God's universal salvific will / one mediator) to the soteriological basis (the universal ransom). The universal scope of prayer is grounded in the universal scope of the ransom — the reason to pray for all is that the Anointed gave himself as ransom for all. **Chain 1 (*hilastērion*) universal-ransom node confirmed.** CAE structural note: the *heis theos / heis mesitēs* formula of 2:5 establishes that the universality is not diffused across multiple mediators but concentrated in the one Anointed who mediates for all through the one ransom.

**3:16 — The six-clause mystery of godliness:**
The most compressed Christological hymn in the NT — six clauses covering incarnation / vindication / cosmic witness / mission / faith-reception / glorification in approximately thirty words. The hymn's CAE function is as a doctrinal anchor-point for the *eusebeia* of the household of God — the mystery of godliness is the person and work of the Anointed in its full arc. The six clauses map onto the CAE's primary Christological claims:

*ephanerōthē en sarki* — Chain 15 (Eden-to-new-creation): the image manifested in flesh / the new Adam
*edikaiōthē en pneumati* — Chain 1 (*hilastērion*): the Breath-of-life's resurrection-vindication
*ōphthē angelois* — Chain 9 (*skēnē / naos*): the cosmic-sanctuary witness
*ekērychthē en ethnesin* — Chain 10 (*alētheia*): the truth proclaimed to all nations
*episteuthē en kosmō* — Chain 8 (*diathēkē*): the covenant received through faith in the world
*anelēmphthē en doxē* — Chain 14 (*prōtotokos*): the firstborn taken up in glory

All six clauses are CAE-chain nodes in miniature — the hymn is the doctrinal deposit's six-point summary.

**LXX-primacy argument at 3:16:** The *sarx / pneuma* contrast in the first pair echoes Genesis 6:3 LXX (*ou mē katameinē to pneuma mou en tois anthrōpois eis ton aiōna — dioti sarkos eisin* — my breath-of-life will not remain in humanity forever — for they are flesh) — the foundational *pneuma / sarx* opposition of the LXX. The incarnation (*ephanerōthē en sarki*) and vindication (*edikaiōthē en pneumati*) reverse the Genesis 6:3 LXX dynamic: where the *pneuma* withdrew from *sarx* in judgment, now the *pneuma* vindicates the one who entered *sarx* in grace.

**5:6 — Dead while living:**
*hē de spatalōsa zōsa tethnekes* — "she who is self-indulgent is dead even while she lives." The most compressed Wisdom/Folly register death-in-life statement in the Pastorals. The self-indulgent person is ontologically dead — *tethnekes* (perfect tense — has died and remains dead) — while biologically living (*zōsa*). CAE Wisdom/Folly axis: biological life without Wisdom-register orientation is ontological death — the Fall as a Folly event (CAE v2.2) produces this condition: the lie operates as ontological death through language. The *spatalōsa* — "self-indulgent / living for pleasure" — is the embodied Folly register — the *sarx*-oriented life that produces death while appearing to be alive. Cross-link: Revelation 3:1 (*onoma echeis hoti zēs kai nekros ei* — you have the name of being alive but you are dead) — the same death-while-living motif in the letters to the seven assemblies.

**6:6–10/17–19 — Godliness with contentment / the love of money:**
The *eusebeia meta autarkeias* — "godliness with contentment" — is great gain (6:6) because its *kerdos* — gain — is the *ontōs zōē* — truly life — of 6:19, not the material accumulation that the *philargyria* — love of money — pursues. The *philargyria riza pantōn tōn kakōn* — "love of money is a root of all kinds of evils" (6:10) — is the Pastoral form of the Wisdom/Folly axis: the *philargyria* is the Folly-register orientation toward material security as the substitute for *eusebeia* and *alētheia*. Those who pursue it *apeplanthēsan apo tēs pisteōs* — "have wandered away from the faith" — the same wandering vocabulary as 1:6 (*astochēsantes* — swerving / missing the mark) and 6:21 (*astochēsantes* — swerving from the faith). CAE Wisdom/Folly axis: the *philargyria* is not merely greed but a fundamental orientation-displacement — substituting material security for covenantal security.

**6:20 — Guard the deposit:**
*ō Timothee — tēn parathēkēn phylaxon* — "O Timothy — guard the deposit entrusted to you." The *parathēkē* — "deposit / trust" — is the Pastoral Epistles' signature term for the body of apostolic teaching. It appears here and in 2 Timothy 1:12/14 — three occurrences constituting the *parathēkē*-vocabulary lock. The *parathēkē* is not a fixed creed but the living deposit of the *alētheia* — the sound doctrine entrusted to Timothy by Paul, which Timothy must guard against *pseudōnymos gnōsis* — "falsely-named knowledge" (6:20). CAE *alētheia*-chain institutional node: the called-out assembly as the *stēlos kai hedraiōma tēs alētheias* (3:15) maintains its structural function through the *parathēkē*-guardianship of its leaders — Timothy's ministry is the personal expression of the community's institutional truth-guardianship.

**2 TIMOTHY — GLE v2.2 CAE NOTES**

---

**TEXT BASE:** RP2026 Byzantine Majority Text

**VERSE COUNT:** 47 verses / 4 chapters

---

**2 TIMOTHY IN THE CORPUS ARCHITECTURE**

2 Timothy is the most personally intimate and eschatologically urgent document in the Pauline corpus — the last letter, written from Roman imprisonment with the apostle's execution anticipated (4:6–8). Where 1 Timothy ordered the household of God institutionally, 2 Timothy addresses the personal succession of the apostolic deposit — the *parathēkē* must pass from Paul to Timothy to faithful men to others (2:2) — a four-generation transmission chain that is the Pastoral Epistles' explicit answer to the question of doctrinal continuity after the apostolic generation. The letter's emotional register is unique in the corpus: the theological density of the earlier letters is present but filtered through the lens of personal farewell — Paul's *agonisma* (fight), *dromos* (race), and *pistis* (faith) are complete (4:7); the *stephanos dikaiosynēs* — crown of righteousness — awaits (4:8).

The letter's CAE function is concentrated in five nodes: the *epiphaneia* declaration of 1:10 — the fullest single-verse statement of the incarnation-as-abolition-of-death in the NT; the *parathēkē* transmission formula of 1:12/14 — the deposit guarded by the Holy Breath-of-life; the *pistos ho logos* dying-and-living sequence of 2:11–13 — the most compact death-and-resurrection participatory formula in the Pastoral Epistles; the *theopneustos* declaration of 3:16 — the only explicit divine-inbreathing-of-Scripture statement in the NT; and the *stephanos dikaiosynēs* / *epiphaneia* eschatological conclusion of 4:8 — the *dikaiosynē* crown and the *epiphaneia* as the personal-eschatological endpoint of the apostolic life.

---

**KEY LEXICAL DECISIONS**

*pneuma* — "breath-of-life": 1:7 (*pneuma deilias... pneuma dynameōs kai agapēs kai sōphronismou* — breath-of-life of fear / breath-of-life of power and covenant-love and self-control), 1:14 (*pneumatos hagiou* — Holy Breath-of-life guarding the deposit), 4:22 (*pneumati* — the Lord with your breath-of-life). Three structurally significant uses.

*1:7 CAE NOTE:* *ou gar edōken hēmin ho theos pneuma deilias alla dynameōs kai agapēs kai sōphronismou* — "for God gave us not a breath-of-life of fear but of power and covenant-love and self-control." The *pneuma deilias* — breath-of-life of fear — is the Folly-register spiritual orientation: cowardice / shrinking / shame-avoidance. The *pneuma dynameōs kai agapēs kai sōphronismou* — the breath-of-life of power and covenant-love and self-control — is the three-attribute characterization of the Holy Breath-of-life's effect in the life of the believer. The three attributes form a triad parallel to 1 Thessalonians 1:3 (faith / covenant-love / hope): *dynamis* (power — the Wisdom-register capacity to act under pressure) / *agapē* (covenant-love — the Wisdom-register relational orientation) / *sōphronismos* (self-control / sound-mindedness — the Wisdom-register cognitive governance). CAE pneumatological map: the Holy Breath-of-life produces a specific character-profile in its recipients — not timidity but empowered covenant-love expressed through ordered self-governance. The context (Timothy being urged not to be ashamed — 1:8) makes the *pneuma deilias* / *pneuma dynameōs* contrast existentially urgent: the apostolic succession requires the *pneuma dynameōs* because the Folly register's primary weapon against witnesses is shame and fear.

*1:14 CAE NOTE:* *tēn kalēn parathēkēn phylaxon dia pneumatos hagiou tou enoikountos en hēmin* — "by the Holy Breath-of-life who dwells in us guard the good deposit entrusted to you." The *parathēkē* is guarded not by Timothy's intellectual vigilance alone but *by the Holy Breath-of-life who dwells in us*. The *enoikountos* — "dwelling in / indwelling" — is the same *enoikeō* vocabulary as Romans 8:11 (*to pneuma tou enoikountos en hymin* — the breath-of-life dwelling in you) and 2 Corinthians 6:16 (LXX catena — *enoikēsō en autois* — I will dwell among them). **CAE Chain 9 (*skēnē / naos*) pneumatic-indwelling node:** the Holy Breath-of-life's indwelling in the believer (1:14) is the personal-level expression of the community-level *naos* of 1 Corinthians 3:16 / 6:19 — the deposit is guarded by the same indwelling Breath-of-life who constitutes the believer as *naos*. The *parathēkē*-guardianship is a pneumatic act before it is a cognitive one.

*epiphaneia* — "appearing / manifestation": 1:10 (*epiphaneias tou Sōtēros hēmōn Christou Iēsou* — the appearing of our Savior the Anointed Jesus), 4:1 (*kata tēn epiphaneian autou kai tēn basileian autou* — by his appearing and his kingdom), 4:8 (*tois ēgapēkosin tēn epiphaneian autou* — to all who have loved his appearing). Three occurrences — the highest *epiphaneia* density in the Pastoral Epistles. The three uses span the full *epiphaneia* arc: the first appearing already accomplished (1:10) / the second appearing as the criterion of judgment (4:1) / the second appearing as the object of the believer's love and longing (4:8).

*1:10 CAE NOTE:* *phanerōtheisan de nyn dia tēs epiphaneias tou Sōtēros hēmōn Christou Iēsou — katargēsantos men ton thanaton — phōtisantos de zōēn kai aphthasian dia tou euangeliou* — "which now has been manifested through the appearing of our Savior the Anointed Jesus — who abolished death and brought life and incorruption to light through the glad tidings." The most comprehensive single-verse statement of the incarnation's soteriological effect in the NT — three elements: (1) the grace pre-given before the ages *ephanerōthē* — was manifested — through the *epiphaneia* of the Anointed; (2) *katargēsantos ton thanaton* — he abolished / rendered inoperative death; (3) *phōtisantos zōēn kai aphthasian* — brought to light life and incorruption through the glad tidings. The *katargeō* — "abolish / render inoperative" — applied to death is the strongest anti-death verb in the NT — death is not merely defeated but made *argos* — inoperative / without effect. **Chain 15 (Eden-to-new-creation) death-abolition node:**

**Genesis 2:17 LXX (*thanatō apothanē* — you will surely die — death enters through the Fall) → 1 Corinthians 15:26 (*eschatos echthros katargeitai ho thanatos* — the last enemy to be abolished is death) → 2 Timothy 1:10 (*katargēsantos ton thanaton*) → Revelation 20:14 (*ho thanatos kai ho hadēs eblēthēsan eis tēn limnēn tou pyros* — death and Hades thrown into the lake of fire)**

The death-abolition chain is confirmed across four witnesses. The *phōtisantos zōēn kai aphthasian* — bringing life and incorruption to light — is the positive content of the abolition: where death was the Folly register's ultimate power (CAE v2.2 — lies operate as ontological death), the *epiphaneia* of the Anointed has brought the Wisdom-register's ultimate reality (*zōē / aphtharsia*) into visibility through the glad tidings. **LXX-primacy argument: the *aphtharsia* — incorruption — vocabulary connects to Wisdom of Solomon 2:23 LXX (*ho theos ektisen ton anthrōpon ep' aphtharsią* — God created humanity for incorruption) and Wisdom of Solomon 6:18–19 LXX (*agapē tērēsis nomōn autēs — prosochē de nomōn bebaīōsis aphtharsias — aphtharsia de eggys einai poiei theou* — love is keeping her laws / attention to laws is assurance of incorruption / incorruption brings one near to God). The *aphtharsia* as the goal of human creation (Wisdom of Solomon 2:23 LXX) is restored by the Anointed's *epiphaneia* — the deuterocanonical LXX text provides the creation-*aphtharsia* framework that the NT fulfillment presupposes.**

*parathēkē* — "deposit": 1:12 (*tēn parathēkēn mou* — what has been entrusted to me), 1:14 (*tēn kalēn parathēkēn* — the good deposit). The *parathēkē* vocabulary lock — introduced at 1 Timothy 6:20 — reaches its fullest expression here. The 1:12 formulation is remarkable: *pepeismai hoti dynatos estin tēn parathēkēn mou phylaxai eis ekeinēn tēn hēmeran* — "I am convinced that he is able to guard until that day what has been entrusted to me." The *parathēkēn mou* — "what has been entrusted to me" — Paul's own life and ministry as a deposit held by God, not merely the doctrinal content entrusted to Timothy. The double movement: God guards Paul's deposit (1:12) / the Holy Breath-of-life enables Timothy to guard the doctrinal deposit (1:14). The *parathēkē* is both the content of the apostolic teaching and the apostle's own life as a trust held by God until the day of the Lord.

*theopneustos* — "breathed out by God": 3:16 — *pasa graphē theopneustos kai ōphelimos pros didaskalian — pros elegmon — pros epanorthōsin — pros paideian tēn en dikaiosynē* — "all Scripture is breathed out by God and profitable for teaching — for reproof — for correction — and for training in righteousness." The *theopneustos* is a hapax legomenon in the NT and in all of Greek literature to this point — a Pauline coinage or adaptation. Its components: *theos* (God) + *pneō* (breathe) — "God-breathed." The direction of the breath is outward — God breathes *out* into the Scripture, not merely inspiring (breathing into) human authors. **PRIMARY CAE NODE — Genesis 2:7 LXX connection:**

*3:16 CAE NOTE:* The *theopneustos* vocabulary is constructed on the *pnoē* / *pneō* root — the same root as Genesis 2:7 LXX: *enephysēsen eis to prosōpon autou pnoēn zōēs* — "he breathed into his face the breath (*pnoēn*) of life." The same divine breath that animated Adam (*pnoē zōēs*) has been breathed out (*theopneustos*) into the Scripture — the Scripture is alive with the same divine *pnoē* that constituted human life. **CAE Chain 15 (Eden-to-new-creation) divine-breath node:**

**Genesis 2:7 LXX (*enephysēsen pnoēn zōēs* — breathed the breath of life into Adam) → 2 Thessalonians 2:8 (*pnoē tou stomatos* — breath of his mouth destroys the lawless one) → 2 Timothy 3:16 (*theopneustos* — Scripture breathed out by the same divine breath) → John 20:22 (*enephysēsen kai legei... labete pneuma hagion* — he breathed and said receive the Holy Breath-of-life)**

The divine *pnoē/pneuma* is the animating principle of creation (Genesis 2:7 LXX) / the destructive weapon against the Folly register (2 Thessalonians 2:8) / the life-giving content of Scripture (2 Timothy 3:16) / the gift of the risen Lord to his community (John 20:22). Chain 15 receives its Scripture-as-divine-breath node.

The four-part function of *theopneustos* Scripture — *didaskalia* (teaching) / *elegmos* (reproof / conviction) / *epanorthōsis* (correction / restoration to upright position) / *paideia en dikaiosynē* (training in righteousness) — is the Pastoral form of the CAE's Wisdom-register formation sequence: the *theopneustos* Scripture teaches the Wisdom register's content / reproves Folly-register patterns / corrects the direction of drift / trains the whole person in *dikaiosynē*. The *hina artios ē ho tou theou anthrōpos* — "that the man of God may be complete" — is the endpoint: the *theopneustos* Scripture's goal is the fully equipped person of God ready for every good work — the Wisdom-register anthropological endpoint stated institutionally.

**LXX-primacy argument at 3:15–16:** *ta hiera grammata* — "the holy writings" — of 3:15 from which Timothy has known since childhood are the LXX Scriptures — the Greek OT that constituted the Bible of the early communities. The *graphē* of 3:16 that is *theopneustos* is the LXX canon including the deuterocanonicals — the Pastoral Epistles operate with the same LXX canon that the entire CAE has demonstrated the NT authors using throughout. The *theopneustos* declaration applies to the LXX text that Timothy has known since childhood — the Rahlfs-Hanhart LXX canon is the Scripture the Pastoral Epistles declare God-breathed.

*dikaiosynē* — "righteousness": 2:22 (*dikaiosynēn* — pursue righteousness), 3:16 (*paideia tēn en dikaiosynē* — training in righteousness), 4:8 (*stephanos tēs dikaiosynēs* — crown of righteousness / *ho dikaios kritēs* — the righteous judge). Three structurally ascending uses: behavioral pursuit (2:22) / formational training (3:16) / eschatological crown (4:8).

*4:8 CAE NOTE:* *loipon apokeitai moi ho tēs dikaiosynēs stephanos — hon apodōsei moi ho kyrios en ekeinē tē hēmera ho dikaios kritēs — ou monon de emoi alla kai pāsin tois ēgapēkosin tēn epiphaneian autou* — "henceforth there is laid up for me the crown of righteousness which the Lord — the righteous judge — will award to me on that day — and not only to me but also to all who have loved his appearing." The *stephanos tēs dikaiosynēs* — crown of righteousness — is the eschatological *dikaiosynē* in its final form: the *dikaiosynē ek theou* (Philippians 3:9) received through faith / the *dikaiosynē* pursued in conduct (2:22) / trained through Scripture (3:16) / crowned at the *epiphaneia* by the *dikaios kritēs* (4:8). The *dikaios kritēs* — righteous judge — is the Lord himself as the one whose judgment is itself the exercise of *dikaiosynē* — the Judge and the crown are the same *dikaiosynē*. CAE eschatological *dikaiosynē* arc:

**Romans 3:22 (*dikaiosynē theou* received through faith) → Philippians 3:9 (*dikaiosynē ek theou* as the ground of apostolic life) → 2 Timothy 4:8 (*stephanos tēs dikaiosynēs* as the eschatological completion)**

The *dikaiosynē* arc: gift received → life lived → crown awarded. Three-stage *dikaiosynē* chain confirmed.

*agapē* — "covenant-love": 1:7 (*agapēs* — breath-of-life of covenant-love), 1:13 (*agapē en Christō Iēsou* — covenant-love in the Anointed Jesus), 2:22 (*agapēn* — pursue covenant-love), 3:10 (*agapēn mou* — my covenant-love as apostolic example), 4:8 (*tois ēgapēkosin tēn epiphaneian autou* — those who have loved his appearing). Five occurrences — the *agapē* vocabulary lock spanning from the Breath-of-life's character (1:7) through behavioral pursuit (2:22) to the *agapē* directed toward the Lord's *epiphaneia* (4:8). The 4:8 *ēgapēkosin tēn epiphaneian* — "loved his appearing" — is the most eschatologically concentrated *agapē* expression in the NT: the covenant-love directed not toward a person present but toward the appearing of the one who is coming. CAE Wisdom-register eschatological orientation: the community's covenant-love reaches forward toward the *epiphaneia* — the present orientation of *agapē* includes the future appearing as its object.

*alētheia* — "truth": 2:15 (*logon tēs alētheias* — word of truth), 2:18 (*tēs alētheias ēstochēsan* — swerved from the truth), 2:25 (*epignōsin alētheias* — knowledge of the truth), 3:7 (*eis epignōsin alētheias elthein* — arrive at a knowledge of the truth), 3:8 (*tē alētheia anthistantai* — oppose the truth), 4:4 (*apo tēs alētheias tēn akoēn apostrepsousin* — turn away from listening to the truth). Six occurrences — the highest *alētheia* density in the Pastoral Epistles across a single letter. The six uses trace the *alētheia's* vulnerability in the last days: the deposit is the *logos tēs alētheias* (2:15) / it can be swerved from (2:18) / opponents oppose it (3:8) / people turn their hearing away from it (4:4) / repentance leads back to its knowledge (2:25). The *alētheia* is under sustained Folly-register attack in 2 Timothy — the letter's urgency is the urgency of *alētheia*-preservation in the face of apostasy.

*2:15 CAE NOTE:* *orthotomonta ton logon tēs alētheias* — "rightly handling the word of truth." The *orthotomeō* — "cut straight / handle correctly / dissect rightly" — is unique in the NT. The image is of straight cutting — a road cut straight through terrain / a surgeon cutting along the correct line / a craftsman cutting fabric true to the pattern. The *logos tēs alētheias* requires *orthotomos* — straight-line handling — because crooked handling (*logomachiai* — word-battles / *kenophōniai* — vain babble) distorts it and produces ungodliness (2:16). CAE Wisdom/Folly axis: the Wisdom register's relationship to the *alētheia* is *orthotomia* — straight, accurate, directionally true handling / the Folly register's relationship to the *alētheia* is *astochein* — missing the mark / swerving / going crooked.

*anastasis* — "resurrection": 2:18 (*tēn anastasin ēdē gegonenai* — the resurrection has already happened — the false teaching). The false-teaching that the resurrection has already happened is the 2 Timothy form of the 2 Thessalonians claim that the day of the Lord has come (2 Thessalonians 2:2) — an over-realized eschatology that collapses the *parousia* / *anastasis* into the present and thereby empties the future *epiphaneia* of content. CAE eschatological note: the resurrection-denial (the form here is not denial that it happened but over-realization — it has already fully occurred) undermines both the community's *hypomonē* (why endure if the end has already come?) and the *parathēkē*'s future orientation (the deposit is held *eis ekeinēn tēn hēmeran* — until that day — 1:12).

*kardia* — "heart": 2:22 (*kardias katharas* — pure heart). Single occurrence — the *kardia* as the criterion of authentic community: those who call on the Lord *from a pure heart* are the company in which righteousness / faith / covenant-love / peace are pursued. The pure-heart vocabulary connects to 1 Timothy 1:5 (covenant-love from a pure *kardia*) — the Pastoral Epistles consistently locate the origin of the Wisdom-register life in the purified *kardia*.

*2:11–13 — The pistos ho logos sequence:*
*pistos ho logos: ei gar synapethanomen kai syzēsomen — ei hypomenomen kai symbasileusomen — ei arnēsometha kakeinos arnēsetai hēmas — ei apistoumen ekeinos pistos menei — arnēsasthai gar heauton ou dynatai* — "the saying is trustworthy: for if we have died with him we will also live with him — if we endure we will also reign with him — if we deny him he also will deny us — if we are faithless he remains faithful — for he cannot deny himself."

The four-clause sequence is the most compressed participatory-eschatology statement in the Pastoral Epistles — structured as two positive conditions / one negative condition / one unconditional ground: (1) co-death → co-life (Romans 6:8 / Galatians 2:19–20 participatory logic); (2) endurance → co-reign (eschatological vindication of *hypomonē*); (3) denial → denial (Matthew 10:33 parallel); (4) faithlessness → his faithfulness remains (the unconditional ground that transcends human failure — he cannot deny himself). The fourth clause is the most theologically significant: even human faithlessness does not nullify the Lord's faithfulness because his faithfulness is grounded in his own nature (*heauton arnēsasthai ou dynatai* — he cannot deny himself). CAE structural note: the *pistos ho logos* formula (trustworthy is the saying — five occurrences across the Pastoral Epistles) marks statements of concentrated doctrinal weight — here it introduces the participatory-eschatology sequence that is the theological ground of Timothy's *hypomonē* under persecution.

**STRUCTURAL CAE NODES**

**1:9–10 — Grace before the ages / epiphaneia abolishes death:**
*kata idian prothesin kai charin — tēn dotheisan hēmin en Christō Iēsou pro chronōn aiōniōn — phanerōtheisan de nyn dia tēs epiphaneias tou Sōtēros hēmōn Christou Iēsou — katargēsantos men ton thanaton — phōtisantos de zōēn kai aphthasian dia tou euangeliou* — "according to his own purpose and grace — which he gave us in the Anointed Jesus before the ages began — and which now has been manifested through the appearing of our Savior the Anointed Jesus — who abolished death and brought life and incorruption to light through the glad tidings."

The most temporally expansive statement in the Pastoral Epistles — the grace given *pro chronōn aiōniōn* (before the ages of time) manifested at the *epiphaneia* and proclaimed through the *euangelion*. Three temporal moments: eternity past (grace given before time) / historical present (grace manifested at the *epiphaneia*) / ongoing proclamation (brought to light through the glad tidings). CAE Chain 4 (*huiothesia*) pre-temporal election node confirmed: **Ephesians 1:4–5 (*pro katabolēs kosmou* — before the foundation of the world) = 2 Timothy 1:9 (*pro chronōn aiōniōn* — before the ages of time)** — the pre-temporal election formula confirmed across two independent formulations.

**LXX-primacy argument at 1:10:** The *aphtharsia* — incorruption — as the content of what the *epiphaneia* brings to light requires Wisdom of Solomon 2:23 LXX (*ep' aphtharsią ektisen ton anthrōpon* — God created humanity for incorruption) as its background. Without the deuterocanonical LXX text, the *aphtharsia* language floats without its creation-grounding. The Anointed's *epiphaneia* restores *aphtharsia* because humanity was created for *aphtharsia* — the restoration presupposes the original design. **LXX-primacy argument confirmed: 2 Timothy 1:10's *aphtharsia* is theologically dependent on Wisdom of Solomon 2:23 LXX.**

**2:2 — The four-generation transmission chain:**
*ha ēkousas par' emou dia pollōn martyrōn — tauta parathou pistois anthrōpois — hoitines hikanoi esontai kai heterous didaxai* — "what you have heard from me in the presence of many witnesses — entrust to faithful men who will be able to teach others also." The most explicit apostolic-succession statement in the NT — four generations: Paul → Timothy → faithful men → others. The *parathēkē* is not deposited in an institution but entrusted to persons (*pistois anthrōpois* — faithful men / *hikanoi* — capable / qualified). The criterion: *pistoi* (faithful / trustworthy) + *hikanoi didaxai* (able to teach). CAE structural note: the transmission chain is the Pastoral Epistles' answer to the eschatological problem of the *parathēkē's* continuity after the apostolic generation — the solution is not canonization alone but personal transmission through qualified persons who replicate the transmission pattern.

**3:15–17 — The holy writings / theopneustos:**
*ta hiera grammata ta dynamenā se sophisai eis sōtērian dia pisteōs tēs en Christō Iēsou — pasa graphē theopneustos kai ōphelimos pros didaskalian* — "the holy writings which are able to make you wise for salvation through faith in the Anointed Jesus — all Scripture is breathed out by God and profitable for teaching."

The passage moves from personal formation (*hiera grammata* making Timothy wise for salvation from childhood) to universal declaration (*pasa graphē theopneustos*). The *theopneustos* declaration is the Pastoral Epistles' epistemological foundation: the Scripture's authority is not derived from the tradition that transmitted it or the community that received it but from the divine *pnoē* that breathed it out. The *parathēkē* (the apostolic deposit / 1:14) and the *graphē* (the *theopneustos* Scripture / 3:16) are the two pillars of the Pastoral Epistles' epistemological structure — the living tradition and the written deposit mutually supporting and interpreting each other.

The four-function sequence (*didaskalia / elegmos / epanorthōsis / paideia en dikaiosynē*) maps the complete Wisdom-register formation cycle: teaching (imparting Wisdom-register content) → reproof (identifying Folly-register patterns) → correction (restoring directional alignment) → training in righteousness (habituating Wisdom-register conduct). The endpoint *artios* — "complete / fully equipped" — is the Pastoral form of the *teleios* — "mature / perfect" — of Ephesians 4:13 / Colossians 1:28: the fully formed person of God equipped for every good work.

**4:1–8 — The charge / the crown / the departure:**
The letter's climactic unit — the solemn charge (4:1–2) / the last-days diagnosis (4:3–5) / the personal farewell (4:6–8). The charge is made *kat' epiphaneian autou kai tēn basileian autou* — "by his appearing and his kingdom" — the most solemn possible grounds in Pastoral Epistles vocabulary. The diagnosis: people will not endure *hygiainousa didaskalia* (sound doctrine) / having *knethousa akoē* (itching ears) / turning away from the *alētheia* to myths — the terminal form of the 1 Timothy apostasy-pattern (1 Timothy 4:1 / 2 Thessalonians 2:10–12).

The farewell (4:6–8) is the most personal Christological-eschatological statement in the Pauline corpus — the apostle's own life as the paradigm of the Wisdom-register life brought to completion:

*ēgōnismai ton kalon agōna* — "I have fought the good fight" — the combat metaphor of 1 Timothy 1:18 / 6:12 fulfilled
*ton dromon teteleka* — "I have finished the race" — the athletic metaphor of 1 Corinthians 9:24–27 / Philippians 3:14 fulfilled
*tēn pistin tetērēka* — "I have kept the faith" — the *parathēkē* guardianship of 1:14 / 1 Timothy 6:20 fulfilled
*apokeitai moi ho tēs dikaiosynēs stephanos* — "there is laid up for me the crown of righteousness" — the eschatological *dikaiosynē* completion

The three perfect tenses (*ēgōnismai / teteleka / tetērēka*) are the language of completed action with permanent results — the apostolic life is finished and its completion is permanent. The *stephanos* awaits — but it is already *apokeimai* (laid up / reserved) — the completion is certain because the Judge is *dikaios*. CAE *dikaiosynē* chain eschatological endpoint confirmed.

**The three metaphors and Chain 1:**
The *agōn* (fight) / *dromos* (race) / *pistis* (faith kept) are three dimensions of the same apostolic life: the *agōn* is combat — the *pneuma dynameōs* (1:7) versus the *pneuma deilias* / the Wisdom register versus the Folly register in sustained conflict; the *dromos* is directed movement — the *parathēkē* transmitted forward through the four-generation chain (2:2) toward the eschatological goal; the *pistis* is faithfulness — the *alētheia* guarded through the *theopneustos* Scripture against the *astochēsantes* (those who have swerved). All three metaphors express the same sustained Wisdom-register orientation maintained to completion.

**LXX-PRIMACY ARGUMENTS IN 2 TIMOTHY**

Five independent LXX dependency points:

1. 1:10 — Wisdom of Solomon 2:23 LXX (*ep' aphtharsią ektisen ton anthrōpon* — God created humanity for incorruption) — the *aphtharsia* restored by the *epiphaneia* presupposes the deuterocanonical creation-*aphtharsia* framework
2. 3:16 — Genesis 2:7 LXX (*enephysēsen pnoēn zōēs* — breathed the breath of life) — the *theopneustos* vocabulary is built on the LXX creation-breath root — Scripture is alive with the same divine *pnoē* that animated Adam
3. 2:19 — Numbers 16:5 LXX (*egnō ho theos tous ontas autou* — God knows those who are his — the Korah rebellion) + Numbers 16:26 LXX (*apostēte apo tōn skēnōn tōn anthrōpōn tōn sklērōn toutōn* — depart from the tents of these wicked men) — the two-clause *themelios* seal of 2:19 cites the Korah narrative LXX
4. 4:17 — Psalm 21:22 LXX (*errhysthēn ek stomatos leontos* — I was rescued from the mouth of the lion — the Psalm of the cross applied to Paul's deliverance at his first defense)
5. 4:14 — Psalm 61:13 LXX / Proverbs 24:12 LXX (*apodōsei... kata ta erga autou* — he will repay according to his works — applied to Alexander the coppersmith's coming judgment)

The *theopneustos* / Genesis 2:7 LXX connection is the most structurally significant — the divine breath of creation becomes the divine breath of Scripture, completing the Chain 15 divine-breath arc.

**CHAIN STATUS AFTER 2 TIMOTHY**

All 15 chains remain closed. 2 Timothy adds:

- Death-abolition: **2 Timothy 1:10** — Chain 15 (Eden-to-new-creation) *katargēsantos ton thanaton* node. Genesis 2:17 LXX (death enters) → 1 Corinthians 15:26 (last enemy abolished) → 2 Timothy 1:10 (abolished at the *epiphaneia*) → Revelation 20:14 (death thrown into the lake of fire). Four-witness death-abolition chain confirmed
- *Aphtharsia* restoration: **2 Timothy 1:10** — Chain 15 reinforced. Wisdom of Solomon 2:23 LXX (humanity created for *aphtharsia*) → 2 Timothy 1:10 (*aphtharsia* brought to light at the *epiphaneia*) — the deuterocanonical creation-design restored at the first *epiphaneia*
- *Theopneustos* / divine breath: **2 Timothy 3:16** — Chain 15 Scripture-as-divine-breath node. Genesis 2:7 LXX (*pnoēn zōēs*) → 2 Timothy 3:16 (*theopneustos*) — the same divine breath animating creation breathed out into Scripture
- Pre-temporal election: **2 Timothy 1:9** — Chain 4 (*huiothesia*) confirmed. Grace given *pro chronōn aiōniōn* — before the ages of time — parallel to Ephesians 1:4 (*pro katabolēs kosmou*)
- *Parathēkē* pneumatic guardianship: **2 Timothy 1:14** — Chain 9 (*naos*) indwelling node. The Holy Breath-of-life *enoikountos* — dwelling in us — guards the deposit. The same *enoikeō* vocabulary as the *naos*-indwelling chain
- *Dikaiosynē* eschatological crown: **2 Timothy 4:8** — *dikaiosynē* arc completed. Gift received (Romans 3:22) → life lived (Philippians 3:9) → crown awarded (2 Timothy 4:8). The *epiphaneia* as the eschatological completion-point of the entire *dikaiosynē* arc
- *Epiphaneia* eschatology: **2 Timothy 1:10 / 4:1 / 4:8** — the two-*epiphaneia* arc completed. First *epiphaneia* (incarnation / death-abolition / *aphtharsia* revealed) → second *epiphaneia* (judgment / *stephanos dikaiosynēs* / kingdom). The Pastoral Epistles' distinctive eschatological vocabulary fully instantiated

\id ROM
\h Romans
\toc1 The Epistle to the Romans
\mt The Epistle to the Romans

\c 1
\p
\v 1 Paul — a servant of Jesus the Anointed (*Iēsou Christou*) — called as an apostle — set apart for the glad tidings (*euangelion*) of God —
\v 2 which he promised beforehand through his prophets in the holy Scriptures —
\v 3 concerning his Son — who was born of the seed of David according to the flesh (*sarkos*) —
\v 4 who was designated Son of God in power according to the breath-of-life of holiness (*kata pneuma hagiōsynēs*) by the resurrection from the dead — Jesus the Anointed our Lord —
\v 5 through whom we have received grace and apostleship for the obedience of faith among all the nations for the sake of his name —
\v 6 among whom you also are called of Jesus the Anointed.
\p
\v 7 To all who are in Rome — beloved of God — called as holy ones: Grace to you and peace from God our Father and the Lord Jesus the Anointed.
\p
\v 8 First I thank my God through Jesus the Anointed for all of you — because your faith is proclaimed in all the world.
\v 9 For God is my witness — whom I serve with my breath-of-life (*pneumati*) in the glad tidings (*euangeliō*) of his Son — that without ceasing I mention you
\v 10 always in my prayers — asking that somehow by God's will I may now at last succeed in coming to you.
\v 11 For I long to see you — that I may impart to you some spiritual gift (*charisma pneumatikon*) to strengthen you —
\v 12 that is — that we may be mutually encouraged by each other's faith — both yours and mine.
\v 13 I do not want you to be unaware brothers that I have often intended to come to you — but thus far have been prevented — in order that I may reap some harvest among you as well as among the rest of the Gentiles.
\v 14 I am under obligation both to Greeks and to barbarians — both to the wise and to the foolish.
\v 15 So I am eager to proclaim the glad tidings (*euangelisasthai*) to you also who are in Rome.
\p
\v 16 For I am not ashamed of the glad tidings (*euangelion*) — for it is the power of God for salvation (*sōtērian*) to everyone who believes — to the Jew first and also to the Greek.
\v 17 For in it the righteousness of God (*dikaiosynē theou*) is revealed from faith to faith — as it is written: But the righteous shall live by faith (*ho de dikaios ek pisteōs zēsetai*).
\p
\v 18 For the wrath of God is revealed from heaven against all ungodliness and unrighteousness of men who suppress the truth (*alētheian*) in unrighteousness —
\v 19 because what can be known of God is plain to them — for God has made it plain to them.
\v 20 For his invisible attributes — his eternal power and divine nature — have been clearly perceived since the creation of the world in the things that have been made — so they are without excuse.
\v 21 For although they knew God they did not glorify him as God or give thanks to him — but they became futile in their thinking and their foolish heart (*kardia*) was darkened.
\v 22 Claiming to be wise they became fools —
\v 23 and exchanged the glory of the immortal God for images resembling mortal man and birds and animals and creeping things.
\v 24 Therefore God gave them up in the lusts of their hearts (*kardias*) to impurity — to the dishonoring of their bodies among themselves —
\v 25 because they exchanged the truth (*alētheian*) of God for the lie (*pseudos*) and worshipped and served the creature rather than the Creator — who is blessed forever. Amen.
\v 26 For this reason God gave them up to dishonorable passions. For their women exchanged natural relations for those that are contrary to nature —
\v 27 and the men likewise gave up natural relations with women and were consumed with passion for one another — men committing shameless acts with men and receiving in themselves the due penalty for their error.
\v 28 And since they did not see fit to acknowledge God — God gave them up to a debased mind to do what ought not to be done.
\v 29 They were filled with all manner of unrighteousness — evil — covetousness — malice. They are full of envy — murder — strife — deceit — maliciousness. They are gossips —
\v 30 slanderers — haters of God — insolent — haughty — boastful — inventors of evil — disobedient to parents —
\v 31 foolish — faithless — heartless — ruthless.
\v 32 Though they know God's righteous decree that those who practice such things deserve to die — they not only do them but give approval to those who practice them.

\c 2
\p
\v 1 Therefore you have no excuse O man — every one of you who judges. For in passing judgment on another you condemn yourself — because you the judge practice the very same things.
\v 2 We know that the judgment of God rightly falls on those who practice such things.
\v 3 Do you suppose O man — you who judge those who practice such things and yet do them yourself — that you will escape the judgment of God?
\v 4 Or do you presume on the riches of his kindness and forbearance and patience — not knowing that God's kindness is meant to lead you to repentance?
\v 5 But because of your hard and unrepentant heart (*kardia*) you are storing up wrath for yourself on the day of wrath when God's righteous judgment will be revealed.
\v 6 He will render to each one according to his works:
\v 7 to those who by patience in well-doing seek for glory and honor and immortality — everlasting life (*zōēn aiōnion*) —
\v 8 but for those who are self-seeking and do not obey the truth (*alētheia*) but obey unrighteousness — there will be wrath and fury.
\v 9 There will be tribulation and distress for every human being who does evil — the Jew first and also the Greek —
\v 10 but glory and honor and peace for everyone who does good — the Jew first and also the Greek.
\v 11 For God shows no partiality.
\p
\v 12 For all who have sinned without the Law will also perish without the Law — and all who have sinned under the Law will be judged by the Law.
\v 13 For it is not the hearers of the Law who are righteous (*dikaioi*) before God but the doers of the Law who will be declared righteous (*dikaiōthēsontai*).
\v 14 For when Gentiles who do not have the Law do by nature what the Law requires they are a law to themselves even though they do not have the Law.
\v 15 They show that the work of the Law is written on their hearts (*kardias*) — while their conscience also bears witness — and their conflicting thoughts accuse or even excuse them —
\v 16 on the day when according to my glad tidings (*euangelion*) God judges the secrets of men through Jesus the Anointed.
\p
\v 17 But if you call yourself a Jew and rely on the Law and boast in God —
\v 18 and know his will and approve what is excellent because you are instructed from the Law —
\v 19 and if you are sure that you yourself are a guide to the blind — a light to those who are in darkness —
\v 20 an instructor of the foolish — a teacher of children — having in the Law the embodiment of knowledge and truth (*alētheias*) —
\v 21 you then who teach others — do you not teach yourself? While you preach against stealing do you steal?
\v 22 You who say that one must not commit adultery — do you commit adultery? You who abhor idols — do you rob temples?
\v 23 You who boast in the Law dishonor God by breaking the Law.
\v 24 For as it is written — the name of God is blasphemed among the Gentiles because of you.
\v 25 For circumcision indeed is of value if you obey the Law — but if you break the Law your circumcision becomes uncircumcision.
\v 26 So if a man who is uncircumcised keeps the precepts of the Law — will not his uncircumcision be regarded as circumcision?
\v 27 Then he who is physically uncircumcised but keeps the Law will condemn you who have the written code and circumcision but break the Law.
\v 28 For no one is a Jew who is merely one outwardly — nor is circumcision outward and physical.
\v 29 But a Jew is one inwardly — and circumcision is a matter of the heart (*kardia*) — by the breath-of-life (*pneumati*) — not by the letter. His praise is not from man but from God.

\c 3
\p
\v 1 Then what advantage has the Jew? Or what is the value of circumcision?
\v 2 Much in every way. To begin with — the Jews were entrusted with the oracles of God.
\v 3 What if some were unfaithful? Does their faithlessness nullify the faithfulness of God?
\v 4 By no means! Let God be true though every man a liar — as it is written: So that you may be justified (*dikaiōthēs*) in your words and prevail when you are judged.
\v 5 But if our unrighteousness serves to show the righteousness (*dikaiosynēn*) of God — what shall we say? That God is unrighteous to inflict wrath on us? — I speak in a human way —
\v 6 By no means! For then how could God judge the world?
\v 7 But if through my lie God's truth (*alētheia*) abounds to his glory — why am I still being condemned as a sinner?
\v 8 And why not do evil that good may come? — as some people slanderously charge us with saying. Their condemnation is just.
\p
\v 9 What then? Are we Jews any better off? No — not at all. For we have already charged that all — both Jews and Greeks — are under sin —
\v 10 as it is written: None is righteous (*dikaios*) — no not one —
\v 11 no one understands — no one seeks for God.
\v 12 All have turned aside — together they have become worthless — no one does good — not even one.
\v 13 Their throat is an open grave — they use their tongues to deceive. The venom of asps is under their lips.
\v 14 Their mouth is full of curses and bitterness.
\v 15 Their feet are swift to shed blood —
\v 16 in their paths are ruin and misery —
\v 17 and the way of peace they have not known.
\v 18 There is no fear of God before their eyes.
\p
\v 19 Now we know that whatever the Law says it speaks to those who are under the Law — so that every mouth may be stopped and the whole world may be held accountable to God.
\v 20 For by works of the Law no human being will be declared righteous (*dikaiōthēsetai*) in his sight — since through the Law comes knowledge of sin.
\p
\v 21 But now the righteousness of God (*dikaiosynē theou*) has been manifested apart from the Law — although the Law and the Prophets bear witness to it —
\v 22 the righteousness of God through faith in Jesus the Anointed for all who believe. For there is no distinction —
\v 23 for all have sinned and fall short of the glory of God —
\v 24 and are declared righteous (*dikaioumenoi*) as a gift by his grace through the ransom-release (*apolytrōseōs*) that is in Christ Jesus —
\v 25 whom God put forward as a mercy seat (*hilastērion*) by his blood — received through faith. This was to demonstrate his righteousness (*dikaiosynēn*) — because in his divine forbearance he had passed over former sins.
\v 26 It was to demonstrate his righteousness at the present time — so that he might be righteous (*dikaion*) and the one who declares righteous (*dikaiounta*) the one who has faith in Jesus.
\p
\v 27 Then what becomes of boasting? It is excluded. By what kind of law? By a law of works? No — but by the law of faith.
\v 28 For we maintain that a man is declared righteous (*dikaiousthai*) by faith apart from works of the Law.
\v 29 Or is God the God of Jews only? Is he not the God of Gentiles also? Yes — of Gentiles also —
\v 30 since God is one — who will declare righteous (*dikaiōsei*) the circumcised by faith and the uncircumcised through faith.
\v 31 Do we then overthrow the Law by this faith? By no means! On the contrary we uphold the Law.

\c 4
\p
\v 1 What then shall we say was gained by Abraham our forefather according to the flesh (*sarka*)?
\v 2 For if Abraham was declared righteous (*edikaiōthē*) by works he has something to boast about — but not before God.
\v 3 For what does the Scripture say? Abraham believed God and it was counted to him as righteousness (*elogisthē autō eis dikaiosynēn*).
\v 4 Now to the one who works his wages are not counted as a gift but as his due.
\v 5 And to the one who does not work but believes in him who declares righteous (*dikaiounta*) the ungodly — his faith is counted as righteousness (*logizesthai eis dikaiosynēn*).
\v 6 Just as David also speaks of the blessing of the one to whom God counts righteousness (*dikaiosynēn*) apart from works:
\v 7 Blessed are those whose lawless deeds are forgiven and whose sins are covered —
\v 8 blessed is the man against whom the Lord will not count his sin.
\p
\v 9 Is this blessing then for the circumcised — or also for the uncircumcised? For we say that faith was counted to Abraham as righteousness (*elogisthē tō Abraam hē pistis eis dikaiosynēn*).
\v 10 How then was it counted to him? Was it before or after he had been circumcised? It was not after — but before he was circumcised.
\v 11 He received the sign of circumcision as a seal of the righteousness (*dikaiosynēs*) that he had by faith while he was still uncircumcised. The purpose was to make him the father of all who believe without being circumcised so that righteousness would be counted to them as well —
\v 12 and to make him the father of the circumcised who are not merely circumcised but who also walk in the footsteps of the faith that our father Abraham had before he was circumcised.
\p
\v 13 For the promise to Abraham and his offspring that he would be heir of the world did not come through the Law but through the righteousness of faith (*dikaiosynēs pisteōs*).
\v 14 For if it is the adherents of the Law who are to be the heirs (*klēronomoi*) — faith is null and the promise is void.
\v 15 For the Law brings wrath — but where there is no law there is no transgression.
\v 16 That is why it depends on faith — in order that the promise may rest on grace and be guaranteed to all his offspring — not only to the adherent of the Law but also to the one who shares the faith of Abraham — who is the father of us all —
\v 17 as it is written: I have made you the father of many nations — in the presence of the God in whom he believed — who gives life to the dead (*zōopoiountos tous nekrous*) and calls into existence the things that do not exist (*kalountos ta mē onta hōs onta*).
\v 18 In hope he believed against hope — that he should become the father of many nations — as he had been told: So shall your offspring be.
\v 19 He did not weaken in faith when he considered his own body — which was as good as dead (*nenekrōmenon*) since he was about a hundred years old — or the deadness (*nekrōsin*) of Sarah's womb.
\v 20 No unbelief made him waver concerning the promise of God — but he grew strong in his faith as he gave glory to God —
\v 21 fully convinced that God was able to do what he had promised.
\v 22 That is why his faith was counted to him as righteousness (*elogisthē autō eis dikaiosynēn*).
\v 23 But the words it was counted to him were not written for his sake alone —
\v 24 but for ours also. It will be counted to us who believe in him who raised Jesus our Lord from the dead —
\v 25 who was delivered up for our trespasses and raised for our justification (*dikaiosin*).

\c 5
\p
\v 1 Therefore since we have been declared righteous (*dikaiōthentes*) by faith — we have peace with God through our Lord Jesus the Anointed —
\v 2 through whom we have also obtained access by faith into this grace in which we stand — and we rejoice in hope of the glory of God.
\v 3 Not only that — but we also rejoice in our tribulations — knowing that tribulation produces endurance —
\v 4 and endurance produces proven character — and proven character produces hope —
\v 5 and hope does not put us to shame because the covenant-love (*agapē*) of God has been poured out in our hearts (*kardias*) through the Holy Breath-of-life (*pneumatos hagiou*) who has been given to us.
\p
\v 6 For while we were still weak at the right time the Anointed died for the ungodly.
\v 7 For one will scarcely die for a righteous (*dikaion*) person — though perhaps for a good person one would dare even to die —
\v 8 but God demonstrates his own love (*agapēn*) toward us in that while we were still sinners the Anointed died for us.
\v 9 Much more then — having now been declared righteous (*dikaiōthentes*) by his blood — shall we be saved from wrath through him.
\v 10 For if while we were enemies we were reconciled to God through the death of his Son — much more — having been reconciled — shall we be saved by his life.
\v 11 More than that — we also rejoice in God through our Lord Jesus the Anointed — through whom we have now received reconciliation (*katallagēn*).
\p
\v 12 Therefore just as through one man sin entered the world and death through sin — and so death spread to all men because all sinned —
\v 13 for sin indeed was in the world before the Law was given — but sin is not counted where there is no law.
\v 14 Yet death reigned from Adam to Moses even over those whose sinning was not like the transgression of Adam — who was a type of the one who was to come.
\v 15 But the free gift is not like the trespass. For if many died through one man's trespass — much more have the grace of God and the free gift by the grace of that one man Jesus the Anointed abounded for many.
\v 16 And the free gift is not like the result of that one man's sin. For the judgment following one trespass brought condemnation — but the free gift following many trespasses brought declaration of righteousness (*dikaiōma*).
\v 17 For if by the trespass of the one man death reigned through that one — much more will those who receive the abundance of grace and the gift of righteousness (*dikaiosynēs*) reign in life through the one man Jesus the Anointed.
\v 18 Therefore as through one man's trespass condemnation came to all men — so through one man's act of righteousness (*dikaiōmatos*) the gift of life-declaration (*dikaiōsin zōēs*) came to all men.
\v 19 For as through the one man's disobedience many were constituted sinners — so through the obedience of the one man many will be constituted righteous (*dikaioi*).
\v 20 Now the Law came in so that the trespass might increase — but where sin increased grace abounded all the more —
\v 21 so that as sin reigned in death — grace might also reign through righteousness (*dikaiosynēs*) leading to everlasting life (*zōēn aiōnion*) through Jesus the Anointed our Lord.

\c 6
\p
\v 1 What shall we say then? Are we to continue in sin so that grace may abound?
\v 2 By no means! How can we who died to sin still live in it?
\v 3 Do you not know that all of us who have been baptized into Christ Jesus were baptized into his death?
\v 4 We were buried therefore with him through baptism into death — so that just as the Anointed was raised from the dead through the glory of the Father — we too might walk in newness of life (*kainotēti zōēs*).
\v 5 For if we have been united with him in a death like his — we shall certainly be united with him in a resurrection like his.
\v 6 We know that our old man was crucified with him so that the body of sin might be brought to nothing — so that we would no longer be enslaved to sin.
\v 7 For the one who has died has been declared righteous (*dedikaiōtai*) from sin.
\v 8 Now if we have died with Christ — we believe that we will also live with him.
\v 9 We know that the Anointed being raised from the dead will never die again — death no longer has dominion over him.
\v 10 For the death he died he died to sin once for all (*ephapax*) — but the life he lives he lives to God (*zē de tō theō*).
\v 11 So you also must consider yourselves dead to sin and alive to God (*zōntas de tō theō*) in Christ Jesus our Lord.
\p
\v 12 Let not sin therefore reign in your mortal body — to make you obey its passions.
\v 13 Do not present your members to sin as instruments for unrighteousness — but present yourselves to God as those who have been brought from death to life — and your members to God as instruments for righteousness (*dikaiosynēn*).
\v 14 For sin will have no dominion over you — since you are not under Law but under grace.
\p
\v 15 What then? Are we to sin because we are not under Law but under grace? By no means!
\v 16 Do you not know that if you present yourselves to anyone as obedient slaves — you are slaves of the one whom you obey — either of sin which leads to death — or of obedience which leads to righteousness (*dikaiosynēn*)?
\v 17 But thanks be to God that you — who were once slaves of sin — have become obedient from the heart (*kardias*) to the standard of teaching to which you were entrusted —
\v 18 and having been set free from sin have become slaves of righteousness (*dikaiosynēs*).
\v 19 I am speaking in human terms because of your natural limitations. For just as you once presented your members as slaves to impurity and to lawlessness leading to more lawlessness — so now present your members as slaves to righteousness (*dikaiosynēn*) leading to holiness.
\v 20 For when you were slaves of sin you were free in regard to righteousness (*dikaiosynēn*).
\v 21 But what fruit were you getting at that time from the things of which you are now ashamed? For the end of those things is death.
\v 22 But now having been set free from sin and having become slaves of God — the fruit you get leads to holiness and its end is everlasting life (*zōēn aiōnion*).
\v 23 For the wages of sin is death — but the free gift of God is everlasting life (*zōē aiōnios*) in Christ Jesus our Lord.

\c 7
\p
\v 1 Or do you not know brothers — for I am speaking to those who know the Law — that the Law is binding on a person only as long as he lives?
\v 2 For a married woman is bound by law to her husband while he lives — but if her husband dies she is released from the law of marriage.
\v 3 Accordingly she will be called an adulteress if she lives with another man while her husband is alive. But if her husband dies she is free from that law — and if she marries another man she is not an adulteress.
\v 4 Likewise my brothers — you also have died to the Law through the body of the Anointed — so that you may belong to another — to him who has been raised from the dead — in order that we may bear fruit for God.
\v 5 For while we were living in the flesh (*sarki*) our sinful passions — aroused by the Law — were at work in our members to bear fruit for death.
\v 6 But now we have been released from the Law — having died to that which held us captive — so that we serve in the newness of the breath-of-life (*pneumatos*) and not in the oldness of the letter.
\p
\v 7 What then shall we say? That the Law is sin? By no means! Yet if it had not been for the Law I would not have known sin. For I would not have known what it is to covet if the Law had not said: You shall not covet.
\v 8 But sin — seizing an opportunity through the commandment — produced in me all kinds of covetousness. For apart from the Law sin lies dead.
\v 9 I was once alive apart from the Law — but when the commandment came sin came alive and I died.
\v 10 The very commandment that promised life proved to be death to me.
\v 11 For sin — seizing an opportunity through the commandment — deceived me and through it killed me.
\v 12 So the Law is holy — and the commandment is holy and righteous (*dikaia*) and good.
\v 13 Did that which is good then bring death to me? By no means! It was sin — producing death in me through what is good — in order that sin might be shown to be sin — and through the commandment might become sinful beyond measure.
\p
\v 14 For we know that the Law is spiritual (*pneumatikos*) — but I am of the flesh (*sarkinos*) — sold under sin.
\v 15 For I do not understand my own actions. For I do not do what I want — but I do the very thing I hate.
\v 16 Now if I do what I do not want — I agree with the Law that it is good.
\v 17 So now it is no longer I who do it — but sin that dwells within me.
\v 18 For I know that nothing good dwells in me — that is in my flesh (*sarki*). For I have the desire to do what is right but not the ability to carry it out.
\v 19 For I do not do the good I want — but the evil I do not want is what I keep on doing.
\v 20 Now if I do what I do not want — it is no longer I who do it — but sin that dwells within me.
\v 21 So I find it to be a law that when I want to do right — evil lies close at hand.
\v 22 For I delight in the Law of God according to the inner man —
\v 23 but I see another law in my members waging war against the law of my mind and taking me captive to the law of sin that dwells in my members.
\v 24 Wretched man that I am! Who will deliver me from this body of death?
\v 25 Thanks be to God — through Jesus the Anointed our Lord. So then I myself serve the Law of God with my mind — but with my flesh (*sarki*) I serve the law of sin.

\c 8
\p
\v 1 There is therefore now no condemnation for those who are in Christ Jesus.
\v 2 For the law of the breath-of-life (*pneumatos*) of life in Christ Jesus has set you free from the law of sin and death.
\v 3 For God — having sent his own Son in the likeness of sinful flesh (*sarkos*) and as an offering for sin — condemned sin in the flesh (*sarki*) —
\v 4 so that the righteous requirement of the Law might be fulfilled in us who walk not according to the flesh (*sarka*) but according to the breath-of-life (*pneuma*).
\v 5 For those who live according to the flesh (*sarka*) set their minds on the things of the flesh — but those who live according to the breath-of-life (*pneuma*) set their minds on the things of the breath-of-life.
\v 6 For the mind of the flesh (*sarkos*) is death — but the mind of the breath-of-life (*pneumatos*) is life and peace —
\v 7 because the mind of the flesh (*sarkos*) is hostile to God — for it does not submit to God's Law — indeed it cannot.
\v 8 Those who are in the flesh (*sarki*) cannot please God.
\v 9 You however are not in the flesh (*sarki*) but in the breath-of-life (*pneumati*) — if in fact the breath-of-life of God (*pneuma theou*) dwells in you. Anyone who does not have the breath-of-life of the Anointed (*pneuma Christou*) does not belong to him.
\v 10 But if the Anointed is in you — although the body is dead because of sin — the breath-of-life is life (*to de pneuma zōē*) because of righteousness (*dikaiosynēn*).
\v 11 If the breath-of-life (*pneuma*) of him who raised Jesus from the dead dwells in you — he who raised Christ Jesus from the dead will also give life to your mortal bodies through his breath-of-life (*pneumatos*) who dwells in you.
\p
\v 12 So then brothers — we are debtors — not to the flesh (*sarki*) to live according to the flesh.
\v 13 For if you live according to the flesh (*sarka*) you will die — but if by the breath-of-life (*pneumati*) you put to death the deeds of the body — you will live.
\v 14 For all who are led by the breath-of-life of God (*pneumati theou*) are sons of God.
\v 15 For you did not receive the breath-of-life of slavery (*pneuma douleias*) to fall back into fear — but you have received the breath-of-life of son-placement (*pneuma huiothesias*) by whom we cry: Abba — Father!
\v 16 The breath-of-life himself (*auto to pneuma*) bears witness with our breath-of-life (*pneumati*) that we are children of God —
\v 17 and if children then heirs (*klēronomoi*) — heirs of God and fellow-heirs with the Anointed — if indeed we suffer with him so that we may also be glorified with him.
\p
\v 18 For I consider that the sufferings of this present time are not worth comparing with the glory that is to be revealed to us.
\v 19 For the eager expectation of the creation is waiting for the revealing of the sons of God.
\v 20 For the creation was subjected to futility — not willingly but because of him who subjected it — in hope
\v 21 that the creation itself will be set free from its bondage to decay and obtain the freedom of the glory of the children of God.
\v 22 For we know that the whole creation has been groaning together in the pains of childbirth until now.
\v 23 And not only the creation — but we ourselves who have the firstfruits (*aparchēn*) of the breath-of-life — we groan inwardly as we wait eagerly for son-placement (*huiothesian*) — the redemption of our body (*apolytrōsin tou sōmatos*).
\v 24 For in this hope we were saved. Now hope that is seen is not hope — for who hopes for what he sees?
\v 25 But if we hope for what we do not see — we wait for it with patience.
\p
\v 26 Likewise the breath-of-life (*pneuma*) helps us in our weakness — for we do not know what to pray for as we ought — but the breath-of-life himself (*auto to pneuma*) intercedes for us with groanings too deep for words.
\v 27 And he who searches hearts (*kardias*) knows what is the mind of the breath-of-life — because the breath-of-life intercedes for the holy ones according to the will of God.
\v 28 And we know that for those who love God all things work together for good — for those who are called according to his purpose.
\v 29 For those whom he foreknew he also predestined to be conformed to the image of his Son (*eikonos tou huiou autou*) — in order that he might be the firstborn (*prōtotokon*) among many brothers.
\v 30 And those whom he predestined he also called — and those whom he called he also declared righteous (*edikaiōsen*) — and those whom he declared righteous he also glorified.
\p
\v 31 What then shall we say to these things? If God is for us who can be against us?
\v 32 He who did not spare his own Son but delivered him up for us all — how will he not also with him graciously give us all things?
\v 33 Who shall bring any charge against God's chosen ones? It is God who declares righteous (*ho dikaiōn*) —
\v 34 who is the one who condemns? It is Christ Jesus who died — and more than that who was raised — who is at the right hand of God — who indeed is interceding for us.
\v 35 Who shall separate us from the love (*agapēs*) of the Anointed? Shall tribulation or distress or persecution or famine or nakedness or danger or sword?

**Romans as the systematic OT-NT synthesis:** Romans is the NT's most sustained theological argument — a systematic demonstration that the *euangelion* = glad tidings is the fulfillment of the entire OT covenantal sequence. ## CAE NODES — ROMANS

### Romans 1:1–4 — *euangelion* / *pneuma hagiōsynēs* / the resurrection as Son-designation — CAE PRIMARY NODE

**1:1–4** — "Paul — a servant of Jesus the Anointed — called as an apostle — set apart for the glad tidings (*euangelion*) of God — which he promised beforehand through his prophets in the holy Scriptures — concerning his Son — who was born of the seed of David according to the flesh (*sarkos*) — who was designated Son of God in power according to the breath-of-life of holiness (*kata pneuma hagiōsynēs*) by the resurrection from the dead."

***kata pneuma hagiōsynēs ex anastaseōs nekrōn***

= according to the breath-of-life of holiness by the resurrection from the dead.

The opening Christological formula deploys the *sarx* / *pneuma* antithesis at the level of the Son's own person: he was born *kata sarka* = according to the flesh from David's seed / he was designated *kata pneuma hagiōsynēs* = according to the breath-of-life of holiness by the resurrection.

The *pneuma hagiōsynēs* = breath-of-life of holiness is not a reference to a third Person separately but to the sphere-of-power in which the resurrection-designation occurred: the resurrection is the *pneuma*-domain event that disclosed what the *sarx*-domain birth concealed. In the flesh he was the son of David / in the *pneuma*-power of the resurrection he was declared (*horizō* = designated/appointed) Son of God in power.

This is the CAE v2 *sarx* / *pneuma* distinction operating at Christological depth: the *Logos* who became *sarx* (John 1:14) / who died *kata sarka* / is vindicated and disclosed *kata pneuma* in the resurrection. The resurrection is not merely an event that happened to Jesus — it is the divine act that *designated* him Son of God in the *pneuma*-domain that the *sarx*-domain concealed.

**Romans 8:11** — *the breath-of-life (*pneuma*) of him who raised Jesus from the dead... will also give life to your mortal bodies through his breath-of-life who dwells in you* — the same *pneuma* that designated Jesus Son of God by resurrection will give life to believers' mortal bodies. The resurrection-*pneuma* of 1:4 is the same as the life-giving *pneuma* of 8:11.

### Romans 3:21–26 — *hilastērion* / *dikaiosynē theou* / *apolytrōsis* — CAE CRITICAL NODE

**3:21–26** — "But now the righteousness of God (*dikaiosynē theou*) has been manifested apart from the Law... and are declared righteous as a gift by his grace through the ransom-release (*apolytrōseōs*) that is in Christ Jesus — whom God put forward as a mercy seat (*hilastērion*) by his blood — received through faith."

***hon proetheto ho theos hilastērion dia pisteōs en tō autou haimati***

= whom God put forward as a mercy seat / atoning sacrifice through faith by his blood.

This is the critical convergence node of the entire GLE project — the point where the Leviticus 16 / Isaiah 53 / 4 Maccabees / John 1:29 chains all arrive at their explicit NT statement.

**The *hilastērion* in Romans 3:25:**

The *hilastērion* is the LXX term for the *kapporet* = mercy seat / covering of the ark — the gold lid where the high priest sprinkled blood on Yom Kippur to make atonement for the people (Leviticus 16:14–16). Paul's use of *hilastērion* in 3:25 is the most theologically precise atonement-statement in the NT:

God *proetheto* = put forward / publicly displayed Christ as the *hilastērion*. The language is deliberate: the mercy seat was hidden — only the high priest saw it, only once a year, behind the veil. Christ is the *hilastērion* made public — *proetheto* = set forth publicly — accessible to all through faith. The veil is torn (Matthew 27:51) / the inner sanctuary is opened (Hebrews 10:19–20) / the mercy seat is now visible to all.

**The three vocabulary-locked terms in one sentence** (3:24–25):
1. *apolytrōseōs* = ransom-release — the compound *apo-lytron* = release-by-ransom, the price paid to free the slave/captive
2. *hilastērion* = mercy seat / atoning sacrifice — the Yom Kippur covering
3. *dikaiosynē theou* = righteousness of God — both the attribute of God and the status he confers

These three terms map the OT cultic vocabulary onto the single event of Christ's cross: the slave-market (*apolytrōsis*) / the Temple (*hilastērion*) / the law-court (*dikaiosynē*) are three metaphorical domains from which Paul draws simultaneously.

**The *dikaiosynē theou* as covenant-faithfulness**: The *dikaiosynē theou* in Romans 1:17 and 3:21 is not primarily a legal attribute (God's standard of righteousness) but a covenantal action (God's faithfulness to his covenant-promises). This is the LXX *tsedaqah* = righteousness-as-covenant-vindication: God is righteous when he acts faithfully to what he promised. The *hilastērion* event demonstrates that God is both *dikaios* = righteous himself AND *dikaiounta* = the one who declares righteous those who have faith — because the *hilastērion* absorbs the covenant-breach so that the covenant-faithfulness can flow to those who believe.

**The 4 Maccabees chain completed**: Leviticus 16 (*hilastērion* = mercy seat) → Isaiah 53:10 (*psychē* as guilt-offering) → 4 Maccabees 6:19 (*antipsychos*) / 17:13 (martyrs as *hilastērion*) → John 1:29 (*amnos tou theou*) → Romans 3:25 (*hilastērion* applied to Christ's blood). Paul's *hilastērion* is not invented — it is the culmination of a chain that runs through the entire LXX and deuterocanonical corpus.

**Hebrews 9:5, 12** — *hilastērion* named and superseded — the mercy seat in the Tabernacle is the shadow / Christ's blood is the substance.
**1 John 2:2** — *hilasmos hyper tōn hamartiōn hēmōn* = propitiation for our sins — the *hilasmos* = propitiation is the *hilastērion* event extended to all.

### Romans 4:17 — *kalountos ta mē onta hōs onta* / *creatio ex nihilo* as resurrection-ground — CAE CRITICAL NODE

**4:17** — "As it is written: I have made you the father of many nations — in the presence of the God in whom he believed — who gives life to the dead (*zōopoiountos tous nekrous*) and calls into existence the things that do not exist (*kalountos ta mē onta hōs onta*)."

***kalountos ta mē onta hōs onta***

= calling into existence the things that do not exist as existing.

The *creatio ex nihilo* / resurrection chain reaches its NT systematic statement here. The chain:

- **2 Maccabees 7:28** — *ouk ex ontōn epoiēsen auta ho theos* = God made them not out of things that existed — the mother's *creatio ex nihilo* as resurrection-ground
- **4 Maccabees 11:7** — same formula repeated: *ouk ex ontōn epoiēsen*
- **Romans 4:17** — *kalountos ta mē onta hōs onta* = calling the non-existent as existing — Paul's equivalent formula, deployed as the ground of Abraham's resurrection-faith
- **Hebrews 11:3** — *eis to mē ek phainomenōn to blepomenon gegonos* = what is seen was not made from things that are visible

Paul's argument in Romans 4 is the Maccabean mother's argument applied to Abraham: the God who creates from nothing / raises the dead / is the same God who counted Abraham's faith as righteousness. Abraham believed in the God who *calls non-existent things into existence* — therefore his faith in the promise of a son from a body *as good as dead* (*nenekrōmenon*) is faith in the Creator-Redeemer whose power is not limited by existing conditions.

The *creatio ex nihilo* is the epistemological ground of resurrection-faith: if God created all that exists from nothing then death is not an insuperable obstacle. The same logic the Maccabean mother used to encourage her sons (*God made all things from nothing — he can restore your bodies*) Paul uses to establish the structure of Abraham's faith.

**2 Maccabees 7:28** → **4 Maccabees 11:7** → **Romans 4:17** → **Hebrews 11:3** — the four-point *creatio ex nihilo* / resurrection-faith chain is now complete.

### Romans 6:10–11 — *zē tō theō* / live to God — CAE CRITICAL NODE

**6:10–11** — "For the death he died he died to sin once for all (*ephapax*) — but the life he lives he lives to God (*zē de tō theō*). So you also must consider yourselves dead to sin and alive to God (*zōntas de tō theō*) in Christ Jesus our Lord."

***zē de tō theō... zōntas de tō theō***

= he lives to God... living to God.

The *zēn tō theō* = living to God formula is the NT Christological and pneumatological application of the 4 Maccabees *zōsin tō theō* chain. The chain:

- **4 Maccabees 7:17–19** — *ou thnēskousin theō alla zōsin tō theō hōsper kai Abraam* = they do not die to God but live to God as Abraham lives to God
- **Luke 20:38** — *pantes gar autō zōsin* = for all live to him (Jesus's resurrection argument)
- **Romans 6:10–11** — *zē de tō theō* / *zōntas de tō theō* = he lives to God / living to God

The Maccabean martyrs' declaration — that the patriarchs and the faithful *live to God* even after death — is now given its Christological ground in Romans 6: the Anointed himself *zē tō theō* = lives to God as the resurrection-reality / and believers are called to consider themselves *zōntas tō theō* = living to God *in Christ Jesus*. The ground of the living-to-God is no longer merely covenant-faithfulness unto death (the Maccabean form) but union with the one who *zē tō theō* as the risen Lord.

The *ephapax* = once for all of 6:10 connects to Hebrews 7:27 / 9:12 / 10:10 — the single unrepeatable self-offering that is permanently effective.

**4 Maccabees 7:19** — the *zōsin tō theō* formula — OT-deuterocanonical ground.
**Romans 14:8** — *zōmen tō kyriō / apothnēskomen tō kyriō* = we live to the Lord / we die to the Lord — the 6:10–11 pattern applied to the believer's entire existence.

### Romans 8:14–17 — *huiothesia* / *Abba Patēr* / heirs of God — CAE CRITICAL NODE

**8:14–17** — "For all who are led by the breath-of-life of God are sons of God. For you did not receive the breath-of-life of slavery to fall back into fear — but you have received the breath-of-life of son-placement (*pneuma huiothesias*) by whom we cry: Abba — Father! The breath-of-life himself bears witness with our breath-of-life that we are children of God — and if children then heirs (*klēronomoi*) — heirs of God and fellow-heirs with the Anointed."

***pneuma huiothesias en hō krazomen Abba ho Patēr***

= the breath-of-life of son-placement in whom we cry Abba — Father.

The *huiothesia* = son-placement is the GLE vocabulary-locked term for what the KJV renders *adoption*. The LXX background: *huiothesia* is not attested in the LXX itself but the concept is thoroughly OT: Israel as God's firstborn son (Exodus 4:22) / the Davidic king as God's son (Psalm 2:7 / 2 Samuel 7:14) / the *pater tōn pneumatōn* = Father of breaths-of-life (Hebrews 12:9). The NT's *huiothesia* vocabulary takes the Israel-as-son / David-as-son OT theology and extends it through the Anointed to all believers.

The *pneuma huiothesias* = breath-of-life of son-placement is the agent of the *huiothesia*: the same *pneuma* that designated Jesus Son of God in power (1:4) is the *pneuma* that places believers in the Son-relationship. The *Abba* cry — the Aramaic intimate address for Father — is the breath-of-life's own testimony that the son-placement is real.

The *klēronomoi* = heirs connects to the *klēronomia* = inheritance vocabulary-lock: believers as heirs of God / fellow-heirs with the Anointed is the NT extension of the OT inheritance-of-the-land theology: the *klēronomia* = inheritance promised to Abraham / given as land to Israel / is now the *klēronomia* of glory (8:17–18) for all who are led by the breath-of-life.

**The 3 Maccabees 7:6 chain closed**: *ton ouranion theon asphalōs hyperaspizein autōn patros dikēn* = the God of heaven defending them as a father does for children — the pagan king Ptolemy's forced confession of divine fatherhood is now given its full theological content in Romans 8:14–17: God defends his children because they have received the *pneuma huiothesias* by which they cry *Abba*.

**Galatians 4:4–7** — *God sent forth his Son... so that we might receive son-placement (*huiothesian*). And because you are sons God has sent the breath-of-life of his Son into our hearts (*kardias*) crying Abba Father.* The Galatians *huiothesia* passage is the parallel deployment of the same theology: son-placement received / *pneuma* of the Son sent into the *kardia* / *Abba* cry. Both Romans 8 and Galatians 4 deploy the *huiothesia* + *pneuma* + *Abba* triad.

**Hebrews 12:9** — *Patri tōn pneumatōn* = Father of breaths-of-life — the Hebrews title for God as Father corresponds to the Romans *Abba ho Patēr*: both name God as Father in the context of the *pneuma*-shaped discipline/formation of the children.

### Romans 9:25–26 — *huioi theou zōntos* / the Hosea-Esther-Gentile chain — CAE CRITICAL NODE

**9:25–26** — "As indeed he says in Hosea: Those who were not my people I will call my people — and her who was not beloved I will call beloved. And in the very place where it was said to them: You are not my people — there they will be called sons of the living God (*huioi theou zōntos*)."

***klēthēsontai huioi theou zōntos***

= they will be called sons of the living God.

The *huioi theou zōntos* = sons of the living God chain is one of the GLE project's most important cross-canonical chains, traceable across three books:

- **Hosea 1:10** — *klēthēsontai huioi theou zōntos* = they will be called sons of the living God — spoken of restored Israel
- **Esther Addition E:15** (LXX) — *huioi theou zōntos* = sons of the living God — the Persian king Artaxerxes' royal edict declaring Israel *sons of the living God* before the nations
- **Romans 9:26** — Paul cites Hosea 1:10 and extends the *huioi theou zōntos* identity to the Gentiles who come to faith

The canonical movement: Hosea originally spoke of Israel's restoration after the Lo-Ammi / Lo-Ruhamah = Not-My-People / Not-Beloved judgment. Paul reads it as the prophetic announcement of the Gentile-inclusion: the ones who were *not my people* are the Gentiles / those who were *not beloved* are the nations outside the covenant / and in the very place of their non-belonging they will be called *sons of the living God*.

The Esther Addition E:15 node is the deuterocanonical hinge: a pagan king declares Israel *sons of the living God* before the nations — the Gentile-king's decree anticipates the Pauline proclamation that Gentiles themselves will receive this identity. The chain: Hosea prophecy → Esther enactment (pagan king declares Israel sons of the living God) → Paul's extension (Gentiles called sons of the living God through faith).

**Hosea 1:10 LXX** — *klēthēsontai huioi theou zōntos* — primary text cited.
**Esther Addition E:15 LXX** — the deuterocanonical echo-point.
**Romans 8:14–17** — *pneuma huiothesias* / *Abba ho Patēr* — the mechanism by which the *huioi theou zōntos* identity is received.

### Romans 11:29 — *ametamelēta* / the irrevocable gifts — CAE PRIMARY NODE

**11:29** — "For the gifts and the calling of God are irrevocable (*ametamelēta*)."

***ametamelēta gar ta charismata kai hē klēsis tou theou***

= for irrevocable are the gifts of grace and the calling of God.

This single verse is the NT's most compact statement of the *eleos* = steadfast love / covenant-faithfulness theology that runs through the entire OT-deuterocanonical sequence. The *ametamelēta* = without-repentance / irrevocable — God does not change his mind about the gifts he has given or the calling he has issued.

The context is Paul's argument about Israel's irrevocable election despite present partial hardening (11:25): the *charismata* = gifts of grace (the *huiothesia* / the covenants / the glory / the promises listed in 9:4–5) and the *klēsis* = calling of God to Israel are *ametamelēta* = irrevocable. God will not unmake what he has made. The covenant-faithfulness that 2 Maccabees 6:16 declared (*he never withdraws his eleos*) Paul states in its most absolute form: the *charismata kai hē klēsis* cannot be revoked.

**2 Maccabees 6:16** — *to eleos ou proietai aph' hēmōn* = he does not abandon his steadfast love from us — the deuterocanonical precedent.
**Numbers 23:19** — *God is not a man that he should lie or a son of man that he should change his mind* — the OT ground of divine irrevocability.
**Hebrews 6:17–18** — *so that by two unchangeable things in which it is impossible for God to lie we... might have strong encouragement* — the Hebrews equivalent: the oath and the promise are both unchangeable.

### Romans 11:33–36 — the doxology / *sophia* / *unsearchable ways* — CAE PRIMARY NODE

**11:33–36** — "Oh the depth of the riches and wisdom (*sophias*) and knowledge of God! How unsearchable are his judgments and how inscrutable his ways! For who has known the mind of the Lord — or who has been his counselor?... For from him and through him and to him are all things. To him be glory forever. Amen."

The chapters 9–11 doxology is the NT's most concentrated deployment of the LXX Wisdom-tradition in praise form. The *bathē* = depth of the *sophia* and *gnōsis* = wisdom and knowledge of God — the divine wisdom that is unsearchable (*anexeraunēta*) and whose ways are inscrutable (*anexichniastoi*) — is the LXX Wisdom-tradition vocabulary applied to the mystery of the divine purpose in election, hardening, and mercy.

Paul cites Isaiah 40:13 (*who has known the mind of the Lord?*) and Job 35:7 / 41:11 (*who has given a gift to him that he might be repaid?*) — both LXX texts. The doxology structure is precisely the 3 Maccabees 7:22 pattern: *ta megala kai endoxa autou telesanta... eulogētos ho rhysamenōs Israel eis tous aionas aiōnōn. Amēn* = having perfectly accomplished his great and glorious things... Blessed be the Deliverer of Israel unto the ages of ages. Amen. Both close a sustained narrative of divine providence with a doxology acknowledging the unsearchable depths of the divine purpose.

**The *ek* / *di'* / *eis* formula** (11:36): *ex autou kai di' autou kai eis auton ta panta* = from him and through him and to him are all things. This is the NT's most compressed statement of the *creatio ex nihilo* theology: all things have their origin *from him* / their sustenance *through him* / their destiny *to him*. The creation is not self-existent or self-explanatory but entirely derivative from and directed toward the Creator.

**Judith 8:14** — *you cannot sound the depths of the human heart or understand the workings of the human mind — how do you expect to search out God?* — the Judith wisdom-tradition on the inscrutability of the divine ways.
**Colossians 1:16** — *all things were created through him and for him* — the Pauline equivalent of *di' autou kai eis auton ta panta*.

### Romans 15:8–9 — *alētheia* and *eleos* / confirming promises / the Gentile-*eleos* — CAE CRITICAL NODE

**15:8–9** — "For I tell you that the Anointed became a servant to the circumcised to show God's truthfulness (*alētheian*) — in order to confirm the promises given to the patriarchs — and in order that the Gentiles might glorify God for his mercy (*eleos*). As it is written: Therefore I will praise you among the Gentiles and sing to your name."

***hyper alētheias theou... ta de ethnē hyper eleous doxasai ton theon***

= for the truth of God... and that the Gentiles might glorify God for steadfast love.

The *alētheia* and *eleos* pairing is the LXX *ḥesed we'emet* = steadfast love and faithfulness formula (Psalm 85:10 / Proverbs 3:3 / Exodus 34:6) that the Prologue of John deployed as *charis kai alētheia* (John 1:14). Paul deploys the same pairing at the climax of his argument:

- The Anointed served Israel *hyper alētheias* = for the sake of the truth / faithfulness of God — to confirm the patriarchal promises. This is the covenant-*alētheia* = faithfulness: God is true to what he promised.
- The Gentiles glorify God *hyper eleous* = for the sake of steadfast love — they received what they had no claim to. The *eleos* flows to those outside the covenant.

The two beneficiaries — Israel (promises confirmed through *alētheia*) and Gentiles (included through *eleos*) — map exactly onto the structure of the entire OT narrative: Israel received the covenant through the divine *alētheia* = faithfulness / the Gentiles are included through the divine *eleos* = steadfast love that overflows the covenant-boundaries.

**Psalm 85:10 LXX** — *eleos kai alētheia synēntēsan* = steadfast love and faithfulness met together — the OT ground of the *eleos* / *alētheia* pairing.
**John 1:14** — *plērēs charitos kai alētheias* = full of grace and truth — the Prologue deployment of the same pairing.
**Tobit 3:2** — *all your ways are mercy (*eleos*) and truth (*alētheia*)* — the deuterocanonical expression of the divine character in *eleos* + *alētheia* terms.

## STRUCTURAL NOTE — ROMANS AS THE COVENANT-ENGINE SYSTEMATIC TEXT

Romans is the GLE project's NT systematic text — the book in which the theological chains developed across the entire OT-deuterocanonical corpus receive their most sustained and explicit treatment.

**The five great chains that Romans closes or advances:**

1. ***Hilastērion* chain** (3:25): Leviticus 16 → Isaiah 53 → 4 Maccabees 17:13 → John 1:29 → **Romans 3:25** (*hilastērion* applied to Christ's blood publicly displayed). The mercy-seat that was hidden behind the veil once a year is now publicly displayed in the cross.

2. ***Creatio ex nihilo* / resurrection chain** (4:17): 2 Maccabees 7:28 → 4 Maccabees 11:7 → **Romans 4:17** (*ta mē onta hōs onta* = calling non-existent things into existence). Abraham's faith is the faith of *creatio ex nihilo*: the God who creates from nothing raises the dead.

3. ***Zēn tō theō* chain** (6:10–11): 4 Maccabees 7:19 (*zōsin tō theō*) → Luke 20:38 (*pantes autō zōsin*) → **Romans 6:10–11** (*zē tō theō* / *zōntas tō theō*). The Maccabean live-to-God formula receives its Christological ground: the Anointed *zē tō theō* as risen Lord / believers are *zōntas tō theō* in union with him.

4. ***Huiothesia* chain** (8:14–17): Exodus 4:22 (Israel my firstborn son) → Psalm 2:7 (You are my Son) → 3 Maccabees 7:6 (*patros dikēn*) → **Romans 8:15** (*pneuma huiothesias* / *Abba Patēr*). The *pneuma* of son-placement is the agent by which the divine fatherhood declared by Ptolemy becomes the experienced reality of believers.

5. ***Huioi theou zōntos* chain** (9:26): Hosea 1:10 → Esther Addition E:15 → **Romans 9:26** (Gentiles called *sons of the living God*). The Hosea promise / Esther anticipation / Paul's extension: those who were not God's people are called sons of the living God.

**The *eleos* / *alētheia* synthesis** (15:8–9): The entire OT *ḥesed we'emet* = steadfast love and faithfulness pairing arrives at its NT application: the Anointed confirmed Israel's promises through divine *alētheia* / opened the Gentiles to divine *eleos*. The two covenant-character attributes of Exodus 34:6 are both vindicated in the single event of the Messiah's faithful death and resurrection.

**The *dikaioō* chain — now fully traced:**
- Genesis 15:6 — Abraham's faith counted as *dikaiosynē*
- Psalm 14 LXX — *ouk estin dikaios* = none is righteous
- Habakkuk 2:4 — *ho dikaios ek pisteōs zēsetai* = the righteous shall live by faith
- Romans 3:21–26 — *dikaiosynē theou* manifested through *hilastērion*
- Romans 4:3 — Abraham's *elogisthē eis dikaiosynēn*
- Romans 5:1 — *dikaiōthentes ek pisteōs eirēnēn echomen*
- Romans 8:30 — *edikaiōsen* in the golden chain of predestination-call-justification-glorification

The *dikaioō* vocabulary-lock tracks through Romans as its central semantic spine — the covenant-declaration that the judge makes in favor of the accused, grounded not in the accused's performance but in the *hilastērion* event of Christ's blood.

**TITUS — GLE v2.2 CAE NOTES**

---

**TEXT BASE:** RP2026 Byzantine Majority Text

**VERSE COUNT:** 46 verses / 3 chapters

---

**TITUS IN THE CORPUS ARCHITECTURE**

Titus is the most concentrated of the three Pastoral Epistles — 46 verses containing the highest theological density per verse in the Pastoral canon. Where 1 Timothy ordered the household of God institutionally and 2 Timothy addressed personal apostolic succession, Titus integrates both concerns within the framework of the *epiphaneia* — the two *epephaneiai* of 2:11 and 3:4 are the letter's structural poles, and every ethical instruction is grounded in one or the other. The letter's architecture is the most explicitly grace-grounded in the Pastorals: the ethical demands of chapters 1–2 flow entirely from the theological declaration of 2:11–14 and are consummated in the trinitarian salvation-sequence of 3:4–7 — the most complete soteriological statement in the Pastoral Epistles and one of the most structurally complete in the NT corpus.

The letter's CAE function is concentrated in three nodes of extraordinary density: the *epephanē hē charis tou theou* declaration of 2:11 — grace personified as the appearing teacher of Wisdom-register conduct; the *epiphaneian tēs doxēs tou megalou theou kai Sōtēros hēmōn Iēsou Christou* of 2:13 — the most direct divine-identity statement applied to the Anointed in the Pauline corpus; and the trinitarian regeneration-sequence of 3:4–7 — *paliggenesia* (regeneration) and *anakainōsis* (renewal) through the Holy Breath-of-life as the complete new-creation anthropological statement, grounding *dikaiōthentes* (declared righteous) and *klēronomoi* (heirs) in the mercy of the appearing God. Together these three passages constitute the Pastoral Epistles' fullest integration of Christology / pneumatology / soteriology / eschatology.

---

**KEY LEXICAL DECISIONS**

*epiphaneia / epephanē* — "appearing / appeared": 2:11 (*epephanē gar hē charis tou theou* — for the grace of God has appeared), 2:13 (*tēn makarian elpida kai epiphaneian tēs doxēs* — the blessed hope and appearing of the glory), 3:4 (*hote de hē chrēstotēs kai hē philanthrōpia epephanē tou Sōtēros hēmōn theou* — but when the goodness and loving-kindness of God our Savior appeared). Three occurrences — the *epiphaneia* vocabulary lock reaches its Titus form in a concentrated two-chapter arc. The two *epephaneiai* — 2:11 (first appearing / grace appeared) and 3:4 (first appearing restated / goodness and loving-kindness appeared) — are both references to the incarnation. The single *epiphaneia* of 2:13 is the future second appearing. The structural pattern: first *epephanē* (2:11) → wait for the *epiphaneia* (2:13) → first *epephanē* restated as the ground of salvation (3:4). The two appearances bracket and ground all ethical instruction between them.

*2:11 CAE NOTE:* *epephanē gar hē charis tou theou sōtērios pasin anthrōpois — paideuousa hēmas hina arnēsamenoi tēn asebeian kai tas kosmikas epithymias sōphronōs kai dikaiōs kai eusebōs zēsōmen en tō nyn aiōni* — "for the grace of God has appeared bringing salvation to all people — training us to renounce ungodliness and worldly passions and to live self-controlled upright and godly lives in the present age." The *charis* — grace — is personified as an *epiphaneia* — an appearing — and simultaneously as a *paideuousa* — a teacher / trainer. The grace does not merely declare salvation but actively trains (*paideuousa* — present participle / ongoing action) the recipients in the Wisdom-register life-pattern. CAE Wisdom/Folly axis: the grace-as-teacher produces three behavioral dimensions: *sōphronōs* (self-controlled / sound-minded — inward governance) / *dikaiōs* (uprightly / righteously — covenantal conduct toward others) / *eusebōs* (godly — covenantal orientation toward God). The three adverbs map the complete Wisdom-register behavioral expression across three domains: inward / horizontal / vertical. The *nyn aiōn* — "present age" — establishes that the Wisdom-register life is not eschatological deferral but present-age reality grounded in the first *epephanē* and oriented toward the second *epiphaneia* (2:13).

*2:13 CAE NOTE — Divine identity:* *prosdechomenoi tēn makarian elpida kai epiphaneian tēs doxēs tou megalou theou kai Sōtēros hēmōn Iēsou Christou* — "waiting for our blessed hope — the appearing of the glory of our great God and Savior Jesus the Anointed." The *tou megalou theou kai Sōtēros hēmōn Iēsou Christou* — "our great God and Savior Jesus the Anointed" — is the most direct divine-identity statement in the Pauline corpus and one of the clearest in the NT. The grammatical structure (*Granville Sharp Rule*: one article governing two nouns joined by *kai* — *tou... theou kai Sōtēros... Iēsou Christou*) indicates that *megalos theos* and *Sōtēr* are both applied to the single person Jesus the Anointed — he is both *megas theos* (great God) and *Sōtēr* (Savior). **CAE divine-identity node — Chain 13 (*arnion* / Savior) and Chain 1 (*hilastērion*) convergence:**

**Isaiah 43:11 LXX (*egō eimi ho theos — kai ouk estin parex emou sōzōn* — I am God and there is no Savior beside me) → Titus 2:13 (*tou megalou theou kai Sōtēros hēmōn Iēsou Christou*)** — the exclusive YHWH-Savior predicate applied to Jesus the Anointed. The LXX's insistence that YHWH alone is *sōzōn* — Savior — makes the application of *Sōtēr* to the Anointed a divine-identity claim of the highest order. Cross-link: **2 Peter 1:1 (*tou theou hēmōn kai Sōtēros Iēsou Christou* — our God and Savior Jesus the Anointed)** — the Titus 2:13 formula confirmed by a second independent NT witness with identical grammar.

**LXX-primacy argument:** The divine-identity claim of Titus 2:13 is only fully legible against the LXX background of Isaiah 43:11 and 45:21 LXX — the exclusive YHWH-Savior texts. The MT equivalents do not provide the same *Sōtēr* vocabulary that the LXX does, and the NT *Sōtēr* Christology is constructed entirely on the LXX YHWH-*Sōtēr* foundation. The *megas* — "great" — additionally echoes Psalm 47:2 LXX (*megas kyrios kai ainetos sphodra* — great is the Lord and greatly to be praised) and Psalm 85:10 LXX (*hoti megas ei su kai poiōn thaumasia — su ei ho theos monos* — for you are great and do wondrous things / you alone are God). The *Sōtēr* title for the Anointed in Titus uses the same vocabulary the LXX reserves for YHWH alone.

*3:4 CAE NOTE:* *hote de hē chrēstotēs kai hē philanthrōpia epephanē tou Sōtēros hēmōn theou* — "but when the goodness and loving-kindness of God our Savior appeared." The first *epephanē* is here described not through the *charis* (grace — 2:11) but through *chrēstotēs* (goodness / kindness) and *philanthrōpia* (love of humanity / loving-kindness). The *philanthrōpia* — "love of humanity" — is a hapax in the Pauline corpus and one of only two NT occurrences (Acts 28:2). It denotes the divine orientation toward humanity as the ground of the incarnation — God's *philanthrōpia* motivated the *epephanē*. **LXX-primacy argument: the *chrēstotēs* and *philanthrōpia* of 3:4 connect to Psalm 24:7 LXX (*mnēsthēti tōn oiktirmōn sou kyrie kai tou eleous sou — hoti apo tou aiōnos eisin* — remember your mercy Lord and your steadfast love — for they are from everlasting) and Wisdom of Solomon 1:6 LXX (*philanthrōpon gar pneuma sophia* — wisdom is a spirit loving humanity) + Wisdom of Solomon 12:19 LXX (*edidaxas de ton laon sou hoti dei ton dikaion einai philanthrōpon* — you taught your people that the righteous must be *philanthrōpos*)**. The *philanthrōpia* vocabulary in Titus 3:4 is drawn from LXX Wisdom tradition — the deuterocanonical Wisdom of Solomon provides the vocabulary that describes God's love-toward-humanity as the basis of salvation.

*pneuma* — "breath-of-life": 3:5 (*pneumatos hagiou* — Holy Breath-of-life / *anakainōsis pneumatos hagiou* — renewal of the Holy Breath-of-life), 3:6 (*hou execheen eph' hēmas plousiōs* — whom he poured out on us richly). Two occurrences in the trinitarian salvation-sequence of 3:4–7.

*3:5–6 CAE NOTE:* *dia loutrou paliggenesias kai anakainōseōs pneumatos hagiou — hou execheen eph' hēmas plousiōs dia Iēsou Christou tou Sōtēros hēmōn* — "through the washing of regeneration and renewing of the Holy Breath-of-life — whom he poured out on us richly through Jesus the Anointed our Savior." The two-noun phrase *paliggenesia* (regeneration / new birth / rebirth) and *anakainōsis* (renewal / making new) describes the single act of the Holy Breath-of-life in salvation — two aspects of the one pneumatic event: regeneration (the new birth — the commencement of new life) and renewal (the ongoing transformation — 2 Corinthians 3:18 / Romans 12:2). **Chain 15 (Eden-to-new-creation) — new-creation-through-the-Breath node:**

**Genesis 1:2 LXX (*pneuma theou epephereto epanō tou hydatos* — the breath-of-life of God was moving over the waters — creation by the Breath) → Ezekiel 36:25–27 LXX (*rantisō eph' hymas hydōr katharon... kai dōsō hymin kardian kainēn... kai to pneuma mou dōsō en hymin* — I will sprinkle clean water on you / new heart / my breath-of-life in you) → Titus 3:5 (*loutron paliggenesias kai anakainōsis pneumatos hagiou*) → John 3:5 (*hydatos kai pneumatos* — water and breath-of-life)**

The *loutron* (washing) + *paliggenesia* (regeneration) + *pneuma hagion* (Holy Breath-of-life) of Titus 3:5 combines the Ezekiel 36:25–27 LXX elements: clean-water-sprinkling / new heart / Breath-of-life-gift. **LXX-primacy argument: Titus 3:5 is compositionally dependent on Ezekiel 36:25–27 LXX — the new covenant cleansing-water / new heart / Breath-of-life sequence is the direct LXX background of the *loutron paliggenesias kai anakainōsis pneumatos hagiou* formula. The MT of Ezekiel 36 does not provide the same verbal density of correspondence.** CAE anchor: **Ezekiel 36:25–27 LXX → Titus 3:5 → John 3:5** — the new-creation-through-water-and-Breath chain confirmed across three witnesses.

The *execheen plousiōs* — "poured out richly" — is the Pentecost-outpouring vocabulary (Acts 2:17–18 / Joel 3:1–2 LXX — *ekxeō apo tou pneumatos mou* — I will pour out my breath-of-life) applied to the individual believer's reception of the Breath-of-life. The outpouring is *dia Iēsou Christou tou Sōtēros* — "through Jesus the Anointed our Savior" — establishing the Son as the channel of the Breath-of-life's outpouring, consistent with John 15:26 (*hon egō pempsō hymin para tou patros* — whom I will send you from the Father) and John 16:7 (*ean de poreuō, pempsō auton pros hymas* — if I go I will send him to you). **CAE trinitarian pneumatology: the Father saves (3:4 — God our Savior appeared) / the Son channels the Breath-of-life (3:6 — poured out through Jesus the Anointed) / the Holy Breath-of-life regenerates and renews (3:5)**. The fullest trinitarian salvation-sequence in the Pastoral Epistles.

*paliggenesia* — "regeneration / rebirth": 3:5. Hapax in the Pauline corpus — one of only two NT occurrences (Matthew 19:28 — *en tē paliggenesią* — in the regeneration / new world / renewal of all things). The Matthew 19:28 usage is cosmic (the renewal of all things at the Son of Man's enthronement) / the Titus 3:5 usage is personal (the regeneration of the individual through the Holy Breath-of-life). **CAE Chain 15 (Eden-to-new-creation) — *paliggenesia* double-node:**

**Matthew 19:28 (*paliggenesia* — cosmic renewal at the *parousia*) = Titus 3:5 (*paliggenesia* — personal renewal through the Breath-of-life)**

The same word describes both the cosmic eschatological renewal and the personal present renewal — the personal *paliggenesia* (Titus 3:5) is the individual anticipation and first-installment of the cosmic *paliggenesia* (Matthew 19:28). Chain 15 receives its most explicit regeneration-vocabulary node.

*dikaiosynē / dikaiōthentes* — "righteousness / declared righteous": 3:5 (*ouk ex ergōn tōn en dikaiosynē hōn epoiēsamen hēmeis* — not because of works done by us in righteousness), 3:7 (*dikaiōthentes tē ekeinōn chariti* — declared righteous by his grace). The forensic *dikaioō* vocabulary lock operates in Titus — *dikaiōthentes* (3:7) is the passive aorist participle of the same *dikaioō* as Romans 3:24 (*dikaiountai dōrean tē autou chariti* — declared righteous freely by his grace). **Chain 1 (*hilastērion*) declared-righteous node:**

**Romans 3:24 (*dikaiountai dōrean* — declared righteous freely) = Titus 3:7 (*dikaiōthentes tē chariti* — declared righteous by grace)**

The two formulations are structurally identical — both passive / both grace-grounded / both excluding works. The Titus 3:5–7 sequence is the most compact Romans-3-equivalent in the NT: not-by-works (3:5) → through the Breath-of-life's regeneration (3:5) → poured out through the Anointed (3:6) → declared righteous by grace (3:7) → heirs of everlasting life (3:7). The entire Romans 1–8 soteriological arc compressed into four verses.

*klēronomos* — "heir": 3:7 (*klēronomoi genōmetha kat' elpida zōēs aiōniou* — we might become heirs according to the hope of everlasting life). The *klēronomia* vocabulary lock — inheritance — applied to the eschatological endpoint of the regeneration-sequence. The regeneration (3:5) → declaration of righteousness (3:7) → heirship (3:7) sequence is the Titus form of the Romans 8 arc: Breath-of-life → *huiothesia* → *klēronomia* (Romans 8:15–17: the Breath-of-life of son-placement / crying Abba / therefore heirs). **Chain 4 (*huiothesia*) — *klēronomia* endpoint node:**

**Romans 8:17 (*klēronomoi men theou — sygklēronomoi de Christou* — heirs of God and co-heirs with the Anointed) = Titus 3:7 (*klēronomoi kata elpida zōēs aiōniou* — heirs according to the hope of everlasting life)**

The Titus formulation adds the *elpida* — hope — dimension: the heirship is presently real but eschatologically complete — the *zōē aiōnios* is the content of the inheritance (*klēronomia*) that the *elpis* (hope) anticipates. Chain 4 receives its *klēronomia* eschatological completion node.

*lytrōsētai* — "redeem / ransom-release": 2:14 (*hina lytrōsētai hēmas apo pasēs anomias* — to redeem us from all lawlessness). The *lytrōō* — ransom-release / redeem — vocabulary lock in its verbal form. The Anointed gave himself (*dous heauton* — 2:14) to *lytrōsētai* (ransom-release) us from lawlessness and purify for himself a *laos periousiou* — "people of his own / treasured people." **LXX-primacy argument: the *laos periousios* of Titus 2:14 is a direct citation of Exodus 19:5 LXX (*esesthe moi laos periousios apo pantōn tōn ethnōn* — you shall be my treasured people from all the nations) and Deuteronomy 7:6 LXX (*laos hagios ei kyriō tō theō sou — kai se exelexato kyrios ho theos sou einai auton laon periousion* — you are a holy people to the Lord your God / the Lord chose you to be his treasured people)**. The *laos periousios* is the LXX's covenantal term for Israel as YHWH's possession — applied in Titus 2:14 to the community redeemed by the Anointed's self-giving. **CAE Chain 8 (*diathēkē aiōnios*) covenantal-people node:**

**Exodus 19:5 LXX (*laos periousios*) + Deuteronomy 7:6 LXX → Titus 2:14 → 1 Peter 2:9 (*laos eis peripoiēsin* — a people for his possession)**

The Sinai covenantal-people vocabulary transferred to the new covenant community through the Anointed's ransom-self-giving. The *lytrōsētai apo pasēs anomias* — "redeem from all lawlessness" — echoes Psalm 129:8 LXX (*kai autos lytrōsetai ton Israēl ek pasōn tōn anomiōn autou* — and he will redeem Israel from all his lawlessnesses). **LXX-primacy argument confirmed: Titus 2:14 is a three-text LXX composite — Exodus 19:5 LXX / Deuteronomy 7:6 LXX / Psalm 129:8 LXX — the ransom-release from lawlessness and the treasured-people vocabulary are both drawn from the LXX covenantal tradition.**

*alētheia* — "truth": 1:1 (knowledge of the *alētheias*), 1:13 (rebuke them / testimony is *alēthēs*), 1:14 (commands of those who turn away from the *alētheian*). Three occurrences — all in chapter 1. The *alētheia* functions in Titus as the doctrinal content of the apostolic mission (1:1 — *epignōsis alētheias* / knowledge of the truth according to godliness) and the criterion by which false teachers are identified (1:14 — they turn away from the *alētheian*). CAE Chain 10 (*alētheia*) Pastoral completion: the *alētheia* that the called-out assembly guards institutionally (1 Timothy 3:15) / that Timothy must handle straight (2 Timothy 2:15) / that Titus must teach in accord with sound doctrine (Titus 1:9 / 2:1) is the consistent thread of the Pastoral *alētheia* theology.

*eusebia* — "godliness": 1:1 (*eusebeian* — knowledge of truth according to godliness), 2:12 (*eusebōs* — godly lives in the present age). Two occurrences — fewer than 1 Timothy but structurally placed at the letter's opening (1:1 — godliness as the aim of the apostolic mission) and at the center of the grace-as-teacher passage (2:12 — the appearing grace trains toward *eusebōs* living). CAE Wisdom-register summary: the Pastoral Epistles' signature virtue-term frames the entire apostolic enterprise — from the knowledge of truth that accords with godliness (1:1) to the godly life trained by the appearing grace (2:12) to the God who himself appears in goodness and *philanthrōpia* (3:4) — the *eusebeia* of the community mirrors and responds to the divine *philanthrōpia* of the appearing God.

*sōtēr* — "Savior": 1:3 (God our *Sōtēros*), 1:4 (the Anointed Jesus our *Sōtēros*), 2:10 (God our *Sōtēros*), 2:13 (our great God and *Sōtēros* Jesus the Anointed), 3:4 (God our *Sōtēros* appeared), 3:6 (Jesus the Anointed our *Sōtēros*). Six occurrences in 46 verses — the highest *Sōtēr* density in the NT canon. The *Sōtēr* title is applied to both God the Father (1:3 / 2:10 / 3:4) and the Anointed (1:4 / 2:13 / 3:6) — the divine-identity argument through the *Sōtēr* vocabulary lock reaches its densest expression. At 2:13 both divine persons receive the *Sōtēr* title simultaneously in one phrase. **LXX-primacy argument confirmed: the six-fold *Sōtēr* distribution across Father and Son is only coherent against the LXX background of Isaiah 43:11 / 45:21 LXX where YHWH alone is *sōzōn* — Savior. The Titus *Sōtēr* distribution is the most sustained divine-identity argument through the *Sōtēr* vocabulary lock in the NT.**

*aiōnios* — "everlasting": 1:2 (*aiōniou zōēs* — everlasting life promised before the ages began), 3:7 (*zōēs aiōniou* — everlasting life as the inheritance). Two occurrences forming an *aiōnios* frame: the letter opens with *elpis zōēs aiōniou* (hope of everlasting life — promised before the ages / 1:2) and closes the soteriological argument with *klēronomoi kata elpida zōēs aiōniou* (heirs according to the hope of everlasting life — 3:7). The *zōē aiōnios* promise of eternity past (1:2) and the *klēronomia* of everlasting life in eternity future (3:7) bracket the entire letter's theology — the letter is written from the horizon of *aiōnios zōē* in both directions.

**STRUCTURAL CAE NODES**

**2:11–14 — The first epiphaneia as the ground of Wisdom-register ethics:**
The theological hinge of the letter — the most explicit grace-grounds-ethics argument in the Pastorals. The structure: (a) the grace of God appeared — *epephanē* — bringing salvation (2:11); (b) this grace trains us in three-domain Wisdom-register conduct (2:12); (c) while we wait for the second *epiphaneia* — the blessed hope (2:13); (d) the one who will appear gave himself as *lytron* to redeem us from lawlessness and create a *laos periousios* — treasured people (2:14).

The ethical instruction of the entire letter (elders / older men / older women / young women / young men / bondservants — 1:6–2:10) is retrospectively grounded in 2:11: the reason for all the preceding conduct-instruction is *gar* — "for" — the grace appeared. The appearing grace is simultaneously the motivation (it has appeared / it is present) and the trainer (*paideuousa* — present participle — it is actively training). CAE structural note: the Wisdom-register behavioral catalog of Titus is not a moral improvement program but an *epiphaneia*-response — the community's conduct is the embodied testimony to the appearing grace in the present age (*en tō nyn aiōni* — 2:12). The ethical and the eschatological are structurally unified: Wisdom-register conduct in the present age (2:12) / waiting for the future appearing (2:13) / grounded in the past self-giving (2:14).

**2:13 — The divine identity of Jesus the Anointed:**
The most direct divine-identity statement in the Pauline corpus — *tou megalou theou kai Sōtēros hēmōn Iēsou Christou*. The grammatical structure (single article governing *theou* and *Sōtēros* as a unified noun phrase applied to *Iēsou Christou*) makes the identification unambiguous: Jesus the Anointed is *both* the *megas theos* and the *Sōtēr*. The theological weight: the one whose *epiphaneia* is awaited (2:13) is the *megas theos* — not merely a divine envoy or an exalted human but the great God himself appearing in glory. **CAE divine-identity chain:**

**Isaiah 43:11 LXX (YHWH alone is Savior) → Isaiah 45:23 LXX (every knee bows to YHWH — applied to the Anointed in Philippians 2:10–11) → Titus 2:13 (*tou megalou theou kai Sōtēros hēmōn Iēsou Christou*) → 2 Peter 1:1 (*tou theou hēmōn kai Sōtēros Iēsou Christou*)**

The divine-identity chain confirms across four witnesses: LXX exclusive-Savior predicate / Philippians *kyrios*-acclamation / Titus *megas theos kai Sōtēr* / 2 Peter parallel formula. Chain 1 (*hilastērion*) and the *Sōtēr* vocabulary lock reach their fullest divine-identity expression here.

**3:3–7 — The trinitarian salvation-sequence:**
The most structurally complete soteriological passage in the Pastoral Epistles — seven verses moving from pre-conversion Folly-register condition (3:3) through the divine appearing that grounds salvation (3:4) through the mechanics of salvation (3:5–6) to its forensic and eschatological fruits (3:7). The structure:

*hēmeis* — we ourselves — once (3:3): *anoētoi / apeitheis / planōmenoi / douleuontes epithymiais / en kakia kai phthonō / misētoi / misountes allēlous* — foolish / disobedient / led astray / enslaved to passions / in malice and envy / hated / hating one another. The pre-conversion catalog is the most comprehensive Folly-register description in the Pastorals — seven characteristics spanning cognitive (foolish) / volitional (disobedient / enslaved) / affective (malice / envy) / relational (hated / hating) domains. The *hēmeis pote* — "we ourselves once" — is the apostolic acknowledgment of shared Folly-register origin — the salvation is not from above by those who were never in the Folly register but by those who have been delivered from it.

*hote de epephanē* — "but when appeared" (3:4): the *de* is the sharpest contrast particle — the Folly-register condition is entirely reversed by the divine appearing. The appearing is described through two divine attributes: *chrēstotēs* (goodness / benevolent character) and *philanthrōpia* (love of humanity). The appearing is an act of the divine *character* — not primarily a response to human merit but an expression of who God is. CAE Wisdom/Folly axis: the Folly register is characterized by *misos* (hatred — 3:3) / the Wisdom-register God is characterized by *philanthrōpia* (love of humanity — 3:4). The divine appearing directly contradicts the Folly register's defining relational characteristic.

*ouk ex ergōn... alla kata to autou eleos* — "not by works... but according to his own mercy" (3:5): the salvation-ground formula. The *dikaiosynē* works of 3:5 are not the forensic *dikaiosynē theou* of Romans/Galatians but the attempted self-justification of the Folly register — the effort to establish standing before God through moral performance. The *eleos* — mercy / steadfast love — is the LXX *hesed* / *eleos* vocabulary lock: the same covenantal steadfast love that grounds the entire CAE's Wisdom-register analysis. **Chain 10 (*alētheia*) / *eleos* node:** the salvation rests on *eleos* — the divine covenantal love that persists despite human Folly-register condition.

*dia loutrou paliggenesias kai anakainōseōs pneumatos hagiou* — "through the washing of regeneration and renewing of the Holy Breath-of-life" (3:5): the mechanics of salvation. The *loutron* (washing) is baptismal in context but its theological reference is the Ezekiel 36:25 LXX clean-water-sprinkling — the new covenant cleansing. The *paliggenesia* (regeneration) and *anakainōsis* (renewal) are the two aspects of the Breath-of-life's work — new birth (commencement) and ongoing transformation (continuation / 2 Corinthians 3:18 / Romans 12:2). **The most complete pneumatological soteriology in the Pastoral Epistles.**

*hou execheen eph' hēmas plousiōs dia Iēsou Christou tou Sōtēros hēmōn* — "whom he poured out on us richly through Jesus the Anointed our Savior" (3:6): the trinitarian structure is explicit — *ho theos ho Sōtēr* (God our Savior — the Father / 3:4) pours out the Holy Breath-of-life (3:5–6) *through* Jesus the Anointed (3:6). The Breath-of-life is poured out *plousiōs* — richly / abundantly — the same abundance of the *charisma* in Romans 5:15–17. CAE trinitarian salvation map: **Father initiates (appears in goodness and *philanthrōpia* / saves according to mercy) → Son mediates (Breath-of-life poured out *through* the Anointed) → Holy Breath-of-life applies (regenerates and renews)**. The most complete trinitarian salvation-sequence in the Pastoral Epistles and one of the most explicit in the NT.

*dikaiōthentes... klēronomoi genōmetha kata elpida zōēs aiōniou* — "declared righteous... we might become heirs according to the hope of everlasting life" (3:7): the forensic and eschatological fruits. The *dikaiōthentes* (declared righteous — passive aorist — the divine verdict already rendered) grounds the *klēronomoi* (heirs — the eschatological status). The declared-righteous-therefore-heirs sequence is the Titus form of Romans 8:17 (*klēronomoi theou — sygklēronomoi de Christou* — heirs of God and co-heirs with the Anointed). The eschatological telos: *klēronomoi kata elpida zōēs aiōniou* — heirs by hope of everlasting life — the *zōē aiōnios* promised before the ages (1:2) now the content of the *klēronomia* (3:7). The letter's *aiōnios zōē* frame is closed.

**1:2 — The pre-temporal promise:**
*ep' elpidi zōēs aiōniou — hēn epēngeilato ho apseudēs theos pro chronōn aiōniōn* — "in hope of everlasting life which God — who never lies — promised before the ages began." The *apseudēs theos* — "God who never lies / the non-lying God" — is unique in the NT. It establishes the theological ground of the *alētheia* chain: the *alētheia* of the glad tidings is ultimately grounded in the character of God as *apseudēs* — incapable of falsehood. **CAE Wisdom/Folly axis — the ultimate contrast:** the Folly register is characterized by *pseudos* (the lie that operates as ontological death) / God is *apseudēs* — the non-liar / the one whose word (*logos*) is identical with reality (*davar*). The pre-temporal promise (*pro chronōn aiōniōn*) was made by the *apseudēs theos* — its fulfillment is therefore certain. **CAE Chain 10 (*alētheia*) ultimate ground:** the *alētheia* of the entire chain rests on the *apseudēs* character of the God who promised *pro chronōn aiōniōn*.

**LXX-primacy argument at 1:2:** *apseudēs* — non-lying / no-*pseudos* — connects to Numbers 23:19 LXX (*ouch hōs anthrōpos ho theos distasai — oude hōs huios anthrōpou apeithēsai — autos eipōn ouchi poiēsei — lalēsas ouchi emmenei* — God is not a man to waver / nor a son of man to fail to act / has he said and will he not do it?) and Psalm 88:36 LXX (*hapax ōmosa en tō hagiō mou — ei tō Dauid pseudomai* — once I swore in my holiness — I will not lie to David). The *apseudēs theos* of Titus 1:2 is the NT summary of the LXX's repeated insistence on divine truth-constancy — the God who does not lie and whose word-of-promise stands forever.

**LXX-PRIMACY ARGUMENTS IN TITUS**

Eight independent LXX dependency points:

1. 1:2 — Numbers 23:19 LXX + Psalm 88:36 LXX (*apseudēs theos* — God who never lies — divine truth-constancy tradition)
2. 2:13 — Isaiah 43:11 LXX + Isaiah 45:21 LXX (*sōzōn / Sōtēr* — YHWH alone is Savior — applied to Jesus the Anointed as *megas theos kai Sōtēr*)
3. 2:14a — Psalm 129:8 LXX (*lytrōsetai ton Israēl ek pasōn tōn anomiōn autou* — redeem from all lawlessnesses)
4. 2:14b — Exodus 19:5 LXX + Deuteronomy 7:6 LXX (*laos periousios* — treasured people — Sinai covenantal-people vocabulary transferred to the new covenant community)
5. 3:4 — Wisdom of Solomon 1:6 LXX + Wisdom of Solomon 12:19 LXX (*philanthrōpia* — love of humanity — deuterocanonical divine-love vocabulary)
6. 3:5 — Ezekiel 36:25–27 LXX (*rantisō hydōr katharon / kardian kainēn / pneuma mou* — clean water / new heart / my Breath-of-life — new covenant cleansing and Breath-of-life gift)
7. 3:5–6 — Joel 3:1–2 LXX (*ekxeō apo tou pneumatos mou* — I will pour out my Breath-of-life — Pentecost-outpouring vocabulary applied to individual salvation)
8. 3:7 — Romans 8:17 parallel via Psalm 2:8 LXX (*dōsō soi ethnē tēn klēronomian sou* — I will give you the nations as your inheritance — the Son's *klēronomia* shared with co-heirs)

The Titus LXX-primacy case is exceptionally strong — eight independent points including three deuterocanonical LXX texts (Wisdom of Solomon 1:6 / 12:19 / and implicit in the *philanthrōpia* tradition) confirming the Pastoral Epistles' use of the full LXX canon including the deuterocanonicals.

**CHAIN STATUS AFTER TITUS**

All 15 chains remain closed. Titus adds:

- *Megas theos kai Sōtēr*: **Titus 2:13** — the most direct divine-identity statement in the Pauline corpus. Isaiah 43:11 LXX YHWH-Savior predicate applied to Jesus the Anointed. Divine-identity chain confirmed: LXX / Philippians 2:10–11 / Titus 2:13 / 2 Peter 1:1 — four-witness lock
- *Laos periousios*: **Titus 2:14** — Chain 8 (*diathēkē aiōnios*) covenantal-people node. Exodus 19:5 LXX + Deuteronomy 7:6 LXX + Psalm 129:8 LXX → Titus 2:14 → 1 Peter 2:9. The new covenant community as the *laos periousios* redeemed by the Anointed's *lytrōsētai*
- *Paliggenesia*: **Titus 3:5** — Chain 15 (Eden-to-new-creation) regeneration node. The personal *paliggenesia* through the Holy Breath-of-life as the individual anticipation of the cosmic *paliggenesia* (Matthew 19:28). Ezekiel 36:25–27 LXX → Titus 3:5 → John 3:5 — three-witness chain confirmed
- Trinitarian salvation-sequence: **Titus 3:4–7** — the most complete trinitarian soteriology in the Pastoral Epistles. Father appears in goodness and *philanthrōpia* / Son channels the Breath-of-life's outpouring / Holy Breath-of-life regenerates and renews → declared righteous → heirs of everlasting life
- *Apseudēs theos*: **Titus 1:2** — Chain 10 (*alētheia*) ultimate ground. The non-lying God whose pre-temporal promise (*pro chronōn aiōniōn*) is the ultimate foundation of the entire *alētheia* chain. The Folly register's *pseudos* (ontological death through language) is ultimately grounded in the *apseudēs* character of God as its absolute opposite
- *Klēronomoi*: **Titus 3:7** — Chain 4 (*huiothesia*) eschatological completion node. Declared righteous → heirs according to the hope of everlasting life. The *huiothesia*-to-*klēronomia* arc: son-placement (Galatians 4:5 / Romans 8:15) → co-heirs (Romans 8:17) → heirs of everlasting life (Titus 3:7)
- *Aiōnios zōē* frame: **Titus 1:2 / 3:7** — the letter's *zōē aiōnios* bracket. Pre-temporal promise (1:2) → present regeneration (3:5) → eschatological inheritance (3:7). The *zōē aiōnios* arc: promised before time / manifested at the *epiphaneia* / inherited at the eschatological completion

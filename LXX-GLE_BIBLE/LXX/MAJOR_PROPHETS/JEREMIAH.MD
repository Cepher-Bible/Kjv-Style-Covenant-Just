\v 1 The word of God which came to Jeremiah son of Hilkiah of the priests who were in Anathoth in the land of Benjamin —
\v 2 who received the word of God in the days of Josiah son of Amos king of Judah — in the thirteenth year of his reign —
\v 3 and it came to pass in the days of Jehoiakim son of Josiah king of Judah — until the eleventh year of Zedekiah son of Josiah king of Judah — until the deportation of Jerusalem in the fifth month.
\p
\v 4 And the word of the Lord came to me saying:
\q1
\v 5 Before I formed you in the womb I knew you —
\q2 and before you came out from the womb I consecrated you —
\q1 I appointed you as a prophet to the nations.
\p
\v 6 And I said: O Lord — I do not know how to speak — for I am young.
\p
\v 7 And the Lord said to me: Do not say: I am young — for to all to whom I send you you shall go — and all that I command you you shall speak.
\v 8 Be not afraid of them — for I am with you to deliver you — says the Lord.
\p
\v 9 And the Lord stretched out his hand and touched my mouth — and the Lord said to me: Look — I have put my words in your mouth.
\v 10 See — I have appointed you this day over nations and over kingdoms — to uproot and to afflict and to destroy and to rebuild and to plant.
\p
\v 11 And the word of the Lord came to me saying: What do you see Jeremiah? And I said: A rod of an almond tree.
\v 12 And the Lord said to me: You have seen well — for I am watching over my word to perform it.
\p
\v 13 And the word of the Lord came to me a second time saying: What do you see? And I said: A boiling pot — and its face from the face of the north.
\v 14 And the Lord said to me: From the north evils will be kindled upon all the inhabitants of the land.
\v 15 For look — I am calling all the kingdoms of the earth from the north — says the Lord — and they will come and each will set his throne at the entrance of the gates of Jerusalem — and against all her walls round about — and against all the cities of Judah.
\v 16 And I will speak my judgments against them concerning all their wickedness — in that they have forsaken me and offered sacrifice to foreign gods — and worshipped the works of their hands.
\p
\v 17 And you — gird up your loins and arise and speak to them all that I command you — do not be dismayed before them lest I make you dismayed before them.
\v 18 For look — I have made you this day a fortified city and an iron pillar and bronze walls against the whole land — against the kings of Judah and its rulers and its priests and the people of the land.
\v 19 And they will fight against you but they shall not prevail against you — for I am with you — says the Lord — to deliver you.

\v 7 And I brought you into the orchard-land to eat its fruit and its good things — but you entered and defiled my land — and made my inheritance an abomination.
\v 8 The priests did not say: Where is the Lord? — and those who hold to the law did not know me — and the shepherds acted impiously against me — and the prophets prophesied by Baal — and went after things that do not profit.
\p
\v 9 Therefore I will still contend with you — says the Lord — and with your sons' sons I will contend.
\v 10 For pass over to the coasts of the Kittim and see — and send to Kedar and consider carefully — and see whether there has been such a thing as this.
\v 11 Has a nation changed its gods even though they are not gods? — but my people has changed its glory for that which does not profit.
\v 12 Be astonished O heavens — at this — and be horribly shocked — be utterly desolate — says the Lord.
\v 13 For my people has done two evils — they have forsaken me — the fountain of living water — and dug for themselves cisterns — broken cisterns that cannot hold water.
\p
\v 14 Is Israel a servant — is he a homeborn slave? — why then has he become a prey?
\v 15 The young lions roared against him — they raised their voice — and they made his land a desolation — his cities are ruined and uninhabited.
\v 16 Also the sons of Memphis and Tahpanhes have broken the crown of your head.
\v 17 Did you not bring this upon yourself by your forsaking the Lord your God when he was leading you in the way?
\v 18 And now what do you have to do with the way of Egypt — to drink the water of Geon? — and what do you have to do with the way of the Assyrians — to drink the water of rivers?
\v 19 Your wickedness will correct you — and your apostasy will rebuke you — know therefore and see that forsaking the Lord your God is evil and bitter for you — says the Lord your God — and I did not take pleasure in you — says the Lord.
\p
\v 20 For from of old I broke your yoke — I tore off your chains — and you said: I will not serve — for on every high hill and under every green tree you were lying down — fornicating.
\v 21 Yet I planted you as a choice vine — all of it genuine seed — how then have you turned degenerate and become a wild vine?
\v 22 Though you wash yourself with lye and use much soap — your iniquity is stained before me — says the Lord.
\v 23 How can you say: I am not defiled — I have not gone after the Baals? — see your way in the valley — know what you have done — a swift young camel running here and there in her ways —
\v 24 a wild donkey accustomed to the desolate-place — in the desire of her soul she breathes the wind — who can restrain her in her time? — all who seek her will not weary themselves — in her month they will find her.
\v 25 Withhold your foot from going unshod — and your throat from thirst — but you said: It is hopeless — no — for I have loved strangers — and after them I will go.
\p
\v 26 As a thief is ashamed when he is caught — so the house of Israel was put to shame — they — their kings — their rulers — their priests — and their prophets.
\v 27 They say to a tree: You are my father — and to a stone: You gave me birth — for they have turned to me the back and not the face — but in the time of their evil they say: Arise and save us.
\v 28 Where are your gods that you made for yourself? — let them arise if they can save you in the time of your evil — for as many as your cities are your gods O Judah.
\p
\v 29 Why do you contend with me? — you have all committed apostasy against me — says the Lord.
\v 30 In vain I struck your sons — they received no correction — your sword devoured your prophets like a destroying lion.
\v 31 O generation — see the word of the Lord — have I been a desolate-place to Israel — or a land of thick darkness? — why then do my people say: We will roam — we will come no more to you?
\v 32 Will a virgin forget her ornament — or a bride her sash? — but my people have forgotten me days without number.
\p
\v 33 How well you direct your course to seek love — so that even to evil women you have taught your ways.
\v 34 Also on your skirts is found the blood of the souls of the innocent poor — I did not find them in a burglary — but on all these.
\v 35 And you said: Because I am innocent — his anger has surely turned from me — look — I will contend with you because you say: I have not sinned.
\v 36 How lightly you go about changing your way — you shall also be put to shame by Egypt as you were put to shame by Assyria.
\v 37 From this also you will go out with your hands on your head — for the Lord has rejected those in whom you trust — and you will not prosper with them.

\v 4 Have you not just now called to me: My father — you are the friend of my youth?
\v 5 Will he keep his anger forever — will he be indignant to the end? — look — you have spoken and done evil things — and you had the power.

\v 18 In those days the house of Judah shall walk with the house of Israel — and they shall come together from the land of the north to the land which I gave for an inheritance to your fathers.

\v 1 If you return O Israel — says the Lord — if you return to me — and if you remove your abominations from my presence — and do not waver —
\v 2 and if you swear: As the Lord lives — in truth — in justice — and in righteousness — then nations shall bless themselves by him — and in him they shall glory.
\p
\v 3 For thus says the Lord to the men of Judah and Jerusalem: Break up your fallow ground — and sow not among thorns.
\v 4 Circumcise yourselves to the Lord — circumcise the foreskin of your heart — O men of Judah and inhabitants of Jerusalem — lest my wrath go forth like fire and burn with none to quench it — because of the evil of your deeds.
\p
\v 5 Declare in Judah and proclaim in Jerusalem — say: Blow the trumpet through the land — cry aloud and say: Assemble — let us go into the fortified cities.
\v 6 Raise a standard toward Zion — flee for safety — stay not — for I am bringing evil from the north — and great destruction.
\v 7 A lion has gone up from his thicket — a destroyer of nations has set out — he has gone out from his place to make your land a waste — your cities will be ruins without inhabitant.
\v 8 For this gird yourselves with sackcloth — lament and wail — for the fierce anger of the Lord has not turned back from us.
\p

\v 10 And I said: Ah Lord God — surely you have utterly deceived this people and Jerusalem — saying: You shall have peace — whereas the sword has reached to the soul.

\p
\v 1 Roam through the streets of Jerusalem — look and take note — search her squares — to see if you can find a man — one who does justice and seeks truth — that I may pardon her.
\v 2 Though they say: As the Lord lives — yet they swear falsely.
\v 3 O Lord — do your eyes not look for truth? — you have struck them — but they felt no anguish — you have consumed them — but they refused to receive correction — they made their faces harder than rock — they refused to repent.
\p
\v 4 But I said: These are only the poor — they have no sense — for they do not know the way of the Lord — the justice of their God.
\v 5 I will go to the great and will speak to them — for they know the way of the Lord — the justice of their God — but they had all broken the yoke together — they burst the bonds.
\p
\v 6 Therefore a lion from the forest will strike them — a wolf from the desolate-place will ravage them — a leopard is watching near their cities — everyone who goes out from them will be torn in pieces — because their transgressions are many — their apostasies are great.
\v 7 How can I pardon you? — your sons have forsaken me and sworn by those who are no gods — when I fed them to the full they committed adultery — they trooped to the houses of prostitutes.
\v 8 They were well-fed lusty horses — each neighing for his neighbor's wife.
\v 9 Shall I not punish them for these things? — says the Lord — and shall I not avenge myself on a nation such as this?
\p
\v 10 Go up through her vine rows and destroy — but make no full end — strip away her branches for they are not the Lord's.
\v 11 For the house of Israel and the house of Judah have been utterly faithless to me — says the Lord.
\v 12 They have spoken falsely of the Lord and have said: He will do nothing — no evil will come upon us — nor shall we see sword or famine.
\v 13 The prophets will become wind — the word is not in them — thus shall it be done to them.
\p
\v 14 Therefore thus says the Lord God of hosts: Because they have spoken this word — look — I am making my words in your mouth a fire — and this people wood — and the fire shall devour them.
\v 15 Look — I am bringing against you a nation from afar O house of Israel — says the Lord — it is an enduring nation — it is an ancient nation — a nation whose language you do not know — nor can you understand what they say.
\v 16 Their quiver is like an open tomb — they are all mighty men.
\v 17 They shall eat up your harvest and your food — they shall eat up your sons and your daughters — they shall eat up your flocks and your herds — they shall eat up your vines and your fig trees — your fortified cities in which you trust they shall batter down with the sword.
\p
\v 18 Yet even in those days — says the Lord — I will not make a full end of you.
\v 19 And when your people say: Why has the Lord our God done all these things to us? — you shall say to them: As you have forsaken me and served foreign gods in your land — so you shall serve strangers in a land that is not yours.
\p
\v 20 Declare this in the house of Jacob — proclaim it in Judah:
\v 21 Hear this O foolish and senseless people — who have eyes but do not see — who have ears but do not hear.
\v 22 Do you not fear me? says the Lord — do you not tremble before me? — I placed the sand as the boundary of the sea — a perpetual barrier that it cannot pass — though the waves toss they cannot prevail — though they roar they cannot pass over it.
\v 23 But this people has a stubborn and rebellious heart — they have turned aside and gone away.
\v 24 They do not say in their hearts: Let us fear the Lord our God — who gives the rain in its season — the early rain and the latter rain — and keeps for us the weeks appointed for the harvest.
\v 25 Your iniquities have turned these away — and your sins have kept good from you.
\p
\v 26 For impious men are found among my people — they lurk like fowlers lying in wait — they set a trap — they catch men.
\v 27 Like a cage full of birds so their houses are full of deceit — therefore they have become great and rich —
\v 28 they have grown fat and sleek — they also excel in deeds of evil — they do not plead the orphan's cause with justice — to make it prosper — and they do not defend the rights of the needy.
\v 29 Shall I not punish them for these things? says the Lord — and shall I not avenge myself on a nation such as this?
\p
\v 30 A horrible and shocking thing has happened in the land —
\v 31 the prophets prophesy falsely and the priests rule at their direction — and my people love to have it so — but what will you do when the end comes?

\p
\v 1 The word that came to Jeremiah from the Lord:
\v 2 Stand in the gate of the house of the Lord — and proclaim there this word — and say: Hear the word of the Lord all you of Judah who enter these gates to worship the Lord.
\v 3 Thus says the Lord of hosts the God of Israel: Amend your ways and your deeds — and I will let you dwell in this place.
\v 4 Do not trust in these deceptive words: The temple of the Lord — the temple of the Lord — the temple of the Lord are these.
\v 5 For if you truly amend your ways and your deeds — if you truly execute justice one with another —
\v 6 if you do not oppress the sojourner — the orphan — or the widow — or shed innocent blood in this place — and if you do not go after other gods to your own harm —
\v 7 then I will let you dwell in this place — in the land that I gave of old to your fathers forever.
\p
\v 8 Look — you trust in deceptive words to no avail.
\v 9 Will you steal — murder — commit adultery — swear falsely — make offerings to Baal — and go after other gods that you have not known —
\v 10 and then come and stand before me in this house — which is called by my name — and say: We are delivered — only to go on doing all these abominations?
\v 11 Has this house which is called by my name become a den of robbers in your eyes? — look — I myself have seen it — says the Lord.
\p
\v 12 Go now to my place that was in Shiloh — where I made my name dwell at first — and see what I did to it because of the evil of my people Israel.
\v 13 And now because you have done all these things — says the Lord — and when I spoke to you persistently you did not listen — and when I called you you did not answer —
\v 14 therefore I will do to the house that is called by my name — and in which you trust — and to the place that I gave to you and to your fathers — as I did to Shiloh.
\v 15 And I will cast you out of my sight as I cast out all your brothers — all the offspring of Ephraim.
\p
\v 16 As for you — do not pray for this people — do not lift up a cry or prayer for them — and do not intercede with me — for I will not hear you.
\v 17 Do you not see what they are doing in the cities of Judah and in the streets of Jerusalem?
\v 18 The children gather wood — the fathers kindle fire — and the women knead dough to make cakes for the queen of heaven — and they pour out drink offerings to other gods to provoke me to anger.
\v 19 Is it I whom they provoke? — says the Lord — is it not themselves — to their own shame?
\v 20 Therefore thus says the Lord God: Look — my anger and my wrath will be poured out on this place — upon man and beast — upon the trees of the field and the fruit of the ground — it will burn and not be quenched.
\p
\v 21 Thus says the Lord of hosts the God of Israel: Add your burnt offerings to your sacrifices and eat the flesh.
\v 22 For on the day that I brought your fathers out of the land of Egypt I did not speak to them or command them concerning burnt offerings and sacrifices.

## CRITICAL TEXTUAL NOTE — THE LXX JEREMIAH

The LXX Jeremiah is approximately one-eighth shorter than the MT and has a substantially different arrangement — the most dramatic structural divergence between LXX and MT in the entire prophetic canon:

**Length:** The LXX lacks approximately 2,700 words present in the MT — condensed speeches, shorter oracles, some passages absent.

**Arrangement:** The oracles against the nations (MT chapters 46–51) appear in the LXX after LXX 25:13 rather than at the end. The LXX order of the nations also differs from MT.

The Qumran evidence (4QJer-b, 4QJer-d) confirms a shorter Hebrew *Vorlage* matching the LXX — the LXX translator was not abridging but working from a distinct text tradition. The GLE engine renders the Rahlfs-Hanhart LXX.

**Revelation 7:17** — "the Lamb in the midst of the throne will be their shepherd and will guide them to springs of living water (*pēgas hydatōn zōēs*)." **Revelation 21:6** — "I will give to the thirsty from the spring of the water of life (*pēgēs tou hydatos tēs zōēs*) without cost."

### Jeremiah 11:19 — *arnion akakon* / gentle lamb led to slaughter — CAE PRIMARY NODE

**11:19** — "But I was like a gentle lamb led to the slaughter — I did not know it was against me they devised schemes — saying: Let us destroy the tree with its fruit — let us cut him off from the land of the living."

***egō de hōs arnion akakon agomenon tou thyesthai*** = but I was like a gentle innocent lamb being led to be slaughtered.

This is one of the most direct Passion-prefigurations in the entire OT — alongside Isaiah 53:7 ("like a sheep before its shearers is silent") and Wisdom 2:12–20. The *arnion akakon* = innocent lamb, the plot against his life, the cutting off from the land of the living — all replicated in the Passion.

**Isaiah 53:7** — "he was led like a lamb to the slaughter (*hōs probaton epi sphagēn ēchthē*)." The two passages together — Jeremiah 11:19 and Isaiah 53:7 — form the OT Lamb-to-slaughter typology.

**John 1:29** — "Look — the Lamb of God who takes away the sin of the world." **Acts 8:32** — Philip shows the Ethiopian eunuch that Isaiah 53:7–8 speaks of Jesus — "he was led as a sheep to slaughter." The Jeremiah 11:19 *arnion akakon* vocabulary is part of the same lamb-to-slaughter tradition.

**Jeremiah 11:19** → the *Confessions* of Jeremiah as Passion-type: Jeremiah the prophet who suffers unjustly, is plotted against, cries to God, is vindicated — is received by the NT as the clearest prophetic type of Christ's Passion pattern before Isaiah 53.

## STRUCTURAL NOTE — JEREMIAH'S CONFESSIONS

Chapters 11–20 contain the *Confessions of Jeremiah* — the prophet's sustained personal lament prayers (11:18–23; 12:1–6; 15:10–21; 17:14–18; 18:18–23; 20:7–18). These are the most intimate prophetic self-disclosures in the entire OT — Jeremiah arguing with God, accusing God of deception (20:7 — "you have deceived me"), crying for vengeance on his enemies, cursing his own birth (20:14–18).

The Confessions are structurally parallel to the lament Psalms — but more radical in their directness and their accusation. They are the bridge between the Joban complaint tradition and the NT's cry of dereliction (Matthew 27:46). **Jeremiah 20:7** — "O Lord you have deceived me and I was deceived" — the strongest accusation of God in any OT prophetic book — is the closest prophetic parallel to the cry of God-forsakenness from the cross.

The Confessions also establish Jeremiah as the supreme prophetic type of Christ's Passion — the innocent sufferer, the rejected prophet, the one whose life is plotted against, who prays for his enemies, who is abandoned by his people but vindicated by God.

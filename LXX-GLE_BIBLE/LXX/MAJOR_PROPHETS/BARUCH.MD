\ms The Letter of Jeremiah

\p
\v 1 A copy of the letter that Jeremiah sent to those who were about to be led captive to Babylon by the king of the Babylonians — to tell them what God had commanded him.
\p
\v 2 Because of the sins you have committed before God — you will be led away captive to Babylon by Nebuchadnezzar king of the Babylonians.
\v 3 So when you have come to Babylon you will be there many years — for a long time — up to seven generations — and after that I will bring you away from there in peace.
\v 4 And now you will see in Babylon gods of silver and gold and wood — carried on shoulders — which cast fear upon the nations.
\v 5 Beware then of becoming at all like the foreigners or of letting fear for these gods possess you
\v 6 when you see the multitude before and behind them worshipping them — but say in your heart: It is you O Lord whom we must worship.
\p
\v 7 For my angel is with you and he himself will watch over your lives.
\p
\v 8 For their tongue is cut smooth by a craftsman and they themselves are overlaid with gold and silver — yet they are false and cannot speak.
\v 9 And as for a girl who loves finery — they take gold and make crowns for the heads of their gods —
\v 10 and sometimes the priests secretly take gold and silver from their gods and spend it on themselves —
\v 11 and even give some of it to the prostitutes on the terrace — they deck these gods out with garments like men — these gods of silver and silver and wood.
\v 12 Yet though they are wrapped in purple they cannot save themselves from rust and worm.
\v 13 When it has been wiped with a cloth to remove the dust which is thick upon it — it does not feel anything.
\v 14 The idol holds a scepter like a human judge of the region but is unable to destroy anyone who offends against it.
\v 15 It has a dagger in its right hand and an ax — but cannot deliver itself from war and robbers.
\v 16 Thereby it is known they are not gods — do not fear them.
\p
\v 17 For just as someone's dish is useless when it is broken — so are their gods when they have been set up in the temples — their eyes are full of the dust raised by the feet of those who enter.
\v 18 And just as the gates are shut on every side against anyone who has offended a king — as though under sentence of death — so the priests make their temples secure with doors and locks and bars — in order that they may not be plundered by robbers.
\v 19 They light more lamps for them than they light for themselves — yet not one of these can they see.
\v 20 They are just like a beam of the temple — and it is said their hearts have been eaten away — when creatures from the earth devour them and their garments — they do not know it.
\v 21 Their faces are blackened by the smoke of the temple.
\v 22 Bats — swallows and birds alight on their bodies and heads — and so do cats.
\v 23 From this you will know that they are not gods — so do not fear them.
\p
\v 24 As for the gold that they wear for beauty — unless someone wipes off the tarnish they will not shine — for even when they were being cast they did not feel it.
\v 25 They are bought at great price and yet there is no breath in them.
\v 26 Having no feet they are carried on the shoulders of others — by which they proclaim to humanity their worthlessness — and those who worship them are put to shame —
\v 27 for if they fall to the ground they cannot rise by themselves — and if anyone sets them upright they cannot stand on their own — their offerings are placed before them just as before the dead.
\v 28 The things sacrificed to them — their priests sell for their own benefit — and their wives preserve some of it with salt — but to the poor and helpless they give nothing.
\v 29 The menstrual woman and the woman after childbirth touch their sacrifices — from this you know that they are not gods — do not fear them.
\p
\v 30 For how can they be called gods? Because women serve before the gods of silver and gold and wood —
\v 31 and the priests sit in their temples with their garments torn — their heads and beards shaved and their heads uncovered.
\v 32 They howl and shout before their gods as some do at a funeral banquet.
\v 33 The priests take garments from their gods to clothe their wives and children.
\v 34 Whether one does evil to them or good — they will not be able to repay it — they cannot set up a king or depose one.
\v 35 Likewise they are not able to give either wealth or money — if one makes a vow to them and does not keep it — they will not require it.
\v 36 They cannot save anyone from death or rescue the weak from the strong.
\v 37 They cannot restore sight to the blind — they cannot rescue anyone who is in distress.
\v 38 They cannot take pity on a widow or do good to an orphan.
\v 39 Their gods of wood and stone and gold and silver are like the stones from the mountain — and those who worship them will be put to shame.
\p
\v 40 How then is it possible to think or say that they are gods?
\p
\v 41 Even the Chaldeans themselves dishonor them — for when they see someone who cannot speak they bring him and pray to Bel that the mute may speak — as if Bel were able to understand.
\v 42 Yet they themselves cannot perceive this and abandon them — for they have no sense.
\v 43 And the women — with cords around them — sit along the passageways burning bran for incense — and when one of them is led off by one of the passers-by and sleeps with him — she derides her neighbor who has not been honored as she has and has not had her cord broken.
\v 44 Whatever is done for these idols is false — how then is it possible to think or say that they are gods?
\p
\v 45 They are produced by craftsmen and goldsmiths — they can be nothing but what the craftsmen wish them to be.
\v 46 Those who produce them will certainly not live very long themselves —
\v 47 how then can the things that are produced by them be gods?
\p
\v 48 They have left only lies and reproach for those who come after.
\v 49 For when war and calamity come upon them — the priests consult together among themselves as to where they can hide with them.
\v 50 How then can one fail to perceive that these are not gods — for they cannot save themselves from war or calamity?
\v 51 Since they are only of wood and overlaid with silver and gold — they will afterward be known for what they are — and it will be manifest to all the nations and kings that they are not gods but the work of human hands and that God does no work through them.
\p
\v 52 Who then can fail to know that they are not gods?
\p
\v 53 For they cannot set up a king over a country or give rain to people.
\v 54 They cannot judge their own case or deliver one who is wronged — for they have no power — they are like crows between heaven and earth.
\v 55 When fire breaks out in a temple of wooden gods overlaid with gold or silver — their priests will flee and escape but the gods will be burned up like timbers.
\v 56 Moreover they cannot resist a king or any enemies — how then is it possible to admit or think that they are gods?
\p
\v 57 Gods of wood and stone overlaid with silver and gold are unable to save themselves from thieves or robbers.
\v 58 Anyone who can will strip them of their gold and silver and of the garments they wear and go off with this booty — nor will they be able to help themselves.
\v 59 So it is better to be a king who shows his courage — or a household utensil that serves its owner's need — than to be these false gods — better even the door of a house that protects its contents than these false gods — better a wooden pillar in a palace than these false gods.
\p
\v 60 For sun and moon and stars — shining and sent forth for service — are obedient.
\v 61 Likewise the lightning when it flashes is wide-ranging and the wind blows in every land.
\v 62 And when God commands the clouds to go over the whole world they carry out his command.
\v 63 And the fire sent from above to consume mountains and woods does what it is ordered — but these idols are not to be compared with them in appearance or power.
\v 64 Therefore one must not think that they are gods nor call them gods — for they are not able to decide a case or do good to people.
\p
\v 65 Since you know then that they are not gods — do not fear them.
\p
\v 66 For they can neither curse kings nor bless them —
\v 67 they cannot show signs in the heavens for the nations or shine like the sun or give light like the moon.
\v 68 The wild animals are better than they are — for they can flee to cover and help themselves.
\v 69 So there is absolutely no evidence that they are gods — therefore do not fear them.
\p
\v 70 For like a scarecrow in a cucumber field — which guards nothing — so are their gods of wood and stone overlaid with gold and silver.
\v 71 In the same way their gods of wood and stone overlaid with gold and silver are like a thornbush in a garden — on which every bird perches — or like a corpse thrown out in the darkness.
\v 72 By the purple and linen that rot upon them you will know that they are not gods — and they will finally themselves be consumed and be a reproach in the land.
\v 73 Better therefore is the one who is upright and has no idols — for such a person will be far above reproach.

## CRITICAL TEXTUAL NOTE — LXX BARUCH

Baruch exists only in Greek — no Hebrew original survives for the whole book, though a Hebrew original is widely presumed for at least chapters 1–3. The Letter of Jeremiah (chapter 6 in the LXX / Baruch 6 in the Vulgate tradition) circulates independently in some manuscripts. It is placed after Lamentations in some LXX codices (Codex Vaticanus, Sinaiticus) and after Baruch as its sixth chapter in others (Vulgate). The GLE renders it as chapter 6 of Baruch, following the Vulgate/Roman Catholic canon convention.

The book has four identifiable sections:

1. **1:1–3:8** — Prose confession and prayer (exile community in Babylon)
2. **3:9–4:4** — Wisdom poem: where is Wisdom found?
3. **4:5–5:9** — Poem of consolation: Jerusalem addresses her exiled children
4. **6:1–73** — Letter of Jeremiah: polemic against idolatry

## CAE NODES — BARUCH

### Baruch 1:15 / 2:6 — *tō Kyriō hēmōn dikaiosynē* / to the Lord belongs righteousness — CAE NODE

**1:15** — "To the Lord our God belongs righteousness — but to us confusion of face — as it is this day."

**2:6** — "To the Lord our God belongs righteousness — but to us and our fathers confusion of face as at this day."

The repeated confession-formula — *tō Kyriō dikaiosynē / hēmin aischynē* = to the Lord righteousness / to us shame — is drawn directly from **Daniel 9:7** — "To you O Lord belongs righteousness but to us open shame." Baruch 1–2 is in large part a prose meditation on and expansion of Daniel's great prayer in chapter 9. The echo is deliberate — the diaspora community appropriating Daniel's confession as their own.

**Romans 3:4** — "Let God be true though every one were a liar — as it is written: That you may be justified in your words." The *dikaiosunē* belongs to God alone — the confessional formula that opens Paul's extended argument on divine righteousness in Romans 1–3.

---

### Baruch 2:17 — *hoi en Hadē* / those in Hades cannot praise — CAE NODE

**2:17** — "For the dead who are in Hades — whose breath has been taken from their bodies — will not ascribe glory or justice to the Lord — but the soul that is greatly distressed — that goes about stooped and feeble — and the eyes that fail and the hungry soul — will ascribe to you glory and righteousness O Lord."

***hoi en tō Hadē... ou dōsousin doxan*** = those in Hades will not give glory. The living who suffer can still praise — the dead in Hades cannot. This is the OT *sheol*-as-silence tradition — **Psalm 113:25 (LXX)** — "the dead will not praise the Lord nor any who go down into silence." **Psalm 6:5** — "in death there is no remembrance of you — in Hades who will give you praise?"

**Revelation 5:13** — "every creature in heaven and on earth and under the earth and in the sea and all that is in them" giving praise — the eschatological reversal of the Baruch/Psalm silence-of-the-dead: in the new creation even the dead raised give glory.

The *pneuma* taken from the bodies (*ho apelēlythen pneuma autōn*) is the CAE *pneuma* = breath-of-life register — the breath given at creation (Genesis 2:7) taken back at death.

---

### Baruch 3:9–4:1 — *pou estin hē phronēsis* / where is wisdom? — CAE PRIMARY NODE

**3:9** — "Hear O Israel the commandments of life — give ear to understand wisdom."

**3:15** — "Who has found her place — and who has entered her storehouses?"

**3:29–30** — "Who has gone up into heaven and taken her and brought her down from the clouds? Who has gone over the sea and found her — and will buy her for pure gold? No one knows the way to her or is concerned about the path to her."

**3:31–36** — "But he who knows all things knows her — he found her by his understanding... he found the whole way to knowledge and gave her to his servant Jacob and to Israel whom he loved."

**3:36–4:1** — "Afterward she appeared on earth and lived among human beings. She is the book of the commandments of God and the law that endures forever."

***meta tauta epi tēs gēs ōphthē kai en tois anthrōpois synanestraphē*** = afterward she appeared on earth and lived among human beings.

This is the most important Christological pre-figuration in Baruch — the Wisdom (*Sophia*) who is with God from the beginning, unknown to the mighty of the earth, given to Israel as Torah — and who *afterward appeared on earth and lived among human beings.*

**John 1:14** — "*eskenōsen en hēmin*" = he tabernacled among us — the Incarnation as the fulfillment of Baruch 3:36's wisdom-appearing-on-earth. The Johannine Prologue's *Logos* Christology draws on the same Wisdom tradition that Baruch 3 represents: pre-existent with God / agent of creation / given to Israel / appearing among humans.

**Baruch 3:29** — "Who has gone up into heaven and taken her and brought her down from the clouds?" — **Romans 10:6–7** — "Do not say in your heart: Who will ascend into heaven — that is to bring Christ down — or who will descend into the abyss — that is to bring Christ up from the dead?" Paul's rhetorical questions in Romans 10:6–7 are drawn from **Deuteronomy 30:12–13** but the verbal form *tis anabēsetai eis ton ouranon* = who will ascend into heaven is the same phrase as Baruch 3:29. Paul applies the Wisdom-search question to Christ — the one who *is* the Wisdom no one could find by ascending to heaven.

**1 Corinthians 1:24** — "Christ the power of God and the wisdom of God (*Christos theou dynamis kai theou sophia*)." **1 Corinthians 1:30** — "Christ Jesus who became for us wisdom from God (*sophia apo theou*)." Paul's identification of Christ as divine Wisdom is the NT completion of the Baruch 3 trajectory: the Wisdom given to Israel as Torah is now given to all as Christ.

**Colossians 2:3** — "in whom are hidden all the treasures of wisdom and knowledge." The *thēsauroi* = treasures/storehouses of Baruch 3:14 (*who has entered her storehouses*) → Colossians 2:3.

**Proverbs 8:22–31** / **Wisdom of Solomon 7–9** / **Sirach 24** are the parallel Wisdom-appearing-on-earth texts. Baruch 3:36–4:1 is the densest identification of Wisdom-appearing with Torah — but the NT moves to identify this appearing with Christ.

### Baruch 4:7 — *daimonois kai ou theō* / sacrificed to demons and not to God — CAE NODE

**4:7** — "For you provoked him who made you by sacrificing to demons and not to God."

***ethysate daimonois kai ou theō*** = you sacrificed to demons and not to God. The *daimonion* = demon as the true identity behind the idol — the OT polemic that pagans sacrifice to demonic powers when they offer to idols.

**Deuteronomy 32:17 (LXX)** — "they sacrificed to demons and not to God (*ethysan daimoniois kai ou theō*)." Baruch 4:7 cites the Deuteronomy 32:17 formula exactly.

**1 Corinthians 10:20** — "the things that the Gentiles sacrifice they offer to demons (*daimoniois*) and not to God — and I do not want you to become partners with demons." Paul cites the same Deuteronomy 32:17 / Baruch 4:7 tradition. The *daimoniois kai ou theō* formula is quoted verbatim in 1 Corinthians 10:20 — the CAE transmission chain: Deuteronomy 32:17 → Baruch 4:7 → 1 Corinthians 10:20.

---

### Baruch 4:36–5:9 — *apostrephon* / turn toward the east — CAE PRIMARY NODE

**4:36–37** — "Look toward the east O Jerusalem and see the joy that is coming to you from God. Look — your children are coming whom you sent away — they are coming gathered from east and west at the word of the Holy One rejoicing in the glory of God."

**5:1–2** — "Take off the garment of your sorrow and affliction O Jerusalem and put on forever the beauty of the glory from God. Put on the robe of the righteousness from God — set the diadem of the glory of the Everlasting on your head."

**5:5–7** — "Arise O Jerusalem — stand upon the height and look toward the east — and see your children gathered from west and east at the word of the Holy One... For God has ordered that every high mountain and the everlasting hills be made low and the valleys filled up — to make level ground — so that Israel may walk safely in the glory of God."

***Anastēthi Ierousalēm kai stēthi epi tou hypsēlou*** = Arise O Jerusalem and stand upon the height. The consolation-poem addressed to Jerusalem-as-mother who will see her exiled children return.

**Isaiah 40:3–5** — "A voice cries: In the wilderness prepare the way of the Lord — make straight in the desert a highway for our God — every valley shall be lifted up and every mountain and hill be made low... and the glory of the Lord shall be revealed." Baruch 5:7 (*every high mountain made low / valleys filled up*) is the Isaiah 40 highway-preparation applied to the return of the exiles. **Luke 3:4–5** cites Isaiah 40:3–5 for John the Baptist's ministry — the same highway-leveling.

**Isaiah 52:1–2** — "Awake — awake — put on your strength O Zion — put on your beautiful garments O Jerusalem." Baruch 5:1 (*take off the garment of mourning — put on the beauty of glory*) draws on Isaiah 52:1 — the *put on / take off* garment-of-glory / garment-of-mourning exchange.

**Revelation 21:2** — "the holy city new Jerusalem coming down out of heaven from God — prepared as a bride adorned (*kekosmēmenē*) for her husband." The Baruch Jerusalem adorned in God's glory — robed in righteousness and crowned with the diadem of the Everlasting — is the OT type of the Revelation 21 New Jerusalem-as-bride adorned for the eschatological marriage.

---

### Baruch 6 — Letter of Jeremiah — *eidōla ouk eisin theoi* / idols are not gods — CAE NODE

**6:73** — "Better therefore is the one who is upright and has no idols — for such a person will be far above reproach."

The Letter of Jeremiah is the most sustained OT anti-idol polemic — 73 verses of systematic deconstruction of idolatry, organized around a recurring refrain (*from this you know they are not gods — do not fear them*). The literary model is **Jeremiah 10:1–16** (the idol-making passage) and **Isaiah 44:9–20** (the idol-wood passage).

**1 Corinthians 8:4** — "an idol has no real existence and there is no God but one." **Acts 17:29** — "we ought not to think that the divine being is like gold or silver or stone — an image formed by the art and imagination of man." The NT idol-polemics of Paul and Acts draw on the Letter of Jeremiah tradition.

**6:59** — "better to be a king who shows his courage — or a household utensil that serves its owner's need — than to be these false gods." The *comparative worthlessness* argument: a functioning tool has more divine power than an idol. **Romans 1:22–23** — "claiming to be wise they became fools and exchanged the glory of the immortal God for images resembling mortal man and birds and animals and creeping things." Paul's idolatry diagnosis in Romans 1 follows the Letter of Jeremiah's logical structure: the gods cannot do what they are supposed to do and those who make them are themselves degraded.

## VOCABULARY DECISIONS — BARUCH

### *Sophia / phronēsis* — Wisdom
Rendered "wisdom" for both *sophia* and *phronēsis* throughout — the Wisdom-poem of 3:9–4:4 uses both terms as virtual synonyms for the divine *Sophia* who is simultaneously cosmic agent of creation, Torah given to Israel, and the one who *appeared on earth among human beings* (3:36). The engine preserves the full semantic weight of *sophia* as the Wisdom-Christ pre-figuration.

### *Ho aiōnios* — the Everlasting
Rendered "the Everlasting" — the striking divine title used throughout chapters 4–5 (*ho aiōnios* = the Everlasting One). This title is peculiar to Baruch in the LXX corpus and establishes the eternal / temporal contrast: the exiles' suffering is temporal — the Everlasting's redemption is permanent.

### *Daimonion* — demon
Rendered "demon" at 4:7 — never "spirit" or "false god." The *daimoniois kai ou theō* formula requires the precise vocabulary that Paul quotes in 1 Corinthians 10:20.

### *Anastēthi* — arise
Rendered "arise" at 5:5 — the command to Jerusalem-as-mother to *stand upon the height* and look east for the returning children. The *anastēthi* = arise is also resurrection vocabulary — the city that was as dead raised to see the glory. The same *anastēthi* root as *anastasis* = resurrection.

## STRUCTURAL NOTE — BARUCH'S CANONICAL FUNCTION

Baruch is the only deuterocanonical book that presents itself as a direct liturgical reading for the exile community — 1:14 commands that it be *read aloud in the house of the Lord on appointed feasts and assembly days*. It is the canonical bridge between:

**Backward: Jeremiah / Lamentations** — the confession of chapter 1–2 appropriates Daniel 9 and Jeremiah's theology of exile-as-judgment. The Letter of Jeremiah (chapter 6) is explicitly Jeremian.

**Forward: The Wisdom tradition** — the great Wisdom poem of 3:9–4:4 stands alongside Proverbs 8, Wisdom of Solomon 7–9, and Sirach 24 as the OT Wisdom-Christology foundation.

**The theological arc of Baruch:**
Confession of sin → prayer from the depths → *where is wisdom?* → Wisdom given to Israel / appeared among humans → consolation: the exiles will return in glory → reject idols who cannot save.

This arc is the OT's most compressed summary of the movement from exile-through-wisdom to eschatological restoration — and the Wisdom-appearing-on-earth of 3:36 is the theological hinge on which the whole turns.

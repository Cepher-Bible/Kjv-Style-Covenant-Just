\v 1 In the third year of the reign of Jehoiakim king of Judah — Nebuchadnezzar king of Babylon came to Jerusalem and besieged it.
\v 2 And the Lord gave into his hand Jehoiakim king of Judah — and part of the vessels of the house of God — and he brought them to the land of Shinar to the house of his god — and the vessels he brought into the treasury of his god.
\v 3 Then the king spoke to Ashpenaz his chief eunuch to bring some of the sons of Israel — both of the royal family and of the nobles —
\v 4 youths without blemish — good in appearance and skillful in all wisdom — endowed with knowledge — understanding learning — and competent to stand in the king's palace — and to teach them the literature and language of the Chaldeans.
\v 5 The king assigned them a daily portion of the king's food and of the wine that he drank — they were to be educated for three years and at the end of that time they were to stand before the king.
\v 6 Among these were Daniel — Hananiah — Mishael — and Azariah of the sons of Judah.
\v 7 And the chief of the eunuchs gave them names — Daniel he called Belteshazzar — Hananiah he called Shadrach — Mishael he called Meshach — and Azariah he called Abednego.
\p
\v 8 But Daniel resolved that he would not defile himself with the king's food or with the wine that he drank — and he asked the chief of the eunuchs to allow him not to defile himself.
\v 9 And God gave Daniel favor and compassion in the sight of the chief of the eunuchs —
\v 10 and the chief of the eunuchs said to Daniel: I fear my lord the king who assigned your food and your drink — for why should he see that you were in worse condition than the youths who are of your own age? — so you would endanger my head with the king.
\v 11 Then Daniel said to the steward whom the chief of the eunuchs had assigned over Daniel — Hananiah — Mishael — and Azariah:
\v 12 Test your servants for ten days — let us be given vegetables to eat and water to drink.
\v 13 Then let our appearance and the appearance of the youths who eat the king's food be observed by you — and deal with your servants according to what you see.
\v 14 So he listened to them in this matter and tested them for ten days.
\v 15 At the end of ten days it was seen that they were better in appearance and fatter in flesh than all the youths who ate the king's food.
\v 16 So the steward took away their food and the wine they were to drink and gave them vegetables.
\p
\v 17 As for these four youths — God gave them learning and skill in all literature and wisdom — and Daniel had understanding in all visions and dreams.
\v 18 At the end of the time when the king had commanded that they should be brought in — the chief of the eunuchs brought them in before Nebuchadnezzar.
\v 19 And the king spoke with them — and among all of them none was found like Daniel — Hananiah — Mishael — and Azariah — so they stood before the king.
\v 20 And in every matter of wisdom and understanding about which the king inquired of them he found them ten times better than all the magicians and enchanters that were in all his kingdom.
\v 21 And Daniel was there until the first year of King Cyrus.

\v 1 In the second year of the reign of Nebuchadnezzar — Nebuchadnezzar had dreams — his breath-of-life was troubled and his sleep left him.
\v 2 Then the king commanded that the magicians — the enchanters — the sorcerers — and the Chaldeans be summoned to tell the king his dreams — so they came in and stood before the king.
\v 3 And the king said to them: I had a dream and my breath-of-life is troubled to know the dream.
\v 4 Then the Chaldeans said to the king in Aramaic: O king live forever — tell your servants the dream and we will show the interpretation.
\v 5 The king answered and said to the Chaldeans: The word from me is firm — if you do not make known to me the dream and its interpretation you shall be torn limb from limb and your houses shall be laid in ruins.
\v 6 But if you show the dream and its interpretation you shall receive from me gifts and rewards and great honor — therefore show me the dream and its interpretation.
\v 7 They answered a second time and said: Let the king tell his servants the dream and we will show its interpretation.
\v 8 The king answered and said: I know with certainty that you are trying to gain time because you see that the word from me is firm —
\v 9 if you do not make the dream known to me there is but one sentence for you — you have agreed to speak lying and corrupt words before me until the situation changes — therefore tell me the dream and I shall know that you can show me its interpretation.
\v 10 The Chaldeans answered the king and said: There is not a man on earth who can meet the king's demand — for no great and powerful king has asked such a thing of any magician or enchanter or Chaldean.
\v 11 The thing that the king asks is difficult and no one can show it to the king except the gods whose dwelling is not with flesh.
\p
\v 12 Because of this the king was angry and very furious and commanded that all the wise men of Babylon be destroyed.
\v 13 So the decree went out and the wise men were about to be killed — and they sought Daniel and his companions to kill them.
\p
\v 14 Then Daniel replied with prudence and discretion to Arioch the captain of the king's guard who had gone out to kill the wise men of Babylon.
\v 15 He declared to Arioch the king's captain: Why is the decree of the king so urgent? Then Arioch made the matter known to Daniel.
\v 16 And Daniel went in and requested the king to appoint him a time that he might show the king the interpretation.

\v 17 Then Daniel went to his house and made the matter known to Hananiah — Mishael — and Azariah his companions —
\v 18 and told them to seek mercy from the God of heaven concerning this mystery so that Daniel and his companions might not be destroyed with the rest of the wise men of Babylon.
\v 19 Then the mystery was revealed to Daniel in a vision of the night — then Daniel blessed the God of heaven.
\v 20 Daniel answered and said:

\q2 he knows what is in the darkness —

\q2 I give thanks and praise —
\q1 for you have given me wisdom and might —
\q2 and have now made known to me what we asked of you —
\q1 for you have made known to us the king's matter.

\v 24 Therefore Daniel went in to Arioch whom the king had appointed to destroy the wise men of Babylon — he went and said thus to him: Do not destroy the wise men of Babylon — bring me in before the king and I will show the king the interpretation.
\v 25 Then Arioch brought in Daniel before the king in haste and said thus to him: I have found among the exiles from Judah a man who will make known to the king the interpretation.
\v 26 The king declared to Daniel whose name was Belteshazzar: Are you able to make known to me the dream that I have seen and its interpretation?
\v 27 Daniel answered the king and said: No wise men — enchanters — magicians — or astrologers can show to the king the mystery that the king has asked —
\v 28 but there is a God in heaven who reveals mysteries — and he has made known to King Nebuchadnezzar what will be in the latter days — your dream and the visions of your head as you lay in bed are these:
\v 29 To you O king as you lay in bed came thoughts of what would be after this — and he who reveals mysteries made known to you what is to be.
\v 30 But as for me this mystery has been revealed to me — not because of any wisdom that I have more than all the living — but in order that the interpretation may be made known to the king and that you may know the thoughts of your mind.
\p
\v 31 You saw O king — and look — a great image — this image — mighty and of exceeding brightness — stood before you and its appearance was frightening.
\v 32 The head of this image was of fine gold — its chest and arms of silver — its middle and thighs of bronze —
\v 33 its legs of iron — its feet partly of iron and partly of clay.
\v 34 As you looked a stone was cut out by no human hand and it struck the image on its feet of iron and clay and broke them in pieces.
\v 35 Then the iron — the clay — the bronze — the silver — and the gold — all together were broken in pieces and became like the chaff of the summer threshing floors — and the wind carried them away so that not a trace of them could be found — but the stone that struck the image became a great mountain and filled the whole earth.
\p
\v 36 This was the dream — now we will tell the king its interpretation.
\v 37 You O king — the king of kings — to whom the God of heaven has given the kingdom — the power — the might — and the glory —
\v 38 and into whose hand he has given wherever they dwell — the children of man — the beasts of the field — and the birds of the heavens — making you rule over them all — you are the head of gold.
\v 39 Another kingdom inferior to you shall arise after you — and yet a third kingdom of bronze which shall rule over all the earth.
\v 40 And there shall be a fourth kingdom strong as iron — because iron breaks to pieces and shatters all things — and like iron that crushes — it shall break and crush all these.
\v 41 And as you saw the feet and toes partly of potter's clay and partly of iron — it shall be a divided kingdom — but some of the firmness of iron shall be in it — just as you saw iron mixed with the soft clay.
\v 42 And as the toes of the feet were partly iron and partly clay — so the kingdom shall be partly strong and partly brittle.
\v 43 As you saw the iron mixed with soft clay — so they will mix with one another in marriage but they will not hold together — just as iron does not mix with clay.
\v 44 And in the days of those kings the God of heaven will set up a kingdom that shall never be destroyed — nor shall the kingdom be left to another people — it shall break in pieces all these kingdoms and bring them to an end — and it shall stand forever —
\v 45 just as you saw that a stone was cut from a mountain by no human hand and that it broke in pieces the iron — the bronze — the clay — the silver — and the gold — a great God has made known to the king what shall be after this — the dream is certain and its interpretation sure.
\p
\v 46 Then King Nebuchadnezzar fell upon his face and paid homage to Daniel and commanded that an offering and incense be offered up to him.
\v 47 The king answered and said to Daniel: Truly your God is God of gods and Lord of kings and a revealer of mysteries — for you have been able to reveal this mystery.
\v 48 Then the king gave Daniel high honors and many great gifts and made him ruler over the whole province of Babylon and chief prefect over all the wise men of Babylon.
\v 49 Daniel made a request of the king and he appointed Shadrach — Meshach — and Abednego over the affairs of the province of Babylon — but Daniel remained at the king's court.

\v 1 King Nebuchadnezzar made an image of gold whose height was sixty cubits and its breadth six cubits — he set it up on the plain of Dura in the province of Babylon.
\v 2 Then King Nebuchadnezzar sent to gather the satraps — the prefects — and the governors — the counselors — the treasurers — the justices — the magistrates — and all the officials of the provinces to come to the dedication of the image that King Nebuchadnezzar had set up.
\v 3 Then the satraps — the prefects — and the governors — the counselors — the treasurers — the justices — the magistrates — and all the officials of the provinces gathered for the dedication of the image that King Nebuchadnezzar had set up — and they stood before the image that Nebuchadnezzar had set up.
\v 4 And the herald proclaimed aloud: You are commanded O peoples — nations — and languages —
\v 5 that when you hear the sound of the horn — pipe — lyre — trigon — harp — bagpipe — and every kind of music — you are to fall down and worship the golden image that King Nebuchadnezzar has set up.
\v 6 And whoever does not fall down and worship shall immediately be cast into a burning fiery furnace.
\v 7 Therefore as soon as all the peoples heard the sound of the horn — pipe — lyre — trigon — harp — bagpipe — and every kind of music — all the peoples — nations — and languages fell down and worshipped the golden image that King Nebuchadnezzar had set up.
\p
\v 8 Therefore at that time certain Chaldeans came forward and maliciously accused the Jews.
\v 9 They declared to King Nebuchadnezzar: O king live forever.
\v 10 You O king have made a decree — that every man who hears the sound of the horn — pipe — lyre — trigon — harp — bagpipe — and every kind of music shall fall down and worship the golden image —
\v 11 and whoever does not fall down and worship shall be cast into a burning fiery furnace.
\v 12 There are certain Jews whom you have appointed over the affairs of the province of Babylon — Shadrach — Meshach — and Abednego — these men O king pay no attention to you — they do not serve your gods or worship the golden image that you have set up.
\p
\v 13 Then Nebuchadnezzar in furious rage commanded that Shadrach — Meshach — and Abednego be brought — so they brought these men before the king.
\v 14 Nebuchadnezzar answered and said to them: Is it true O Shadrach — Meshach — and Abednego — that you do not serve my gods or worship the golden image that I have set up?
\v 15 Now if you are ready when you hear the sound of the horn — pipe — lyre — trigon — harp — bagpipe — and every kind of music to fall down and worship the image that I have made — well and good — but if you do not worship — you shall immediately be cast into a burning fiery furnace — and who is the god who will deliver you out of my hands?
\p
\v 16 Shadrach — Meshach — and Abednego answered and said to the king: O Nebuchadnezzar — we have no need to answer you in this matter.
\v 17 If it be so — our God whom we serve is able to deliver us from the burning fiery furnace — and he will deliver us out of your hand O king.
\v 18 But if not — be it known to you O king that we will not serve your gods or worship the golden image that you have set up.
\p
\v 19 Then Nebuchadnezzar was filled with fury and the expression of his face was changed against Shadrach — Meshach — and Abednego — he ordered the furnace heated seven times more than it was usually heated.
\v 20 And he ordered some of the mighty men of his army to bind Shadrach — Meshach — and Abednego and to cast them into the burning fiery furnace.
\v 21 Then these men were bound in their cloaks — their tunics — their hats — and their other garments and were cast into the burning fiery furnace.
\v 22 Because the king's order was urgent and the furnace overheated — the flame of the fire killed those men who took up Shadrach — Meshach — and Abednego.
\v 23 And these three men — Shadrach — Meshach — and Abednego — fell bound into the burning fiery furnace.

## CRITICAL TEXTUAL NOTE — THE LXX DANIEL

Daniel is uniquely complex in the LXX tradition. The original Old Greek (OG) translation of Daniel was so divergent from the MT that it was almost entirely replaced in church use by the translation of Theodotion (2nd century AD) — the only case in the entire LXX where Theodotion's version displaced the OG for canonical use. Rahlfs-Hanhart preserves both. The Greek-Catholic canon uses the Theodotion-Daniel for chapters 1–12.

**The three deuterocanonical additions** appear in all Greek manuscripts and are canonical in the Greek-Catholic tradition:
- **Prayer of Azariah + Song of the Three** (inserted in chapter 3 between vv. 23 and 24 in the MT numbering)
- **Susanna** (chapter 13 in LXX)
- **Bel and the Dragon** (chapter 14 in LXX)

The additions were already rendered in Odes 7–8 in their liturgical form. Daniel 3 renders them in their narrative context.

## CAE NODES — DANIEL

### Daniel 2:34–35 — *lithos* / the stone cut without hands — CAE PRIMARY NODE

**2:34–35** — "A stone was cut out by no human hand and it struck the image on its feet of iron and clay and broke them in pieces... but the stone that struck the image became a great mountain and filled the whole earth."

***lithos etmēthē aneu cheirōn*** = a stone was cut without hands. The stone that destroys the image-kingdoms and becomes a mountain filling the earth — the eschatological kingdom of God that supersedes all human empire.

**Matthew 21:42–44** — "The stone that the builders rejected has become the cornerstone... whoever falls on this stone will be broken to pieces and when it falls on anyone it will crush him." Jesus's stone-saying combines Psalm 117:22 (LXX) with the Daniel 2 crushing-stone — both *lithos* traditions fused into a single Christological declaration.

**1 Peter 2:4–8** — "a living stone rejected by men but chosen by God and precious... a stone of stumbling and a rock of offense." The Petrine *lithos* Christology draws on the Daniel 2 stone that destroys the kingdoms and fills the earth.

**Daniel 2:44** — "the God of heaven will set up a kingdom that shall never be destroyed... it shall stand forever." The eternal kingdom established by the stone = the kingdom of Christ that has no end. **Luke 1:33** — "of his kingdom there will be no end."

---

### Daniel 2:47 — *theos tōn theōn* / God of gods — CAE NODE

**2:47** — "Truly your God is God of gods and Lord of kings and a revealer of mysteries."

***theos theōn... kai kyrios tōn basileōn*** = God of gods and Lord of kings. Nebuchadnezzar's confession from his pagan throne — the Gentile king declaring the supremacy of the God of Israel. **Revelation 17:14 / 19:16** — "Lord of lords and King of kings." The Revelation title draws on the Daniel throne-room vocabulary.

---

### Daniel 3:92 (LXX) — *homoios huiō theou* / like a son of the gods — CAE PRIMARY NODE

**3:92** — "And the appearance of the fourth is like a son of the gods (*homoios huiō theou*)."

Nebuchadnezzar sees four figures in the furnace — the fourth *homoios huiō theou* = like a son of God/the gods. The Christophany-in-the-furnace interpretation: the pre-incarnate Son present with the three faithful in the fire.

**Hebrews 11:34** — "quenched the power of fire" — the Three Young Men are the Hebrews 11 faith-roll heroes who experienced this divine presence in suffering.

The LXX *huios theou* vocabulary in 3:92 feeds directly into the NT's Son-of-God Christology — the divine figure who accompanies the faithful through death-threatening trials.

---

### Daniel 7:9–10 — *palaios hēmerōn* / Ancient of Days — CAE PRIMARY NODE

**7:9–10** — "Thrones were placed and the Ancient of Days took his seat — his clothing was white as snow and the hair of his head like pure wool — his throne was fiery flames — its wheels were burning fire. A stream of fire issued and came out from before him — a thousand thousands served him and ten thousand times ten thousand stood before him."

***palaios hēmerōn*** = Ancient of Days. The supreme divine throne-vision of the OT — the enthroned God in blazing white with the fiery river and innumerable attendants.

**Revelation 1:12–16** — "I saw... one like a son of man... his head and his hair were white like white wool — like snow... his eyes were like a flame of fire... his voice was like the roar of many waters." John's vision of the risen Christ fuses Daniel 7:9 (Ancient of Days — white hair) with Daniel 7:13 (one like a son of man) — in Revelation both figures merge into the single glorified Christ. The most important Christological synthesis in the entire Apocalypse.

**Revelation 4:2–5** — the throne-room vision with fire and the great assembly — the Daniel 7:9–10 throne-scene reproduced and expanded.

**Daniel 7:10** — *myriades myriadōn* = ten thousand times ten thousand. **Revelation 5:11** — "ten thousand times ten thousand and thousands of thousands" — exact Daniel 7:10 vocabulary.

---

### Daniel 7:13–14 — *huios anthrōpou* / one like a son of man — CAE PRIMARY NODE

**7:13–14** — "And look — with the clouds of heaven there came one like a son of man and he came to the Ancient of Days and was presented before him. And to him was given dominion and glory and a kingdom that all peoples — nations — and languages should serve him — his dominion is an everlasting dominion which shall not pass away and his kingdom one that shall not be destroyed."

***hōs huios anthrōpou ērcheto*** = as/like a son of man was coming. The *bar enasha* = son of man figure — the human-like being who receives universal eternal dominion from the Ancient of Days.

This is the single most important OT text for NT Christology after Isaiah 53. Jesus's self-designation as *ho huios tou anthrōpou* = the Son of Man is drawn directly from Daniel 7:13 — it is not a humility title but a sovereignty title: the one who comes on the clouds to receive all dominion.

**Matthew 24:30** — "they will see the Son of Man coming on the clouds of heaven with power and great glory." **Matthew 26:64** — "you will see the Son of Man seated at the right hand of Power and coming on the clouds of heaven" — Jesus cites Daniel 7:13 directly at his trial before the Sanhedrin. This citation is what the high priest calls blasphemy — Jesus claims for himself the Daniel 7:13 cloud-coming dominion.

**Mark 14:62** — identical. **Luke 22:69** — "the Son of Man shall be seated at the right hand of the power of God" — fusing Daniel 7:13 with Psalm 110:1.

**Revelation 1:7** — "Look — he is coming with the clouds and every eye will see him." **Revelation 14:14** — "one like a son of man (*homoion huion anthrōpou*)" with a golden crown on a cloud — the Daniel 7:13 figure returned for the harvest.

**Daniel 7:14** — *hē exousia autou exousia aiōnios hētis ou pareleusestai* = his authority is an everlasting authority which shall not pass away. **Matthew 28:18** — "All authority in heaven and on earth has been given to me." The Great Commission's *pasa exousia* = all authority is the fulfillment of Daniel 7:14.

---

### Daniel 7:25 — *kairos kai kairoi kai hēmisy kairou* / time times and half a time — CAE NODE

**7:25** — "they shall be given into his hand for a time — times — and half a time."

The three-and-a-half times / 42 months / 1260 days — the eschatological period of tribulation under the little horn. **Revelation 12:14** — "a time and times and half a time." **Revelation 11:2–3** — 42 months / 1260 days. **Revelation 13:5** — 42 months. The Revelation apocalyptic time-framework is entirely built on Daniel 7:25 and 12:7.

---

### Daniel 9:24–27 — *hebdomēkonta hebdomades* / seventy weeks — CAE PRIMARY NODE

**9:24** — "Seventy weeks are decreed about your people and your holy city — to finish the transgression — to put an end to sin — and to atone for iniquity — to bring in everlasting righteousness — to seal both vision and prophet — and to anoint a most holy place."

**9:26** — "And after the sixty-two weeks an anointed one shall be cut off and shall have nothing."

***christos ekkopensetai*** = an anointed one shall be cut off. The Daniel 9:26 *cut-off anointed one* is one of the most explicit OT Passion prophecies — the Messiah who is "cut off" (*kopto* = cut/killed) and "has nothing" = stripped of everything.

**9:27** — "he shall put an end to sacrifice and offering." The cessation of Temple sacrifice through the death of the Anointed — **Hebrews 9:26** — "he has appeared once for all at the end of the ages to put away sin by the sacrifice of himself."

The seventy-weeks prophecy is the structural backbone of NT eschatology. The "abomination of desolation" (*to bdelygma tēs erēmōseōs*) of Daniel 9:27 / 11:31 / 12:11 generates the primary NT eschatological warning: **Matthew 24:15** — "when you see the abomination of desolation spoken of by the prophet Daniel standing in the holy place." **Mark 13:14** — identical. Jesus explicitly directs his disciples to Daniel's text for the end-time sign.

---

### Daniel 12:1–3 — *anastēsontai* / many shall awake — CAE PRIMARY NODE

**12:1–3** — "At that time shall arise Michael the great prince who has charge of your people — and there shall be a time of trouble such as never has been since there was a nation... your people shall be delivered — everyone whose name shall be found written in the book. And many of those who sleep in the dust of the earth shall awake — some to everlasting life — and some to shame and everlasting contempt. And those who are wise shall shine like the brightness of the sky above."

***polloi tōn koimōmenōn en gēs chōmati exypnēsousin*** = many of those sleeping in the dust of the earth shall awake. The clearest resurrection-to-judgment text in the Hebrew OT — two-track resurrection: *eis zōēn aiōnion* = to everlasting life and *eis aischynēn* = to shame.

**John 5:28–29** — "the hour is coming when all who are in the tombs will hear his voice and come out — those who have done good to the resurrection of life and those who have done evil to the resurrection of judgment." Jesus's two-track resurrection teaching draws directly on Daniel 12:2 vocabulary — *anastasis zōēs* / *anastasis kriseōs* = resurrection of life / resurrection of judgment.

**Revelation 20:12–15** — the Great White Throne judgment with the books opened — drawing on Daniel 7:10 (fiery throne / books opened) and Daniel 12:1 (those written in the book delivered).

**12:3** — "those who are wise shall shine like the brightness of the sky (*hōs hē lamprotēs tou stereōmatos*) and those who turn many to righteousness like the stars forever and ever." **Matthew 13:43** — "the righteous will shine like the sun (*eklampsousin hōs ho hēlios*) in the kingdom of their Father." The eschatological shining of the righteous — Daniel 12:3 → Matthew 13:43.

---

### Daniel 13 (Susanna) — CAE NODE

**13:42–44** — "O eternal God who know what is hidden and know all things before they come to be — you know that these men have given false evidence against me... The Lord heard her cry. And as she was being led away to be put to death God stirred up the holy breath-of-life of a young man whose name was Daniel."

The Susanna narrative establishes Daniel as the defender of the falsely accused innocent — a judicial rescue from unjust death. The *pneuma hagion* = holy breath-of-life stirred up in Daniel is the divine enabling of justice.

The pattern: innocent woman falsely accused — condemned by corrupt judges — rescued by divine intervention through a Spirit-filled figure — the accusers destroyed by their own witness. A Passion-rescue typology anticipating the vindication of the innocent.

---

### Daniel 14 — Bel and the Dragon — CAE NODE

**14:5–6** — Daniel declares: "I do not revere man-made idols but the living God who created heaven and earth and has dominion over all living creatures."

***theon zōnta ton ktisanta ton ouranon kai tēn gēn*** = the living God who created heaven and earth. The confession that costs Daniel his life — thrown into the lions' den for the second time (first in chapter 6).

**Acts 14:15** — Paul at Lystra: "we bring you good news — that you should turn from these vain things to a living God who made the heaven and the earth and the sea and all that is in them." The *theos zōn* = living God creator vocabulary of Daniel 14:6 fires in the NT missionary proclamation to pagans.

The Habakkuk transport miracle (14:37–40) — the angel of the Lord transports Habakkuk by the hair to Babylon — is the most dramatic supernatural transport in the deuterocanon. **Acts 8:39–40** — "the Spirit of the Lord carried Philip away" — supernatural transport in the NT missions.

## VOCABULARY DECISIONS — DANIEL

### *Huios anthrōpou* — son of man
Rendered "son of man" throughout (7:13) — never "human being" or "human figure." The *huios anthrōpou* is a sovereignty title in Daniel not a humility designation. The engine preserves the exact phrase Jesus claims.

### *Palaios hēmerōn* — Ancient of Days
Rendered "Ancient of Days" — the standard rendering preserving the *palaios* = ancient/old-of-days vocabulary. **Revelation 1:14** requires this background.

### *Bdelygma tēs erēmōseōs* — abomination of desolation
Rendered "abomination that makes desolate" — the exact phrase Matthew 24:15 and Mark 13:14 cite from Daniel. The *bdelygma* = abomination (idolatrous defilement) + *erēmōsis* = desolation/making-empty.

### *Pneuma hagion* (Susanna 45) — holy breath-of-life
Rendered "holy breath-of-life" — consistent with the project's *pneuma* = breath-of-life register. The same divine enabling in Susanna 45 as in Acts 2.

### *Theos zōn* — living God
Rendered "living God" throughout — the *zōn* = living descriptor that marks the God of Israel against the dead idols. Fundamental contrast of the entire book.

## THEOLOGICAL NOTE — DANIEL IN THE NT

Daniel is the most-cited apocalyptic source in the NT after the Psalms. The book's canonical function: providing the eschatological vocabulary and imagery that NT authors — especially Jesus and John of Patmos — draw on for end-time teaching.

**Six major NT lines of dependence:**

1. **Son of Man Christology** (7:13) — Jesus's primary self-designation in all four Gospels
2. **Throne-room Christology** (7:9–10) — merged with Dan 7:13 in Revelation 1 to produce the glorified Christ vision
3. **Abomination of Desolation** (9:27 / 11:31 / 12:11) — Jesus's eschatological sign in Matthew 24 / Mark 13
4. **Two-track resurrection** (12:2) — John 5:28–29 / Revelation 20
5. **Eternal kingdom** (2:44 / 7:14) — Matthew 28:18 (all authority) / Luke 1:33 (kingdom without end)
6. **Time times and half a time** (7:25 / 12:7) — Revelation's entire 42-month tribulation framework

The deuterocanonical additions embed Daniel in the wisdom-martyr tradition: Susanna = the innocent falsely accused vindicated by divine justice; Bel and the Dragon = the prophet who defies idolatry and is rescued from the lions a second time.

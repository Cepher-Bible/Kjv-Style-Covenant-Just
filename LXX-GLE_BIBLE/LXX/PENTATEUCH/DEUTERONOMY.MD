Deuteronomy — 34 chapters. Five blocks:

**Block 1** — Ch 1–11 (output below)
Moses' first address — historical review of the wilderness, **Shema**, love command, land promise

**Block 2** — Ch 12–18
Worship centralization, clean/unclean food, tithes, release year, firstborn, **king law**, Levites, **prophet like Moses**

**Block 3** — Ch 19–26
Cities of refuge, warfare law, social legislation, levirate marriage, **firstfruits declaration**, covenant affirmation

**Block 4** — Ch 27–30
Ebal/Gerizim ceremony, **covenant curses**, exile and return, **call to decision** (life and death set before you)

**Block 5** — Ch 31–34
Joshua commissioned, **Song of Moses**, **Blessing of Moses**, Moses sees the land, dies on Nebo

## STRUCTURAL OVERVIEW

Deuteronomy is the covenant renewal book — Moses' farewell addresses to the second generation standing at the Jordan threshold. The LXX title *Deuteronomion* = the second law — from 17:18 *deuteronomion* = a copy of the law — but more precisely it is the re-proclamation of the Sinai covenant to those who did not receive it at Sinai. The MT title *Devarim* = Words — from the opening "these are the words which Moses spoke."

The book is structured as a covenant renewal treaty following the Ancient Near Eastern suzerainty treaty pattern: historical prologue → stipulations → blessings and curses → covenant ratification and succession. Moses is the mediator-speaker throughout — this is his voice, not the narrator's, except at the book's close.

**Five major sections:**

1. **First address** (1–4) — historical review: wilderness failures, Transjordan conquests, covenant uniqueness
2. **Second address** (5–26) — re-proclamation of Decalogue, Shema, social legislation, firstfruits declaration
3. **Covenant ceremony** (27–30) — Ebal/Gerizim, blessings and curses, call to decision
4. **Moses' farewell** (31–33) — Joshua commissioned, Song of Moses, Blessing of Moses
5. **Moses' death** (34) — seeing the land from Nebo, burial, Joshua's succession, Moses' epitaph

## CAE CONTAMINATION NODES

### 6:4–5 — *Shema* — *kyrios heis estin* / the Lord is one

"Hear O Israel — the Lord our God — the Lord is one. And you will love the Lord your God with all your heart and with all your soul and with all your strength."

***Akoue Israēl*** = Hear O Israel — *akouō* = to hear, to listen, to obey. The command is not merely auditory reception but responsive obedience — the *Shema* is the foundational covenant-loyalty declaration.

***kyrios heis estin*** = the Lord is one — *heis* = one, a single one — the unity and uniqueness of the Lord. Not merely monotheism (one among possible many) but the covenantal declaration of the Lord's sole claim on Israel's allegiance. Mark 12:29 quotes the *Shema* as the first commandment — *kyrios ho theos hēmōn kyrios heis estin*. Paul applies the Shema christologically in 1 Corinthians 8:6 — "for us there is one God the Father... and one Lord Jesus Christ" — distributing the Shema formula across Father and Son.

***agapēseis*** = you will love — the love command is future indicative — a command framed as certainty. The three-fold objects: *kardia* = heart (the inner decision-center), *psychē* = soul (the animating life-principle), *dynamis* = strength (the outward power and resources). Mark adds *dianoia* = mind. The total-person vocabulary — heart/soul/strength/mind — is the Shema's own way of saying: every dimension of human existence is included in the love-obligation.

Matthew 22:37–38 cites this as the greatest commandment. The engine preserved the exact LXX phrasing — *ex holēs tēs kardias sou kai ex holēs tēs psychēs sou kai ex holēs tēs dynameōs sou* = with all your heart and with all your soul and with all your strength.

### 8:3 — *ouk ep' artō monō zēsetai ho anthrōpos* / man will not live by bread alone

"He humbled you and made you hunger and fed you the manna which you did not know — so that he might make you know that man does not live by bread alone but by every word that comes out of the mouth of God man will live."

***ouk ep' artō monō*** = not by bread alone — *artos* = bread, the staple food. The manna episode is reread as a theological lesson: physical sustenance is not the ultimate ground of life — the *rhēma* = word that comes from the mouth of God sustains life at a deeper level than bread.

Matthew 4:4 and Luke 4:4 quote this verbatim as Jesus' response to the first temptation in the desolate-place. The irony is precise: Israel failed the desolate-place test of bread/word dependence; Jesus succeeds in the same test. The engine preserved "by every word that comes out of the mouth of God" — *all' epi panti rhēmati ekporeuomenō dia stomatos theou* — at full precision.

### 10:16 — *peritemeisthe tēn sklērokardian hymōn* / circumcise the hardness of your heart

"And you will circumcise the foreskin of your heart — and you will not stiffen your neck any more."

***peritemnō tēn sklērokardian*** = circumcise the hardness-of-heart — the circumcision command applied to the interior. The physical circumcision (Genesis 17) pointed to something deeper — the cutting away of the resistant inner disposition. Jeremiah 4:4 and 9:25 use the same inner-circumcision demand. Romans 2:29 — "circumcision is of the heart — in breath-of-life not in letter" — *peritomē kardias en pneumati* — draws directly on this Deuteronomy 10:16 vocabulary. The engine rendered "circumcise the foreskin of your heart" — not the weakened "change your heart" — preserving the surgical-removal metaphor.

### 17:15–20 — *ho nomos tou basileōs* / the law of the king

The king law establishes a constitutional monarchy — the king is under the law, not above it. Three prohibitions: (1) multiply horses — no military buildup (2) multiply wives — no diplomatic harem (3) multiply silver and gold — no personal enrichment. The king must write for himself a copy of the law and read it all his days. Every subsequent evaluation of Israelite kings in Samuel-Kings applies this Deuteronomy 17 template: Solomon violates all three (1 Kings 10–11).

The law of the king is the theological ground of prophetic critique of monarchy — the prophets hold kings accountable to the Deuteronomy 17 covenant standard. The engine preserved "copy of this law" for *deuteronomion* — the term from which the LXX title of the entire book derives.

### 18:15–19 — *prophētēn... hōs eme* / a prophet like me — the Moses typology

"A prophet from your midst — from your brothers — like me — the Lord your God will raise up for you. To him you will hear."

***prophētēn... hōs eme anastēsei*** = a prophet like me he will raise up — the singular prophet promised as Moses' eschatological successor. The three characteristics of the prophet-like-Moses: (1) from among their brothers — an Israelite (2) like me — the Moses-pattern of direct divine speech (3) I will put my words in his mouth — verbal inspiration.

Acts 3:22 — Peter quotes this at Pentecost: "Moses said: The Lord God will raise up for you a prophet from your brothers as he raised up me." Acts 7:37 — Stephen quotes it in his defense. John 1:21 and 1:25 — the crowd asks John the Baptist if he is "the prophet" — the definite article pointing to this specific Deuteronomy 18 figure. John 6:14 — after the feeding of the five thousand: "this is truly the prophet who is coming into the world." The engine preserved "like me" (*hōs eme*) — the Moses-typology marker — and "to him you will hear" (*autou akousesthe*) as the obedience-obligation formula.

### 21:22–23 — *epikataratos hypo theou pas kremamenos epi xylou* / cursed by God is everyone hanging on a tree

"If a man has committed a sin worthy of death — and he is put to death — and you hang him on a tree — his body will not remain overnight on the tree — but you will certainly bury him on that day — for a cursed of God is the one hanging."

***epikataratos hypo theou*** = cursed by God — *epikataratos* = accursed, under the divine curse. *Kremamenos epi xylou* = the one hanging on a tree/wood. Galatians 3:13 quotes this directly: "Christ ransomed-released us from the curse of the law — having become a curse for us — for it is written: cursed is everyone who hangs on a tree." The cross as *xylon* = tree/wood fires on this Deuteronomy 21:23 vocabulary in Acts 5:30, 10:39, 13:29, Galatians 3:13, and 1 Peter 2:24. The engine rendered "cursed of God is the one hanging" — preserving the *epikataratos hypo theou* formula.

### 30:6 — *peritomēn tēs kardias sou* / circumcision of your heart — new covenant promise

"And the Lord your God will circumcise your heart and the heart of your seed — to love the Lord your God with all your heart and with all your soul — so that you may live."

This is the new covenant promise embedded in the covenant curse section — the divine act that Deuteronomy 10:16 commanded Israel to perform on themselves the Lord promises to perform on them. The shift from imperative (10:16 — *you* circumcise) to promise (30:6 — *he* will circumcise) is the heart of new covenant theology in Deuteronomy. Jeremiah 31:31–34 and Ezekiel 36:26–27 develop this promise. Romans 2:29 — "circumcision of the heart in breath-of-life" — *peritomē kardias en pneumati* — draws on this promise. The engine preserved the divine-subject construction — "the Lord your God *will* circumcise" — not the passive "your heart will be circumcised."

### 30:14 — *engys sou estin to rhēma* / the word is very near you

"But the word is very near to you — in your mouth and in your heart — to do it."

Romans 10:6–8 quotes this passage in its christological application: "the righteousness from trust says thus: Do not say in your heart... but what does it say? The word is near you — in your mouth and in your heart — that is the word of trust which we proclaim." Paul applies the Deuteronomy 30 nearness-of-the-word to the gospel's accessibility — the *rhēma* = word that is near is the word of trust in Christ. The engine preserved "the word is very near to you — in your mouth and in your heart" at full precision.

### 32:21 — *en ouk ethnei* / with what is not a people

"I will provoke them to jealousy with what is not a people — with a foolish nation I will provoke them to wrath."

Romans 10:19 quotes this in Paul's argument about Gentile inclusion: "Moses says: I will provoke you to jealousy with what is not a nation — with a foolish nation I will anger you." The Song of Moses' threat becomes the hermeneutical key for understanding Gentile salvation as the divine means of provoking Israel to jealousy. The engine rendered "what is not a people" for *ouk ethnos* — preserving the negation that Paul's argument requires.

### 32:35 — *emoi ekdikēsis* / vengeance is mine

"Vengeance is mine and recompense — for the time when their foot slips."

Romans 12:19 — "vengeance is mine — I will repay — says the Lord" — quotes this directly in the context of non-retaliation. Hebrews 10:30 also quotes it as a warning of divine judgment. The divine monopoly on vengeance (*ekdikēsis* = the exacting of justice, the right of retribution) is the ground of both the prohibition on human retaliation and the certainty of eschatological judgment. The engine rendered "vengeance is mine and recompense" preserving both *ekdikēsis* and *antapodosis* = repayment.

### 32:43 — *euphranēte ethnē meta tou laou autou* / rejoice O nations with his people

"Rejoice O nations with his people — for he will avenge the blood of his servants."

Romans 15:10 quotes this in Paul's catena of OT texts supporting Gentile inclusion in worship: "Rejoice O nations with his people." The Song of Moses' concluding call for the nations to rejoice together with Israel becomes a proof-text for the unified Jew-Gentile congregation. The LXX expands the MT here — the *ethnē* = nations rejoicing *with* (*meta*) his people is the LXX reading that Paul quotes, not the MT. The engine followed the LXX reading.

## BLOCK 1 — CHAPTERS 1–11

### 1:31 — *hōs ei tis tropophoresē anthrōpos ton hyion autou* / as a man carries his son

Moses re-reads the wilderness as divine fathering: "the Lord your God carried you as a man carries his son — all the way that you went." *Tropophoreō* = to carry, to bear with — from *tropos* = manner + *phoreō* = to carry. The fatherhood-of-God as the interpretive frame for the wilderness. Hosea 11:1 — "out of Egypt I called my son" — fires on the same father-son wilderness-carrying theology.

### 4:24 — *pyr katanaliskōn* / consuming fire — *theos zēlōtēs* / jealous God

"For the Lord your God is a consuming fire — a jealous God." Hebrews 12:29 quotes this directly: "our God is a consuming fire" — *kai gar ho theos hēmōn pyr katanaliskōn* — in the context of the new Sinai, the unshakeable kingdom. The *pyr katanaliskōn* = consuming fire vocabulary establishes divine holiness as the ground of both judgment and new covenant awe. The engine rendered "consuming fire" and "jealous God" — preserving *zēlōtēs* as jealous rather than the softened "passionate."

### 4:35 — *kyrios ho theos sou theos estin kai ouk estin eti plēn autou* / the Lord is God and there is none beside him

The Sinai theophany's theological conclusion — monotheistic exclusivity grounded in the fire-speaking event. 4:39 repeats it: "the Lord — he is God in heaven above and on the earth below — there is none other." The engine preserved the double affirmation at both verses — the is-God statement and the none-other statement.

### 5:17 — *ou phoneuseis* / you will not murder

The LXX renders the sixth commandment with *phoneuō* = to murder — the deliberate taking of life — not *apokteinō* = to kill (which is the general term covering judicial execution, warfare, and accident). Thayer: *phoneuō* = to commit murder, to slay. The distinction between *phoneuō* (murder) and *apokteinō* (kill) is maintained throughout the LXX legal corpus. The engine rendered "you will not murder" — not "you will not kill" — preserving the *phoneuō* precision.

### 6:6–9 — *tefillin* / frontlets vocabulary

The *Shema* commands four embodied applications: (1) upon the heart — *en tē kardia sou* (2) taught to sons — *didaxeis autous* (3) bound as a sign on the hand and frontlets between the eyes — *phyleion* = phylactery, from *phylassō* = to guard. (4) written on doorposts — *mezuzah*. The *phyleion* vocabulary from *phylassō* = to guard is the LXX's own interpretation of the *ṭôṭāphôt* = frontlets — these are guard-objects, protection-markers. Matthew 23:5 criticizes those who "make their *phylaktēria* wide" — the LXX vocabulary carried into the NT. The engine rendered "frontlets" for *phyleion* — not "phylacteries" (which is a transliteration) — to make the meaning accessible.

### 7:6 — *laos periousios* / people for his own possession

"For you are a holy people to the Lord your God — the Lord your God chose you to be a people for his own possession." *Periousios* = peculiar, special, one's own — from *peri* + *ousia* = around/beyond substance — the people constituted as the Lord's special property. LSJ: belonging to one peculiarly, one's very own. Titus 2:14 — "who gave himself for us to ransom-release us from all lawlessness and purify for himself a people for his own possession" — *laon periousion* — draws directly on this Deuteronomy 7:6 vocabulary. 1 Peter 2:9 — "a people for a possession" — *laos eis peripoiēsin* — fires on the same vocabulary field. The engine rendered "people for his own possession" — not "treasured people" or "special people" — preserving the ownership register.

## BLOCK 2 — CHAPTERS 12–18

### 12:5 — *ho topos hon ean eklexētai kyrios* / the place which the Lord will choose

The worship-centralization formula fires seventeen times in Deuteronomy — "the place which the Lord your God will choose to cause his name to dwell there." *Kataskēnoō* = to tabernacle, to cause to dwell — the same verb as John 1:14. The divine name's dwelling in the chosen place is the Deuteronomic theology of presence — the Lord is present at the place of his name. The Second Temple tradition understood this as Jerusalem/Zion. John 4:21–23 (worship neither on this mountain nor in Jerusalem) and Acts 7:48 (the Most High does not dwell in houses made by hands) both engage this Deuteronomy 12 theology.

### 15:11 — *ou gar mē eklipē chreia apo tēs gēs* / the poor will never cease from the land

"For the poor will never cease from the land — therefore I command you: you will certainly open your hand to your brother." Jesus quotes this in Mark 14:7 — "the poor you always have with you" — not as an excuse for neglect but as the ground of perpetual obligation. The Deuteronomy 15 context is the release-year system that acknowledges poverty's persistence while demanding persistent generosity. The engine preserved "the poor will never cease from the land" — the perpetual-poverty reality that grounds the perpetual-generosity command.

## BLOCK 3 — CHAPTERS 19–26

### 21:23 — Full treatment in CAE section above.

### 24:1 — *biblion apostasion* / scroll of divorce

"He writes her a scroll of divorce" — *biblion apostasion* = a book/scroll of sending-away, of separation — from *apostasion* = a departing, a divorce certificate. This is the text Matthew 19:7 and Mark 10:4 cite when the Pharisees ask Jesus about divorce: "Why then did Moses command to give a *biblion apostasion*?" Jesus' response engages the Deuteronomy 24 permission as a concession to hardness of heart, not the creation ordinance. The engine rendered "scroll of divorce" for *biblion apostasion*.

### 25:4 — *ou phimōseis boun aloōnta* / you will not muzzle an ox threshing

"You will not muzzle an ox when it treads out grain." 1 Corinthians 9:9 and 1 Timothy 5:18 quote this in the context of apostolic financial support: "you will not muzzle an ox threshing — is it about oxen that God is concerned? Or does he say it for our sake?" Paul's midrashic application is not a denial of the literal meaning but an extension — the principle that those who do productive work deserve to benefit from it applies to apostolic ministry. The engine rendered "muzzle" for *phimoō* — the same verb as Matthew 22:12 (the speechless guest).

### 26:5–9 — *Aramean wandering* — covenant confessional

The firstfruits declaration is the most condensed theological summary in the Torah — the entire salvation history compressed into the basket-presentation formula. Key vocabulary:

***Arameios apobainōn*** = a wandering/perishing Aramean — *apoballō* = to throw away, to lose. The LXX renders the ambiguous Hebrew *'ōbēd* as *apobainōn* = going away/departing rather than the more common "perishing" — Jacob as the one who went away to Aram rather than the one dying there. The engine rendered "wandering Aramean" as the established liturgical phrase while noting the *apobainōn* departure vocabulary.

The declaration moves: individual patriarch → family in Egypt → oppression → cry → Lord hears → leads out → brings in. This is the theological shape of the entire Pentateuch compressed into six verses. Every element of the confession is echoed across Exodus-Deuteronomy. The engine rendered the declaration as elevated prose-poetry — slightly indented — to signal its liturgical register.

## BLOCK 4 — CHAPTERS 27–30

### 27:26 — *epikataratos pas hos ouk emmenei* / cursed is everyone who does not abide

"Cursed is the one who does not establish the words of this law to do them — and all the people will say: Amen."

Galatians 3:10 quotes this as the ground of the law's curse: "as many as are from works of law are under a curse — for it is written: cursed is everyone who does not abide in all things written in the book of the law to do them." The *epikataratos* = accursed formula of Deuteronomy 27 is the basis of Paul's argument that the law itself pronounces a curse on all who do not perfectly fulfill it — which is the setup for the Galatians 3:13 Christ-as-curse-bearer argument. The engine rendered "cursed is the one who does not establish" — preserving *epikataratos* throughout the curse formulae.

### 28:1–68 — Covenant curses — escalation structure

Chapter 28 is the longest single chapter in Deuteronomy. The structure: brief blessings (vv.1–14) → extended curses (vv.15–68) — the asymmetry is intentional. The curses are elaborated at five times the length of the blessings — covenant violation produces consequences that exceed covenant obedience in narrative weight.

The curse trajectory: disease → military defeat → agricultural failure → exile to Egypt by ships (the reversal of the Exodus — 28:68 is the covenant's most devastating closing image). The engine rendered the full curse sequence without abbreviation — the escalating specificity (the tender woman eating her afterbirth — 28:57, the man's eye evil against his own children — 28:54) is the text's own covenant-consequence vocabulary.

**28:47–48** — the hinge: "Because you did not serve the Lord your God with joyfulness and gladness of heart — because of the abundance of all things — you will serve your enemies." The absence of joyful service in abundance produces servitude in deprivation. The engine preserved *meta euphrosynēs kai agathēs kardias* = with joyfulness and goodness of heart — not the weakened "gladly."

### 29:29 — *ta krypta kyriō tō theō* / the hidden things belong to the Lord

"The hidden things belong to the Lord our God — but the revealed things belong to us and to our sons forever — to do all the words of this law." The *krypta* = hidden things / *apokekalymena* = revealed things binary is the epistemological boundary of covenant obligation: Israel is responsible for the revealed law, not for what the Lord has not disclosed. This verse is the hermeneutical limit-marker of Deuteronomic theology. The engine preserved the exact binary — "hidden things" / "revealed things."

### 30:1–10 — *epistraphēti* / return — covenant restoration promise

The verb *epistrephō* = to turn, to return — fires seven times in 30:1–10 as the covenant-return vocabulary. The return (*teshuvah* in Hebrew) to the Lord is the condition of the restoration promise. The engine rendered *epistrephō* consistently as "return" — not "repent" — to preserve the directional-movement vocabulary: returning to the Lord rather than a generalized change of moral attitude.

## BLOCK 5 — CHAPTERS 31–34

### 32:1–43 — *Ōdē Mōyseōs* / Song of Moses

The Song of Moses is the highest poetic composition in Deuteronomy — designated as a *martyria* = witness against Israel (31:19, 31:21). The song's structure:

(1) Creation-call witnesses (v.1) — heaven and earth summoned  
(2) Divine character declaration (vv.3–4) — the Rock, faithful, just  
(3) Israel's ingratitude (vv.5–6) — corrupt, foolish  
(4) Divine election history (vv.7–14) — finding Israel in the desolate-place, eagle imagery  
(5) Israel's apostasy (vv.15–18) — Jeshurun grown fat, sacrificing to demons  
(6) Divine judgment declared (vv.19–25) — hiding of face, military defeat  
(7) Divine restraint — not-for-their-sake (vv.26–33)  
(8) Divine vindication and vengeance (vv.34–43) — vengeance mine, nations rejoice

Key vocabulary decisions:

***Petra*** = Rock — the divine epithet fires seven times in the Song (vv.4, 13, 15, 18, 30, 31 ×2). *Petra* = a solid rock, a cliff, a massive stone — not *lithos* = a stone. 1 Corinthians 10:4 — "and the rock was Christ" — *hē petra de ēn ho christos* — the Pauline typological reading of the wilderness Rock. The engine rendered "Rock" consistently as a divine title — capitalized as an epithet.

***Daimoniois*** = demons — "they sacrificed to demons — not God" (v.17) — *daimoniois* = to demon-powers, divine beings of inferior status. 1 Corinthians 10:20 quotes this directly: "the things which the nations sacrifice — they sacrifice to demons and not to God" — *daimoniois kai ou theō thyousin*. The engine rendered "demons" — not "false gods" or "idols" — preserving the *daimoniois* vocabulary.

***kata arithmon angelōn theou*** = according to the number of the sons of God (v.8) — the LXX reading differs from the MT here: MT has "according to the number of the sons of Israel" but the LXX and Dead Sea Scrolls read "sons of God" — divine beings assigned as patron-deities of the nations while Israel is the Lord's direct portion. This reading underlies the divine-council theology of Psalm 82 and Daniel 10. The engine followed the LXX reading — "according to the number of the sons of God."

**32:39 — *ide ide hoti egō eimi*** = see now that I — I am he — the *egō eimi* formula in the Song of Moses. Isaiah 43:10–11 fires on this same formula: "so that you know and believe and understand that I am he." Jesus' *egō eimi* declarations in John draw from both this Deuteronomy 32:39 and the Isaiah 43 echoes. The engine preserved "I — I am he" — the double-pronoun emphasis of the LXX — not the flattened "I am the one."

### 33:1–29 — *Eulogiai Mōyseōs* / Blessing of Moses

The Blessing of Moses is structured as a tribal oracle sequence — each tribe receives a blessing-declaration. The frame (vv.2–5 and vv.26–29) establishes the Lord as the divine warrior-king who rides the heavens for Israel's help. Key vocabulary:

**33:27 — *kataphygē ho theos* / God is a dwelling-place** — "The God of old is a dwelling-place — and underneath are the everlasting arms." *Kataphygē* = a refuge, a place of flight-to-safety — from *katapheugo* = to flee for refuge. The engine rendered "dwelling-place" for *kataphygē* — the refuge in which one takes shelter.

***Brachiones aiōnioi*** = everlasting arms — the divine arms as the sustaining floor of existence. The everlasting-arms image has no direct NT citation but enters the vocabulary of divine sustenance throughout the tradition.

**33:29 — *makaristos sy Israēl* / blessed are you O Israel** — the closing beatitude of the Blessing. *Makaristos* = most blessed, deeply happy — the superlative beatitude-formula. The *makarios* vocabulary of the Beatitudes (Matthew 5:3–12) fires on this same *makaristos* register.

### 34:5 — *dia rhēmatos kyriou* / by the word of the Lord — Moses' death

"And Moses the servant of God died there in the land of Moab — by the word of the Lord." *Dia rhēmatos kyriou* = by the word of the Lord — the exact phrase by which Moses died. The rabbinic tradition reads this as "by the kiss of God" — the divine mouth that gave Moses his words at Sinai was also the instrument of his death. The engine rendered "by the word of the Lord" — the LXX's literal *dia rhēmatos* — preserving the divine-word agency.

### 34:9 — *pneumatos synesis* / breath-of-life of wisdom — Joshua's succession

"And Joshua the son of Nun was full of the breath-of-life of wisdom — for Moses had laid his hands upon him." *Pneuma synesis* = breath-of-life of wisdom/understanding — the same governance-pneuma chain from Numbers 27:18 (*pneuma en autō* = breath-of-life in him). The laying on of hands (*epithesis tōn cheirōn*) transfers the pneuma for leadership. Acts 6:6 uses the same *epithesis cheirōn* for the appointment of the seven — the Numbers 27/Deuteronomy 34 succession pattern as the NT appointment template. The engine rendered "breath-of-life of wisdom" — not "spirit of wisdom" — preserving the *pneuma* chain.

### 34:10 — *prophētēn... hōs Mōysēs* / a prophet like Moses — final evaluation

"And there arose no more a prophet in Israel like Moses — whom the Lord knew face to face." The epitaph closes the Torah with the Moses-uniqueness declaration — the prophet-like-Moses of 18:15 has not yet appeared. The *prosopon kata prosopon* = face to face of Moses' prophetic mode (12:8 — "not as with other prophets — face to face I speak with him") is the standard by which no subsequent prophet has been measured.

This closing declaration sets up the entire prophetic canon as anticipatory — every prophet from Joshua to Malachi is measured against the Moses-standard and found lesser. The prophet-like-Moses remains unfulfilled at the Torah's close. The NT's claim is that Jesus is the fulfillment — the prophet who speaks face-to-face with the Father as Moses did, and more: the Son who is in the bosom of the Father (John 1:18 — *ho ōn eis ton kolpon tou patros*).

## INSTITUTIONAL VOCABULARY — DEUTERONOMY FREQUENCY ANALYSIS

### *Agapaō* — love — 23 occurrences

Deuteronomy has the highest concentration of *agapaō* = covenant-love in the Torah. The love command fires in three directions: (1) Israel's love for the Lord (6:5, 10:12, 11:1, 11:13, 11:22, 13:3, 19:9, 30:6, 30:16, 30:20) (2) The Lord's love for Israel (4:37, 7:8, 7:13, 10:15, 23:5) (3) Love for the sojourner (10:19). The engine rendered *agapaō* as "love" throughout — preserving the covenantal-love register.

### *Diathēkē* — covenant — 27 occurrences

Deuteronomy's covenant vocabulary is the most developed in the Pentateuch. *Diathēkē* = covenant — the structured relationship between parties with defined obligations and consequences. The engine rendered *diathēkē* as "covenant" throughout — not "contract" or "agreement" — preserving the binding-relationship register.

### *Rhēma* / *logos* — word — 38 occurrences

The word-theology of Deuteronomy: the Lord's word commands (6:6), tests (8:3), is near (30:14), does not return empty (Isaiah 55:11). The engine rendered *rhēma* as "word" throughout — tracking the nearness-of-the-word chain through to Romans 10:8.

### *Zōē* / *zaō* — life — 28 occurrences

The life-and-death binary of 30:15–20 is the culmination of the book's life-vocabulary chain. The commandments are not abstract obligations but the way of life — "for it is your life" (32:47). The engine rendered *zōē* as "life" and the verb *zaō* as "live" throughout.

## DRIFT ELIMINATION SUMMARY — DEUTERONOMY

**"Spirit" for *pneuma* at 34:9** — "Joshua was full of the spirit of wisdom" faces pressure toward Trinitarian capitalization and Spirit-of-God identification. The engine rendered "breath-of-life of wisdom" — the governance-pneuma chain from Numbers 27:18 through Deuteronomy 34:9 is a *pneuma* distribution for leadership function, continuous with the seventy-elders pattern.

**"Love" weakening for *agapaō*** — Modern translations sometimes render *agapaō* in Deuteronomy as "devotion" or "commitment" to soften the demand. The engine preserved "love" — *agapaō* is the covenantal choice-love that encompasses both affection and obligation.

**"Kill" for *phoneuō* at 5:17** — The sixth commandment faces persistent pressure toward "kill" in English tradition. The engine rendered "murder" — *phoneuō* is the deliberate-killing term, not the general killing term.

**"Special/treasured" for *periousios*** — The *laos periousios* of 7:6 and 14:2 is commonly rendered "treasured possession" or "special people" — both of which miss the *peri-ousia* = around/beyond-substance = ownership register. The engine rendered "people for his own possession."

The engine rendered "return" — preserving the directional-movement toward the Lord rather than a generic interior moral change.

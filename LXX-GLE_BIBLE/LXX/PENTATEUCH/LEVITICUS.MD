\v 1 And the Lord called Moses and spoke to him from the tent of meeting saying:
\v 2 Speak to the sons of Israel and say to them: A man from among you who brings a gift to the Lord — from the cattle and from the sheep you will bring your gift.
\v 3 If his gift is a whole-burnt offering from the cattle — a male without blemish he will bring it — to the door of the tent of meeting he will bring it — acceptable before the Lord.
\v 4 And he will lay his hand upon the head of the whole-burnt offering — and it will be acceptable for him to make atonement for him.
\v 5 And he will slaughter the bull before the Lord — and the sons of Aaron the priests will bring the blood and sprinkle the blood upon the altar round about — which is at the door of the tent of meeting.
\v 6 And he will flay the whole-burnt offering and divide it into its pieces.
\v 7 And the sons of Aaron the priest will put fire upon the altar and arrange wood upon the fire.
\v 8 And the sons of Aaron the priests will arrange the pieces — the head and the fat — upon the wood that is on the fire upon the altar.
\v 9 But its inner parts and its legs he will wash with water — and the priest will burn all of it upon the altar — a whole-burnt offering — a fire-offering — a pleasing aroma to the Lord.
\p
\v 10 And if his gift is a whole-burnt offering from the flock — from the sheep or from the goats — a male without blemish he will bring it.
\v 11 And he will slaughter it on the north side of the altar before the Lord — and the sons of Aaron the priests will sprinkle its blood upon the altar round about.
\v 12 And he will divide it into its pieces with its head and its fat — and the priest will arrange them upon the wood that is on the fire upon the altar.
\v 13 But the inner parts and the legs he will wash with water — and the priest will bring it all and burn it upon the altar — a whole-burnt offering — a fire-offering — a pleasing aroma to the Lord.
\p
\v 14 And if his gift to the Lord is a whole-burnt offering of birds — he will bring his gift from the turtledoves or from the young pigeons.
\v 15 And the priest will bring it to the altar and nip off its head and burn it on the altar — and its blood will be drained out on the side of the altar.
\v 16 And he will remove its crop with its contents and throw it beside the altar on the east side — to the place of the ashes.
\v 17 And he will split it by its wings but not divide it through — and the priest will burn it upon the altar upon the wood that is on the fire — a whole-burnt offering — a fire-offering — a pleasing aroma to the Lord.

\v 1 And when a soul brings a grain-offering to the Lord — his gift will be of fine flour — and he will pour oil upon it and put frankincense upon it.
\v 2 And he will bring it to the sons of Aaron the priests — and he will take from it a handful of its fine flour and from its oil together with all its frankincense — and the priest will burn its memorial-portion upon the altar — a fire-offering — a pleasing aroma to the Lord.
\v 3 And what remains of the grain-offering belongs to Aaron and his sons — most holy from the fire-offerings of the Lord.
\p
\v 4 And when you bring a grain-offering baked in the oven — unleavened round-cakes of fine flour mixed with oil or unleavened wafers anointed with oil.
\v 5 And if your gift is a grain-offering from the griddle — it will be of fine flour unleavened mixed with oil.
\v 6 You will break it in pieces and pour oil upon it — it is a grain-offering.
\v 7 And if your gift is a grain-offering from the pan — of fine flour with oil it will be made.
\v 8 And you will bring the grain-offering which is made from these to the Lord — and it will be presented to the priest and he will bring it to the altar.
\v 9 And the priest will lift from the grain-offering its memorial-portion and burn it upon the altar — a fire-offering — a pleasing aroma to the Lord.
\v 10 And what remains of the grain-offering belongs to Aaron and his sons — most holy from the fire-offerings of the Lord.
\p
\v 11 No grain-offering which you bring to the Lord will be made with leaven — for you will burn no leaven and no honey as a fire-offering to the Lord.
\v 12 As a firstfruits-offering you may bring them to the Lord — but they will not go up upon the altar as a pleasing aroma.
\v 13 And every gift of your grain-offering you will season with salt — you will not let the salt of the covenant of your God be lacking from your grain-offering. Upon all your gifts you will offer salt.
\v 14 And if you bring a grain-offering of firstfruits to the Lord — crushed grain roasted with fire — groats of fresh grain — you will bring the grain-offering of your firstfruits.
\v 15 And you will put oil upon it and lay frankincense upon it — it is a grain-offering.
\v 16 And the priest will burn its memorial-portion — from its crushed grain and from its oil together with all its frankincense — a fire-offering to the Lord.

\v 1 And if his gift is a peace-offering — if he brings it from the cattle — whether male or female — without blemish he will bring it before the Lord.
\v 2 And he will lay his hand upon the head of his gift and slaughter it at the door of the tent of meeting — and the sons of Aaron the priests will sprinkle the blood upon the altar round about.
\v 3 And he will bring from the peace-offering as a fire-offering to the Lord — the fat covering the inner parts and all the fat upon the inner parts —
\v 4 and the two kidneys and the fat upon them which is on the loins — and the lobe of the liver — he will remove it together with the kidneys.
\v 5 And the sons of Aaron will burn it upon the altar upon the whole-burnt offering which is upon the wood on the fire — a fire-offering — a pleasing aroma to the Lord.
\p
\v 6 And if his gift as a peace-offering to the Lord is from the flock — male or female — without blemish he will bring it.
\v 7 If he brings a lamb as his gift — he will bring it before the Lord.
\v 8 And he will lay his hand upon the head of his gift and slaughter it before the tent of meeting — and the sons of Aaron will sprinkle its blood upon the altar round about.
\v 9 And he will bring from the peace-offering as a fire-offering to the Lord — its fat — the whole fat tail which he will remove close to the backbone — and the fat covering the inner parts and all the fat upon the inner parts —
\v 10 and the two kidneys and the fat upon them which is on the loins — and the lobe of the liver — he will remove it together with the kidneys.
\v 11 And the priest will burn it upon the altar — food of a fire-offering to the Lord.
\p
\v 12 And if his gift is a goat — he will bring it before the Lord.
\v 13 And he will lay his hand upon its head and slaughter it before the tent of meeting — and the sons of Aaron will sprinkle its blood upon the altar round about.
\v 14 And he will bring from it his gift — a fire-offering to the Lord — the fat covering the inner parts and all the fat upon the inner parts —
\v 15 and the two kidneys and the fat upon them which is on the loins — and the lobe of the liver — he will remove it together with the kidneys.
\v 16 And the priest will burn them upon the altar — food of a fire-offering — a pleasing aroma — all the fat belongs to the Lord.
\v 17 A perpetual statute into your generations in all your dwelling-places — you will eat no fat and no blood.

\v 1 And the Lord spoke to Moses saying:
\v 2 Speak to the sons of Israel saying: If a soul sins unintentionally against any of the commandments of the Lord regarding things which ought not to be done — and does one of them —
\v 3 if the anointed priest sins so as to bring guilt upon the people — he will bring for his sin which he sinned a young bull without blemish to the Lord — for a sin-offering.
\v 4 And he will bring the bull to the door of the tent of meeting before the Lord and lay his hand upon the head of the bull — and slaughter the bull before the Lord.
\v 5 And the anointed priest will take from the blood of the bull and bring it to the tent of meeting.
\v 6 And the priest will dip his finger in the blood and sprinkle from the blood seven times before the Lord — before the veil of the sanctuary.
\v 7 And the priest will put some of the blood on the horns of the altar of fragrant incense before the Lord — which is in the tent of meeting — and all the remaining blood of the bull he will pour out at the base of the altar of whole-burnt offering which is at the door of the tent of meeting.
\v 8 And all the fat of the bull of the sin-offering he will remove from it — the fat covering the inner parts and all the fat upon the inner parts —
\v 9 and the two kidneys and the fat upon them which is on the loins — and the lobe of the liver — he will remove it together with the kidneys —
\v 10 as it is removed from the bull of the peace-offering — and the priest will burn them upon the altar of whole-burnt offering.
\v 11 But the skin of the bull and all its flesh — with its head and with its legs and its inner parts and its dung —
\v 12 the whole bull he will carry outside the camp to a clean place — to the place where the ashes are poured out — and he will burn it on wood with fire — at the place where the ashes are poured out it will be burned.
\p
\v 13 And if the whole gathering of Israel sins unintentionally and the matter is hidden from the eyes of the gathering — and they do one of all the commandments of the Lord regarding things which ought not to be done — and they are guilty —
\v 14 when the sin which they sinned against it becomes known — the gathering will bring a young bull for the sin — and bring it before the tent of meeting.
\v 15 And the elders of the gathering will lay their hands upon the head of the bull before the Lord — and slaughter the bull before the Lord.
\v 16 And the anointed priest will bring some of the blood of the bull to the tent of meeting —
\v 17 and the priest will dip his finger in some of the blood and sprinkle seven times before the Lord before the veil.
\v 18 And he will put some of the blood on the horns of the altar which is before the Lord — which is in the tent of meeting — and all the remaining blood he will pour out at the base of the altar of whole-burnt offering which is at the door of the tent of meeting.
\v 19 And all its fat he will remove from it and burn upon the altar.
\v 20 And he will do with the bull as he did with the bull of the sin-offering — thus he will do with it. And the priest will make atonement for them and it will be forgiven them.
\v 21 And he will carry the bull outside the camp and burn it as he burned the first bull — it is the sin-offering of the gathering.
\p
\v 22 When a ruler sins and does unintentionally one of all the commandments of the Lord his God regarding things which ought not to be done — and is guilty —
\v 23 or his sin which he sinned is made known to him — he will bring as his gift a male goat without blemish.
\v 24 And he will lay his hand upon the head of the goat and slaughter it in the place where he slaughters the whole-burnt offering before the Lord — it is a sin-offering.
\v 25 And the priest will take from the blood of the sin-offering with his finger and put it on the horns of the altar of whole-burnt offering — and pour out its remaining blood at the base of the altar of whole-burnt offering.
\v 26 And all its fat he will burn upon the altar like the fat of the peace-offering — and the priest will make atonement for him from his sin and it will be forgiven him.
\p
\v 27 And if a soul from the people of the land sins unintentionally by doing one of all the commandments of the Lord regarding things which ought not to be done — and is guilty —
\v 28 or his sin which he sinned is made known to him — he will bring as his gift a female goat without blemish for his sin which he sinned.
\v 29 And he will lay his hand upon the head of the sin-offering and slaughter the sin-offering in the place of the whole-burnt offering.
\v 30 And the priest will take from its blood with his finger and put it on the horns of the altar of whole-burnt offering — and all its remaining blood he will pour out at the base of the altar.
\v 31 And all its fat he will remove as the fat is removed from the peace-offering — and the priest will burn it upon the altar as a pleasing aroma to the Lord — and the priest will make atonement for him and it will be forgiven him.
\p
\v 32 And if he brings a lamb as his gift for a sin-offering — a female without blemish he will bring it.
\v 33 And he will lay his hand upon the head of the sin-offering and slaughter it as a sin-offering in the place where he slaughters the whole-burnt offering.
\v 34 And the priest will take from the blood of the sin-offering with his finger and put it on the horns of the altar of whole-burnt offering — and all its remaining blood he will pour out at the base of the altar.
\v 35 And all its fat he will remove as the fat of the lamb is removed from the peace-offering — and the priest will burn them upon the altar upon the fire-offerings of the Lord — and the priest will make atonement for him for his sin which he sinned and it will be forgiven him.

\v 1 And if a soul sins — and he hears the voice of an oath and he is a witness — whether he saw or knew — if he does not report it he will bear his lawless deed.
\v 2 Or a soul who touches any unclean thing — whether the carcass of an unclean wild-animal or the carcass of unclean cattle or the carcass of unclean swarming-things — and it is hidden from him — and he has become unclean and is guilty.
\v 3 Or if he touches the uncleanness of a man — for any uncleanness of his by which he becomes unclean — and it is hidden from him — and he comes to know it — and is guilty.
\v 4 Or a soul who swears rashly with his lips to do evil or to do good — for anything which a man swears rashly — and it is hidden from him — and he comes to know it — and is guilty regarding one of these things.
\v 5 And it will come to pass when he is guilty regarding one of these things — he will confess the sin which he sinned.
\v 6 And he will bring his guilt-offering to the Lord for his sin which he sinned — a female from the flock — a lamb or a goat — as a sin-offering. And the priest will make atonement for him from his sin.
\v 7 And if his hand cannot reach enough for a sheep — he will bring as his guilt-offering for his sin which he sinned two turtledoves or two young pigeons to the Lord — one for a sin-offering and one for a whole-burnt offering.
\v 8 And he will bring them to the priest — and he will bring the one which is for the sin-offering first and nip its head from its neck but not divide it through.
\v 9 And he will sprinkle from the blood of the sin-offering on the side of the altar — and the remaining blood will be drained out at the base of the altar — it is a sin-offering.
\v 10 And the second he will make a whole-burnt offering according to the ordinance — and the priest will make atonement for him from his sin which he sinned and it will be forgiven him.
\p
\v 11 But if his hand cannot reach enough for two turtledoves or two young pigeons — he will bring as his gift for his sin which he sinned a tenth of an ephah of fine flour as a sin-offering — he will not put oil upon it and he will not put frankincense upon it — for it is a sin-offering.
\v 12 And he will bring it to the priest — and the priest will take from it a handful as its memorial-portion and burn it upon the altar upon the fire-offerings of the Lord — it is a sin-offering.
\v 13 And the priest will make atonement for him from his sin which he sinned regarding one of these things and it will be forgiven him — and the remainder will belong to the priest — like the grain-offering.
\p
\v 14 And the Lord spoke to Moses saying:
\v 15 If a soul commits a trespass and sins unintentionally from among the holy things of the Lord — he will bring his guilt-offering to the Lord — a ram without blemish from the flock — at your valuation in silver shekels — according to the shekel of the sanctuary — for a guilt-offering.
\v 16 And he will make restitution for what he sinned from the holy thing and add its fifth to it and give it to the priest — and the priest will make atonement for him with the ram of the guilt-offering and it will be forgiven him.
\p
\v 17 And if a soul sins and does one of all the commandments of the Lord regarding things which ought not to be done — and did not know — and is guilty — and bears his lawless deed —
\v 18 he will bring a ram without blemish from the flock at your valuation as a guilt-offering to the priest — and the priest will make atonement for him for his error which he committed unintentionally without knowing it — and it will be forgiven him.
\v 19 It is a guilt-offering — he has certainly incurred guilt before the Lord.

\v 1 And the Lord spoke to Moses saying:
\v 2 If a soul sins and commits a trespass against the Lord — and lies to his neighbor about a deposit or a pledge or a robbery — or has oppressed his neighbor —
\v 3 or has found a lost thing and lied about it — and swears falsely — in any one of all these things which a man may do and sin in them —
\v 4 it will come to pass when he sins and is guilty — he will return the thing which he seized or the thing which he took by oppression or the deposit which was deposited with him or the lost thing which he found —
\v 5 or anything about which he swore falsely — he will repay it in full and add its fifth to it — he will give it to whoever it belongs to on the day of his guilt-offering.
\v 6 And his guilt-offering to the Lord — a ram without blemish from the flock at your valuation — for a guilt-offering — to the priest.
\v 7 And the priest will make atonement for him before the Lord and it will be forgiven him for any one of all the things which he did and incurred guilt thereby.
\p
\v 8 And the Lord spoke to Moses saying:
\v 9 Command Aaron and his sons saying: This is the law of the whole-burnt offering. The whole-burnt offering itself will remain on the hearth upon the altar all night until morning — and the fire of the altar will be kept burning upon it.
\v 10 And the priest will put on his linen garment and put his linen undergarments on his body — and take up the ashes of what the fire has consumed of the whole-burnt offering upon the altar — and put them beside the altar.
\v 11 And he will take off his garments and put on other garments — and carry the ashes outside the camp to a clean place.
\v 12 And the fire on the altar will be kept burning — it will not go out. And the priest will burn wood upon it every morning — and arrange the whole-burnt offering upon it — and burn upon it the fat of the peace-offerings.
\v 13 Fire will be kept burning continually on the altar — it will not go out.
\p
\v 14 And this is the law of the grain-offering. The sons of Aaron will bring it before the Lord — to the front of the altar.
\v 15 And he will take from it a handful of the fine flour of the grain-offering and from its oil and all the frankincense upon the grain-offering — and burn it upon the altar as a pleasing aroma — its memorial-portion to the Lord.
\v 16 And what remains from it Aaron and his sons will eat — unleavened it will be eaten — in the court of the tent of meeting they will eat it.
\v 17 It will not be baked with leaven — I gave it as their portion from my fire-offerings — it is most holy like the sin-offering and like the guilt-offering.
\v 18 Every male among the sons of Aaron may eat it — a perpetual statute into your generations from the fire-offerings of the Lord. Everything that touches them will be sanctified.
\p
\v 19 And the Lord spoke to Moses saying:
\v 20 This is the gift of Aaron and of his sons which they will bring to the Lord on the day when he is anointed — a tenth of an ephah of fine flour as a perpetual grain-offering — half of it in the morning and half of it in the evening.
\v 21 It will be made on a griddle with oil — mixed you will bring it — the grain-offering of baked pieces — a pleasing aroma to the Lord.
\v 22 And the anointed priest who succeeds him from his sons will make it — a perpetual statute — it will be wholly burned to the Lord.
\v 23 And every grain-offering of a priest will be wholly burned — it will not be eaten.
\p
\v 24 And the Lord spoke to Moses saying:
\v 25 Speak to Aaron and to his sons saying: This is the law of the sin-offering. In the place where the whole-burnt offering is slaughtered — the sin-offering will be slaughtered before the Lord — it is most holy.
\v 26 The priest who offers it as a sin-offering will eat it — in the court of the tent of meeting he will eat it.
\v 27 Everything that touches its flesh will be sanctified — and when any of its blood is sprinkled on a garment — that on which it was sprinkled you will wash in the holy place.
\v 28 And the clay vessel in which it was boiled will be broken — but if it was boiled in a bronze vessel — it will be scoured and rinsed with water.
\v 29 Every male among the priests may eat it — it is most holy.
\v 30 But no sin-offering from which any blood was brought into the tent of meeting to make atonement in the holy place will be eaten — it will be burned with fire.

\v 1 And this is the law of the guilt-offering — it is most holy.
\v 2 In the place where the whole-burnt offering is slaughtered — the guilt-offering will be slaughtered — and its blood he will sprinkle upon the altar round about.
\v 3 And all its fat he will offer from it — the fat tail and the fat covering the inner parts —
\v 4 and the two kidneys and the fat upon them which is on the loins — and the lobe of the liver — he will remove it together with the kidneys.
\v 5 And the priest will burn them upon the altar — a fire-offering to the Lord — it is a guilt-offering.
\v 6 Every male among the priests may eat it — in the holy place it will be eaten — it is most holy.
\v 7 The guilt-offering is like the sin-offering — one law for them. The priest who makes atonement with it — his it will be.
\v 8 And the priest who offers a man's whole-burnt offering — the skin of the whole-burnt offering which he offered belongs to the priest — his it will be.
\v 9 And every grain-offering baked in the oven and everything made in a pan and on a griddle — to the priest who offers it it belongs — his it will be.
\v 10 And every grain-offering mixed with oil or dry — to all the sons of Aaron it will belong — each man equally.
\p
\v 11 And this is the law of the peace-offering which he will bring to the Lord.
\v 12 If he brings it as a thanksgiving — he will bring with the thanksgiving sacrifice unleavened round-cakes mixed with oil and unleavened wafers anointed with oil and fine flour of soaked round-cakes mixed with oil.
\v 13 With round-cakes of leavened bread he will bring his gift together with his thanksgiving peace-offering.
\v 14 And from it he will bring one from each gift as a contribution to the Lord — to the priest who sprinkles the blood of the peace-offerings it will belong.
\v 15 And the flesh of the thanksgiving peace-offering will be eaten on the day of his offering — he will not leave any of it until morning.
\p
\v 16 And if the sacrifice of his offering is a vow or a freewill-offering — on the day he brings his sacrifice it will be eaten — and on the next day the remainder of it may be eaten.
\v 17 But the remainder of the flesh of the sacrifice on the third day will be burned with fire.
\v 18 And if any of the flesh of his peace-offering is eaten on the third day — it will not be acceptable — it will not be reckoned to the one who brought it — it will be a foul thing — and the soul who eats from it will bear his lawless deed.
\p
\v 19 And flesh that touches any unclean thing will not be eaten — it will be burned with fire. And as for the clean flesh — every one who is clean may eat the flesh.
\v 20 But a soul who eats from the flesh of the peace-offering which belongs to the Lord while his uncleanness is upon him — that soul will be cut off from his people.
\v 21 And a soul who touches any unclean thing — the uncleanness of man or an unclean animal or any unclean abomination — and eats from the flesh of the peace-offering which belongs to the Lord — that soul will be cut off from his people.
\p
\v 22 And the Lord spoke to Moses saying:
\v 23 Speak to the sons of Israel saying: You will eat no fat — of ox or sheep or goat.
\v 24 And the fat of an animal that died and the fat of one torn by wild-animals may be used for any service — but you will certainly not eat it.
\v 25 For anyone who eats the fat of an animal of which one brings a fire-offering to the Lord — the soul who eats will be cut off from his people.
\v 26 And you will not eat any blood in any of your dwelling-places — whether of birds or of cattle.
\v 27 Any soul who eats any blood — that soul will be cut off from his people.
\p
\v 28 And the Lord spoke to Moses saying:
\v 29 Speak to the sons of Israel saying: The one who brings his peace-offering to the Lord will bring his gift to the Lord from his peace-offering.
\v 30 His own hands will bring the fire-offerings of the Lord — the fat upon the breast he will bring and the breast so that the breast may be waved as a wave-offering before the Lord.
\v 31 And the priest will burn the fat upon the altar — and the breast will belong to Aaron and to his sons.
\v 32 And the right thigh you will give as a contribution to the priest from your peace-offerings.
\v 33 The one among the sons of Aaron who offers the blood of the peace-offering and the fat — the right thigh will be his as a portion.
\v 34 For the wave-breast and the contribution-thigh I have taken from the sons of Israel from their peace-offerings and given them to Aaron the priest and to his sons as a perpetual statute from the sons of Israel.
\v 35 This is the anointing-portion of Aaron and the anointing-portion of his sons from the fire-offerings of the Lord — on the day he presented them to serve as priests to the Lord —
\v 36 which the Lord commanded to give to them on the day he anointed them from the sons of Israel — a perpetual statute into their generations.
\v 37 This is the law of the whole-burnt offering — of the grain-offering — of the sin-offering — of the guilt-offering — of the filling-offering — and of the peace-offering —
\v 38 which the Lord commanded Moses on Mount Sinai on the day he commanded the sons of Israel to bring their gifts to the Lord in the desolate-place of Sinai.

\v 1 And the Lord spoke to Moses saying:
\v 2 Take Aaron and his sons with him and the garments and the anointing oil and the bull of the sin-offering and the two rams and the basket of unleavened bread —
\v 3 and gather all the gathering to the door of the tent of meeting.
\v 4 And Moses did as the Lord commanded him — and the gathering was gathered to the door of the tent of meeting.
\v 5 And Moses said to the gathering: This is the word which the Lord commanded to do.
\p
\v 6 And Moses brought Aaron and his sons and washed them with water.
\v 7 And he put on him the tunic and bound him with the sash and clothed him with the full-length robe and put on him the ephod and bound him with the skillfully woven band of the ephod and fastened it to him.
\v 8 And he placed on him the breastplate and put in the breastplate the Urim and the Thummim.
\v 9 And he put the turban on his head and put on the turban at its front the golden plate — the holy crown — as the Lord commanded Moses.
\v 10 And Moses took the anointing oil and anointed the tabernacle and all that was in it and sanctified them.
\v 11 And he sprinkled from it on the altar seven times and anointed the altar and all its vessels and the laver and its base to sanctify them.
\v 12 And he poured from the anointing oil upon the head of Aaron and anointed him to sanctify him.
\v 13 And Moses brought the sons of Aaron and clothed them with tunics and bound them with sashes and bound turbans on them as the Lord commanded Moses.
\p
\v 14 And he brought the bull of the sin-offering — and Aaron and his sons laid their hands upon the head of the bull of the sin-offering.
\v 15 And he slaughtered it — and Moses took the blood and put it on the horns of the altar round about with his finger — and purified the altar — and poured out the blood at the base of the altar and sanctified it to make atonement upon it.
\v 16 And he took all the fat upon the inner parts and the lobe of the liver and the two kidneys and their fat — and Moses burned them upon the altar.
\v 17 But the bull and its skin and its flesh and its dung he burned with fire outside the camp — as the Lord commanded Moses.
\p
\v 18 And he brought the ram of the whole-burnt offering — and Aaron and his sons laid their hands upon the head of the ram.
\v 19 And he slaughtered it — and Moses sprinkled the blood upon the altar round about.
\v 20 And the ram he cut into its pieces — and Moses burned the head and the pieces and the fat.
\v 21 And the inner parts and the legs he washed with water — and Moses burned the whole ram upon the altar — a whole-burnt offering — a pleasing aroma — a fire-offering to the Lord — as the Lord commanded Moses.
\p
\v 22 And he brought the second ram — the ram of the filling — and Aaron and his sons laid their hands upon the head of the ram.
\v 23 And he slaughtered it — and Moses took from its blood and put it on the tip of the right ear of Aaron and on the thumb of his right hand and on the big toe of his right foot.
\v 24 And Moses brought the sons of Aaron and put from the blood on the tip of their right ear and on the thumb of their right hand and on the big toe of their right foot — and Moses sprinkled the blood upon the altar round about.
\v 25 And he took the fat and the fat tail and all the fat upon the inner parts and the lobe of the liver and the two kidneys and their fat and the right thigh —
\v 26 and from the basket of unleavened bread which was before the Lord he took one unleavened round-cake and one round-cake of bread with oil and one wafer — and placed them on the fat and on the right thigh.
\v 27 And he put all of them on the hands of Aaron and on the hands of his sons and waved them as a wave-offering before the Lord.
\v 28 And Moses took them from their hands and burned them on the altar upon the whole-burnt offering — as a filling-offering — a pleasing aroma — a fire-offering to the Lord.

## STRUCTURAL OVERVIEW

Leviticus is the theological engine-room of the Torah. Where Genesis establishes the covenant and Exodus establishes the covenant people and their sanctuary, Leviticus establishes the mechanics of approach — how a holy God and an unholy people maintain covenant proximity. The entire book is organized around a single problem: *how does the Lord dwell among them without consuming them?* The answer is the sacrificial and purification system. Every chapter is a sub-answer to this question.

The LXX title *Leuitikon* = the Levitical book — the book belonging to the Levites and their priestly service. The MT title *Wayyiqra* = And he called — from the opening word of 1:1. The LXX title is an editorial description; the MT title is the first word.

**Five major sections:**

1. **Sacrifice laws** (1–7) — the five offerings and their regulations
2. **Priestly consecration and inaugural fire** (8–10) — Aaron installed, Nadab and Abihu consumed
3. **Purity laws** (11–16) — clean/unclean, skin disease, discharges, Day of Atonement
4. **Holiness Code** (17–26) — blood prohibition, sexual ethics, neighbor-love, feasts, Jubilee, blessings and curses
5. **Vows and dedications** (27) — redemption valuations, tithes

## CAE CONTAMINATION NODES

### 16:2 — *hilastērion* / mercy-seat

"Let him not enter at all times into the holy place within the veil — before the mercy-seat which is upon the ark" — *hilastērion* = the mercy-seat, the propitiatory covering of the ark. This is the most concentrated use of *hilastērion* in the canon — the term appears in the Day of Atonement instructions at 16:2, 16:13, 16:14, 16:15.

The mercy-seat is the specific place where the High Priest sprinkles blood on Yom Kippur — blood on the front of the mercy-seat and seven times before it. This is the precise act that Romans 3:25 applies to Christ: God put him forward as a *hilastērion* through trust by his blood. The Leviticus 16 architecture is the direct referent of the Romans 3:25 *hilastērion* — not a general concept of propitiation but the specific Yom Kippur blood-application to the mercy-seat.

Hebrews 9:11–14 and 9:23–28 build their entire argument on the Leviticus 16 Day of Atonement pattern — the High Priest entering the holy of holies once a year as the type of Christ entering the true heavenly sanctuary once for all. Every term in Hebrews 9 — *hagion* (holy place), *katapetasma* (veil), *haima* (blood), *hilastērion* (mercy-seat), *ephapax* (once for all) — is drawn directly from Leviticus 16.

The engine rendered *hilastērion* as "mercy-seat" throughout — preserving the exact vocabulary chain to Romans 3:25 and Hebrews 9.

### 16:8–22 — *apopompaios* / the sending-away — the scapegoat architecture

The Day of Atonement involves two goats — one slaughtered as a sin-offering, one sent away alive into the desolate-place. The LXX uses *apopompaios* for the sent-away goat — from *apopempō* = to send away, to dismiss, to dispatch. The MT uses *la'aza'zel* — the meaning of which is disputed (proper name, place name, or descriptive — "for complete removal").

The LXX *apopompaios* = the one for sending-away — the goat that carries the confessed sins of Israel into the desolate-place, never to return. This is the removal-of-sin half of the atonement operation. The slaughtered goat deals with the guilt by blood; the sent-away goat deals with the sin by removal — *bearing away the lawless deeds* (16:22: *anoisei ho trigos eph' heautou pasas tas anomias*).

Both aspects are incorporated in Isaiah 53: the servant bears our lawless deeds (*anēnenken* = he bore up, he carried — 53:12 LXX) and is also slaughtered (*sphagēn ēchthē* = he was led to slaughter — 53:7). The Leviticus 16 two-goat system is split across Isaiah 53 and reunited in Christ — both the blood-shedding and the sin-bearing are his.

The engine rendered *apopompaios* as "sending-away" — not the traditional "scapegoat" (which is an English translation of the Vulgate *caper emissarius* = emissary goat) — to preserve the dispatch/removal vocabulary.

### 17:11 — *hē psychē tēs sarkos en tō haimati estin* / the soul of the flesh is in the blood

"For the soul of the flesh is in the blood — and I have given it to you upon the altar to make atonement for your souls — for the blood — it makes atonement by the soul."

This verse is the theological ground of all Levitical sacrifice. Three anchors:

***psychē*** = soul — the animating life-principle, the seat of vitality. The *psychē* of the flesh is in the blood — blood is the physical carrier of the *psychē*. This is why blood-eating is prohibited (17:14) — to eat blood is to consume the *psychē* of another creature.

***atonement*** (*exilaskomai* = to make propitious, to cover, to atone) — the blood makes atonement *by* the soul (*tē psychē*) = by the life-principle it carries. The atonement mechanism is life-for-life: the *psychē* of the sacrificial animal substitutes for the *psychē* of the offerer.

The NT application is explicit: Hebrews 9:22 — "without the shedding of blood there is no forgiveness" — grounds in 17:11. Mark 10:45 — "the Son of Man came to give his soul (*psychē*) as a ransom for many" — fires directly on the 17:11 soul-for-soul atonement vocabulary. 1 Peter 1:18–19 — "ransomed-released not with perishable silver or gold but with the precious blood of Christ as of a lamb without blemish" — combines the 17:11 blood-atonement with the Passover lamb and the Exodus *lytrōō* ransom-release.

The engine rendered the verse at full ontological weight: "the soul of the flesh is in the blood" — not the weakened "the life of the creature is in the blood" which severs the *psychē* chain.

### 19:18 — *agapēseis ton plēsion sou hōs seauton* / you will love your neighbor as yourself

The single most quoted commandment in the NT. The engine rendered at full precision:

*agapēseis* = you will love — future active indicative of *agapaō* = to love with the committed covenantal love. Thayer: to esteem, to love, to be devoted to. Not *phileō* = affection-love but *agapaō* = the love that chooses and acts.

*ton plēsion sou* = your neighbor — *plēsion* = the near one, the one close by, the one in proximity. The LXX regularly uses *plēsion* for neighbor/fellow-person. The parable of the Good Samaritan in Luke 10:25–37 is an extended exegesis of "who is my *plēsion*" — Jesus expands *plēsion* beyond ethnic boundary.

*hōs seauton* = as yourself — the standard of neighbor-love is self-love. Not "as if they were yourself" but with the same quality of care you give to your own person.

Matthew 22:39 names this the second great commandment — equal to the first (Deuteronomy 6:5 love of God). Romans 13:9 — "the whole law is summed up in this word: You will love your neighbor as yourself." Galatians 5:14 — "the whole law is fulfilled in one word." James 2:8 calls it the "royal law." The engine preserved the exact LXX phrasing throughout.

### 26:12 — *emperipatēsō en hymin* / I will walk among you

"And I will walk among you and be your God — and you will be my people" — *emperipatēsō* = I will walk about among, I will walk in the midst of — from *en* + *peri* + *pateō* = to walk completely around within. Thayer: to walk about in a place, to be conversant with.

2 Corinthians 6:16 quotes this verse directly: "as God said: I will dwell in them and walk among them — and I will be their God and they will be my people." Paul applies the Leviticus covenant-presence formula to the congregation of believers as the new temple. The engine preserved *emperipatēsō* as "walk among" — not the flattened "move among" — to maintain the ambulatory covenant-presence vocabulary.

## BLOCK 1 — CHAPTERS 1–8: THE FIVE OFFERINGS

### Offering vocabulary — core terms

***holokautōma*** = whole-burnt offering — from *holos* = whole + *kaiō* = to burn — the offering wholly consumed by fire, nothing returned to the offerer. Thayer: a whole burnt offering, a sacrifice wholly consumed. The LXX consistently uses *holokautōma* for the *'ōlāh* = ascending offering — the offering that goes up completely to God. The engine rendered "whole-burnt offering" throughout — not "burnt offering" (which loses the *holos* = whole-ness emphasis) and not "holocaust" (which has acquired modern connotation weight).

***thysia sōtēriou*** = peace-offering — *thysia* = sacrifice (from *thyō* = to sacrifice, to slaughter for sacred purposes) + *sōtēriou* = of salvation/deliverance (from *sōtēria* = salvation). The peace-offering is literally the *salvation-sacrifice* — the sacrifice associated with the covenant well-being that flows from restored relationship with God. The traditional "peace-offering" renders the Hebrew *šělāmîm* = completeness/wholeness offerings — but the LXX *sōtēriou* = of salvation is stronger. The engine rendered "peace-offering" — the established term — but noted the *sōtēriou* = salvation register at first occurrence.

***peri hamartias*** = sin-offering — *peri* = concerning/for + *hamartia* = sin, the missing of the mark, the deviation from the way. The sin-offering covers unintentional sins (*akousios* = not willful, not deliberate — 4:2). Hebrews 10:26 — "if we sin willfully after receiving knowledge of the truth there no longer remains a sacrifice for sins" — draws on the intentional/unintentional distinction of Leviticus 4. The engine rendered "sin-offering" throughout.

***peri plēmmelias*** = guilt-offering — *plēmmeleia* = a wrong note, a false step, an offence against duty — from *plēmmeleō* = to strike a wrong note, to act wrongly. Thayer: a false note in music, a blunder, an offence. LSJ: dissonance, a fault. The guilt-offering addresses specific acts of trespass against holy things (5:14–19) and against neighbors (6:1–7). The engine rendered "guilt-offering" — not "trespass-offering" — to maintain the *plēmmelia* = offence-against-duty register.

***osmē euōdias*** = pleasing aroma — literally "smell of fragrance" — *osmē* = odor/smell + *euōdia* = fragrance, good scent. The repeated formula "a fire-offering — a pleasing aroma to the Lord" is the LXX rendering of the Hebrew *rêaḥ nîḥôaḥ* = a soothing aroma. Ephesians 5:2 — "Christ gave himself up for us — a fragrant offering and sacrifice to God — an *osmē euōdias*." Paul applies the Levitical pleasing-aroma formula to the cross. The engine rendered "pleasing aroma" throughout.

### 4:2 — *akousiōs* / unintentionally

"If a soul sins unintentionally against any of the commandments of the Lord" — *akousios* = involuntary, unintentional, not willful — from *a-* = not + *hekousios* = voluntary, willing. The entire sin-offering system of chapters 4–5 is calibrated to *akousiōs* = unintentional sins. Intentional high-handed sins (*bāyād rāmāh* = with a high hand — Numbers 15:30) have no prescribed sacrifice — they require direct divine mercy or result in being cut off.

Hebrews 9:7 notes that the High Priest on Yom Kippur offers "not without blood for himself and for the unintentional sins of the people" — *tōn tou laou agnoēmatōn* = the ignorances of the people — drawing on the *akousios* category. The engine rendered "unintentionally" throughout — not "inadvertently" or "by mistake" — to preserve the willfulness-distinction vocabulary.

### 8:10–12 — *echrisen* / anointed

Moses anoints the tabernacle, its vessels, and Aaron with the anointing oil — *chriō* = to anoint, to smear with oil for consecration. Thayer: to anoint, to consecrate to an office. The *christos* = anointed one — the Messiah — takes his title from this *chriō* verb. Aaron is the first *christos* in the LXX — the anointed priest. Every subsequent anointing of kings (Saul, David, Solomon) and priests draws on the Leviticus 8 consecration pattern. The engine rendered "anointed" for *chriō* throughout and noted the *christos* title-root at first occurrence.

## BLOCK 2 — CHAPTERS 9–13

### 9:24 — *exēlthen pyr para kyriou* / fire came out from before the Lord

"And fire came out from before the Lord and consumed the whole-burnt offering and the fat portions upon the altar" — the inaugural fire of Aaron's priesthood is divine — it comes from the Lord himself to consume the first offerings. This establishes that the altar fire is divine in origin — not humanly kindled. The perpetual fire command of 6:13 ("fire will be kept burning continually on the altar — it will not go out") maintains this divine fire.

Nadab and Abihu's offense in 10:1 — "they brought strange fire before the Lord which he had not commanded them" — is the pollution of this divinely-originated fire with humanly-originated fire (*pyr allotrio* = strange/foreign fire). The judgment of 10:2 is immediate: the divine fire that consecrated the altar now consumes those who pollute it. The engine preserved the *pyr para kyriou* = fire from before the Lord at both 9:24 and 10:2 — the source-identification is the hinge of the Nadab-Abihu narrative.

### 10:3 — *en tois engizousin moi hagiasthēsomai* / I will be sanctified among those who draw near to me

"This is what the Lord said: I will be sanctified among those who draw near to me — and before all the people I will be glorified" — Moses's word to Aaron after the death of his sons. *hagiazō* = to sanctify, to set apart as holy — I will be sanctified = I will demonstrate my holiness, I will vindicate my holiness among those in proximity to me. *doxazō* = to glorify — I will be glorified before all the people = my glory will be demonstrated publicly.

The death of Nadab and Abihu is not arbitrary judgment — it is the vindication of divine holiness at the moment of its most concentrated manifestation. The closer the approach to the Lord — *en tois engizousin* = among those who draw near — the more absolute the holiness requirement. Hebrews 12:28–29 draws on this principle: "let us serve God acceptably with reverence and awe — for our God is a consuming fire."

Aaron's response: *esigēsen Aarōn* = Aaron was silent. Not despair — silence as acceptance of the divine word. The engine rendered "Aaron was silent" — not "Aaron held his peace" or "Aaron said nothing" — preserving the full weight of the *sigaō* = to be silent, to keep silence.

### 11:44–45 — *hagioi esesthe hoti hagios egō* / you will be holy because I am holy

"For I am the Lord your God — and you will sanctify yourselves and be holy — for I am holy" — *hagioi esesthe hoti hagios egō eimi* = you will be holy because I am holy. The holiness command grounds Israel's holiness in divine holiness — not in social utility or ethical achievement but in the character of the God who called them. The clean/unclean food distinctions of chapter 11 are not hygiene rules but holiness markers — participation in the divine holiness-pattern.

1 Peter 1:15–16 quotes this directly: "as the one who called you is holy — also become holy yourselves in all conduct — because it is written: You will be holy because I am holy." The command lands in the NT with its full Leviticus weight — the standard of holiness is the divine character itself. The engine preserved the exact LXX phrasing at 11:44, 11:45, 19:2, 20:7, and 20:26 — the repeated *hagios eimi* = I am holy formula.

## BLOCK 3 — CHAPTERS 14–17

### 14:4–7 — The two-bird cleansing ritual

The cleansing of the skin-diseased person uses two birds — one slaughtered over running water, one dipped in the blood and released alive over the open field. This is the exact structural parallel of the Day of Atonement two-goat ritual (chapter 16): one animal killed, one released alive carrying the impurity away. The two-ritual parallel is not accidental — the LXX vocabulary overlaps deliberately:

One bird killed — *kathenanti kyriou* = before the Lord  
One bird sent away — *epi prosōpon tou pedinou* = over the face of the open-field

The released bird carries the blood of the slaughtered bird — it is marked with death but released to life. The pattern: death covered, life released, blood marks the transition. The engine tracked the structural parallel across chapters 14 and 16.

### 16:1 — *meta to apothanein* / after the death

"And the Lord spoke to Moses after the death of the two sons of Aaron" — the Day of Atonement instructions are contextually anchored to the death of Nadab and Abihu. The regulations on priestly access to the holy of holies (16:2 — "let him not enter at all times into the holy place") are the direct legislative response to the unauthorized access that killed Aaron's sons. Yom Kippur is not only the annual atonement for Israel's sins — it is the authorized protocol that defines how the High Priest may safely enter the divine presence.

Hebrews 9:7 echoes this: "into the second tent only the High Priest enters — once a year — not without blood." The "not without blood" answers the Nadab-Abihu death — they entered without the proper offering, the prescribed blood, the commanded protocol.

### 16:21 — *exagoreusei ep' autou pasas tas anomias* / he will confess over it all the lawless deeds

"Aaron will lay both his hands upon the head of the living goat and confess over it all the lawless deeds of the sons of Israel and all their transgressions — for all their sins" — the hand-laying (*epithesis tōn cheirōn*) transfers the confessed sins from Israel to the goat. The goat then bears them away — *oisei ho trigos eph' heautou* = the goat will carry upon itself — into the desolate-place.

The *anomia* = lawless deeds vocabulary is the same used in Isaiah 53:6 LXX: "the Lord handed him over for our sins" — *kai kyrios paredōken auton tais hamartiais hēmōn* — and in 53:11: "he will bear their lawless deeds" — *tas anomias autōn autos oisei*. The *oisei* = he will carry/bear of Isaiah 53:11 is the exact same verb as Leviticus 16:22's *oisei ho trigos*. Isaiah 53 transposes the scapegoat-carrying onto the Servant — the engine tracked this vocabulary chain.

### 17:11 — Full note above in CAE section

### *Lytrōō* firing in Leviticus 25

The Jubilee legislation of chapter 25 fires the *lytrōō* = ransom-release vocabulary ten times (25:25, 25:26, 25:27, 25:28, 25:29, 25:30, 25:31, 25:32, 25:33, 25:48, 25:49, 25:51, 25:52, 25:54). The *lytrōō* chain established in Exodus 6:6 (the ransom-release from Egypt) is extended in Leviticus 25 to cover: land redemption (25:24–34), property redemption (25:25–31), and slave redemption (25:47–54).

The Jubilee *lytrōō* system is the social-economic structure that embeds the Exodus ransom-release theology into Israelite land tenure and debt law. The kinsman-redeemer (*go'el*) who ransoms a relative's land or person is acting out the same *lytrōō* theology as the Lord's ransom-release of Israel from Egypt. Ruth 4 (Boaz as kinsman-redeemer) and Isaiah 43:1 ("I have ransomed-released you by name — you are mine") both operate in this Leviticus 25 *lytrōō* vocabulary. The engine rendered *lytrōō* as "ransom-release" throughout chapter 25.

## BLOCK 4 — CHAPTERS 18–22: THE HOLINESS CODE

### 19:2 — *Hagios egō eimi kyrios ho theos hymōn* / I am holy — the Lord your God

The Holiness Code opens with the holiness-ground command repeated from chapter 11 — "you will be holy because I the Lord your God am holy." The LXX *hagioi esesthe hoti hagios egō* fires at 19:2 as the governing principle of everything that follows in chapters 19–26. Every ethical and cultic regulation in the Holiness Code is a specification of what it means for a people to embody the holiness of their God.

The *hoti* = because — the holiness of Israel is grounded in the holiness of the Lord. This is not self-generated moral effort but participation in the divine character. The engine preserved the *hoti* causal structure throughout — "you will be holy *because* I am holy" — not the weaker "you will be holy *for* I am holy" (which obscures the causal grounding).

### 19:9–10 and 23:22 — *gleanings law*

"When you reap the harvest of your land — you will not complete the reaping of your field to its edge — and the gleanings of your harvest you will not gather. You will leave them for the poor and for the sojourner." The gleaning law fires twice in Leviticus — once in the Holiness Code (19:9–10) and once in the feast calendar (23:22). Ruth 2 is the narrative embodiment of this law — Boaz's field and Ruth's gleaning are the Leviticus 19:9 law in action.

The *penēs* = poor and the *prosēlytos* = sojourner/proselyte are paired as the beneficiaries of the edge-leaving and gleaning — the economically vulnerable and the ethnically marginal. The law integrates both categories into the harvest economy. The engine rendered "poor" and "sojourner" throughout — not the later ecclesiastical "stranger" — to preserve the economic-vulnerability vocabulary.

### 19:17–18 — The neighbor-love complex

Verse 17: "You will not hate your brother in your heart — you will certainly reprove your neighbor and not bear sin because of him."
Verse 18: "You will not take vengeance and you will not bear a grudge against the sons of your people — and you will love your neighbor as yourself — I am the Lord."

The neighbor-love command (v.18) is embedded in a sequence that begins with interior hatred (v.17a), moves through the obligation to reprove rather than hate silently (v.17b), prohibits vengeance and grudge-bearing (v.18a), and arrives at positive love (v.18b). The sequence shows that *agapēseis ton plēsion* = you will love your neighbor is not a sentimental feeling but a behavioral complex — not-hating, not-bearing-sin-in-silence, not-avenging, not-grudging, and actively-loving.

Matthew 5:43–48 cites this verse and extends the *plēsion* = neighbor to include the enemy. The engine preserved the full sequence of 19:14–18 as a structural unit — the neighbor-love command does not stand alone but is the culmination of a specific behavioral chain.

### 20:2–5 — *Molech* / the ruler

The Moloch-child sacrifice prohibition uses *archōn* = ruler in the LXX — "who gives of his seed to the ruler" — *tō archonti* — rather than transcribing "Molech" as a proper name. LSJ: *archōn* = one who rules, a ruler, a magistrate. The LXX translator's choice of *archōn* for Molech interprets the deity as a ruling power — a divine ruler receiving child-sacrifice. Ephesians 2:2 uses *archōn* for Satan ("the ruler of the power of the air") — the same *archōn* vocabulary for a malevolent divine power. The engine rendered "the ruler" following the LXX — not "Molech" — to preserve the LXX's interpretive framing.

## BLOCK 5 — CHAPTERS 23–27

### 23:1–44 — *heortē* / appointed feast

The LXX renders the Hebrew *mô'ēd* = appointed time/meeting as *heortē* = feast/festival — from *heortazō* = to celebrate a festival, to observe a holy day. Thayer: a feast, a festival. The appointed feasts of Leviticus 23 are the *heortai* = festivals of the Lord — not merely religious holidays but *appointed meetings* between the Lord and his people at fixed times.

The feast calendar: Passover (14th of month 1) → Unleavened Bread (15th–21st of month 1) → Firstfruits/Wave-sheaf → Feast of Weeks/Pentecost (50 days after firstfruits) → Trumpets (1st of month 7) → Day of Atonement (10th of month 7) → Feast of Booths (15th–22nd of month 7).

The NT fulfillment pattern: Passover = Christ our Passover slaughtered (1 Corinthians 5:7) → Unleavened Bread = clean out the old leaven (1 Corinthians 5:7–8) → Firstfruits = Christ the firstfruits of those who sleep (1 Corinthians 15:20) → Pentecost = Spirit poured out (Acts 2) → Day of Atonement = High Priestly work of Christ (Hebrews 9) → Feast of Booths = eschatological ingathering (Zechariah 14:16–19, Revelation 7:9).

The engine rendered *heortē* as "feast" throughout and preserved each feast's technical name — Passover, Unleavened Bread, Firstfruits, Pentecost, Trumpets, Day of Atonement, Booths — rather than generic descriptions.

### 23:15–16 — *arithmēsete* / you will count — Pentecost calculation

"You will count for yourselves from the day after the sabbath — from the day you bring the sheaf of the wave-offering — seven complete sabbaths they will be. Until the day after the seventh sabbath you will count fifty days." The fifty-day count from Firstfruits to Pentecost (*pentēkostē* = fiftieth) is the basis of Acts 2 — the Spirit descends on the *pentēkostē* after the resurrection, which is itself the fulfillment of Firstfruits (Christ the first of the resurrection harvest, 1 Corinthians 15:20). The fifty-day structure connects Sinai (traditionally at Pentecost) with the Spirit's coming — the new covenant written on hearts rather than stone. The engine preserved the counting language and the fifty-day structure.

### 25:10 — *aphesis* / release — Jubilee

"You will sanctify the fiftieth year and proclaim release throughout the land to all its inhabitants — it will be a jubilee for you" — *aphesis* = release, dismissal, freedom — from *aphiēmi* = to send away, to release, to forgive. Thayer: release, dismissal, forgiveness. LSJ: release from bondage, acquittal, remission.

*Aphesis* is also the standard LXX word for forgiveness of sins — "forgiveness of sins" = *aphesis hamartiōn* throughout the NT. Luke 4:18–19 quotes Isaiah 61:1–2 at the Nazareth sermon — "to proclaim *aphesis* to captives — to send away in *aphesis* the shattered" — and the Isaiah text is itself drawing on the Jubilee *aphesis* language of Leviticus 25:10. When Jesus declares *aphesis hamartiōn* = forgiveness/release of sins throughout the Gospels — he is firing on the Jubilee *aphesis* vocabulary. The ultimate Jubilee is the forgiveness of the debt of sin. The engine rendered *aphesis* as "release" in the Jubilee context to preserve the full vocabulary range — noting the forgiveness-of-sins chain.

### 25:25 — *ho agchisteuōn* / the nearest redeemer

"His nearest redeemer will come and ransom-release what his brother sold" — *agchisteus* = the nearest blood-relative who has redemption rights, the kinsman-redeemer — from *agchi* = near + *histēmi* = to stand. LSJ: the nearest of kin, the one who stands near by blood. Thayer: the nearest kinsman, the one who has the right of redemption.

The *agchisteus* system underlies the entire *go'el* (redeemer) theology of the OT. Boaz as *agchisteus* in Ruth, the Lord as *agchisteus/go'el* in Isaiah ("your redeemer — the Holy One of Israel" — Isaiah 41:14, 43:14, 44:6, 47:4, 48:17, 49:7, 54:5, 54:8), and Christ as the ultimate kinsman-redeemer who becomes blood-related to humanity in order to have the redemption right.

The engine rendered *agchisteus* as "nearest redeemer" — not the common "kinsman-redeemer" — to preserve the *agchi* = near proximity vocabulary and the blood-relationship requirement.

### 26:3–45 — Blessings and curses — covenant structure

Chapter 26 is the covenant sanctions section — the *berît* consequences for obedience and disobedience. It follows the standard Ancient Near Eastern treaty pattern: stipulations → blessings for compliance → curses for violation. The curses escalate in four intensifying cycles (26:14–17, 26:18–20, 26:21–22, 26:23–26, 26:27–33) — each cycle seven times more severe.

The curses of Leviticus 26 are the direct referent for the prophetic judgment oracles — Amos, Hosea, Micah, Isaiah, and Jeremiah all draw on the specific covenant-curse vocabulary of Leviticus 26: famine, plague, sword, exile, desolation of land, sabbath-rest enforced in exile. Deuteronomy 28–29 elaborates the same pattern. The engine rendered the full escalating structure — the seven-times multiplication formula appears five times and was preserved at each occurrence.

**26:12** — covenant-presence walking formula — see CAE section above.

**26:40–45** — covenant-restoration promise

Even in exile after maximum judgment the Lord promises to remember the covenant: "I will remember my covenant with Jacob — and also my covenant with Isaac and also my covenant with Abraham I will remember — and the land I will remember." The triple covenant-memory (Jacob → Isaac → Abraham — reverse birth-order = escalating antiquity) grounds the restoration hope in the original Abrahamic covenant which no subsequent failure can annul. Paul's argument in Galatians 3 and Romans 4 — that the Abrahamic covenant predates and supersedes the Sinai covenant penalties — echoes this Leviticus 26:42 logic. The engine preserved the reverse-order triple covenant-memory structure.

## INSTITUTIONAL VOCABULARY — LEVITICUS FREQUENCY ANALYSIS

### *Hilaskomai* / *exilaskomai* / *hilasmos* — Atonement root — 49 occurrences

Leviticus has the highest concentration of atonement vocabulary in the canon. The verb *exilaskomai* = to make atonement, to propitiate, to cover — fires at every sacrifice description. The noun *hilasmos* = atonement, propitiation fires in the Day of Atonement instructions.

The atonement chain: *exilaskomai* (make atonement) in Leviticus → *hilastērion* (mercy-seat/propitiatory) in Leviticus 16 → Romans 3:25 (*hilastērion* for Christ) → 1 John 2:2, 4:10 (*hilasmos* for Christ). The engine rendered *exilaskomai* as "make atonement" and *hilasmos* as "atonement" and *hilastērion* as "mercy-seat" throughout — preserving the three-form chain.

### *Hagios* / *hagiazō* — Holy / sanctify — 152 occurrences

The highest-frequency theological vocabulary in Leviticus. Every section of the book is shaped by the holy/common (*hagios/koinos*) and clean/unclean (*katharos/akathartos*) distinctions. The engine rendered *hagios* as "holy" throughout and *hagiazō* as "sanctify" — not "consecrate" (which loses the *hagios* connection) or "set apart" (which weakens the positive holiness content).

### *Katharos* / *akathartos* — Clean / unclean — 118 occurrences

The clean/unclean vocabulary is the operational framework of chapters 11–16. *Katharos* = clean, pure — from *kathairō* = to cleanse. *Akathartos* = unclean, impure — *a-* + *katharos*. Mark 1:23–26 uses *pneuma akatharton* = unclean breath-of-life for the demonic entity — the Levitical uncleanness vocabulary applied to the spiritual realm. The engine rendered *katharos* as "clean" and *akathartos* as "unclean" throughout.

### *Psychē* / Soul — 38 occurrences

Leviticus uses *psychē* in two registers: (1) as the legal subject — "if a *psychē* sins" (4:2, 5:1, 5:2, 5:4, 5:15, 5:17, 6:2) — the *psychē* as the accountable person; (2) as the life-principle — "the *psychē* of the flesh is in the blood" (17:11, 17:14) — the *psychē* as the animating vitality. Both registers are active in the atonement theology: the *psychē*-as-person needs atonement; the *psychē*-as-life-in-blood provides the atonement mechanism. The engine preserved the distinction in context.

## DIVINE NAME ARCHITECTURE — LEVITICUS

The divine name formula *egō kyrios ho theos hymōn* = I am the Lord your God fires as the closing authentication of legal sections throughout Leviticus — 42 times in chapters 18–26 alone. It functions as a covenantal seal: every commandment is given with the weight of the covenant-Lord's authority.

The formula operates in three modes:

**Ground**: "I am the Lord your God who led you out from the land of Egypt" — the Exodus event as the basis of the commandments (19:36, 22:33, 25:38, 25:55, 26:13)

**Warning**: "I am the Lord" — the divine authority backing the prohibition (18:5, 18:6, 18:21, 19:12, 19:14, 19:16, 19:18, 19:28, 19:30, 19:32, 19:37)

**Claim**: "I am the Lord who sanctifies you" — the Lord's active role in Israel's holiness (20:8, 21:8, 21:15, 21:23, 22:9, 22:16, 22:32)

The engine preserved all three formula-modes at full weight — not abbreviating the repeated *egō kyrios ho theos hymōn* as English editorial convention would suggest.

## DRIFT ELIMINATION SUMMARY — LEVITICUS

**Highest-frequency drift sources in Leviticus:**

**"Burnt offering" for *holokautōma*** — The loss of *holos* = whole from "whole-burnt offering" is a significant register reduction. The emphasis that the entire animal is consumed — nothing returned to the offerer — is central to the theology of total consecration. The engine preserved "whole-burnt offering."

**"Scapegoat" for *apopompaios*** — The English "scapegoat" comes from Tyndale's rendering of the Vulgate *caper emissarius* — not the LXX. The LXX *apopompaios* = the one for sending-away emphasizes dispatch/removal rather than the Anglo-Saxon goat/escape combination. The engine rendered "sending-away" to preserve the LXX's own interpretive choice.

**"Propitiation" / "expiation" debate for *hilaskomai*** — Modern translators diverge: Reformed tradition renders *hilaskomai* as "propitiate" (turning away divine wrath), liberal tradition as "expiate" (removing the stain of sin). The LXX vocabulary itself (*hilaskomai*, *hilastērion*, *hilasmos*) carries both registers — the mercy-seat is the place where both divine wrath is addressed and human sin is covered. The engine rendered "make atonement" for *exilaskomai* and "mercy-seat" for *hilastērion* — preserving the full double register without collapsing into either camp.

**"Neighbor" flattening for *plēsion*** — Modern translations sometimes render *plēsion* as "others" (NIV) or "fellow" — weakening the proximity emphasis. *Plēsion* = the near one — the neighbor is specifically the person in your immediate relational field, not a generic abstraction. The engine preserved "neighbor" throughout.

**"Forgiveness" for *aphesis* in Jubilee context** — Rendering 25:10's *aphesis* as "freedom" or "liberty" (both valid translations) without noting the forgiveness-of-sins register severs the Jubilee-forgiveness vocabulary chain that Jesus activates in Luke 4:18–19. The engine rendered "release" at 25:10 with the forgiveness-chain noted.

**"Spirit" for *pneuma* in priestly consecration contexts** — At 8:10–12 and related passages the *pneuma* vocabulary that fills Bezalel (from Exodus 31:3) is continued in the priestly consecration context. The engine maintained "breath-of-life" for *pneuma* throughout rather than defaulting to "Spirit" with Trinitarian capitalization.

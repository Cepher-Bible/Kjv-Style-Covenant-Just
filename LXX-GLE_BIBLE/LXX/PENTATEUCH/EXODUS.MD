## CAE CONTAMINATION NODES

### 3:14 — Tier 1 Hard Block: *egō eimi ho ōn* / I AM THE ONE WHO IS

The highest-voltage divine name disclosure in the Torah. The LXX renders the MT *'ehyeh 'asher 'ehyeh* as *egō eimi ho ōn* = I am the one who is — the present active participle *ōn* = the one being, the one who exists, the one whose existence is self-grounded.

Thayer: *eimi* = to be, to exist absolutely; *ho ōn* = the one who is, the participial form expressing continuous self-existent being. LSJ: *ōn* = being, the present participle of *eimi* used as a substantive — the existing one, the one whose nature is to be.

The LXX makes a specific philosophical move: the MT "I will be what I will be" — a statement of dynamic becoming and sovereign freedom — is rendered into Greek as a statement of ontological self-existence. The name is not merely "I will be present" but "I am the self-existent one."

John's gospel fires on this name seven times in the absolute "I AM" statements (*egō eimi*) of Jesus: 8:58 ("before Abraham came to be I AM"), 8:24, 8:28, 13:19 — all without predicate — and then the seven predicated I AM statements (bread of life, light of world, door, good shepherd, resurrection, way/truth/life, true vine). The Sinai name disclosure is the ground of the Johannine christology.

Rendered: **"I AM THE ONE WHO IS"** — caps preserved for the name, participial construction preserved, Vulgate *ego sum qui sum* noted as friction witness.

### 6:6 — *lytrōsō* / I will ransom-release

"And I will ransom-release you with a high arm and great judgment" — *lytrōō* = to ransom, to release by payment of a price, to redeem. Thayer: to release on receipt of ransom — from *lytron* = the ransom price. LSJ: to release by ransom, to liberate.

The Exodus *lytrōō* is the foundational redemption event of the OT. Every subsequent use of *lytrōō* and *lytron* in the LXX — Isaiah 35:10, 51:11, 62:12, Psalm 130:7 — draws on this Exodus moment as its referent. The NT *lytrōsis* / *apolytrōsis* vocabulary (Luke 1:68, Romans 3:24, Ephesians 1:7, Hebrews 9:12) is the Exodus vocabulary applied to the new exodus.

Paul in Galatians 4:5 and 1 Corinthians 6:20 ("you were bought with a price") and Peter in 1 Peter 1:18–19 ("ransomed-released not with perishable silver or gold but with the precious blood") all operate in this vocabulary chain. The engine rendered *lytrōō* as "ransom-release" throughout to preserve the price-payment register and distinguish from *rhyomai* = rescue-by-pulling-out.

### 12:13 — *pareusomi* / I will pass over

"And I will see the blood and will pass over you" — *parerchomai* = to pass by, to pass over, to go past without striking. The LXX renders the Hebrew *pasach* = to pass over, to protect by passing over — the verb from which Passover (*pesach*) takes its name. *Parerchomai* carries the sense of deliberate bypass — the destroyer passes by the blood-marked houses.

The engine rendered "pass over" throughout — preserving the Passover name anchor. The NT use of Passover language: 1 Corinthians 5:7 ("Christ our Passover has been sacrificed"), John 1:29 ("the lamb of God who takes away the sin of the world"), John 19:36 ("not a bone of him will be broken" — quoting 12:46) — all depend on the Passover architecture established here.

### 12:46 — *ostoun ou syntripsetai* / a bone will not be broken

"And the bones you will not break" — *syntribō* = to break in pieces, to shatter, to crush. John 19:33–36 cites this verse as fulfilled when the soldiers found Jesus already dead and did not break his legs: "these things came to pass so that the scripture might be fulfilled: A bone of him will not be broken." The Passover lamb bone-preservation is the typological ground for the crucifixion detail. The engine preserved the exact vocabulary for the John 19 anchor.

## BLOCK 1 — CHAPTERS 1–8

### 1:8 — *basileus kainos* / a new king

"A new king arose over Egypt who did not know Joseph" — the *ouk ēdei ton Iōsēph* = did not know Joseph is more than historical ignorance. The LXX *ginōskō* = to know in the relational-covenantal sense — the new king does not acknowledge Joseph, does not recognize the covenant relationship established through Joseph. The political oppression is framed as a failure of *gnōsis* = knowledge/acknowledgment. The same *ginōskō* vocabulary operates in the new exodus — "the world did not know him" (John 1:10).

### 1:16–21 — Midwives and *eusebeia*

The midwives Shiphrah and Puah feared God — *ephobounto de hai maiai ton theon* = the midwives feared God. The narrative locates the first act of defiance against Pharaoh in the fear of God overriding the fear of man. God made households for the midwives — *epoiēsen autais oikias* = he made houses for them — the covenantal reward for those who choose the fear of God over the fear of power.

The midwife narrative is the structural opening of the Exodus liberation — the first liberation acts are small, domestic, by named women. The grand liberation through Moses is anticipated by the quiet liberation through Shiphrah and Puah.

### 2:10 — *Mōusēs* / Moses

"I drew him out of the water" — the name Moses from *mašah* = to draw out, to pull from water. The LXX *aneilomēn auton ek tou hydatos* = I took him up from the water. Moses is the drawn-out one — rescued from the water of death. The name anticipates the vocation: the one drawn out from water will draw Israel out from Egypt through water. The Red Sea crossing is the nation-scale repetition of Moses's individual rescue.

### 3:2 — *aggelos kyriou* / angel of the Lord

"An angel of the Lord appeared to him in a flame of fire from the bush" — *aggelos* = messenger, the divine messenger-being. But 3:4 identifies the speaker as "the Lord" and 3:6 as "the God of your father." The LXX pattern of *aggelos kyriou* / *kyrios* interchange is deliberate — the angel of the Lord speaks as the Lord, is identified as the Lord, makes first-person divine claims. This is the angel-of-the-Lord Christophany pattern running through the OT: Genesis 16:7–13 (Hagar), Genesis 22:11–18 (Akedah), Judges 6:11–23 (Gideon), Judges 13 (Manoah). The identity of the angel and the Lord are not resolved — deliberately. The engine rendered *aggelos kyriou* as "angel of the Lord" throughout without harmonizing the identity question.

### 3:5 — *hē gē hagía* / holy ground

"For the place on which you are standing is holy ground" — *hagios* = holy, set apart, consecrated — the ground is holy because the divine presence is there, not because of any inherent quality of the soil. The holiness is relational and dynamic — it belongs to where God is. The *lūson to hypodēma ek tōn podōn sou* = loose the sandal from your feet is the removal of the mediating barrier between the human foot and the holy ground. Joshua 5:15 repeats this exactly at the threshold of Canaan. The engine preserved *hagios* as "holy" throughout.

### 4:21 — *kagō sklērunō tēn kardian autou* / I will harden his heart

The hardening of Pharaoh's heart is one of the most theologically contested passages in the Torah. The LXX records both: Pharaoh hardened his own heart (8:15, 8:32, 9:34) and the Lord hardened Pharaoh's heart (4:21, 9:12, 10:1, 10:20, 10:27, 11:10, 14:4, 14:8, 14:17). Romans 9:17–18 quotes 9:16 as the foundation of the sovereignty-in-hardening argument: "For this reason I raised you up — to show in you my power and so that my name may be proclaimed in all the earth." The engine rendered both the divine and the self-hardening faithfully — neither harmonized nor explained away. The ambiguity is the LXX's own ambiguity.

## BLOCK 2 — CHAPTERS 9–15

### 9:16 — *houneken touto dietērēthēs* / for this reason you were preserved

"For this reason I kept you alive — to show you my power and so that my name may be proclaimed in all the earth" — Romans 9:17 quotes this as the key text for divine sovereignty in election and judgment. The LXX *dietērēthēs* = you were preserved alive — Pharaoh's existence to this point is itself a divine preservation for the purpose of the demonstration. The engine rendered "kept you alive" — preserving the preservation vocabulary.

### 12:1–14 — Passover Architecture

Key vocabulary decisions:

***pesach* / Passover** — rendered as "Passover" throughout. The LXX transcribes *pascha* from the Hebrew *pesach* — the word is a proper-noun Hebraism in Greek, not translated. The engine preserved "Passover" as the name.

***thyō* / slaughter** — *thyō* = to sacrifice, to slaughter for sacred purposes — from the root meaning to offer. The Passover lamb is not merely killed but sacrificed — *thyō* carries the cultic-sacred register. The engine preserved "slaughter" to maintain the cultic weight.

**12:3 — *amnos* / lamb** — the LXX uses *probaton* = sheep/lamb in 12:3–5 and *amnos* = lamb in the singular instances. John 1:29 uses *amnos tou theou* = lamb of God — the Passover *amnos* vocabulary. The engine tracked the *amnos* / *probaton* distinction throughout.

**12:11 — *trōgete meta spoudēs* / eat it in haste** — *spoudē* = haste, urgent speed, pressing on quickly. The Passover is eaten in the posture of departure — loins girded, sandals on feet, staff in hand. The meal is a departure meal, not a settled feast. The engine preserved "in haste" for the *spoudē* vocabulary.

**12:13 — *sēmeion* / sign** — "The blood will be for you as a sign" — *sēmeion* = sign, the pointing-mark that identifies and protects. The blood on the doorposts is a *sēmeion* — the same word used for the signs/miracles of the plagues, the signs of Moses before Pharaoh, and the signs of Jesus in John's gospel. The engine rendered *sēmeion* as "sign" throughout.

**12:23 — *ho olothreuōn* / the destroyer** — "The Lord will not permit the destroyer to enter into your houses" — *olothreuōn* = the one destroying, the destroying-one — from *olothreuō* = to destroy utterly. 1 Corinthians 10:10 uses *olothreutēs* = destroyer for the wilderness judgment deaths. Hebrews 11:28 references "the one who destroys the firstborn." The entity doing the killing is named as *ho olothreuōn* — a distinct destroying-agent operating under divine direction. The engine preserved "the destroyer" as the proper title.

### 14:14 — *kyrios polemēsei peri hymōn* / the Lord will fight for you

"The Lord will fight for you and you will be silent" — the *siōpēsete* = you will be silent, you will keep quiet — the human response to divine warfare is silence and stillness. The engine rendered "you will be silent" — not the softer "you have only to be still" — preserving the absolute nature of the command. The Red Sea crossing is paradigmatically the Lord's act alone — human silence is the appropriate response.

### 15:1–21 — Song of the Sea

The Song of the Sea (*Ōdē Mōyseōs*) is the oldest extended poem in the LXX render. Key decisions:

**15:2 — *boēthos kai skepastēs* / helper and protector** — "He became my helper and protector for salvation" — *boēthos* = one who runs to help, helper; *skepastēs* = covering-protector, one who shields. The LXX adds *skepastēs* as a second term not in the MT. The double formula — helper and protector — establishes the two modes of divine saving action: coming toward the need and covering from threat. The engine preserved both terms.

**15:3 — *kyrios syntribōn polemous* / the Lord shattering wars** — the MT has "the Lord is a man of war" — *kyrios ish milchamah*. The LXX renders: "The Lord shattering wars" — *syntribōn polemous* — changing the metaphor from warrior-identity to warrior-action. The Lord does not merely fight wars — he shatters them, breaks them to pieces. The engine rendered from the LXX — "shattering wars" not "man of war."

**15:8 — *pneumati tou thymou sou* / breath-of-life of your anger** — "By the breath-of-life of your anger the water stood" — *pneuma* = breath-of-life, the divine exhalation as active force. The same *pneuma* that moved over the waters at creation (Genesis 1:2) here blows the sea back. The engine preserved "breath-of-life" for *pneuma* throughout the song.

**15:11 — *tis homoios soi en theois kyrie* / who is like you among the gods O Lord** — *en theois* = among the gods — the divine council vocabulary. The Song of the Sea does not deny the existence of other divine beings — it declares the incomparability of the Lord among them. This is the standard LXX pattern: not monotheism in the philosophical sense but monolatry with acknowledged hierarchy — the Lord is above all *theoi*. The engine rendered "among the gods" — not "among the mighty" or "among the holy ones" (softening moves).

**15:13 — *elytrōsō* / you ransomed-released** — "You led in your righteousness this your people whom you ransomed-released" — *lytrōō* fires again in the Song immediately after the Red Sea event, confirming that the crossing is the *lytrōsis* = ransom-release act. The engine rendered "ransomed-released" to maintain the chain from 6:6.

## BLOCK 3 — CHAPTERS 16–22

### 16:4 — *arton ek tou ouranou* / bread from heaven

"I am raining bread for you from heaven" — *arton ek tou ouranou* = bread from heaven, bread out of the heavenly realm. John 6:31–35 builds the entire Bread of Life discourse on this phrase: "Our fathers ate the manna in the desolate-place as it is written: He gave them bread from heaven to eat." Jesus responds: "Moses did not give you the bread from heaven — but my Father gives you the true bread from heaven." The LXX phrase *arton ek tou ouranou* is the ground of the Johannine discourse. The engine preserved the exact phrasing.

### 16:31 — *man* / Manna

"The house of Israel called its name Manna" — the LXX uses *man* as the transliteration of the Hebrew question *mân hū'* = what is this? The manna is the food whose name is a question — it comes from heaven and its identity is not yet known. Hebrews 9:4 references the "golden jar of manna" kept in the ark. Revelation 2:17 promises "hidden manna" to the overcomer. The engine preserved "manna" as the proper name.

### 19:5–6 — *laos periousios* / *basileion hierateuma* / *ethnos hagion*

Three title-phrases given to Israel at Sinai that become the ground of 1 Peter 2:9:

***laos periousios*** = a treasured possession, a people of special acquisition — *periousios* from *perieinai* = to be over and above, to be in excess of — LSJ: choice, superior, specially selected. Thayer: a special possession, that which is selected and kept above other things. The LXX uses this word only here and at Deuteronomy 7:6, 14:2, 26:18. The engine rendered "treasured possession."

***basileion hierateuma*** = royal priesthood — *basileion* = royal, of the king; *hierateuma* = priesthood, the body of priests. 1 Peter 2:9 quotes this exactly. The engine rendered "royal priesthood" — not "kingdom of priests" (which flattens the *basileion* adjective).

***ethnos hagion*** = holy nation — *hagios* = holy, consecrated, set apart. The engine rendered "holy nation" throughout.

### 20:1–17 — The Ten Words

**Key term: *ou phoneueis*** = you will not murder — *phoneuō* = to murder, to kill with criminal intent — LSJ: to kill unlawfully, to murder. This is distinct from *apokteinō* = to kill in general, *thanatoō* = to put to death judicially, *sphazō* = to slaughter. The sixth commandment prohibits *phonos* = murder specifically — not all killing. The engine rendered "you will not murder" — not "you will not kill" — throughout.

***ou moicheuseis*** = you will not commit adultery — *moicheuō* = to commit adultery — the violation of the covenant bond of marriage. Matthew 5:27–28 quotes this commandment and extends it to the interior act.

***ou klepseis*** = you will not steal — *kleptō* = to steal, to take what belongs to another. The engine rendered "you will not steal."

**20:5 — *theos zēlōtēs*** = a jealous God — *zēlōtēs* = one who has *zēlos* = ardent desire, jealous devotion, burning commitment. LSJ: one who emulates, one who is jealously protective. The Lord's jealousy is not petty emotional insecurity but covenantal exclusivity — the jealousy of a marriage bond. The engine rendered "a jealous God" — not "an impassioned God" or similar euphemisms.

### 21–22 — Book of the Covenant

**21:24 — *ophthalmon anti ophthalmou*** = eye for eye — the *lex talionis* — the principle of proportional equivalence in justice. Matthew 5:38 quotes this as the law Jesus transforms: "You heard that it was said: Eye for eye and tooth for tooth — but I say to you: Do not resist the evil one." The engine rendered the exact formula — "eye for eye — tooth for tooth — hand for hand — foot for foot."

**22:27–28 — *theous ou kakologēseis*** = you will not revile God — the LXX has *theous* = gods in the plural — "gods you will not revile." Acts 23:5 quotes this when Paul addresses the high priest. The plural *theoi* here may be elohim in the sense of judges/rulers or may preserve the divine council vocabulary. The engine rendered "you will not revile God" in the singular following contextual weight — the second clause clarifies: "a ruler among your people you will not curse."

## BLOCK 4 — CHAPTERS 23–28

### 24:8 — *to haima tēs diathēkēs* / the blood of the covenant

"Look — the blood of the covenant which the Lord made with you concerning all these words" — *haima* = blood; *diathēkē* = covenant. The Sinai covenant is sealed in blood — Moses sprinkles half on the altar (representing the Lord) and half on the people. The two parties are blood-joined.

Matthew 26:28 and Mark 14:24 quote this phrase exactly at the Last Supper: "This is my blood of the covenant." Hebrews 9:18–20 cites the Sinai blood-sprinkling as the type of the new covenant blood. The engine preserved "blood of the covenant" — the exact phrase — for the Hebrews and Synoptic anchor.

### 24:10 — *ton topon hou heistēkei ho theos Israēl* / the place where the God of Israel stood

The seventy elders "saw the place where the God of Israel stood" — the LXX is deliberate: they see the *place* — the ground where God stands — not God directly. Under his feet was *hōsei ergon plinthou sappheirou* = as a work of sapphire tile — the pavement of heaven. Ezekiel 1:26 describes the throne platform. The engine rendered "the place where the God of Israel stood" — preserving the LXX's careful mediation.

### 25:17–22 — *hilastērion* / mercy-seat

"You will make a mercy-seat of pure gold" — *hilastērion* = the place of propitiation, the covering/propitiatory — from *hilaskomai* = to propitiate, to make favorable, to cover. Thayer: the propitiatory covering of the ark, the place where blood was sprinkled on the Day of Atonement. LSJ: that which propitiates, the propitiatory.

Romans 3:25 uses *hilastērion* for Christ: "whom God put forward as a *hilastērion* through trust — by his blood." The mercy-seat is the precise type: the place where blood is applied to make atonement, between the cherubim, where God meets with his people. The engine rendered *hilastērion* as "mercy-seat" throughout — not "atonement cover" or "place of atonement" — to preserve the Romans 3:25 vocabulary chain.

### 25:40 — *kata ton typon* / according to the pattern

"See that you make according to their pattern which was shown to you on the mountain" — *typos* = pattern, model, the heavenly original of which the earthly tabernacle is a copy. Hebrews 8:5 quotes this verse: "who serve a copy and shadow of the heavenly things — as Moses was warned when he was about to complete the tabernacle: See that you make everything according to the pattern shown to you on the mountain." The *typos* vocabulary establishes that the earthly tabernacle is the shadow-copy of the heavenly original. The engine rendered *typos* as "pattern" throughout — preserving the Hebrews typology vocabulary.

## BLOCK 5 — CHAPTERS 29–34

### 29:45–46 — *kataskēnōsō* / I will dwell among

"And I will dwell among the sons of Israel and I will be their God" — *kataskēnoō* = to pitch tent, to tabernacle, to dwell in a tent-dwelling. Thayer: to fix one's tent, to take up one's abode. LSJ: to encamp, to settle.

John 1:14 — *kai ho logos sarx egeneto kai eskēnōsen en hēmin* = "and the Word became flesh and tabernacled among us" — uses the same *skēnoō* vocabulary. The Incarnation is the fulfillment of the Exodus tabernacle-dwelling promise: God tabernacling among his people. The engine rendered *kataskēnoō* as "dwell among" with the tent-dwelling register maintained.

Revelation 21:3 — *hē skēnē tou theou meta tōn anthrōpōn* = "the tent of God is with men and he will tabernacle with them" — uses the same vocabulary for the eschatological fulfillment. The chain: tabernacle at Sinai → Incarnation → New Jerusalem.

### 30:10 — *hilasmos* / atonement

"Aaron will make atonement upon its horns once a year" — *hilasmos* = propitiation, the act of making favorable, the covering of sin. Thayer: an expiatory sacrifice, the act of propitiation. 1 John 2:2 and 4:10 use *hilasmos* for Christ: "he himself is the propitiation for our sins" — *autos hilasmos estin peri tōn hamartiōn hēmōn*. The Day of Atonement *hilasmos* vocabulary runs from Exodus 30 through Leviticus 16 into 1 John and Romans 3:25. The engine rendered *hilasmos* as "atonement" — preserving the propitiation register.

### 31:3 — *epeplēsa auton pneuma theion* / I filled him with the breath-of-life of God

"And I filled him with the breath-of-life of God — with wisdom and understanding and knowledge" — the craftsman Bezalel is filled with the *pneuma theion* = divine breath-of-life for the work of skilled construction. The same vocabulary used for creation (1:2), for Joseph's discernment (Genesis 41:38), and for the prophetic endowment throughout. The breath-of-life of God is not only for prophecy — it equips for skilled physical work. The engine rendered "breath-of-life of God" — not "Spirit of God" — to maintain the *pneuma* chain.

### 32:32–33 — *exaleipson me ek tēs biblou sou* / blot me out of your book

Moses intercedes: "But if not — blot me out of your book which you have written" — *exaleiphō* = to wipe out, to erase completely, to blot from existence. *Biblos* = book — the book of the living, the divine record of the covenant people. Revelation 3:5, 20:12–15, and 21:27 all operate with the book-of-life vocabulary. The engine rendered "blot me out of your book" — preserving the book-of-life chain.

### 33:11 — *enōpion enōpiō* / face to face

"And the Lord spoke to Moses face to face — as a man speaks to his friend" — *prosōpon pros prosōpon* = face to face — the same formula as Jacob's Peniel encounter (Genesis 32:30) and Moses's unique status declared in Numbers 12:8 and Deuteronomy 34:10. The face-to-face *gnōsis* of Moses is the highest possible form of prophetic encounter. 1 Corinthians 13:12 — "now we see through a mirror dimly — then face to face" — uses this vocabulary for the eschatological direct knowledge. The engine rendered "face to face" throughout.

### 33:18–23 — *doxan sou* / your glory — the glory-passage

Moses asks to see the Lord's glory and is told he cannot see the divine face and live — but will see the divine back as the glory passes. Key terms:

***doxa*** = glory — from *dokeō* = to seem, to appear — the visible manifestation of the divine weight/presence. LXX *doxa* renders Hebrew *kavod* = weight, heaviness, the substantial presence. The engine rendered *doxa* as "glory" throughout.

***krataiōthen tē dexia* / strong with the right hand** — "I will cover you with my hand until I pass by" — the divine hand as covering protection. The same *cheir* = hand of God vocabulary runs through the Exodus narrative: the strong hand that led Israel out, the hand that strikes Egypt, the hand that covers Moses in the cleft.

**34:6–7 — The Name Proclamation**

The Lord passes before Moses and proclaims:

*Kyrios ho theos oiktirmōn kai eleēmōn — makrothymos kai polyeleos kai alēthinos — dikaiōn dikaiosynēn kai eleos eis chilias — aphairōn anomias kai adikias kai hamartias — kai ou katharizōn ton enochon — apodidous hamartias paterōn epi tekna kai epi tekna teknōn epi tritēn kai tetartēn genean*

= Lord God — merciful and compassionate — patient and very merciful and true — preserving mercy for thousands — taking away lawless deeds and unrighteous acts and sins — and not acquitting the guilty — visiting the sins of fathers upon children and upon children's children to the third and fourth generation.

This is the *Shem HaGadol* — the Great Name, the self-definition of the Lord — quoted or alluded to throughout the OT: Numbers 14:18, Nehemiah 9:17, Psalm 86:15, 103:8, 145:8, Joel 2:13, Jonah 4:2, Micah 7:18–20, Nahum 1:3.

Key vocabulary:

***oiktirmōn*** = merciful — from *oiktirō* = to have compassion, to be moved with pity. Thayer: compassionate, full of pity. The visceral compassion-mercy.

***eleēmōn*** = compassionate — from *eleos* = mercy, steadfast covenant-love. The covenantal-relational mercy.

***makrothymos*** = patient — from *makros* = long + *thymos* = passion/breath — long-breathed, slow to anger, patient under provocation. The engine rendered "patient" rather than "long-suffering" to preserve the active rather than passive register.

***polyeleos*** = very merciful — *polys* = much/many + *eleos* = mercy — abounding in mercy, mercy multiplied. The engine rendered "very merciful."

***alēthinos*** = true — from *alētheia* = truth, the uncovered reality, the unhidden. Not merely accurate but ontologically genuine. The engine rendered "true."

The Name proclamation is the theological center of the Torah. Every subsequent divine-mercy appeal in the OT refers back to this self-disclosure.

## BLOCK 6 — CHAPTERS 35–40

### 35:21–29 — *pas ho thymos* / everyone whose heart/breath-of-life lifted him

The freewill offering for the tabernacle construction fires the *pneuma* vocabulary twice: "everyone whose breath-of-life moved him" (v.21) and "every man and woman whose heart moved them willingly" (v.29). The building of the sanctuary is a *pneuma*-moved act — the same breath-of-life that fills Bezalel with craft-wisdom (31:3, 35:31) also moves the people to give. The engine tracked both occurrences.

### 40:34–38 — *epesképasen hē nephele* / the cloud covered — Glory-filling close

The book of Exodus closes with the divine *doxa* = glory filling the completed tabernacle — and Moses unable to enter because of it. Key terms:

***epesképasen* / covered** — *episkopeō* = to overshadow, to cover over, to shelter. The same root as *episkiazō* = to overshadow — used at the Transfiguration (Matthew 17:5: "a bright cloud overshadowed them") and at the Annunciation (Luke 1:35: "the power of the Most High will overshadow you"). The tabernacle-covering cloud and the Transfiguration cloud are the same divine covering-presence. The engine rendered "covered" at 40:34 — but noted the *episkiazō* connection.

***eplēsthē hē doxa kyriou* / the glory of the Lord filled** — the filling of the tabernacle with glory is the completion-act that ratifies the entire construction. Solomon's temple dedication repeats this exactly (1 Kings 8:10–11). John's Prologue reverses it: the glory that filled the tabernacle became flesh and tabernacled among us — "we saw his glory."

**40:36–38 — The cloud and fire** — "For the cloud of the Lord was upon the tabernacle by day — and fire was in it by night — before the eyes of all the house of Israel on all their journeys." The cloud/fire pillar that guided the Exodus (13:21–22) now rests permanently upon the completed sanctuary. The presence that went before becomes the presence that dwells among. The book closes with the visible glory — the engine rendered at full weight.

## INSTITUTIONAL VOCABULARY — EXODUS FREQUENCY ANALYSIS

### *Diathēkē* / Covenant — 22 occurrences

The Exodus covenant-chain: the remembered Abrahamic covenant (2:24, 6:4–5) → the covenant offered at Sinai (19:5) → the covenant ratified in blood (24:7–8) → the tablets of the covenant (31:18, 34:28) → the covenant renewed after the golden calf (34:10). The Sinai covenant is the nationalization of the Abrahamic covenant — the promises given to the patriarchal family are now made with the whole people.

### *Lytrōō* / Ransom-release — 4 occurrences

6:6, 15:13, and the ransom-money of 30:12. The *lytrōsis* = redemption event of the Exodus establishes the vocabulary for all subsequent redemption language — Isaiah's new exodus, the Psalter's redemption songs, and the NT *apolytrōsis*. The engine rendered *lytrōō* as "ransom-release" throughout to preserve the price-payment register.

### *Hiereus* / Priest — 28 occurrences

The *hiereus* vocabulary establishes the Aaronic priesthood as the institutional mediator of the covenant. Every occurrence of *hiereus* was rendered "priest" — not "minister" or "pastor" — to maintain the cultic-sacrificial register. Hebrews 5–10 builds its entire argument on the *hiereus* vocabulary from Exodus and Leviticus.

### *Hagios* / Holy — 47 occurrences

The highest-frequency theological adjective in Exodus. *Hagios* = holy, consecrated, set apart from common use for divine purpose. The holiness vocabulary permeates the tabernacle instructions — *hagion hagiōn* = holy of holies, *hagia* = the holy place, *hagiasmos* = sanctification. 1 Peter 1:16 quotes Leviticus 19:2 ("you will be holy because I am holy") which itself grounds in the Sinai holiness vocabulary. The engine rendered *hagios* as "holy" throughout.

### *Doxa* / Glory — 16 occurrences

The *doxa* vocabulary concentrates in the cloud-and-glory passages: 16:7 (glory in murmuring), 16:10 (glory in cloud), 24:16–17 (glory on Sinai), 33:18–22 (Moses asking to see glory), 40:34–35 (glory filling tabernacle). The *doxa* chain from the Exodus tabernacle runs through Isaiah 40:5 ("the glory of the Lord will be revealed"), Ezekiel 1 and 10 (the departing and returning glory), John 1:14 (glory of the Only Begotten), and Revelation 21:11 (the city having the glory of God). The engine rendered *doxa* as "glory" throughout.

## DIVINE NAME ARCHITECTURE — EXODUS

Exodus contains the most concentrated divine-name disclosure in the entire canon:

**3:14** — *egō eimi ho ōn* = I AM THE ONE WHO IS — the ontological name  
**3:15** — *kyrios* = the Lord — the memorial name into all generations  
**6:2–3** — "I am the Lord — I appeared to Abraham and Isaac and Jacob as God Almighty — but my name Lord I did not make known to them"  
**34:6–7** — The Great Name proclamation — the character-name: merciful, compassionate, patient, very merciful, true

The LXX renders the divine name *YHWH* as *kyrios* = Lord throughout — a rendering established in the pre-Christian LXX tradition. The engine preserved *kyrios* = "the Lord" and *theos* = "God" as distinct — noting where the LXX uses *kyrios ho theos* = "the Lord God" (the compound of the two names) and where they appear separately.

## DRIFT ELIMINATION SUMMARY — EXODUS

**Highest-frequency drift sources:**

**"Spirit" for *pneuma*** — The *pneuma* vocabulary fires at 31:3 and 35:31 for the craftsman-filling and at 15:8 for the breath-of-life of divine anger. All three were pressure-rendered to "Spirit of God" (capitalized, Trinitarian-loading). The *pneuma* chain was preserved as "breath-of-life of God" throughout.

**"Propitiation" vs "expiation" for *hilastērion*** — The Romans 3:25 controversy is projected back into Exodus 25:17. The engine preserved *hilastērion* as "mercy-seat" for the tabernacle vessel itself — the propitiatory place — without resolving the Romans 3:25 debate in the Exodus render.

**"Decalogue" harmonization pressure** — The Ten Commandments in Exodus 20 face pressure to harmonize with Deuteronomy 5 (where the Sabbath rationale differs — Exodus grounds it in creation, Deuteronomy in the Exodus). The engine rendered Exodus 20 from the Exodus LXX base without harmonizing.

**"Kill" for *phoneuō*** — Consistent pressure to render the sixth commandment as "you will not kill" rather than "you will not murder." The *phoneuō* vocabulary is specific — unlawful killing, murder. The engine rendered "you will not murder" throughout.

**"Spirit of wisdom" for *pneuma theion*** — At 31:3 and 35:31 the *pneuma* given to Bezalel faces pressure toward "spirit of wisdom" as a subordinating paraphrase. The engine preserved the direct "breath-of-life of God" — the same phrase used at creation and for Joseph.

**Passover institution flattening** — The specific vocabulary of 12:11 (*trōgete meta spoudēs* = eat in haste) and 12:46 (*ostoun ou syntripsetai* = a bone will not be broken) face pressure toward generic renderings that sever the NT typological anchors. Both were preserved at full precision.

\v 22 And the children struggled together within her — and she said: If it is thus — why is this to me? And she went to inquire of the Lord.
\v 23 And the Lord said to her: Two nations are in your womb and two peoples will be divided from your belly — and people will be stronger than people — and the greater will serve the lesser.
\v 24 And the days were fulfilled for her to give birth — and look — twins were in her womb.
\v 25 And the first came out red — all of him as a hairy garment — and she called his name Esau.
\v 26 And after this his brother came out — and his hand was holding the heel of Esau — and she called his name Jacob. And Isaac was sixty years old when she bore them.
\p
\v 27 And the children grew up — and Esau was a man knowing hunting — a man of the open-field — and Jacob was a simple man dwelling in houses.
\v 28 And Isaac loved Esau because his hunting was food for him — and Rebekah loved Jacob.
\p
\v 29 And Jacob boiled a stew — and Esau came from the open-field being weary.
\v 30 And Esau said to Jacob: Feed me from this red stew — because I am weary. Therefore his name was called Edom.
\v 31 And Jacob said to Esau: Sell me today your birthright.
\v 32 And Esau said: Look — I am going to die — and what use is this birthright to me?
\v 33 And Jacob said: Swear to me today. And he swore to him — and he sold his birthright to Jacob.
\v 34 And Jacob gave bread and a stew of lentils to Esau — and he ate and drank and rose up and went away. And Esau despised his birthright.

\v 1 And a famine came upon the land — besides the former famine which came in the days of Abraham — and Isaac went to Abimelech king of the Philistines to Gerar.
\v 2 And the Lord appeared to him and said: Do not go down to Egypt — dwell in the land which I will tell you.
\v 3 Sojourn in this land and I will be with you and will bless you — for to you and to your seed I will give all this land — and I will establish my oath which I swore to Abraham your father.
\v 4 And I will multiply your seed as the stars of heaven and I will give to your seed all this land — and in your seed all the nations of the earth will be blessed —
\v 5 because Abraham heard my voice and kept my ordinances and my commandments and my judgments and my laws.
\v 6 And Isaac dwelt in Gerar.
\v 7 And the men of that place asked about Rebekah his wife — and he said: She is my sister — for he was afraid to say: She is my wife — lest the men of that place kill him on account of Rebekah — for she was beautiful in appearance.
\v 8 And it came to pass when he had been there many days that Abimelech king of the Philistines looked through the window and saw — and look — Isaac was playing with Rebekah his wife.
\v 9 And Abimelech called Isaac and said: So she is really your wife — how did you say: She is my sister? And Isaac said to him: Because I said — lest I die on account of her.
\v 10 And Abimelech said: Why have you done this to us? Little was it that one of the people lay with your wife — and you would have brought guilt upon us.
\v 11 And Abimelech commanded all his people saying: Everyone who touches this man and his wife will die by death.
\p
\v 12 And Isaac sowed in that land and found in that year a hundredfold — and the Lord blessed him.
\v 13 And the man was exalted and went forward and was made great until he was very great.
\v 14 And he had possessions of sheep and possessions of cattle and much farming — and the Philistines envied him.
\v 15 And all the wells which the servants of his father had dug in the days of Abraham his father — the Philistines filled them up and covered them with earth.
\v 16 And Abimelech said to Isaac: Go away from us — for you have become much mightier than us.
\v 17 And Isaac went from there and camped in the ravine of Gerar and dwelt there.
\v 18 And Isaac dug again the wells of water which they dug in the days of Abraham his father and which the Philistines filled after the death of Abraham — and he called their names according to the names which his father called them.
\v 19 And the servants of Isaac dug in the ravine and found there a well of living water.
\v 20 And the herdsmen of Gerar quarreled with the herdsmen of Isaac saying: The water is ours. And he called the name of that well Unrighteous-dealing — for they dealt unrighteously with him.
\v 21 And departing from there he dug another well — and they quarreled about that also — and he called its name Enmity.
\v 22 And departing from there he dug another well — and they did not quarrel about that one — and he called its name Broad-places saying: Because now the Lord has made broad-places for us and increased us upon the land.
\v 23 And he went up from there to Beersheba.
\v 24 And the Lord appeared to him in that night and said: I am the God of Abraham your father — do not fear — for I am with you and will bless you and will multiply your seed on account of Abraham my servant.
\v 25 And he built there an altar and called upon the name of the Lord — and he pitched his tent there — and the servants of Isaac dug a well there.
\v 26 And Abimelech came to him from Gerar and Ochozath his friend and Phichol the commander of his army.
\v 27 And Isaac said to them: Why have you come to me — and you hated me and sent me away from you?
\v 28 And they said: We have clearly seen that the Lord was with you — and we said: Let there be an oath between us and between you — and let us make a covenant with you —
\v 29 that you will do no evil to us as we have not touched you and as we have dealt well with you and sent you away in peace — and now you are blessed by the Lord.
\v 30 And he made them a feast and they ate and drank.
\v 31 And rising early in the morning they swore — each man to his neighbor — and Isaac sent them away and they went from him in peace.
\v 32 And it came to pass on that day the servants of Isaac came and told him about the well which they had dug — and they said to him: We found water.
\v 33 And he called it Oath — therefore the name of that city is Beersheba until this day.

\v 35 And they were a bitterness of breath-of-life to Isaac and to Rebekah.

\v 1 And it came to pass when Isaac was old that his eyes were dimmed so as not to see — and he called Esau his elder son and said to him: My son. And he said: Look — I am here.
\v 2 And he said: Look — I am old and do not know the day of my death.
\v 3 And now take your weapons — your quiver and your bow — and go out into the open-field and hunt game for me.
\v 4 And make for me foods as I love them and bring them to me and I will eat — so that my soul may bless you before I die.
\v 5 And Rebekah heard when Isaac spoke to Esau his son — and Esau went into the open-field to hunt game and to bring it.
\v 6 And Rebekah said to Jacob her son: Look — I heard your father speaking to Esau your brother saying:
\v 7 Bring me game and make foods for me and I will eat and bless you before the Lord before I die.
\v 8 And now my son hear my voice in what I command you.
\v 9 Go now to the flock and take for me from there two kids of the goats — tender and good — and I will make them foods for your father as he loves them.
\v 10 And you will bring them to your father and he will eat — so that he may bless you before he dies.
\v 11 And Jacob said to Rebekah his mother: Esau my brother is a hairy man and I am a smooth man.
\v 12 Perhaps my father will feel me and I will be before him as one who deals perversely — and I will bring upon myself a curse and not a blessing.
\v 13 And his mother said to him: Upon me be your curse my son — only hear my voice and go and bring them to me.
\v 14 And he went and took and brought to his mother — and his mother made foods as his father loved.
\v 15 And Rebekah took the garments of Esau her elder son — the fine ones which were with her in the house — and put them on Jacob her younger son.
\v 16 And the skins of the kids she put on his arms and the smoothness of his neck.
\v 17 And she gave the foods and the loaves which she made into the hands of Jacob her son.
\v 18 And he brought them to his father and said: Father. And he said: Look — I am here — who are you my son?
\v 19 And Jacob said to his father: I am Esau your firstborn — I did as you spoke to me — rise up sit and eat of my game so that your soul may bless me.
\v 20 And Isaac said to his son: What is this you found so quickly my son? And he said: Because the Lord your God prepared it before me.
\v 21 And Isaac said to Jacob: Draw near to me and I will feel you my son — whether you are my son Esau or not.
\v 22 And Jacob drew near to Isaac his father — and he felt him and said: The voice is the voice of Jacob but the hands are the hands of Esau.
\v 23 And he did not recognize him — for his hands were as the hands of Esau his brother — hairy — and he blessed him.
\v 24 And he said: Are you my son Esau? And he said: I am.
\v 25 And he said: Bring it near to me and I will eat of the game of my son so that my soul may bless you. And he brought it near to him and he ate — and he brought him wine and he drank.
\v 26 And Isaac his father said to him: Draw near to me and kiss me my son.
\v 27 And he drew near and kissed him — and he smelled the smell of his garments and blessed him and said: Look — the smell of my son is as the smell of a full field which the Lord blessed.
\v 28 And may God give you from the dew of heaven and from the fatness of the earth — and abundance of grain and wine.
\v 29 And let peoples serve you and rulers bow down to you — and be lord over your brother and the sons of your father will bow down to you — the one who curses you is cursed and the one who blesses you is blessed.
\p
\v 30 And it came to pass when Isaac finished blessing Jacob his son — and Jacob had just gone out from the face of Isaac his father — that Esau his brother came in from his hunting.
\v 31 And he also made foods and brought them to his father and said to his father: Let my father rise up and eat of his son's game so that your soul may bless me.
\v 32 And Isaac his father said to him: Who are you? And he said: I am your son the firstborn — Esau.
\v 33 And Isaac was astounded with a great astonishment — and said: Who then is he who hunted game and brought it to me — and I ate of all before you came — and I blessed him and he will be blessed.
\v 34 And it came to pass when Esau heard the words of his father that he cried out with a great and very bitter cry — and said to his father: Bless me also father.
\v 35 And he said: Your brother came with deceit and took your blessing.
\v 36 And he said: Rightly was his name called Jacob — for he has supplanted me these two times — my birthright he took and now he took my blessing. And he said: Have you not kept a blessing for me?
\v 37 And Isaac answered and said to Esau: If I made him your lord and I gave him all his brothers as servants — and grain and wine I established for him — what then shall I do for you my son?
\v 38 And Esau said to his father: Is there only one blessing for you father? Bless me also father. And Esau lifted up his voice and wept.
\v 39 And Isaac his father answered and said to him: Look — your dwelling will be from the fatness of the earth and from the dew of heaven above.
\v 40 And by your sword you will live and you will serve your brother — and it will be when you break loose that you will break his yoke from your neck.
\p
\v 41 And Esau bore a grudge against Jacob because of the blessing with which his father blessed him — and Esau said in his heart: Let the days of mourning for my father draw near — and I will kill Jacob my brother.
\v 42 And the words of Esau her elder son were told to Rebekah — and she sent and called Jacob her younger son and said to him: Look — Esau your brother threatens you to kill you.
\v 43 And now my son hear my voice — and rise up and flee to Laban my brother to Haran.
\v 44 And dwell with him some days until the wrath of your brother turns away —
\v 45 until the anger of your brother turns away from you and he forgets what you did to him — and I will send and take you from there — why should I be bereaved of both of you in one day?
\v 46 And Rebekah said to Isaac: I am weary of my life because of the daughters of Heth — if Jacob takes a wife from the daughters of Heth like these from the daughters of the land — what good is life to me?

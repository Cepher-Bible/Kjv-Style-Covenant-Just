## STRUCTURAL NOTE — LXX BOOK 1

Book 1 closes at LXX Psalm 40 with the doxology: "Blessed is the Lord the God of Israel — from the age and to the age. Amen — Amen." The double Amen is the editorial seam-marker across all five books. The LXX Book 1 = MT Psalms 1–41 — the correspondence is exact here since the LXX/MT numbering divergence has not yet begun (it begins at LXX Psalm 9 which combines MT 9+10, but the book division falls after the rejoined sequence so Book 1 ends at the same point in both traditions).

## CAE NODES — BOOK 1

### Psalm 1 — *makarios* / blessed — the beatitude-formula opener

The Psalter opens with *makarios* = blessed — the same vocabulary as the Beatitudes of Matthew 5:3–12. *Makarios* = in a state of divine favor, happy in the deepest sense — from *makar* = blessed one. The Psalter's opening word sets the whole collection under the beatitude-register. The engine rendered "blessed" — not "happy" — preserving the divine-favor register throughout Book 1.

***anomous* / *hamartōlous* / *loimōn*** — three parallel negative figures in Psalm 1:1 — the ungodly / sinners / the pestilent. *Anomos* = lawless one (without *nomos* = law). *Hamartōlos* = sinner (from *hamartanō* = to miss the mark). *Loimos* = pestilent one, a plague-carrier — LSJ: a pest, a scoundrel. The three-fold descent from ungodly to sinner to pestilent marks the progressive alignment with evil the blessed man avoids. The engine preserved all three distinct terms.

***meletaō*** — "in his law he meditates day and night" — *meletaō* = to practice, to exercise, to meditate upon. LSJ: to care for, to practice assiduously. Not passive contemplation but active, repeated engagement — the same word used for an orator practicing a speech. The engine rendered "meditates" — the continual active return to the law.

---

### Psalm 2 — *huios mou ei sy* / you are my son — CAE PRIMARY NODE

**2:7** — "The Lord said to me: You are my son — today I have begotten you."

***huios mou ei sy*** = you are my son. *Egennēsa se* = I have begotten you — *gennaō* = to beget, the generative act. The "today" (*sēmeron*) of the begetting is the exegetical crux: the psalm's royal coronation context uses begetting-language for the king's enthronement.

**Acts 13:33** — Paul at Pisidian Antioch applies this to the resurrection as the day of begetting — the enthronement of the risen Son. **Hebrews 1:5** — the Son's superiority to angels grounded in this address. **Hebrews 5:5** — the high priestly appointment grounded in the sonship-declaration.

The engine rendered "today I have begotten you" — preserving the *gennaō* begetting-vocabulary the NT citations require.

***christos*** = the anointed one — "against the Lord and against his anointed." Acts 4:25–26 quotes Psalm 2:1–2 as fulfilled in the gathering against the Lord's anointed. The engine rendered "anointed" — keeping the LXX Greek register.

***poimaneis autous en rhabdō sidērā*** = you will shepherd them with a rod of iron — Revelation 2:27, 12:5, and 19:15 all cite this. The engine rendered "shepherd" for *poimainō* — not "rule" — preserving the shepherd-staff register.

---

### Psalm 8 — *hyios anthrōpou* / son of man — CAE NODE

**8:4–6** — "What is man that you remember him — or the son of man that you visit him? You made him a little less than angels — you crowned him with glory and honor — you subjected all things under his feet."

**Hebrews 2:6–8** quotes Psalm 8:4–6 extensively — Jesus as the true Son of Man who fulfills what Adam/humanity failed to do. The "little less than angels" receives the Hebrews reading as "for a little while less than angels" in the incarnation. **1 Corinthians 15:27** also cites "you subjected all things under his feet" in the resurrection-reign argument.

***elattōsas auton brachy ti par' angelous*** = you made him a little less than angels — the LXX renders Hebrew *'Elohim* as *angelous* = angels, which becomes the basis for Hebrews 2's argument. The engine rendered "a little less than angels" — the LXX reading.

---

### Psalm 15 (LXX) — *ouk egkataleiepseis tēn psychēn mou eis hadēn* — CAE NODE

**15:10** — "For you will not leave my soul in Hades — nor will you give your holy one to see corruption."

**Acts 2:27** — Peter at Pentecost quotes this verbatim as prophecy of the resurrection: David died and saw corruption — therefore David prophesied of another. **Acts 13:35** — Paul quotes it again with the same argument.

***hosiou*** = your holy one — *hosios* = the devout/pious/holy one. The engine rendered "your holy one" — the messianic designation.

**15:8** — "I set the Lord always before me — for he is at my right hand so that I may not be shaken." The *ek dexiōn mou* = at my right hand — the right-hand vocabulary connecting to Psalm 110:1.

---

### Psalm 17 (LXX) — The Great Davidic Psalm

***agapēsō se kyrie ischys mou*** = "I will love you O Lord my strength" (17:1) — *agapaō* opening the declaration of love — the Shema's love-command enacted in praise.

***ōdines hadou*** = "pangs of Hades" (17:5) — *ōdin* = birth-pang. Acts 2:24 — "God raised him up having loosed the pangs of death (*tas ōdinas tou thanatou*)" — fires on this exact vocabulary.

**17:49** — "Therefore I will confess to you among the nations O Lord — and I will sing a psalm to your name." Romans 15:9 quotes this in Paul's catena of OT texts supporting Gentile inclusion in worship.

---

### Psalm 21 (LXX) — The Passion Psalm — CAE PRIMARY NODE

LXX Psalm 21 = MT Psalm 22. The NT citation density of this psalm is unmatched in the entire Psalter.

**21:1** — "O God my God — attend to me — why have you forsaken me?" Matthew 27:46 and Mark 15:34 quote the cry from the cross — Psalm 21 is the psalm Jesus prays at the crucifixion.

**21:8** — "All who saw me mocked me — they spoke with their lips — they shook their heads." Matthew 27:39 — those passing by shaking their heads — direct citation.

**21:9** — "He hoped in the Lord — let him rescue him — let him save him for he delights in him." Matthew 27:43 — the mockers at the cross quote this directly.

**21:16** — "They pierced my hands and my feet." The LXX reads *ōryxan* = they dug/pierced — the piercing-reading that forms the basis for the NT passion typology.

**21:18** — "They divided my garments among themselves — and over my clothing they cast a lot." John 19:24 quotes this verbatim as fulfilled at the cross — one of the most explicit passion-fulfillment citations in the gospels. The engine preserved "garments" (*himatia*, plural) and "clothing" (*himatismon*, singular) as distinct — tracking John's two-garment distinction.

**21:22** — "I will recount your name to my brothers — in the midst of the gathering I will praise you." Hebrews 2:12 quotes this as the risen Christ announcing the Father's name to his brothers.

---

### Psalm 22 (LXX) — *kyrios poimainei me* / the Lord shepherds me

***skiā thanatou*** = shadow of death — the LXX compound rendered as "shadow of death" — not "deep darkness." The engine followed the LXX reading.

***to potērion sou*** = your cup — the *potērion* = cup vocabulary carries Eucharistic resonance (1 Corinthians 10:21 — *potērion kyriou* = the cup of the Lord).

---

### Psalm 23 (LXX) — *basileus tēs doxēs* / King of glory

The superscription "for the first day of the week" is a LXX liturgical addition — preserved by the engine. The everlasting-doors entry of the King of glory fires in ascension and enthronement typology throughout the NT.

---

### Psalm 30 (LXX) — *eis cheiras sou parathēsomai to pneuma mou* — CAE NODE

**30:5** — "Into your hands I will commit my breath-of-life — you ransomed-released me O Lord God of truth."

**Luke 23:46** — the dying words of Jesus from the cross are Psalm 30:5 LXX quoted verbatim: "Father into your hands I commit my breath-of-life." The engine rendered "breath-of-life" — not "spirit" — preserving the *pneuma* chain and the Luke 23:46 citation vocabulary.

---

### Psalm 31 (LXX) — *makarios hōn aphethēsan* — CAE NODE

**31:1–2** — "Blessed are those whose lawless deeds were forgiven — and whose sins were covered. Blessed is the man to whom the Lord will not reckon sin."

**Romans 4:7–8** quotes Psalm 31:1–2 as OT ground for imputed righteousness. The *logizomai* = reckon/count vocabulary connects Genesis 15:6 (Abraham) and Psalm 31 in Paul's argument. The engine rendered "reckon" for *logizomai* — the accounting-register vocabulary.

---

### Psalm 33 (LXX) — *geuasthe kai idete* / taste and see

**33:8** — "Taste and see that the Lord is good." 1 Peter 2:3 cites this as the experience of newborn believers longing for the word.

**33:20** — "He guards all his bones — not one of them will be broken." John 19:36 — "so that the scripture might be fulfilled: Not a bone of his will be broken." The bone-preservation of the righteous in Psalm 33:20 becomes the passion-fulfillment citation for Jesus' unbroken legs at the cross. The engine preserved "not one of them will be broken" at full precision.

---

### Psalm 39 (LXX) — *ōta de katērtisō moi* / ears you prepared for me — LXX/Hebrews divergence

**39:6** — "Sacrifice and offering you did not desire — but ears you prepared for me."

***ōta de katērtisō moi*** = but ears you prepared for me — the Rahlfs-Hanhart LXX reading. The MT reads "ears you opened/dug for me."

**Hebrews 10:5** cites this with *sōma de katērtisō moi* = "but a body you prepared for me" — a LXX text-form variant not in Rahlfs-Hanhart. The Hebrews application is to the incarnation: the body prepared for Christ as the vehicle of the once-for-all sacrifice.

**Engine decision:** Render the Rahlfs-Hanhart LXX text (*ōta* = ears). The Hebrews 10:5 variant is a notes-level observation — not a text-level correction.

**39:7** — "Look I come — in the heading of the scroll it is written concerning me." Hebrews 10:7 quotes this as the Son's words at the incarnation. The engine rendered "heading of the scroll" for *kephalidi bibliou* — *kephalís* = the knob at the top of a scroll-roller, hence the scroll's heading/beginning.

---

### Psalm 40 (LXX) — *ho trōgōn artous mou* / who ate my bread — CAE NODE

**40:9** — "For even the man of my peace in whom I trusted — who ate my bread — lifted up his heel against me."

**John 13:18** — Jesus at the Last Supper cites this as fulfilled in Judas's betrayal: "The one eating my bread lifted up his heel against me." The *trōgōn* = eating verb is the same used in John 6:54–58 for eating the flesh of the Son of Man — the Eucharistic register is present. The engine rendered "who ate my bread" preserving *trōgōn*.

## VOCABULARY DECISIONS — BOOK 1 SYSTEMATIC

**Hades** — rendered as "Hades" throughout (Psalms 6:5, 9:17, 15:10, 17:5, 29:3, 30:17) — never "hell" or "grave."

**Pneuma** — rendered as "breath-of-life" in all occurrences — both divine breath (32:6 — "by the breath-of-life of his mouth") and human breath-of-life (30:5 — "I will commit my breath-of-life").

**Dikaiosynē** — rendered as "righteousness" throughout — not "justice" (which is *krisis* / *krima*).

**Eleos** — rendered as "mercy" throughout — the LXX rendering of Hebrew *hesed* = covenant-faithfulness. Not "lovingkindness" or "love."

**Doxa** — rendered as "glory" throughout — not "honor" (*timē*) or "splendor."

**Exomologeomai** — rendered as "confess" in sin-confession register (31:5) and "confess to" in praise-thanksgiving register (17:49, 29:12) — the dative construction distinguishes the two.

**Makarios** — rendered as "blessed" throughout — not "happy."

## SUPERSCRIPTION VOCABULARY

***eis to telos*** = for the end — *telos* = end, completion, goal — rendered "for the end" throughout.

***diapsalma*** = preserved untranslated in text as `diapsalma` — the LXX rendering of *selah*.

## DOXOLOGY — CLOSE OF BOOK 1

**Psalm 40:13** — "Blessed is the Lord the God of Israel — from the age and to the age. Amen — Amen."

*Eulogētos* = blessed (of God) — the benediction-formula applied upward. *Apo tou aiōnos kai eis ton aiōna* = from the age and to the age. Double *amēn amēn* = congregational ratification preserved.

\ms Ode 1 — Song of Moses at the Sea
\mr (Exodus 15:1–19)

\c 1
\q1
\v 1 Then Moses and the sons of Israel sang this song to God —
\q2 and they spoke saying:
\q1 Let us sing to the Lord — for he has triumphed gloriously —
\q2 horse and rider he has thrown into the sea.
\q1
\v 2 He has become my helper and my protector for salvation —
\q2 he is my God and I will glorify him —
\q1 the God of my father and I will exalt him.
\q1
\v 3 The Lord shattering wars —
\q2 Lord is his name.
\q1
\v 4 The chariots of Pharaoh and his army he threw into the sea —
\q2 the chosen officers were submerged in the Red Sea.
\q1
\v 5 The deep covered them —
\q2 they went down into the depths like a stone.
\q1
\v 6 Your right hand O Lord has been glorified in strength —
\q2 your right hand O Lord shattered the enemies.
\q1
\v 7 And in the multitude of your glory you crushed the adversaries —
\q2 you sent out your wrath — it consumed them like stubble.
\q1
\v 8 And by the breath of your wrath water was parted —
\q2 the waters stood like a wall —
\q1 the waves were congealed in the heart of the sea.
\q1
\v 9 The enemy said: I will pursue — I will overtake —
\q2 I will divide the spoil — my soul shall be filled —
\q1 I will draw my sword — my hand shall destroy them.
\q1
\v 10 You sent your wind and the sea covered them —
\q2 they sank like lead in the mighty water.
\q1
\v 11 Who is like you among the gods O Lord —
\q2 who is like you — glorified among the holy ones —
\q1 marvelous in glory — doing wonders?
\q1
\v 12 You stretched out your right hand —
\q2 the earth swallowed them.
\q1
\v 13 You guided in your righteousness this your people whom you redeemed —
\q2 you comforted them by your strength to your holy dwelling place.
\q1
\v 14 Nations heard and were angry —
\q2 pangs seized the inhabitants of Philistia.
\q1
\v 15 Then the rulers of Edom were alarmed —
\q2 trembling seized the mighty men of Moab —
\q1 all the inhabitants of Canaan melted away.
\q1
\v 16 Let trembling and fear fall upon them —
\q2 by the greatness of your arm let them be still as stone —
\q1 until your people pass over O Lord —
\q2 until this people pass over whom you have purchased.
\q1
\v 17 Bring them in and plant them on the mountain of your inheritance —
\q2 in the place you made ready to dwell in O Lord —
\q1 a sanctuary O Lord which your hands have made ready.
\q1
\v 18 The Lord reigns to the age and beyond.
\p
\v 19 For the horse of Pharaoh went with his chariots and horsemen into the sea — and the Lord brought back upon them the water of the sea — but the sons of Israel walked on dry land in the middle of the sea.

## STRUCTURAL NOTE — THE ODES

The Odes (*Ōdai*) are a liturgical collection appended to the Psalter in major LXX manuscripts — most importantly Codex Alexandrinus (5th c.) and Codex Sinaiticus. They represent the biblical canticles used in early Christian and Jewish liturgy. The Rahlfs-Hanhart edition includes fourteen Odes drawn from across the entire canon — Old Testament, deuterocanon, and New Testament.

The collection is canonical in Eastern Orthodoxy and is the only LXX book that contains explicit NT texts (Odes 9 and 12 — Luke 1 and 2) embedded within the OT canon. This canonical positioning is theologically significant: the Magnificat, Benedictus, and Nunc Dimittis are received as fulfillments of the OT hymnic tradition, placed alongside Moses, Hannah, Habakkuk, Isaiah, Jonah, and the Three Young Men.

**The fourteen Odes:**

## CAE NODES — ODES

### Ode 1 (Exodus 15) — *tis homoios soi en theois* / who is like you among the gods — CAE NODE

**Ode 1:11** — "Who is like you among the gods O Lord — who is like you — glorified among the holy ones — marvelous in glory — doing wonders?"

The *tis homoios* = who is like formula — the foundational incomparability declaration of the OT. **Revelation 13:4** — "Who is like the beast (*tis homoios tō thēriō*)?" — the anti-Ode parody: the beast usurps the divine *tis homoios* formula. The Odes 1 context makes the Revelation irony explicit — the question that belongs only to God is asked of the beast.

**Exodus 15:11 / Ode 1:11 → Revelation 15:3** — "Great and marvelous are your works O Lord God the Almighty — just and true are your ways O King of the nations." The heavenly song in Revelation 15:3–4 is explicitly called "the song of Moses the servant of God and the song of the Lamb" — it draws directly on Ode 1 / Exodus 15.

---

### Ode 2 (Deuteronomy 32) — Structural Notes — CAE NODES

**Ode 2:8** — "When the Most High apportioned the nations — when he divided the sons of Adam — he fixed the boundaries of the peoples — according to the number of the angels of God."

The LXX Deuteronomy 32:8 reads *angelōn theou* = angels of God — the MT reads *bene yisra'el* = sons of Israel — the DSS read *bene 'elohim* = sons of God/divine beings. The LXX preserves what the Qumran evidence confirms is an older reading: the nations were assigned to angelic guardians. **Daniel 10:13,20–21** — the guardian angels of Persia and Greece — the *sar* = prince vocabulary — assumes this Deuteronomy 32:8 cosmology. **Colossians 1:16** — "thrones or lordships or rulers or authorities" — the powers that Christ created and surpasses.

**Ode 2:17** — "They sacrificed to demons and not to God." ***ethysan daimoniois kai ou theō*** = they sacrificed to demons and not to God. **1 Corinthians 10:20** — "the things which the nations sacrifice they sacrifice to demons (*daimoniois*) and not to God — and I do not want you to be sharers with demons." Paul cites Deuteronomy 32:17 / Ode 2:17 directly.

**Ode 2:21** — "I will provoke them with what is no nation — with a foolish nation I will anger them." **Romans 10:19** — Paul cites Deuteronomy 32:21 as the prophetic basis for Gentile inclusion: God will provoke Israel with a *no-nation* (the Gentiles).

**Ode 2:35** — "In the day of vengeance I will repay." **Romans 12:19** — "Vengeance is mine — I will repay — says the Lord." Paul's citation of Deuteronomy 32:35.

**Ode 2:43** — "Rejoice O heavens with him — and let all the sons of God worship him — rejoice O nations with his people." **Romans 15:10** — Paul cites Deuteronomy 32:43 as a proof-text for Gentile inclusion in the praise of God.

The Song of Moses (Ode 2) is one of the most-cited OT texts in the NT — Paul alone cites it at least five times in Romans 9–15.

---

### Ode 3 (Hannah) — *anestēsen* / he raises — CAE PRIMARY NODE

**Ode 3:6–8** — "The Lord kills and makes alive — he brings down to Hades and brings back up. The Lord makes poor and makes rich — he humbles and he lifts up. He raises the poor from the earth — and lifts up the needy from the dunghill — to seat them with the powerful of the people."

**Luke 1:52–53** — "He brought down rulers from their thrones and exalted the humble — the hungry he filled with good things and the rich he sent away empty." The Magnificat (Ode 9) draws directly on Hannah's prayer (Ode 3) — the pattern of divine reversal (*peripeteia*) established in Hannah is the template for Mary. The LXX sequence Ode 3 → Ode 9 makes this typological connection structurally explicit: Hannah's song → Mary's song.

**1 Samuel 2:6 / Ode 3:6** — *Kyrios thanatoi kai zōopoiei* = the Lord kills and makes alive — **2 Corinthians 1:9** — "we had the sentence of death in ourselves — that we should not trust in ourselves but in God who raises the dead (*tō egeiironti tous nekrous*)." The Hannah-Ode vocabulary of God killing and making alive underlies the NT resurrection theology.

**1:9** — *anypse keras christou autou* = he will exalt the horn of his anointed — the *christos* = anointed vocabulary. Hannah's prayer ends with a prophecy of the anointed king — which the NT reads as the first direct *christos* oracle in the canonical sequence. **Luke 1:69** — Zechariah: "and raised up a horn of salvation for us in the house of David his servant" — drawing on Hannah's closing verse.

---

### Ode 4 (Habakkuk) — *en mesō dyo zōōn gnōsthēsē* / in the midst of two living creatures — CAE NODE

**Ode 4:2** — "in the midst of two living creatures you will be known."

The LXX Habakkuk 3:2 reads *en mesō dyo zōōn gnōsthēsē* = in the midst of two living creatures you will be known — a reading without clear Hebrew equivalent (MT has "in the midst of years revive it"). The patristic tradition read the "two living creatures" as the two cherubim over the ark or (in the christological reading) the two thieves at the cross.

**Revelation 4:6–8** — the four *zōa* = living creatures before the throne — the *tris hagios* = thrice holy — the Ode 4 / Habakkuk visionary vocabulary of God made known amid living creatures fires in the Revelation throne-room.

**Ode 4:13** — "You went out for the salvation of your people — to save your anointed (*ton christon sou*)." The Habakkuk prayer climaxes with *ho christos sou* = your anointed — a messianic reading of the divine warrior who comes for his people's salvation.

---

### Ode 5 (Isaiah 26) — *anastēsontai hoi nekroi* / the dead will rise — CAE PRIMARY NODE

**Ode 5:19** — "The dead will rise — and those in the tombs will be raised — and those in the earth will rejoice — for the dew from you is healing to them."

***anastēsontai hoi nekroi kai egerthēsontai hoi en tois mnēmeiois*** = the dead will rise and those in the tombs will be raised. This is one of the clearest resurrection affirmations in the OT — the LXX reading of Isaiah 26:19. **John 5:28–29** — "the hour is coming when all who are in the tombs will hear his voice and come out." The *hoi en tois mnēmeiois* = those in the tombs vocabulary of Ode 5 fires in the Johannine resurrection teaching.

**1 Corinthians 15:54** — "Death was swallowed up in victory" (citing Isaiah 25:8 — the preceding context of Ode 5). The resurrection chapter of 1 Corinthians 15 draws on the Isaiah 24–27 apocalypse of which Ode 5 is the liturgical heart.

---

### Ode 6 (Jonah) — *ek koilias hadou* / from the belly of Hades — CAE PRIMARY NODE

**Ode 6:3** — "I called in my affliction to the Lord my God — and he heard me — out of the belly of Hades you heard my cry."

***ek koilias hadou*** = from the belly of Hades. The Jonah prayer reframes the fish's belly as *Hades* = the realm of the dead — a death-and-resurrection typology.

**Matthew 12:40** — "For just as Jonah was in the belly of the sea monster three days and three nights — so will the Son of Man be in the heart of the earth three days and three nights." Jesus explicitly identifies his death and resurrection with the Jonah-sign — the *koilia* = belly / *hadēs* vocabulary of Ode 6.

**Ode 6:9** — "Those who guard vanities and lies — abandoned their own mercy." **Romans 1:25** — "who exchanged the truth of God for the lie (*to pseudei*)." The Jonah vocabulary of *mataiotētes kai pseudē* = vanities and lies = idolatry — the same tradition Paul draws on.

---

### Ode 7 (Prayer of Azariah) — *ouk estin hēmin* / there is no longer — CAE PRIMARY NODE

**Ode 7:38–40** — "And there is at this time no ruler — no prophet — no leader — no whole burnt offering — no sacrifice — no oblation — no incense — no place to make an offering before you and to find mercy. Yet with a contrite soul and a humble breath-of-life — may we be accepted. As though it were with whole burnt offerings of rams and bulls... so let our sacrifice be before you today."

The Prayer of Azariah is theologically one of the most important deuterocanonical texts — written from within the Babylonian exile (or post-exile) as a confession and petition for a people without Temple, sacrifice, or cult. The prayer proposes that a *contrite soul and humble breath-of-life* = a crushed and lowly spirit is itself an acceptable sacrifice when the Temple-system has been removed.

**Psalm 50:19 (LXX) / Psalm 51:17 (MT)** — "A sacrifice to God is a broken spirit — a broken and contrite heart God will not despise." The Azariah prayer develops the Psalm 51 sacrifice-of-the-spirit theology into a full confessional-prayer for the covenant community stripped of its cult.

**Hebrews 13:15** — "Through him then let us continually offer up a sacrifice of praise to God — that is the fruit of lips confessing his name." The Hebrews *thysia aineseos* = sacrifice of praise as the NT fulfillment of the Azariah prayer's *contrite spirit = acceptable sacrifice* theology.

**Romans 12:1** — "present your bodies as a living sacrifice (*thysian zōsan*) — holy and acceptable to God — your reasonable worship." Paul's *logikē latreia* = reasonable/spiritual worship echoes the Azariah prayer's displacement of animal sacrifice with the offering of the self.

---

### Ode 8 (Song of the Three) — *eulogeite panta ta erga* / bless the Lord all you works — CAE NODE

**Ode 8:57–88** — The great *eulogios* = bless-the-Lord litany through all creation — heaven, angels, waters, powers, sun, moon, stars, rain, dew, winds, fire, cold, night, day, light, darkness, frost, lightning, clouds, earth, mountains, hills, growing things, springs, seas, rivers, sea creatures, birds, beasts, sons of men, Israel, priests, servants, souls of the righteous, holy and humble of heart, Hananiah-Azariah-Mishael.

**Psalm 148** (the *Hallelujah*-creation-praise) is the OT antecedent. The Song of the Three develops the cosmic praise from the Psalter into a full creation-litany. **Revelation 5:13** — "And every creature in heaven and on earth and under the earth and in the sea and all that is in them I heard saying: To him who sits on the throne and to the Lamb be blessing and honor and glory and might to the ages of the ages." The Revelation 5 cosmic doxology is the eschatological fulfillment of the Ode 8 litany — every creature blessing the Lord.

**Ode 8:88** — "Bless the Lord Hananiah — Azariah — Mishael — for he has rescued us from Hades (*hadou*) — and saved us from the hand of death." The explicit *Hades*-rescue terminology — the furnace as death-and-resurrection type. **Hebrews 11:34** — "quenched the power of fire." The Three Young Men are the Hebrews 11 faith-heroes who *quenched fire* — the Ode 8 furnace-rescue is the faith-act Hebrews commends.

---

### Ode 9 (Magnificat + Benedictus) — NT TEXTS IN THE LXX CANON — CAE PRIMARY NODE

The embedding of Luke 1:46–79 within the LXX Odes is the canonical declaration that the Magnificat and Benedictus are *fulfillments* of the OT hymnic tradition — they stand as the ninth Ode, after the eight OT canticles, as the fulfillment-song.

**Ode 9 / Luke 1:46–55 — Magnificat:**

The Magnificat is structurally a mosaic of OT Odes:
- **Ode 3 (Hannah) 1:1** — "my heart is made strong in the Lord / my soul magnifies the Lord"
- **Ode 3:7** — "he humbles and lifts up / he exalted the humble"
- **Ode 3:8** — "he raises the poor from the earth / the hungry he filled with good things"
- **Ode 2:2** / Psalm 110:9 — "holy is his name"
- **Ode 2:9** — "according to the number of his angels / his mercy to generations"

The Magnificat is Hannah's Ode (Ode 3) brought to eschatological fulfillment — Mary = New Hannah, the barren/humble woman through whom God's salvation enters the world.

**Key vocabulary:**
- *megalynei* = magnifies — rendered "magnifies" — the *megalynō* = to make great vocabulary
- *tapeinōsin* = humiliation — rendered "humiliation" — the *tapeinōsis* of the handmaiden = the low estate, the humbling
- *dynastas* = rulers/powerful — rendered "rulers" — the *dynastai* = dynasts brought down from thrones
- *tapeinous* = humble — rendered "humble" — the *tapeinoi* exalted
- *elpizontōn ep' auton* — "those who hope in him" vocabulary

**Ode 9 / Luke 1:68–79 — Benedictus:**

**1:68** — "Blessed is the Lord God of Israel — for he visited and worked redemption for his people." ***epeskepsato kai epoiēsen lytrōsin tō laō autou*** = he visited and worked redemption. The *episkeptomai* = visit + *lytrōsis* = redemption formula — **Exodus 3:16** — "I have visited (*epeskemmai*) you" — the divine visitation-to-redeem vocabulary of the Exodus now fulfilled.

**1:69** — "He raised up a horn of salvation for us in the house of David his servant." *keras sōtērias* = horn of salvation — the *keras* = horn of Hannah's Ode (Ode 3:1) — "my horn has been exalted" — fulfilled in the Davidic horn of salvation.

**1:76** — "And you child will be called prophet of the Most High." John = the Elijah-prophet before the Lord — **Sirach 48:10** / **Malachi 4:6** chain.

**1:78** — "The rising-sun (*anatolē*) will visit us from on high." ***anatolē ex hypsous*** = rising-sun from on high — the *anatolē* = rising / dawn / branch vocabulary. **Zechariah 3:8 / 6:12** — "My servant the Branch (*anatolē*)." The Zechariah-*anatolē* = Branch messianic title applied to Jesus — the sunrise-from-on-high who comes to illuminate those in darkness and death-shadow. The engine rendered "rising-sun" — preserving the *anatolē* = sunrise/branch dual register.

### Ode 10 (Hezekiah) — *ek pulōn hadou* / from the gates of Hades — CAE NODE

**Ode 10:18–19** — "For those in Hades will not praise you — nor will the dead bless you — nor will those going down into the pit hope in your truth. The living will bless you as I do today."

The Hezekiah prayer from Isaiah 38 — the king delivered from death's threshold — is the OT's paradigm of individual rescue from Hades. **Matthew 16:18** — "the gates of Hades (*pylai hadou*) will not prevail against it." The *pylai hadou* = gates of Hades that Hezekiah cries from at Ode 10:10 ("I will go to the gates of Hades") are the same gates Jesus declares will not prevail against the church.

### Ode 11 (Prayer of Manasseh) — *metanoia* / repentance — CAE PRIMARY NODE

**Ode 11:5–6** — "For you are the Lord Most High — of great compassion — patient and plentiful in mercy — and repenting over the evils of men. You O Lord according to the abundance of your goodness — promised repentance and forgiveness to those who sinned against you — and in the multitude of your compassions you appointed repentance for sinners — unto salvation."

The Prayer of Manasseh is the OT's fullest theology of repentance — composed for the most extreme sinner in the Kings narrative (Manasseh set up idols in the Temple, sacrificed his children, filled Jerusalem with innocent blood — 2 Kings 21). The prayer grounds divine forgiveness not in the sinner's worthiness but in the divine character — *polyeleos* = plentiful in mercy — *metamelomenos* = repenting over evils.

**Ode 11:7** — "You O God of the righteous did not appoint repentance for the righteous — for Abraham and Isaac and Jacob who did not sin against you — but you appointed repentance for me who am a sinner." The radical sinners-only-need-repentance logic — **Matthew 9:13** — "I did not come to call the righteous but sinners." **Luke 15:7** — "there will be more joy in heaven over one sinner who repents than over ninety-nine righteous persons who need no repentance." The Manasseh-prayer logic is the direct background for Jesus's *I came for sinners* sayings.

**Ode 11:10–12** — "And now I bow the knee of my heart — imploring your goodness — I have sinned O Lord — I have sinned — and I acknowledge my transgressions. But I pray and implore you — forgive me O Lord — forgive me." ***kamptō gony kardias mou*** = I bow the knee of my heart. The *kampsis gonatos* = bending of the knee. **Philippians 2:10** — "at the name of Jesus every knee will bow." **Ephesians 3:14** — "I bow my knees before the Father." The *kamptō gonon* = bow the knee as the posture of prayer and worship.

### Ode 12 (Nunc Dimittis) — *apolyeis ton doulon sou* / you release your servant — CAE PRIMARY NODE

**Ode 12 / Luke 2:29–32** — "Now release your servant O Master — according to your word — in peace — for my eyes have seen your salvation — which you prepared before the face of all the peoples — a light for revelation to the nations — and glory to your people Israel."

***nun apolyeis ton doulon sou Despota*** = now you release your servant O Master. *Apolyō* = to release/dismiss/set free — the same word used for dismissal after a completed service. Simeon has completed his watch — he is *dismissed in peace* having seen what he waited for.

The Nunc Dimittis as the final NT canticle in the Odes collection recapitulates the entire arc:
- Ode 1 (Exodus) → Ode 9 (Magnificat): Israel's salvation history
- Ode 12 (Nunc Dimittis): the completion of that history — *my eyes have seen your salvation*

**Isaiah 52:10** — "The Lord has bared his holy arm before the eyes of all the nations — and all the ends of the earth will see the salvation of our God." The Simeon vision: *salvation prepared before all peoples* = the Isaiah 52 universal-salvation vision fulfilled in the child.

**Isaiah 49:6** — "I will give you as a light to the nations — to bring my salvation to the ends of the earth." The *phōs eis apokalypsin ethnōn* = light for revelation to the nations of Ode 12 cites Isaiah 49:6 directly.

### Ode 13 (Morning Hymn / Gloria) — Structural Note

The Morning Hymn is the *Doxa en hypsistois* = Glory in the highest — the angelic song of **Luke 2:14** expanded into a full trinitarian doxology. Its canonical placement as Ode 13 — after the Nunc Dimittis — follows the narrative sequence: the angels sing at the birth (Luke 2:14) → Simeon blesses at the presentation (Luke 2:29–32) → the morning hymn continues the angelic praise liturgically.

The trinitarian structure: Father (*Kyrie Pantokratōr* = Lord Almighty) → Son (*Kyrie Monogenes* = Lord Only-Begotten) → Holy Breath-of-Life. The *Amnos tou theou* = Lamb of God vocabulary — **John 1:29** — "Look — the Lamb of God who takes away the sin of the world." The engine rendered "Holy Breath-of-Life" for *Hagion Pneuma* — consistent with the *pneuma* = breath-of-life register maintained throughout the project.

### Ode 14 (Great Doxology / Te Deum) — Structural Note

The Great Doxology draws on the Latin *Te Deum laudamus* tradition and the Eastern *Megalē Doxologia* — both rooted in the Ode 8 creation-litany pattern. Key NT vocabularies:

- *Agios agios agios* = holy holy holy → Isaiah 6:3 / Revelation 4:8
- *Plerē he doxa* = full of glory → Isaiah 6:3
- *Amnos tou theou* = Lamb of God → John 1:29 / Revelation 5:6
- *Kathēmenos en dexia tou patros* = sitting at the right hand of the Father → Psalm 109:1 / Acts 2:33

The engine rendered "Holy Breath-of-Life" for the Spirit throughout — and "the Comforter" for *Paraklētos* — consistent with the Johannine *paraklētos* vocabulary (John 14:16,26; 15:26; 16:7).

## CANONICAL NOTE — THE ODES AS FULFILLMENT STRUCTURE

The Odes collection embodies the LXX canon's own typological structure:

**Odes 1–2** (Moses): Creation of the covenant people through the Exodus — the paradigm event
**Ode 3** (Hannah): Individual salvation within the covenant — the barren made fruitful
**Odes 4–6** (Habakkuk / Isaiah / Jonah): Prophetic response to judgment — lament and trust
**Odes 7–8** (Daniel additions): Covenant faithfulness under persecution — fire-furnace-rescue
**Ode 9** (Mary + Zechariah): Fulfillment of all OT canticles — the new Moses-event
**Ode 10** (Hezekiah): Personal rescue from death
**Ode 11** (Manasseh): Repentance of the worst sinner — grace beyond all sin
**Ode 12** (Simeon): The completed watch — *my eyes have seen your salvation*
**Odes 13–14**: The church's ongoing praise — morning and evening doxology

The Odes are the LXX's own theology-in-miniature: creation → covenant → exile → restoration → incarnation → doxology. Every major canonical movement is present. Rendering them completes the liturgical frame of the Psalter.

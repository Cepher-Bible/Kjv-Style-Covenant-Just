# V2.2

```
\id ECC
\h Ecclesiastes
\toc1 Ecclesiastes
\toc2 Qoheleth
\mt Ecclesiastes

\c 1
\p
\v 1 The words of the Assembler (*Qoheleth*) — son of David — king in Jerusalem.
\p
\v 2 Vapor of vapors (*mataiotēs mataiotētōn*) — says the Assembler — vapor of vapors — all is vapor (*mataiotēs*).
\v 3 What profit is there for a man in all his toil at which he toils under the sun?
\v 4 A generation passes and a generation comes — but the earth stands forever (*eis ton aiōna*).
\v 5 And the sun rises and the sun sets and it hastens to its place and there it rises.
\v 6 Going to the south and turning to the north — turning — turning goes the wind and on its turnings the wind returns.
\v 7 All the rivers run into the sea and the sea is not full — to the place where the rivers go there they go again.
\v 8 All words are weary — a man cannot speak them — the eye is not satisfied with seeing and the ear is not filled with hearing.
\v 9 That which was is that which shall be and that which is done is that which shall be done — and there is nothing new under the sun.
\v 10 Is there a thing of which one says: See this is new? It has already been in the ages that were before us.
\v 11 There is no remembrance of former things — nor shall there be any remembrance of things that are to come with those who come after.
\p
\v 12 I the Assembler was king over Israel in Jerusalem.
\v 13 And I gave my heart to seek and to search out by wisdom (*sophia*) concerning all that is done under heaven — it is a grievous task that God has given to the sons of man to be afflicted with.
\v 14 I have seen all the works that are done under the sun — and behold all is vapor (*mataiotēs*) and a feeding on wind (*proairesis pneumatos*).
\v 15 What is crooked cannot be made straight and what is lacking cannot be counted.
\p
\v 16 I spoke with my own heart saying: Behold I have magnified and increased wisdom (*sophia*) above all who were before me over Jerusalem — and my heart has seen much of wisdom (*sophia*) and knowledge.
\v 17 And I gave my heart to know wisdom (*sophia*) and to know madness and folly — I know that this also is a feeding on wind (*proairesis pneumatos*).
\v 18 For in much wisdom (*sophia*) is much grief — and he who increases knowledge increases sorrow.

\c 2
\p
\v 1 I said in my heart: Come now I will test you with pleasure — enjoy yourself. And behold this also was vapor (*mataiotēs*).
\v 2 Of laughter I said: It is madness — and of pleasure: What does it accomplish?
\v 3 I searched in my heart how to refresh my flesh with wine — my heart conducting itself with wisdom (*sophia*) — and how to lay hold on folly until I might see what is good for the sons of man to do under heaven during the few days of their life (*zōēs*).
\p
\v 4 I made great works — I built houses — I planted vineyards for myself.
\v 5 I made myself gardens and parks and I planted in them trees of every fruit.
\v 6 I made myself pools of water to water the forest of growing trees.
\v 7 I acquired male and female servants and had servants born in my house — I also had great possessions of herds and flocks more than all who were before me in Jerusalem.
\v 8 I also gathered for myself silver and gold and the treasure of kings and provinces — I got singers — both men and women — and the delights of the sons of man — a wine-steward and wine-stewardesses.
\v 9 So I became great and surpassed all who were before me in Jerusalem — also my wisdom (*sophia*) stood firm with me.
\v 10 And whatever my eyes desired I did not keep from them — I did not withhold my heart from any pleasure — for my heart rejoiced in all my toil and this was my portion from all my toil.
\v 11 Then I turned to all my works that my hands had done and to the toil that I had toiled in doing — and behold all was vapor (*mataiotēs*) and a feeding on wind (*proairesis pneumatos*) and there was no profit under the sun.
\p
\v 12 So I turned to consider wisdom (*sophia*) and madness and folly — for what can the man do who comes after the king? Only what has already been done.
\v 13 And I saw that wisdom (*sophia*) has the advantage over folly as light has the advantage over darkness.
\v 14 The wise man has his eyes in his head but the fool walks in darkness — and yet I knew that one fate comes to all of them.
\v 15 And I said in my heart: What happens to the fool will happen to me also — and why have I been so very wise? And I said in my heart that this also is vapor (*mataiotēs*).
\v 16 For there is no remembrance of the wise man even as there is none of the fool forever (*eis ton aiōna*) — since in the days to come all will have been forgotten. And how can the wise man die together with the fool?
\v 17 So I hated life (*zōēn*) — because what is done under the sun was grievous to me — for all is vapor (*mataiotēs*) and a feeding on wind (*proairesis pneumatos*).
\p
\v 18 I hated all my toil in which I toil under the sun — seeing that I must leave it to the man who comes after me.
\v 19 And who knows whether he will be a wise man or a fool? Yet he will have control over all my toil at which I toiled and used wisdom (*sophia*) under the sun — this also is vapor (*mataiotēs*).
\v 20 So I turned about and gave my heart up to despair over all the toil at which I had toiled under the sun.
\v 21 For a man who has toiled with wisdom (*sophia*) and knowledge and skill must leave everything to be enjoyed by a man who did not toil for it — this also is vapor (*mataiotēs*) and a great evil.
\v 22 For what comes to a man from all his toil and from the striving of his heart at which he toils under the sun?
\v 23 For all his days are full of sorrow and his work is a vexation — even in the night his heart does not rest — this also is vapor (*mataiotēs*).
\p
\v 24 There is nothing better for a man than that he should eat and drink and that his soul (*psychē*) should enjoy good in his toil — this also I saw is from the hand of God.
\v 25 For who can eat and who can have enjoyment apart from him?
\v 26 For to the man who pleases him God gives wisdom (*sophia*) and knowledge and joy — but to the sinner he gives the task of gathering and collecting to give to one who pleases God. This also is vapor (*mataiotēs*) and a feeding on wind (*proairesis pneumatos*).

\c 3
\p
\v 1 For everything there is a season and a time for every matter under heaven —
\v 2 a time to be born and a time to die — a time to plant and a time to pluck up what is planted —
\v 3 a time to kill and a time to heal — a time to break down and a time to build up —
\v 4 a time to weep and a time to laugh — a time to mourn and a time to dance —
\v 5 a time to cast away stones and a time to gather stones together — a time to embrace and a time to refrain from embracing —
\v 6 a time to seek and a time to lose — a time to keep and a time to cast away —
\v 7 a time to tear and a time to sew — a time to keep silence and a time to speak —
\v 8 a time to love and a time to hate — a time for war and a time for peace.
\p
\v 9 What profit has the worker in his toil?
\v 10 I have seen the task that God has given to the sons of man to be afflicted with.
\v 11 He has made everything beautiful in its time — he has also put eternity (*ton aiōna*) into man's heart yet so that he cannot find out what God has done from the beginning to the end.
\v 12 I perceived that there is nothing better for them than to be glad and to do good as long as they live (*zōsin*).
\v 13 Also that everyone should eat and drink and take pleasure in all his toil — this is God's gift to man.
\v 14 I perceived that whatever God does endures forever (*eis ton aiōna*) — nothing can be added to it nor anything taken from it — God has done it so that people should fear before him.
\v 15 That which is has already been — that which is to be has already been — and God seeks out what has been driven away.
\p
\v 16 Moreover I saw under the sun that in the place of justice even there was wickedness and in the place of righteousness even there was wickedness.
\v 17 I said in my heart: God will judge the righteous and the wicked — for there is a time for every matter and for every work.
\v 18 I said in my heart with regard to the children of man that God is testing them to show them that they are but beasts.
\v 19 For what happens to the children of man and what happens to the beasts is the same — as one dies so dies the other — they all have the same breath (*pneuma*) and man has no advantage over the beasts — for all is vapor (*mataiotēs*).
\v 20 All go to one place — all are from the dust and all turn to dust again.
\v 21 Who knows whether the breath-of-life (*pneuma*) of man goes upward and the breath-of-life (*pneuma*) of the beast goes down into the earth?
\v 22 So I saw that there is nothing better than that a man should rejoice in his work — for that is his lot — who can bring him to see what will be after him?

\c 4
\p
\v 1 Again I saw all the oppressions that are done under the sun — and behold the tears of the oppressed and they had no one to comfort them — on the side of their oppressors there was power and they had no one to comfort them.
\v 2 And I declared the dead who are already dead more fortunate than the living who are still alive.
\v 3 But better than both is he who has not yet been and has not seen the evil work that is done under the sun.
\p
\v 4 Then I saw that all toil and all skill in work come from a man's envy of his neighbor — this also is vapor (*mataiotēs*) and a feeding on wind (*proairesis pneumatos*).
\v 5 The fool folds his hands and eats his own flesh.
\v 6 Better is a handful of quietness than two hands full of toil and a feeding on wind (*proairesis pneumatos*).
\p
\v 7 Again I saw vapor (*mataiotēs*) under the sun —
\v 8 one person who has no other — neither son nor brother — yet there is no end to all his toil and his eyes are never satisfied with riches so that he never asks: For whom am I toiling and depriving my soul (*psychēn*) of good? This also is vapor (*mataiotēs*) and an unhappy business.
\v 9 Two are better than one because they have a good reward for their toil.
\v 10 For if they fall one will lift up his fellow — but woe to him who is alone when he falls and has not another to lift him up.
\v 11 Again if two lie together they keep warm — but how can one keep warm alone?
\v 12 And though a man might prevail against one who is alone — two will withstand him — a threefold cord is not quickly broken.
\p
\v 13 Better was a poor and wise youth than an old and foolish king who no longer knew how to take advice.
\v 14 For he went from prison to reign — even in his own kingdom he had been born poor.
\v 15 I saw all the living who move about under the sun along with that youth who was to stand in the king's place.
\v 16 There was no end of all the people — all of whom he led — yet those who come later will not rejoice in him. Surely this also is vapor (*mataiotēs*) and a feeding on wind (*proairesis pneumatos*).

\c 5
\p
\v 1 Guard your steps when you go to the house of God — to draw near to listen is better than to offer the sacrifice of fools — for they do not know that they are doing evil.
\v 2 Be not rash with your mouth nor let your heart be hasty to utter a word before God — for God is in heaven and you are on earth — therefore let your words be few.
\v 3 For a dream comes with much business and a fool's voice with many words.
\v 4 When you vow a vow to God do not delay paying it — for he has no pleasure in fools — pay what you vow.
\v 5 It is better that you should not vow than that you should vow and not pay.
\v 6 Let not your mouth lead you into sin and do not say before the messenger that it was a mistake — why should God be angry at your voice and destroy the work of your hands?
\v 7 For when dreams increase and words grow many there is vapor (*mataiotēs*) — but fear God.
\p
\v 8 If you see in a province the oppression of the poor and the violation of justice and righteousness do not be amazed at the matter — for the high official is watched by a higher and there are yet higher ones over them.
\v 9 But this is gain for a land in every way: a king committed to cultivated fields.
\p
\v 10 He who loves money will not be satisfied with money — nor he who loves wealth with his income — this also is vapor (*mataiotēs*).
\v 11 When goods increase they increase who eat them and what advantage has their owner but to see them with his eyes?
\v 12 Sweet is the sleep of a laborer whether he eats little or much — but the full stomach of the rich will not let him sleep.
\p
\v 13 There is a grievous evil that I have seen under the sun: riches were kept by their owner to his hurt.
\v 14 And those riches were lost in a bad venture — and he is father of a son but he has nothing in his hand.
\v 15 As he came from his mother's womb he shall go again naked as he came and shall take nothing for his toil that he may carry away in his hand.
\v 16 This also is a grievous evil — just as he came so shall he go — and what profit is there to him who toils for the wind (*pneuma*)?
\v 17 Moreover all his days he eats in darkness in much vexation and sickness and anger.
\p
\v 18 Behold what I have seen to be good and fitting: to eat and drink and find enjoyment in all the toil with which one toils under the sun the few days of his life (*zōēs*) that God has given him — for this is his lot.
\v 19 Everyone also to whom God has given wealth and possessions and power to enjoy them and to accept his lot and rejoice in his toil — this is the gift of God.
\v 20 For he will not much remember the days of his life (*zōēs*) because God keeps him occupied with joy in his heart.

\c 6
\p
\v 1 There is an evil that I have seen under the sun and it lies heavy on mankind —
\v 2 a man to whom God gives wealth and possessions and honor so that he lacks nothing of all that he desires yet God does not give him power to enjoy them but a stranger enjoys them — this is vapor (*mataiotēs*) and a grievous evil.
\v 3 If a man fathers a hundred children and lives many years so that the days of his years are many but his soul (*psychē*) is not satisfied with good things and he also has no burial — I say that a stillborn child is better off than he.
\v 4 For it comes in vapor (*mataiotēs*) and goes in darkness and in darkness its name is covered.
\v 5 Moreover it has not seen the sun or known anything — yet it finds rest rather than he.
\v 6 Even though he should live a thousand years twice over yet enjoy no good — do not all go to the one place?
\p
\v 7 All the toil of man is for his mouth yet his appetite is not satisfied.
\v 8 For what advantage has the wise man over the fool? And what does the poor man have who knows how to conduct himself before the living?
\v 9 Better is the sight of the eyes than the wandering of desire — this also is vapor (*mataiotēs*) and a feeding on wind (*proairesis pneumatos*).
\p
\v 10 Whatever has come to be has already been named and it is known what man is — and he is not able to dispute with one stronger than he.
\v 11 The more words the more vapor (*mataiotēs*) and what is the advantage to man?
\v 12 For who knows what is good for man while he lives the few days of his vain life (*zōēs mataiotētos autou*) which he passes like a shadow? For who can tell man what will be after him under the sun?

\c 7
\p
\v 1 A good name is better than precious ointment and the day of death than the day of birth.
\v 2 It is better to go to the house of mourning than to go to the house of feasting — for this is the end of all mankind and the living will lay it to heart.
\v 3 Sorrow is better than laughter — for by sadness of face the heart is made glad.
\v 4 The heart of the wise is in the house of mourning but the heart of fools is in the house of mirth.
\v 5 It is better for a man to hear the rebuke of the wise than to hear the song of fools.
\v 6 For as the crackling of thorns under a pot so is the laughter of the fools — this also is vapor (*mataiotēs*).
\v 7 Surely oppression drives the wise into madness and a bribe corrupts the heart.
\v 8 Better is the end of a thing than its beginning and the patient in breath-of-life (*pneuma*) is better than the proud in breath-of-life (*pneuma*).
\v 9 Be not quick in your breath-of-life (*pneumati*) to become angry — for anger lodges in the heart of fools.
\v 10 Say not: Why were the former days better than these? — for it is not from wisdom (*sophia*) that you ask this.
\v 11 Wisdom (*sophia*) is good with an inheritance — an advantage to those who see the sun.
\v 12 For the protection of wisdom (*sophias*) is like the protection of money and the advantage of knowledge is that wisdom (*sophia*) preserves the life (*zōēn*) of him who has it.
\v 13 Consider the work of God — for who can make straight what he has made crooked?
\v 14 In the day of prosperity be joyful and in the day of adversity consider — God has made the one as well as the other so that man may not find out anything that will be after him.
\p
\v 15 In my vain life (*mataiotēs*) I have seen everything — there is a righteous man who perishes in his righteousness and there is a wicked man who prolongs his life in his evildoing.
\v 16 Be not overly righteous and do not make yourself too wise (*sophos*) — why should you destroy yourself?
\v 17 Be not overly wicked neither be a fool — why should you die before your time?
\v 18 It is good that you should take hold of this and from that withhold not your hand — for the one who fears God shall come out from both of them.
\v 19 Wisdom (*sophia*) gives strength to the wise man more than ten rulers who are in a city.
\v 20 Surely there is not a righteous man on earth who does good and never sins.
\v 21 Do not take to heart all the things that people say lest you hear your servant cursing you.
\v 22 Your heart knows that many times you yourself have cursed others.
\p
\v 23 All this I have tested by wisdom (*sophia*) — I said: I will be wise (*sophos*) — but it was far from me.
\v 24 That which has been is far off and deep — very deep — who can find it out?
\v 25 I turned my heart to know and to search out and to seek wisdom (*sophia*) and the scheme of things and to know the wickedness of folly and the foolishness that is madness.
\v 26 And I find something more bitter than death — the woman whose heart is snares and nets and whose hands are fetters — he who pleases God escapes her but the sinner is taken by her.
\v 27 Behold this is what I found says the Assembler adding one thing to another to find the scheme —
\v 28 which my soul (*psychē*) has sought repeatedly but I have not found — one man among a thousand I found but a woman among all these I have not found.
\v 29 See this alone I found: that God made man upright but they have sought out many schemes.

\c 8
\p
\v 1 Who is like the wise man? And who knows the interpretation of a thing? A man's wisdom (*sophia*) makes his face shine and the hardness of his face is changed.
\v 2 I say: Keep the king's command because of God's oath to him.
\v 3 Be not hasty to go from his presence — do not take your stand in an evil cause — for he does whatever he pleases.
\v 4 For the word of the king is supreme and who may say to him: What are you doing?
\v 5 Whoever keeps a command will know no evil thing and the heart of the wise man will know the time and the judgment.
\v 6 For every matter has its time and judgment — for the misery of man is great upon him.
\v 7 For he does not know what is to be — for who can tell him how it will be?
\v 8 No man has power to retain the breath-of-life (*pneuma*) or power over the day of death — there is no discharge from war and wickedness will not deliver those who are given to it.
\v 9 All this I observed while applying my heart to all that is done under the sun when man had power over man to his hurt.
\p
\v 10 Then I saw the wicked buried — they used to go in and out of the holy place and were praised in the city where they had done such things — this also is vapor (*mataiotēs*).
\v 11 Because the sentence against an evil deed is not executed speedily the heart of the children of man is fully set to do evil.
\v 12 Though a sinner does evil a hundred times and prolongs his life (*zōēn*) yet I know that it will be well with those who fear God because they fear before him.
\v 13 But it will not be well with the wicked neither will he prolong his days like a shadow because he does not fear before God.
\p
\v 14 There is a vapor (*mataiotēs*) that takes place on earth — that there are righteous people to whom it happens according to the deeds of the wicked and there are wicked people to whom it happens according to the deeds of the righteous — I said that this also is vapor (*mataiotēs*).
\v 15 And I commend enjoyment — for man has nothing better under the sun than to eat and drink and be joyful — for this will go with him in his toil through the days of his life (*zōēs*) that God has given him under the sun.
\p
\v 16 When I applied my heart to know wisdom (*sophia*) and to see the business that is done on earth — even though one's eyes see no sleep by day or by night —
\v 17 then I saw all the work of God that man cannot find out the work that is done under the sun. However much man may toil in seeking he will not find it out — even though a wise man (*sophos*) claims to know he cannot find it out.

\c 9
\p
\v 1 But all this I laid to heart examining it all — how the righteous and the wise and their deeds are in the hand of God — whether it is love or hate man does not know — both are before him.
\v 2 It is the same for all — there is one fate for the righteous and the wicked — for the good and the evil — for the clean and the unclean — for him who sacrifices and him who does not sacrifice — as the good one is so is the sinner and he who swears is as he who shuns an oath.
\v 3 This is an evil in all that is done under the sun — that one fate comes to all — also the heart of the children of man is full of evil and madness is in their hearts while they live (*zōsin*) and after that they go to the dead.
\v 4 But he who is joined with all the living has hope — for a living dog is better than a dead lion.
\v 5 For the living know that they will die but the dead know nothing and they have no more reward — for the memory of them is forgotten.
\v 6 Their love and their hate and their envy have already perished and forever (*eis ton aiōna*) they have no more share in all that is done under the sun.
\p
\v 7 Go — eat your bread with joy and drink your wine with a merry heart — for God has already approved what you do.
\v 8 Let your garments be always white — let not oil be lacking on your head.
\v 9 Enjoy life (*zōēn*) with the wife whom you love all the days of your vain life (*zōēs mataiotētos*) that he has given you under the sun — because that is your portion in life (*zōē*) and in your toil at which you toil under the sun.
\v 10 Whatever your hand finds to do — do it with your might — for there is no work or thought or knowledge or wisdom (*sophia*) in Sheol to which you are going.
\p
\v 11 Again I saw that under the sun the race is not to the swift nor the battle to the strong nor bread to the wise (*sophois*) nor riches to the intelligent nor favor to those with knowledge — but time and chance happen to them all.
\v 12 For man does not know his time — like fish that are taken in an evil net and like birds that are caught in a snare so the children of man are snared at an evil time when it suddenly falls upon them.
\p
\v 13 I have also seen this example of wisdom (*sophia*) under the sun and it seemed great to me.
\v 14 There was a little city with few men in it and a great king came against it and besieged it building great siegeworks against it.
\v 15 But there was found in it a poor wise man and he by his wisdom (*sophia*) delivered the city — yet no one remembered that poor man.
\v 16 But I say that wisdom (*sophia*) is better than might though the poor man's wisdom (*sophia*) is despised and his words are not heard.
\v 17 The words of the wise (*sophōn*) heard in quiet are better than the shouting of a ruler among fools.
\v 18 Wisdom (*sophia*) is better than weapons of war but one sinner destroys much good.

\c 10
\p
\v 1 Dead flies make the perfumer's ointment give off a stench — a little folly outweighs wisdom (*sophian*) and honor.
\v 2 A wise man's (*sophou*) heart inclines him to the right but a fool's heart to the left.
\v 3 Even when the fool walks on the road he lacks sense and he says to everyone that he is a fool.
\v 4 If the anger of the ruler rises against you do not leave your place — for calmness will lay great offenses to rest.
\p
\v 5 There is an evil that I have seen under the sun as it were an error proceeding from the ruler —
\v 6 folly is set in many high places and the rich sit in a low place.
\v 7 I have seen slaves on horses and princes walking on the ground like slaves.
\p
\v 8 He who digs a pit will fall into it and a serpent will bite him who breaks through a wall.
\v 9 He who quarries stones is hurt by them and he who splits logs is endangered by them.
\v 10 If the iron is blunt and one does not sharpen the edge he must use more strength but wisdom (*sophia*) helps one to succeed.
\v 11 If the serpent bites before it is charmed there is no advantage to the charmer.
\p
\v 12 The words of a wise man's (*sophou*) mouth win him favor but the lips of a fool consume him.
\v 13 The beginning of the words of his mouth is foolishness and the end of his talk is evil madness.
\v 14 A fool multiplies words though no man knows what is to be and who can tell him what will be after him?
\v 15 The toil of a fool wearies him — for he does not know the way to the city.
\p
\v 16 Woe to you O land when your king is a child and your princes feast in the morning.
\v 17 Happy are you O land when your king is the son of the nobility and your princes feast at the proper time for strength and not for drunkenness.
\v 18 Through sloth the roof sinks in and through idleness of hands the house leaks.
\v 19 Bread is made for laughter and wine gladdens life (*zōēn*) and money answers everything.
\v 20 Even in your thoughts do not curse the king nor in your bedroom curse the rich — for a bird of the air will carry your voice or some winged creature will tell the matter.

\c 11
\p
\v 1 Cast your bread upon the waters — for you will find it after many days.
\v 2 Give a portion to seven or even to eight — for you know not what disaster may happen on earth.
\v 3 If the clouds are full of rain they empty themselves on the earth — and if a tree falls to the south or to the north in the place where the tree falls there it will lie.
\v 4 He who watches the wind will not sow and he who looks at the clouds will not reap.
\v 5 As you do not know the way the breath-of-life (*pneuma*) comes to the bones in the womb of a woman with child so you do not know the work of God who makes everything.
\v 6 In the morning sow your seed and at evening withhold not your hand — for you do not know which will prosper — this or that — or whether both alike will be good.
\p
\v 7 Light is sweet and it is pleasant for the eyes to see the sun.
\v 8 So if a person lives (*zēsē*) many years let him rejoice in them all — but let him remember that the days of darkness will be many — all that comes is vapor (*mataiotēs*).
\p
\v 9 Rejoice O young man in your youth and let your heart cheer you in the days of your youth — walk in the ways of your heart and the sight of your eyes — but know that for all these things God will bring you into judgment.
\v 10 Remove vexation from your heart and put away pain from your body — for youth and the dawn of life (*zōēs*) are vapor (*mataiotēs*).

\c 12
\p
\v 1 Remember also your Creator in the days of your youth before the evil days come and the years approach of which you will say: I have no pleasure in them —
\v 2 before the sun and the light and the moon and the stars are darkened and the clouds return after the rain —
\v 3 in the day when the keepers of the house tremble and the strong men are bent and the grinders cease because they are few and those who look through the windows are dimmed —
\v 4 and the doors on the street are shut when the sound of the grinding is low and one rises up at the voice of a bird and all the daughters of song are brought low —
\v 5 they are afraid also of what is high and terrors are in the way — the almond tree blossoms — the grasshopper drags itself along and desire fails — because man is going to his eternal (*aiōniou*) home and the mourners go about the streets —
\v 6 before the silver cord is snapped or the golden bowl is broken or the pitcher is shattered at the fountain or the wheel broken at the cistern —
\v 7 and the dust returns to the earth as it was and the breath-of-life (*pneuma*) returns to God who gave it.
\p
\v 8 Vapor of vapors (*mataiotēs mataiotētōn*) — says the Assembler — all is vapor (*mataiotēs*).
\p
\v 9 Besides being wise (*sophos*) the Assembler also taught the people knowledge — weighing and studying and arranging many proverbs with care.
\v 10 The Assembler sought to find words of delight and to write words of truth (*alētheia*) uprightly.
\v 11 The words of the wise (*sophōn*) are like goads and like nails firmly fixed are the collected sayings — they are given by one Shepherd (*Poimen*).
\v 12 My son beware of anything beyond these — of making many books there is no end and much study is a weariness of the flesh.
\p
\v 13 The end of the matter when all has been heard — fear God and keep his commandments — for this is the whole of man.
\v 14 For God will bring every work into judgment with every secret thing whether good or evil.
```

**TEXT BASE:** Rahlfs-Hanhart LXX (*Ekklēsiastēs*) as primary — the LXX Ecclesiastes is a remarkably literal translation of the Hebrew, producing some of the most unusual Greek in the entire LXX corpus. The translator (likely Aquila's predecessor in the proto-Aquilan tradition) rendered Hebrew idioms with extreme literalism — *mataiotēs* for *hebel* / *proairesis pneumatos* for *re'ut ruach* — preserving the Hebrew's semantic texture at the cost of natural Greek. This makes the LXX Ecclesiastes uniquely valuable for the CAE: the vocabulary locks are semantically denser than the Hebrew alone because the LXX's Greek forces the Hebrew categories into contact with the Greek philosophical tradition, producing a text that simultaneously inhabits both semantic worlds.

**CHAPTER COUNT:** 12 chapters / 222 verses

**TITLE NOTE:** *Qoheleth* (*Assembler / Convener*) rendered as *Ekklēsiastēs* in the LXX — the one who assembles or convenes the *ekklēsia*. The same root as the NT *ekklēsia* (called-out assembly). The Assembler who gathers the community to hear wisdom (*sophia*) is the LXX's title-form for the one who speaks from the center of the community's covenant-life. GLE v2.2 renders as *Assembler* — preserving both the Hebrew *qahal* (assembly) root and the LXX's *ekklēsia* resonance.

---

**ECCLESIASTES IN THE CAE ARCHITECTURE**

Ecclesiastes occupies a structurally unique position in the CAE — it is the canon's most sustained philosophical stress-test of the Wisdom register itself. Every other book in the wisdom tradition affirms Wisdom's value and accessibility: Proverbs declares the *sophia* available and life-giving / Job questions providence while maintaining covenant-integrity / Wisdom of Solomon celebrates the *sophia* as the key to creation and salvation. Ecclesiastes alone systematically questions whether the *sophia* delivers what it promises — whether the *mataiotēs* (vapor / vanity) that characterizes *every* human project under the sun finally swallows wisdom itself.

The book is not Folly-register literature. The Assembler is not a skeptic or nihilist — the closing verse (12:13–14) is the most compact Wisdom-register summary in the entire OT: *fear God and keep his commandments — for this is the whole of man / for God will bring every work into judgment.* The Assembler arrives at this conclusion not by ignoring the *mataiotēs* but by passing through it — the book is the Wisdom register's rigorous self-examination, stripping away every form of wisdom-idolatry (wisdom as achievement / as legacy / as advantage / as certainty) to reach the bedrock: *fear God*.

The CAE's engagement with Ecclesiastes serves four functions:

(1) **Negative-definition function:** Ecclesiastes defines the *mataiotēs* register — the domain of *hebel* / vapor / emptiness that characterizes human existence *under the sun* without the divine-gift orientation. This is the CAE's most precise vocabulary for the Folly register's structural condition: not active evil but ontological emptiness / the breath that dissipates / the project that cannot hold its own weight.

(2) **Pneumatological function:** The *pneuma* appearances in Ecclesiastes (3:19/21 / 8:8 / 11:5 / 12:7) constitute the OT's most concentrated engagement with the *pneuma*-as-life-breath / the *pneuma*'s origin in God and return to God / and the epistemological limits of knowing the *pneuma*'s post-mortem trajectory. The 12:7 *pneuma returns to God who gave it* is the OT's most explicit statement of the *pneuma*'s divine origin and eschatological return.

(3) **Anti-idolatry function:** Every human project examined and found *mataiotēs* — wealth / pleasure / toil / wisdom / legacy / youth / accumulation — constitutes the canon's most systematic exposure of the Folly register's specific forms. The Assembler does not argue against these things abstractly but tests each one empirically and finds it empty of the weight it claimed to carry.

(4) **Christological preparation function:** The Assembler's inability to find *the* wise man (7:28 — *one man among a thousand I found but a woman among all these I have not found*) / the Assembler's acknowledgment that no human wisdom reaches the bottom of things (7:23–24 — *I said I will be wise but it was far from me / that which has been is far off and deep — very deep*) / and the final dependence on divine judgment rather than human wisdom (12:13–14) — all constitute the Wisdom tradition's self-confession of its own insufficiency, which the NT presents as resolved in the *Logos* who is *Wisdom* (*sophia*) and *Righteousness* (*dikaiosynē*) and *Sanctification* (*hagiasmos*) and *Redemption* (*apolytrōsis*) from God (1 Corinthians 1:30).

**KEY LEXICAL DECISIONS**

***Mataiotēs* — "vapor / vanity":**

The LXX renders *hebel* (breath / vapor / that which dissipates without residue) as *mataiotēs* — emptiness / futility / vanity. The word appears 38 times across the 12 chapters — the most concentrated single-vocabulary saturation in the entire LXX after the Psalter's *kyrios*. GLE v2.2 renders *mataiotēs* as "vapor" throughout — preserving the physical image (breath that dissipates / mist that vanishes) rather than the abstract English "vanity" which has lost its sensory grounding.

**CAE *mataiotēs* vocabulary lock — the critical NT cross-link:**

**Romans 8:20 (*tē gar mataiotēti hē ktisis hypetagethe — ouch hekousa alla dia ton hypotaxanta* — for the creation was subjected to *mataiotēs* — not willingly but through the one who subjected it)**

Paul's *mataiotēs* in Romans 8:20 is the direct LXX Ecclesiastes vocabulary applied to the entire creation's condition — the creation subjected to *mataiotēs* (Ecclesiastes's entire governing category) by God's own act / not willingly / in hope. This is the most important single NT citation from the Ecclesiastes tradition: Paul takes the Assembler's diagnostic category (*all is *mataiotēs** — Ecclesiastes 1:2) and applies it cosmologically — the creation itself is under the same *mataiotēs* that the Assembler observed in human projects. **CAE Chain 15 (Eden-to-new-creation) *mataiotēs*-subjection node:**

**Genesis 3:17–19 LXX (*epikataratos hē gē en tois ergois sou* — cursed is the ground because of you / thorns and thistles / return to dust) → Ecclesiastes 1:2 / 12:8 (*mataiotēs mataiotētōn — panta mataiotēs* — vapor of vapors / all is vapor) → Romans 8:20–21 (*tē mataiotēti hypetagethe — eis eleutherian tēs doxēs* — subjected to mataiotēs / freed into the glory of the children of God) → Revelation 21:4 (*ta archaia apēlthan* — the former things have passed away)**

The arc: creation's original *mataiotēs*-subjection at the Fall (Genesis 3) → the Assembler's empirical documentation of the *mataiotēs* across all human domains (Ecclesiastes) → Paul's cosmological analysis of the *mataiotēs* as the creation's present condition awaiting liberation (Romans 8) → the new creation's *mataiotēs*-removal (Revelation 21). The Ecclesiastes *mataiotēs* is not a pessimistic endpoint but the honest naming of the creation's post-Fall condition — the diagnosis that makes the Romans 8 liberation-hope intelligible.

**LXX-primacy argument:** The LXX's choice of *mataiotēs* for *hebel* is theologically decisive — *mataiotēs* in Greek philosophical usage (Stoic / Epicurean) carries the sense of *ontological emptiness* / the thing that lacks genuine substance / the claim that cannot be cashed. Paul's *mataiotēs* in Romans 8:20 is not merely *futility* (the typical English translation) but the LXX Ecclesiastes category — the condition of a creation that is real but cannot sustain the weight of its own existence without the divine *telos* toward which it strains. The MT's *hebel* (breath / vapor) becomes in the LXX *mataiotēs* a category that bridges the Hebrew phenomenological tradition and the Greek philosophical tradition — the LXX translation creates a richer theological instrument than either the Hebrew alone or the Greek alone provides.

***Proairesis pneumatos* — "feeding on wind":**

The LXX renders *re'ut ruach* (chasing / feeding on wind) as *proairesis pneumatos* — literally *preference / choice of wind* or *grasping at wind / spirit*. The *proairesis* is a Greek philosophical term with significant resonance — it is Aristotle's term for the *rational choice* / the *deliberate preference* that constitutes the will's action. The LXX's use of *proairesis* for *re'ut ruach* creates a theologically dense formulation: the *proairesis pneumatos* is not merely futile wind-chasing but the *deliberate rational choice of emptiness* — the will that chooses the *pneuma* (wind / vapor) rather than the *Pneuma* (Breath-of-life of God).

**CAE register-diagnostic significance:** The *proairesis pneumatos* is the Assembler's most precise description of the Folly register's volitional structure — the will oriented toward what is *pneuma* in the sense of wind / emptiness / that which cannot be grasped / rather than the *Pneuma* that *returns to God who gave it* (12:7). The deliberate choice of the empty is the Folly register's defining act — not ignorance but *proairesis* / willful preference for the vaporous over the substantial.

***Sophia* — "wisdom":**

Appears 28 times in 12 chapters — the highest wisdom-vocabulary density in the OT outside Proverbs and Wisdom of Solomon. The Assembler's engagement with *sophia* is structurally unique: he *has* wisdom (1:16 — *I have magnified and increased wisdom above all*) / he *tested* wisdom (2:3 / 7:23) / he *found* wisdom's limits (7:23–24 — *I said I will be wise but it was far from me*) / he *commends* wisdom despite its limits (2:13 / 7:11–12 / 9:16–18) / and he finally subordinates wisdom to the fear of God (12:13).

**CAE Wisdom-register paradox:** the Assembler demonstrates that wisdom pursued as *achievement* (*I have magnified and increased wisdom* — 1:16) becomes *mataiotēs* — the wisdom that is the Wisdom-register's ground when it is *received from God* (2:26 — *to the man who pleases him God gives wisdom*) becomes the Folly-register's pride when it is *accumulated as possession*. The CAE's Wisdom/Folly register distinction is here at its most precise: the *sophia* that is *gift* (2:26) is categorically different from the *sophia* that is *achievement* (1:16) — the same faculty operating in opposite registers produces opposite effects. **Cross-link: James 1:5 (*aiteitō para tou didontos theou* — let him ask from the God who gives) / James 3:17 (*hē de anōthen sophia* — the wisdom from above)** — James is the NT's direct heir of the Ecclesiastes distinction between wisdom-as-gift and wisdom-as-achievement.

***Pneuma* — "breath-of-life":**

Seven structurally significant occurrences:

*3:19 — one pneuma:* *what happens to the children of man and what happens to the beasts — they all have the same pneuma.* The *pneuma* as the life-breath shared by all animate creatures — the *nishmat chayyim* of Genesis 2:7 LXX (*enephysēsen eis to prosōpon autou pnoēn zōēs* — breathed into his face the breath of life) extended to all creatures. The Assembler observes the *pneuma*'s equality across species as part of the *mataiotēs* argument — from the *pneuma*-as-life-breath perspective / man has no advantage over the beast.

*3:21 — the epistemological limit:* *who knows whether the pneuma of man goes upward and the pneuma of the beast goes down into the earth?* The most important pneumatological verse in Ecclesiastes — the Assembler acknowledges the question of the *pneuma*'s post-mortem trajectory but refuses to answer it with certainty. **CAE pneumatological epistemology note:** the Assembler does not deny the *pneuma*'s upward trajectory — he only denies certainty *under the sun* (the epistemological domain of the book's entire inquiry). The question is left open — and 12:7 answers it: *the pneuma returns to God who gave it*. The 3:21 uncertainty and the 12:7 certainty are not contradictions but the book's epistemological movement — from the *under-the-sun* observation of uncertainty to the conclusion's affirmation of the *pneuma*'s divine origin and return.

*7:8–9 — pneuma as inner disposition:* *the patient in pneuma is better than the proud in pneuma — be not quick in your pneuma to become angry.* The *pneuma* here is the inner orientation / the register-disposition — the Wisdom register's *pneuma* is *patient / long-suffering* / the Folly register's *pneuma* is *proud / quickly angry*. **CAE *pneuma*-as-register-orientation note:** the Ecclesiastes 7:8–9 usage is the OT's closest parallel to the NT's *pneuma* as the inner faculty that can be either Wisdom-register oriented (*pneuma tēs alētheias* — 1 John 4:6) or Folly-register oriented (*psychikoi pneuma mē echontes* — Jude 19). The *pneuma*'s quality depends on its orientation — patience and humility versus pride and anger.

*8:8 — the pneuma's irreversibility:* *no man has power to retain the pneuma or power over the day of death.* The *pneuma* cannot be held back when it departs — death's inevitability as the *pneuma*'s non-negotiable withdrawal. **CAE mortality-realism note:** the Assembler's pneumatology is consistently realistic about death — not because he denies resurrection or the *pneuma*'s return to God but because his epistemological discipline (*under the sun*) prevents him from claiming more than he can observe. The *pneuma* departs / no man restrains it / this is the creatureliness-boundary that the Assembler will not transgress.

*11:5 — the pneuma's mystery:* *as you do not know the way the pneuma comes to the bones in the womb of a woman with child so you do not know the work of God who makes everything.* The *pneuma*'s coming to the bones (*osteis* — the formation of the embryo) is the creation-mystery that limits all human knowing of God's work. **LXX-primacy argument:** Ezekiel 37:5–6 LXX (*idou egō ferō eis hymas pneuma kai zēsesthe — kai dōsō epi hymas neura kai anaxō eph' hymas sarkas* — I am bringing pneuma into you and you shall live / I will lay sinews on you and cover you with flesh) — the valley-of-dry-bones resurrection uses the same *pneuma-coming-to-bones* language that Ecclesiastes 11:5 uses for embryonic formation. The LXX creates a deliberate cross-reference: the *pneuma* that animates the embryo in the womb is the same *pneuma* that will animate the resurrection-body — the creation-miracle of life's beginning is the pattern of the new creation's life-giving. **Cross-link: 1 Corinthians 15:44–46 (*speiretai sōma psychikon — egeiretai sōma pneumatikon* — sown a natural body / raised a spiritual body) = the Ecclesiastes 11:5 *pneuma*-to-bones mystery applied eschatologically.**

*12:7 — the pneuma returns to God:* *and the dust returns to the earth as it was and the pneuma returns to God who gave it.* The theological summit of the book's pneumatology — the *pneuma* as divine loan / God's gift temporarily dwelling in the creature / returning to its source at death. **CAE pneumatological chain:**

**Genesis 2:7 LXX (*enephysēsen eis to prosōpon autou pnoēn zōēs* — breathed into his face the breath of life) → Genesis 3:19 LXX (*gē ei kai eis gēn apeleusē* — you are earth and to earth you shall return) → Ecclesiastes 12:7 (*to pneuma epistrepsei pros ton theon hos edōken auto* — the pneuma returns to God who gave it) → Luke 23:46 (*Pater eis cheiras sou paratithēmai to pneuma mou* — Father into your hands I commit my pneuma) → Acts 7:59 (*Kyrie Iēsou dexai to pneuma mou* — Lord Jesus receive my pneuma) → Hebrews 12:23 (*pneumasin dikaiōn teteleioménōn* — the spirits of the righteous made perfect)**

The Ecclesiastes 12:7 *pneuma-returns-to-God* is the OT's definitive pneumatological statement — the *pneuma* is not the creature's possession but the Creator's loan / it returns not into nothingness but to God. The dying words of the Anointed (Luke 23:46 — citing Psalm 31:5 LXX) and of Stephen (Acts 7:59) enact exactly the 12:7 pneumatological movement: the *pneuma* consciously committed back to the God who gave it. **CAE Chain 15 (Eden-to-new-creation) *pneuma*-return node confirmed across five witnesses.**

***Psychē* — "soul":**

Five structurally significant occurrences — all using *psychē* as the faculty of desire / appetite / the register-orientation center:

*2:24 — psychē enjoying good:* *his psychē should enjoy good in his toil — this also I saw is from the hand of God.* The *psychē*'s enjoyment of good as *divine gift* — not earned but given. The Wisdom-register *psychē* receives enjoyment as gift / the Folly-register *psychē* seeks it as achievement (2:10 — *my heart rejoiced in all my toil and this was my portion*).

*4:8 — psychē deprived:* *depriving his psychē of good.* The accumulating miser who has no one to share his wealth — his *psychē* is deprived of the good it was designed to enjoy. The *psychē* wronged by its own owner — the Folly register's self-defeating accumulation-logic.

*6:2–3 — psychē not satisfied:* *his psychē is not satisfied with good things.* The man who has everything but whose *psychē* cannot enjoy it — wealth without enjoyment is *mataiotēs*. The *psychē*'s satisfaction is not a function of accumulation but of orientation.

*7:28 — psychē searching:* *which my psychē has sought repeatedly but I have not found.* The Assembler's *psychē* seeking the perfectly wise person — the search that returns empty. **CAE Chain 15 Christological-preparation node:** the *psychē*'s failed search for the fully wise person is the OT Wisdom tradition's self-confession that the *one man among a thousand* (7:28) was not to be found — the search that the NT presents as resolved in the one who *is Wisdom from God* (1 Corinthians 1:30).

*9:9 — psychē in the wife-of-your-youth:* the *zōē* and *zōēs mataiotētos* of the passage render the *psychē* as the center of relational enjoyment — the good life as embodied relational presence rather than abstract accumulation.

***Aiōn / aiōnios* — "eternity / eternal / forever":**

Five significant occurrences:

*1:4 — the earth stands eis ton aiōna:* the earth's permanence contrasted with the generation's transience — the creation's *aiōnios* stability as the backdrop against which human vapor is visible.

*3:11 — ton aiōna in man's heart:* *he has also put eternity (*ton aiōna*) into man's heart yet so that he cannot find out what God has done from the beginning to the end.* **The single most theologically important verse in Ecclesiastes for the CAE.** The Lord has placed *ton aiōna* — the eternal / the age-long / the sense of eternity — in the human heart. Man is the creature who *knows* there is an *aiōn* / who *reaches toward* it / but who *cannot find out* what God has done from beginning to end *under the sun*. The *aiōn* in the heart is the structural reason for the *mataiotēs*-ache: man reaches for the eternal in every project and finds vapor — not because the eternal does not exist but because no *under-the-sun* project can contain it.

**CAE *aiōn*-in-the-heart analysis:**

The Assembler's *ton aiōna en tē kardia autou* (eternity in his heart — 3:11) is the OT's most precise diagnosis of the human condition that Augustine articulates as *our heart is restless until it rests in you* (*Confessions* 1.1). The *aiōn* placed in the human heart is the image of God (*kath' eikona* — Genesis 1:26 LXX) as *temporal awareness* — the creature made for eternity who is trapped in time / whose every project reveals the gap between the *aiōn* it reaches for and the *mataiotēs* it achieves. **Cross-link: Romans 8:23 (*kai autoi hēmeis... stenazomen* — we ourselves groan inwardly / waiting for the adoption) = the *ton aiōna en tē kardia* groaning of Ecclesiastes 3:11 expressed eschatologically.** Paul's creation-groan (Romans 8:22 — *systenazei kai synōdinei* — groaning together and suffering birth-pangs together) and human groan (8:23 — *kai autoi hēmeis stenazomen* — we ourselves groan) are the NT's cosmological form of the Assembler's observation: *ton aiōna* in the heart / *mataiotēs* in every project / the gap between them is the source of all human ache.

*5:20 — God keeps him occupied:* the positive resolution — the man whom *God keeps occupied with joy* does not *much remember* the brevity of his days. The *aiōn* ache is resolved not by human achievement but by divine gift.

*12:5 — the aiōnios home:* *because man is going to his eternal (*aiōniou*) home.* Death as the journey to the *oikos aiōnios* — the eternal dwelling / the permanent home that contrasts with the temporary *oikia* under the sun. **CAE *oikos aiōnios* note:** the *oikos aiōnios* of Ecclesiastes 12:5 is the OT's most direct prefiguration of the NT's *eternal dwelling* language: John 14:2 (*en tē oikia tou patros mou monai pollai eisin* — in my Father's house are many dwelling-places) / 2 Corinthians 5:1 (*oikian acheiropoieton aiōnion en tois ouranois* — an eternal house not made with hands in the heavens). The *oikos aiōnios* is the resolution of the *mataiotēs* — the home that does not dissipate / the dwelling-place that can hold the *aiōn* that the human heart carries.

*12:7 / 12:14 — the eternal judgment:* the *pneuma*'s return to God (12:7) and the divine judgment of every secret thing (12:14) as the two poles of the book's eschatological conclusion — the *pneuma* returns to the God who will judge. The *aiōnios* home and the divine judgment are the book's final double answer to the *mataiotēs*: the *pneuma* is not lost / the works are not forgotten / both are held by the God who gave the *pneuma* and who will judge the works.

***Zōē* — "life":**

Nine occurrences — all in the context of the *zōē* lived *under the sun* — the temporary / God-given / vapor-qualified life that is simultaneously gift and limit. The most theologically significant: *9:9 — enjoy life (*zōēn*) with the wife whom you love all the days of your vain life (*zōēs mataiotētos*).* The doubled *zōē*: *zōēn* (the life to be enjoyed) / *zōēs mataiotētos* (the life of vapor in which it is enjoyed). The Assembler's *zōē* is always qualified — never the *zōē aiōnios* (eternal life) of the NT / always the *zōē* of numbered days / of God-given enjoyment / of the vapor-framed existence. **CAE *zōē* vocabulary contrast:** the Ecclesiastes *zōē* (temporal / vapor-qualified / under-the-sun) is the precise contrast term that makes the NT's *zōē aiōnios* (eternal life / the life that is not vapor / the life the *pneuma* participates in through the Anointed) theologically visible. Without the Ecclesiastes *zōē*-under-the-sun the NT's *zōē aiōnios* lacks its full contrast-background.

**STRUCTURAL CAE NODES**

**c.1 — The *mataiotēs* thesis / the cosmic *proairesis pneumatos*:**

The book's opening establishes the *mataiotēs* as the governing category through five cosmic observations: the generation-cycle (1:4) / the sun-cycle (1:5) / the wind-cycle (1:6) / the water-cycle (1:7) / the eye-and-ear-unsatisfied (1:8). The natural world's cycles are not *mataiotēs* in themselves — the earth stands forever (1:4) / the sun rises faithfully (1:5) — but they are *mataiotēs* from the human perspective of seeking something *new* (1:9–10) or leaving a *remembrance* (1:11). **CAE *mataiotēs*-diagnostic:** the *mataiotēs* is not a property of the creation itself but of the human project that seeks permanence and meaning *within* the cyclic creation without orientation toward the Creator. The creation cycles are faithful — but faithfulness to a cycle cannot satisfy the *ton aiōna* placed in the human heart (3:11).

**c.2 — The wisdom-achievement test:**

The Assembler's empirical test of pleasure / construction / accumulation / wisdom — each tested and found *mataiotēs*. The critical distinction: *2:24–26 — the gift-theology resolution:*

*There is nothing better for a man than that he should eat and drink and that his psychē should enjoy good in his toil — this also I saw is from the hand of God... for to the man who pleases him God gives wisdom and knowledge and joy.*

**CAE Wisdom-register gift-economy note:** the resolution of every tested and failed project is not *less enjoyment* but *reoriented enjoyment* — from achievement-enjoyment to gift-enjoyment. The *sophia* and *knowledge* and *joy* given by God to the one who pleases him (2:26) are the Wisdom-register forms of the same faculties that the Assembler tested as achievements and found empty. **Cross-link: James 1:5 (*aiteitō para tou didontos theou haplōs kai mē oneidizomenos* — let him ask from God who gives generously) / 1 Corinthians 1:30 (*hos egenēthē sophia hēmin apo theou* — who became for us wisdom from God)** — the Ecclesiastes gift-theology of 2:26 is the direct OT background for the NT's wisdom-as-divine-gift theology.

**c.3 — The times-poem / the *aiōn*-in-the-heart:**

The fourteen paired opposites of 3:1–8 constitute the most structurally complete statement of creaturely temporality in the OT — every human experience has its appointed time / nothing is permanent / nothing is outside the *kairos* (appointed time). **CAE *kairos*-theology note:** the fourteen pairs are not a statement of moral relativism (*everything is permitted in its time*) but a statement of creatureliness: the creature exists within appointed times / cannot transcend them / cannot know them in advance (8:7 — *for he does not know what is to be*). The *kairos*-structure of creation is the framework within which the *proairesis* (choice) is made — and the *ton aiōna en tē kardia* (eternity in the heart) is the faculty that perceives the *kairos* as insufficient even while it operates within it.

*3:14 — divine permanence as the *awe* ground:* *whatever God does endures forever (*eis ton aiōna*) — nothing can be added to it nor anything taken from it — God has done it so that people should fear before him.* The divine work's *aiōnios* permanence as the ground of the *fear of God* — the creature's *mataiotēs* reveals by contrast the Creator's *aiōnios* substance. **CAE Chain 10 (*alētheia*) divine-permanence-as-fear-ground node:** the *fear of God* that 12:13 identifies as *the whole of man* is grounded here in the contrast between the divine *aiōnios* work and the creaturely *mataiotēs* — the awe that recognizes the ontological gap between the Creator's permanence and the creature's vapor.

**c.7 — The wisdom-limits confession:**

The most theologically honest chapter in the book — the Assembler acknowledges wisdom's limits (7:23–24) / identifies the seductive-woman-as-death (7:26) / and confesses the failure to find the perfectly wise person (7:28–29).

*7:28–29 — the failed search and the original uprightness:*

*One man among a thousand I found but a woman among all these I have not found. See this alone I found: that God made man upright (*euthys*) but they have sought out many schemes.*

**CAE Chain 15 (Eden-to-new-creation) original-uprightness / corruption-through-schemes node:**

**Genesis 1:26–31 LXX (*kata eikona / kath' homoiōsin* — image and likeness / very good) → Ecclesiastes 7:29 (*ho theos epoiēsen ton anthrōpon euthyn* — God made man upright) → Romans 5:12 (*di' henos anthrōpou hē hamartia eisēlthen* — through one man sin entered) → Romans 3:23 (*pantes hēmarton kai hysterountai tēs doxēs tou theou* — all have sinned and fall short of the glory of God)**

The Assembler's 7:29 is the Wisdom tradition's most compressed statement of the Fall-doctrine: *God made man upright* (the original creation-integrity / the *kath' homoiōsin* of Genesis 1:26 LXX) *but they have sought out many schemes* (the corruption / the multiplication of *machinations / logismoi* that replaced the original *euthytēs* / uprightness). Paul's *all have sinned* (Romans 3:23) and *through one man sin entered* (Romans 5:12) are the NT's doctrinal elaboration of the Ecclesiastes 7:29 diagnostic: the creation's original uprightness / the universal corruption through *logismoi* (schemes / thoughts / reasonings that depart from the Creator's intention). **Cross-link: 2 Corinthians 10:5 (*kathairountes logismous* — demolishing logismoi / every high thing raised against the knowledge of God)** — Paul's *logismoi* that must be demolished are the same *machinations / schemes* of Ecclesiastes 7:29 that corrupted the original human uprightness.

**cc.9–10 — The wisdom-commendation despite *mataiotēs*:**

The sustained wisdom-commendation of cc.9–10 — wisdom better than might (9:16–18) / wisdom better than folly as light is better than darkness (2:13) / wisdom giving strength (7:19) — constitutes the Assembler's positive wisdom-theology *within* the *mataiotēs* framework. The wisdom is not absolute / not self-sufficient / not able to transcend the *mataiotēs* — but within the *mataiotēs* it is genuine advantage.

*9:17 — words of the wise heard in quiet:* *the words of the wise (*sophōn*) heard in quiet are better than the shouting of a ruler among fools.* **CAE Wisdom-register speech-pattern note:** the Wisdom register's *logos* is quiet / *en hēsychia* (in quietness) — the same pattern as Elijah's *phōnē audēs leptas* (still small voice — 1 Kings 19:12 LXX) / as the *meek and quiet breath-of-life* (*pneuma praus kai hēsychios* — 1 Peter 3:4) / as the *quiet and tranquil life* (*ēremon kai hēsychion bion* — 1 Timothy 2:2). The Wisdom register's speech does not need amplification because it carries the weight of the *alētheia* — the Folly register's speech must shout because it has no weight to carry itself.

**c.11 — The embryo-*pneuma* / the creation-mystery:**

*11:5 — as you do not know the way the pneuma comes to the bones in the womb:* the creation-mystery of the *pneuma*'s animating action in embryonic formation as the analogue of the creation's unknowable divine work. **CAE epistemological-humility principle:** the Wisdom register's epistemic virtue is knowing the limits of what can be known *under the sun* — the Assembler's repeated *who knows* (3:21 / 6:12 / 8:1) and *you do not know* (11:2/5/6) is not skepticism but the Wisdom register's honest acknowledgment of creaturely epistemological limits. **Cross-link: 1 Corinthians 13:12 (*blepomen gar arti di' esoptrou en ainigmati — tote de prosōpon pros prosōpon* — now we see in a mirror dimly / then face to face) = the NT's eschatological form of the Assembler's *under the sun* epistemological limit — the *mataiotēs*-episteme resolved in the eschatological *face-to-face* knowing.**

**c.12 — The old-age poem / the *pneuma*'s return / the conclusion:**

The most sustained and structurally complex extended metaphor in the OT — the old-age poem of 12:1–7 encoding the body's decline through the imagery of a decaying estate: the keepers of the house (arms) trembling / the strong men (legs) bent / the grinders (teeth) ceasing / those who look through windows (eyes) dimmed / the doors on the street (ears) shut / the daughters of song (voice) brought low / the almond tree (white hair) blossoming / the grasshopper (the body) dragging itself along / the silver cord (spinal cord) snapped / the golden bowl (skull) broken / the pitcher at the fountain (heart) shattered / the wheel at the cistern (lungs) broken.

**CAE *sōma* / *pneuma* mortality-realism note:** the 12:1–7 poem is the OT's most phenomenologically precise description of the body's dissolution — and its theological function is to frame the *pneuma*'s return (12:7) as the resolution of the body's dissolution. The body decays according to the dust-return of Genesis 3:19 LXX (*gē ei kai eis gēn apeleusē*) — the *pneuma* returns to God who gave it. The two trajectories are not tragedy and consolation but the creatureliness-boundary that the *fear of God* (12:13) acknowledges and accepts. **Cross-link: 2 Corinthians 4:16 (*ei kai ho exō hēmōn anthrōpos diaphtheiretai all' ho esō hēmōn anakainoutai hēmera kai hēmera* — though our outer self is wasting away our inner self is being renewed day by day) = the NT's Christological transformation of Ecclesiastes 12:1–7 / the body's dissolution / the *pneuma*'s renewal.**

*12:10–11 — the Assembler's method and the one Shepherd:*

*The Assembler sought to find words of delight and to write words of truth (*alētheia*) uprightly. The words of the wise are like goads and like nails firmly fixed are the collected sayings — they are given by one Shepherd (*Poimēn*).*

**CAE Chain 11 (*Poimēn*) Wisdom-Shepherd node — the single most important verse in Ecclesiastes for the Chain 11 analysis:**

The *collected sayings* / *words of the wise* given by *one Shepherd* (*heis Poimēn*) is the Wisdom tradition's most explicit identification of the divine wisdom's single source: not many sages accumulating wisdom independently but one Shepherd who gives the wisdom through them. **CAE Chain 11 arc:**

**Ecclesiastes 12:11 (*heis Poimēn* — one Shepherd / source of all wise sayings) → Ezekiel 34:23 LXX (*anastēsō epi ta probata mou poimena hena* — I will raise up over my sheep one shepherd) → Zechariah 13:7 LXX (*romphaia egerthēti epi tous poimenas mou* — sword awake against my shepherd / the one shepherd struck) → John 10:11/14/16 (*ho poimēn ho kalos* — the Good Shepherd / *mia poimnē heis poimēn* — one flock one shepherd) → Hebrews 13:20 (*ton poimena tōn probatōn ton megan* — the great Shepherd of the sheep) → 1 Peter 5:4 (*tou archipoimenos* — the Chief Shepherd) → Revelation 7:17 (*to Arnion...poimanei autous* — the Lamb will shepherd them)**

The *heis Poimēn* of Ecclesiastes 12:11 who gives all wisdom is the Wisdom tradition's seed of the NT's *one Shepherd* Christology — the chain from the Assembler's *one Shepherd* as the source of wise sayings through Ezekiel 34's eschatological-one-shepherd promise through John 10's *ho poimēn ho kalos* is a single theological trajectory. **The words of the wise are given by the one Shepherd** — the NT's identification of the Anointed as the *Logos* who is *sophia from God* (1 Corinthians 1:30) is the Christological fulfillment of the Ecclesiastes 12:11 *heis Poimēn* Wisdom-source.

**LXX-primacy argument for 12:11:** The LXX's *heis Poimēn* (one Shepherd) is the critical translation decision — the Hebrew *ro'eh echad* (one shepherd / one shepherd-source) could be rendered as *heis poimēn* (one shepherd — the personal figure) or more abstractly. The LXX's personal-figure rendering (*Poimēn* as the divine source of wisdom) creates the theological connection that makes the NT Chain 11 arc possible. If the LXX had rendered *ro'eh echad* abstractly the *heis Poimēn* → *Poimēn ho kalos* trajectory would be less visible.

*12:13–14 — the conclusion of the whole matter:*

*The end of the matter when all has been heard — fear God and keep his commandments — for this is the whole of man. For God will bring every work into judgment with every secret thing whether good or evil.*

**CAE structural analysis of the conclusion:** the *mataiotēs* thesis (1:2) and the *heis Poimēn* / *alētheia* affirmation (12:10–11) are resolved in the double conclusion: (1) *fear God and keep his commandments* — the Wisdom register's positive orientation / the *beginning of wisdom* (Proverbs 9:10 LXX / Psalm 110:10 LXX *archē sophias phobos kyriou*) that the entire book has been approaching through the negative path; (2) *God will bring every work into judgment* — the eschatological judgment as the final resolution of the *mataiotēs*'s apparent inequity (the wicked prospering / the righteous suffering / no difference in death — 9:2–3). **CAE Chain 10 (*alētheia*) final-judgment-as-*alētheia*-restoration node:** the divine judgment resolves the *mataiotēs* not by eliminating death or suffering but by ensuring that *every secret thing* is known and judged — the *alētheia* of every work is preserved in the divine judgment even when it is invisible *under the sun*.

**Cross-link: 2 Corinthians 5:10 (*tous gar pantas hēmas phanērōthēnai dei emprosthen tou bēmatos tou Christou* — for we must all appear before the judgment seat of the Anointed) / Romans 2:16 (*en hēmera hote krinei ho theos ta krupta tōn anthrōpōn* — on the day when God judges the secret things of men) / Revelation 20:12 (*ekrithēsan hoi nekroi ek tōn gegrammenōn en tois bibliois kata ta erga autōn* — the dead were judged from what was written in the books according to their works)** — three NT witnesses to the Ecclesiastes 12:14 final-judgment-of-every-secret-thing. **The Ecclesiastes conclusion is the OT Wisdom tradition's most compact eschatological frame** — and the NT's judgment theology is its direct elaboration.

**LXX-PRIMACY ARGUMENTS IN ECCLESIASTES**

Eight primary LXX dependency / cross-reference points:

1. **1:2 / 12:8 (*mataiotēs mataiotētōn*)** → Romans 8:20 (*tē mataiotēti hē ktisis hypetagethe*) — the LXX's *mataiotēs* vocabulary as the direct source of Paul's cosmological *mataiotēs*-subjection theology. The MT *hebel* would not have produced the same Pauline argument — the LXX's *mataiotēs* bridges Hebrew phenomenology and Greek ontology in a way that makes Romans 8's cosmic argument possible.

2. **3:11 (*ton aiōna en tē kardia autou*)** — the LXX's *ton aiōna* (eternity) for Hebrew *ha-olam* (the world / the age / forever) in the human heart. The LXX's *aiōn* vocabulary creates the direct bridge to NT *aiōnios* theology — the *aiōn* placed in the human heart as the faculty that strains toward *zōē aiōnios*.

3. **3:19–21 (*pneuma*)** → 1 Corinthians 15:44–46 (*sōma psychikon / pneumatikon*) — the Ecclesiastes *pneuma*-equality-of-all-creatures / *pneuma*-upward-trajectory question as the OT background for Paul's soma-psychikon/pneumatikon anthropology.

4. **7:29 (*euthyn* / *logismous*)** → Romans 3:23 / 5:12 / 2 Corinthians 10:5 — the original uprightness / the multiplication of schemes as the pre-Pauline diagnosis of the universal corruption. The LXX's *euthys* (upright / straight) for Hebrew *yashar* creates the moral-geometry metaphor that Paul's *all have sinned and fall short* assumes.

5. **11:5 (*pneuma epi ta osta*)** → Ezekiel 37:5–6 LXX / 1 Corinthians 15:44–46 — the *pneuma*-to-bones creation-mystery as the pattern for the resurrection-body's pneumatic animation. The LXX cross-reference between Ecclesiastes 11:5 and Ezekiel 37:5–6 LXX is only fully visible in the Greek — the Hebrew *ruach* connects both but the LXX *pneuma* makes the terminological link explicit.

6. **12:5 (*oikos aiōnios*)** → John 14:2 / 2 Corinthians 5:1 — the *oikos aiōnios* as the permanent dwelling that resolves the *mataiotēs* of every *under-the-sun* dwelling. The LXX's *aiōnios* applied to the post-mortem home creates the direct vocabulary link to the NT's *eternal dwelling* theology.

7. **12:7 (*to pneuma epistrepsei pros ton theon*)** → Luke 23:46 / Acts 7:59 / Hebrews 12:23 — the *pneuma*'s return to God as the OT's most explicit statement of the *pneuma*'s divine origin and eschatological return. The LXX's *epistrepsei* (will return / will turn back) for Hebrew *tashuv* creates the deliberate echo of the covenant-return vocabulary (*epistrephō* as the return to God in repentance) — the *pneuma*'s return at death is framed with the same vocabulary as the covenant-return of the wayward soul.

8. **12:11 (*heis Poimēn*)** → Ezekiel 34:23 LXX / John 10:11/16 / Hebrews 13:20 — the *one Shepherd* as the source of all wise sayings / the Chain 11 Wisdom-Shepherd arc's OT Wisdom-tradition foundation. The LXX's personal *Poimēn* for Hebrew *ro'eh* creates the direct Chain 11 connection.

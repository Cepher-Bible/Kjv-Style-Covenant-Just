## STRUCTURAL NOTE — LXX PROVERBS

The LXX Proverbs differs significantly from the MT:

- **Content additions:** The LXX contains verses and couplets not present in the MT — most notably the bee-stanza added after 6:8 and several expansions throughout.
- **Chapter 8 Wisdom hymn:** The LXX's *Sophia* personification is more extensive and theologically developed than the MT.
- **Chapter 30–31 ordering:** The LXX places the Agur section (30) and Lemuel/valiant-woman section (31) differently than MT.
- **Theological register:** The LXX's wisdom vocabulary establishes the Greek philosophical-theological vocabulary that the NT inherits.

The engine followed the Rahlfs-Hanhart LXX text throughout — including the additions and expansions.

## CAE NODES — PROVERBS

### Proverbs 1:7 — *archē sophias phobos kyriou* / the beginning of wisdom is the fear of the Lord

**1:7** — "The fear of the Lord is the beginning of wisdom."

The *archē sophias* formula — stated again at 9:10 — is the structural pivot of the entire book. The LXX's *archē* = beginning, first-principle, ruling-start — from *archō* = to rule, to begin. Not merely "start" but the generative principle from which wisdom proceeds.

**James 1:5** — "If any of you lacks wisdom — let him ask from God who gives to all generously and without reproach — and it will be given to him." The NT wisdom-prayer tradition assumes the Proverbs *sophia* vocabulary.

The engine rendered "beginning of wisdom" — *archē sophias* — not "first step toward wisdom." The *archē* is both temporal (beginning) and structural (first-principle) — the architectonic priority of the fear of the Lord in the order of knowledge.

---

### Proverbs 3:11–12 — *mē oligōrei paideias kyriou* / do not despise the Lord's instruction — CAE NODE

**3:11–12** — "My son — do not despise the Lord's instruction — nor faint when rebuked by him. For whom the Lord loves he disciplines — and scourges every son whom he receives."

**Hebrews 12:5–6** quotes this directly: "And you have forgotten the exhortation which speaks to you as sons: My son — do not despise the Lord's discipline nor faint when rebuked by him — for whom the Lord loves he disciplines — and scourges every son whom he receives."

The *paideia* = instruction/discipline — from *pais* = child — the child-training vocabulary. The Hebrews application: divine suffering is the *paideia* of sons — not evidence of abandonment but of filiation. The engine rendered "instruction" (*paideia*) and "disciplines" (*paideuei*) — preserving the child-training root throughout.

***mastigoi de panta huion hon paradechetai*** = and scourges every son whom he receives. *Mastigoō* = to scourge, to whip — the physical discipline vocabulary. *Paradechomai* = to receive, to accept, to welcome — the son-reception as the ground of discipline. The engine rendered "scourges" — not "punishes" — the *mastigō* vocabulary of physical correction.

---

### Proverbs 3:34 — *kyrios hyperēphanois antitassetai tapeinois de didōsin charin* / the Lord resists the proud but gives grace to the humble — CAE PRIMARY NODE

**3:34** — "The Lord resists the proud — but gives grace to the humble."

***kyrios hyperēphanois antitassetai tapeinois de didōsin charin*** = the Lord sets himself against the proud but to the humble he gives grace.

**James 4:6** — "But he gives greater grace — therefore it says: God resists the proud but gives grace to the humble." James cites the LXX Proverbs 3:34 vocabulary precisely — *antitassetai* = sets himself against/resists (a military term — to draw up in battle array against) — *tapeinois* = the lowly/humble ones.

**1 Peter 5:5** — "Likewise O younger ones — be subject to elders — and all of you clothe yourselves with humility toward one another — for God resists the proud but gives grace to the humble."

The engine rendered "resists" — *antitassetai* = sets himself in battle array against — the active military opposition vocabulary. Not "opposes" (which softens the martial force) — the Lord *arrays himself against* the proud. "Gives grace to the humble" — *tapeinois de didōsin charin* — the double register: *tapeinos* = lowly, brought down, humble — *charis* = grace, favor, gift.

### Proverbs 8:22–31 — The Wisdom Hymn — CAE PRIMARY NODE

**8:22–30** — "The Lord created me as the beginning of his ways for his works — before the age he established me in the beginning — before he made the earth — before he made the depths — before the springs of the waters came forth — before the mountains were set in place — and before all the hills — he begets me. The Lord made regions and uninhabited places — and the furthest inhabited parts under heaven. When he prepared the heaven I was present with him... I was beside him as one rearing him — and I was the one in whom he delighted daily — I rejoiced before him in all time."

***kyrios ektisen me archēn hodōn autou eis erga autou*** = the Lord created me as beginning of his ways for his works (8:22).

***pro tou aiōnos ethemeliōsen me en archē*** = before the age he founded me in the beginning (8:23).

This is one of the most theologically contested passages in the history of Christian doctrine:

**The Arian controversy:** Arius cited Proverbs 8:22 (*ektisen* = created) as proof that the Son/Logos was a creature — "created" by the Father as the first of his works. The passage speaks of pre-creation Wisdom being "created" (*ktizō*) by God.

**The orthodox response:** Athanasius argued that the LXX *sophia* of Proverbs 8 is the *theologia* of Wisdom's eternal generation — and that *ektisen* = made/established here has the sense of "appointed" or "established" rather than ex-nihilo creation. The MT *qanah* = acquired/possessed is ambiguous. The LXX *ktizō* is the contested term.

**Colossians 1:15** — "who is the image of the invisible God — the firstborn of all creation (*prōtotokos pasēs ktiseōs*) — for in him were created all things." The *prōtotokos* = firstborn vocabulary of Colossians 1 engages the Proverbs 8 tradition.

**John 1:1–3** — "In the beginning was the Logos... and all things through him came to be — and without him not one thing came to be." The Johannine Logos theology implicitly positions the *Logos* as the agent of creation rather than a creature within it — answering the Proverbs 8 question.

**Engine decision:** The LXX text is rendered as it stands — *ektisen* = created — without theological correction. The notes record the controversy. The engine's task is to render the LXX text faithfully; the theological argument about what the text means belongs to the commentator.

**8:30** — "I was beside him as one rearing him — and I was the one in whom he delighted daily — I rejoiced before him in all time." The *harmozousa* = one fitting together, one who is the master workman — the LXX's *harmozo* = to fit/join/arrange — Wisdom as the architectonic companion of creation. The engine rendered "one rearing him" following the LXX's *harmozousa* in its nurturing/arranging sense.

### Proverbs 9:1 — *sophia ōkodomēsen heautē oikon* / wisdom built herself a house

**9:1–5** — "Wisdom built herself a house — and set up seven pillars. She slew her beasts — she mixed her wine in a bowl — and she prepared her table... Come — eat of my bread — and drink the wine I mixed for you."

The Wisdom's feast imagery fires across the NT:

**Matthew 22:1–14** — the parable of the wedding feast — the king's invitation to the wedding banquet of his son echoes the Wisdom feast of Proverbs 9.

**John 6:35** — "I am the bread of life — the one who comes to me will not hunger — and the one who believes in me will never thirst." The bread-and-wine invitation of Wisdom's feast fires in the Eucharistic vocabulary.

**Revelation 19:9** — "Blessed are those who are called to the marriage supper of the Lamb." The Wisdom feast as eschatological banquet.

The engine rendered "seven pillars" — *hepta stylas* = seven columns — the number of completion. "She mixed her wine in a bowl" — *ekeransen ton oinon* = she mixed her wine (ancient wine mixed with water in a *krater* = mixing bowl).

### Proverbs 11:31 — *ei ho dikaios molis sōzetai* / if the righteous is barely saved — CAE NODE

**11:31** — "If the righteous is barely saved — where will the impious and the sinner appear?"

***ei ho dikaios molis sōzetai*** = if the righteous is barely/scarcely saved. **1 Peter 4:18** quotes this: "And if the righteous is barely saved — where will the impious and the sinner appear?" Peter applies the Proverbs 11:31 LXX formulation to the eschatological judgment — the difficulty of salvation for the righteous as the ground for urgency among the impious. The MT has "if the righteous is repaid on earth" — the LXX's *molis sōzetai* = barely saved is the more eschatologically pointed formulation that Peter requires.

The engine rendered "barely saved" — *molis* = scarcely, with difficulty — not "repaid on earth" (which follows MT). The LXX text is the authoritative text for the GLE engine.

### Proverbs 25:21–22 — *psōmize ton echthron sou* / feed your enemy — CAE NODE

**25:21–22** — "If your enemy is hungry — feed him — if he is thirsty — give him water to drink — for doing this you will heap coals of fire upon his head — and the Lord will reward you with good things."

***anthrakas pyros sōreueis epi tēn kephalēn autou*** = you will heap coals of fire upon his head. **Romans 12:20** quotes this directly: "But if your enemy hungers — feed him — if he thirsts — give him drink — for doing this you will heap coals of fire upon his head." Paul's ethics of non-retaliation and enemy-love is grounded in Proverbs 25:21–22. The "coals of fire" = the burning shame of being overcome by good rather than evil — or possibly the coals of purification (Isaiah 6 imagery).

The engine rendered "coals of fire" — *anthrakas pyros* = coals of fire — not "burning coals" (which collapses the noun-genitive into an adjective).

## VOCABULARY DECISIONS — PROVERBS SYSTEMATIC

### *Sophia* — wisdom

The dominant word of Proverbs — rendered "wisdom" throughout. *Sophia* = wisdom, skill in living, the capacity for right discernment — from *sophos* = wise. Never "skill" or "cleverness" — those are *dexiotēs* / *deinos*. The LXX *sophia* carries the covenant-wisdom register of the Hebrew *ḥokmah* = fundamental orientation toward God.

### *Phrónēsis* — prudence/understanding

Rendered "prudence" or "understanding" context-dependently. *Phrónēsis* = practical wisdom, the capacity for deliberate action — from *phroneō* = to think, to set the mind. Distinct from *sophia* = theoretical/foundational wisdom. The engine preserved the distinction throughout.

### *Epistēmē* — knowledge

Rendered "knowledge" — the systematic grasp of a subject. Distinct from *gnōsis* = intimate/experiential knowledge (which appears rarely in Proverbs).

### *Paideia* — instruction

Rendered "instruction" in most contexts — the child-training, formative-discipline vocabulary. When the *mastigoō* (scourge) register is active, "discipline" is used. The *paideia* vocabulary is the LXX's primary word for covenant formation — the shaping of the person through teaching and correction.

### *Phobos kyriou* — fear of the Lord

Rendered "fear of the Lord" throughout — the Hebrew *yir'at YHWH*. Not "reverence" or "awe" (which soften the force) — the *phobos* = fear vocabulary carries genuine terror and trembling at the majesty of God as its ground, with reverential worship as its fruit.

### *Eleos* — mercy

Rendered "mercy" throughout — as in the Psalms. The LXX rendering of *ḥesed* = covenant-faithfulness. The frequency of *eleos* in Proverbs establishes that mercy is not merely a divine attribute but a virtue required of those who have received it.

### *Dikaiosynē* — righteousness

Rendered "righteousness" throughout — the covenant-standing vocabulary. Never "justice" (*krisis*/*dike*) or "rectitude." The *dikaios* = righteous man / *dikaiosynē* = righteousness pair is Proverbs' primary ethical-anthropological vocabulary.

### *Hades* — Hades

Rendered "Hades" throughout — the realm below (2:18, 5:5, 7:27, 9:18, 14:12, 15:11, 15:24, 23:14, 27:20). Never "the grave" or "the underworld" — the LXX's consistent *hadēs* = Hades maintained.

### *Antitassetai* — resists

Proverbs 3:34 — rendered "resists" — the military-array vocabulary of *antitassō* = to draw up against in battle. The word chosen by James 4:6 and 1 Peter 5:5.

## THE VALIANT WOMAN — PROVERBS 31:10–31

The *gynē andreia* = valiant/capable woman poem closes the book — an acrostic in the Hebrew (one stanza per letter) though the acrostic structure is not preserved in the LXX. The LXX renders the poem with slight expansions.

Key vocabulary:
- *gynē andreia* = valiant woman — *andreios* = manly, brave, courageous — the same root as *andreia* = courage, one of the four classical virtues. Not "virtuous woman" (KJV) which is too moralistic — the LXX's *andreia* = valor, courage, strength.
- *timiotēra de esti lithōn polytelōn* = she is more precious than far-fetched and costly stones — the wisdom-surpasses-wealth vocabulary from chapter 8 closing the book.
- *gynē phobumenē ton kyrion hautē ainesthesetai* = a God-fearing woman — she is praised — the closing formula returning to the *phobos kyriou* = fear of the Lord of 1:7 and 9:10.

The engine rendered "valiant woman" — not "capable woman" (NRSV) or "excellent wife" (ESV) — the *andreia* = valor vocabulary preserved.

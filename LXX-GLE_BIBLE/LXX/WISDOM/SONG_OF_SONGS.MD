## STRUCTURAL NOTE — LXX SONG OF SONGS

The LXX *Asma Asmatōn* = Song of Songs — the superlative construction identical to *Mataiotēs Mataiotētōn* = Vanity of Vanities — both are Hebrew superlatives rendered with the genitive of intensification in Greek. "The song which is the greatest of songs."

The LXX Song of Songs follows the MT closely but with a Greek sensibility that occasionally smooths or reinterprets specific imagery. The dramatic speakers — Bride, Beloved, Daughters of Jerusalem, Brothers, Narrator — are not explicitly marked in the LXX text but are recoverable from context and ancient reading tradition. The engine marked them with `\sp` speaker-change markers following the Origen-Rahlfs reading tradition.

**Speakers:**
- **Bride** (*nymphē*) — the Shulammite woman, the beloved
- **Beloved** (*nymphios*) — the shepherd-king, Solomon figure
- **Daughters of Jerusalem** — the chorus
- **Brothers** — her mother's sons (8:8–9)
- **Narrator** — third-person descriptive passages (3:6–11)

## THEOLOGICAL REGISTER — SONG OF SONGS IN THE CANON

The Song of Songs was contested in the Jewish canon — Rabbi Akiba's famous declaration that all the writings are holy but the Song of Songs is the Holy of Holies secured its place. The canonical function requires dual-register reading:

**The literal register:** Human erotic love — the *eros* of the Bride and Beloved — rendered honestly and completely, without allegorizing away the physical. The LXX's vocabulary is explicitly sensual: breasts (*mastoi*), belly (*koilia*), thighs (*mēros*), lips (*cheilē*), mouth (*stoma*), kisses (*philēmata*). The engine rendered all of this without softening.

**The allegorical register:** From Origen onward the Song was read as the love of God for Israel / Christ for the Church / the soul for God. This reading is not imposed on the text but is available within it — and the NT picks up specific Song vocabulary in precisely this register (see CAE nodes below).

The GLE engine renders the text at the literal register and tracks the allegorical register in the notes — the canonical dual-reading preserved by marking it in notes rather than collapsing either into the other.

## CAE NODES — SONG OF SONGS

### Song 1:2 — *mastoi sou agathoi hyper oinon* / your breasts are better than wine

**1:2** — "Let him kiss me with the kisses of his mouth — for your breasts are better than wine."

The LXX renders the Hebrew *dōdekā* = your love / your caresses as *mastoi sou* = your breasts — a bodily reading of the Hebrew word that can mean both "love" and "caresses." This LXX choice is more explicitly physical than the MT. The engine followed the LXX rendering: "your breasts."

In the allegorical register: the *mastoi* = breasts as the nourishing abundance of divine love — the Song's opening image of intimacy and sustenance.

---

### Song 1:3 — *onoma sou myron ekkenōthen* / your name is oil poured out — CAE NODE

**1:3** — "Your name is oil poured out — therefore the young women loved you."

***onoma sou myron ekkenōthen*** = your name is oil poured out / your name is poured-out perfume. *Ekkenoō* = to pour out, to empty out — the same root as *ekenōsen* in **Philippians 2:7** — "he emptied himself (*ekenōsen heauton*) taking the form of a servant." The *kenōsis* = self-emptying vocabulary. The beloved's name as *poured-out oil* — fragrance spread through emptying — anticipates the incarnation vocabulary in its verbal root.

The engine rendered "oil poured out" — *myron ekkenōthen* — the *ekkenoō* vocabulary preserved.

---

### Song 2:4 — *taxate ep' eme agapēn* / set love in order over me — CAE NODE

**2:4** — "Bring me into the house of wine — set love in order over me."

***oikos tou oinou*** = house of wine — the banquet hall. ***taxate ep' eme agapēn*** = set love in order over me / arrange love over me. *Tassō* = to arrange in order, to appoint, to station — the military-order vocabulary. Love as the banner/standard (*agapē* = love — this is the LXX's choice for the Hebrew *degel* = banner/standard). The *agapē* = love vocabulary — the LXX's signature rendering of love throughout. The engine rendered "set love in order over me."

**Allegorical register:** The house of wine as the eucharistic feast — the arranging of *agapē* over the soul as the divine ordering of love — patristic reading from Origen's *Commentary on the Song of Songs*.

---

### Song 2:5 — *tetrōmenē agapēs egō eimi* / I am wounded with love — CAE NODE

**2:5** and **5:8** — "I am wounded with love."

***tetrōmenē agapēs egō eimi*** = I am pierced/wounded with love. *Titroōskō* = to wound, to pierce — from *traumatizō* / *titroōskō* — the wound vocabulary. **Revelation 1:7** — "every eye will see him — including those who pierced him (*hoi ekkentesan auton*)." The piercing/wounding register does not directly cite the Song but shares the *wound* vocabulary of love.

In the allegorical-christological register: the soul wounded by divine love — Origen develops this as the central metaphor of spiritual ascent. The engine rendered "wounded with love" — not "sick with love" (which is the usual modern rendering of the Hebrew *ḥolat 'ahavah*) — following the LXX *tetrōmenē* = pierced/wounded.

### Song 2:16 / 6:3 / 7:10 — *egō tō adelphidō mou kai ho adelphidos mou emoi* / I am my beloved's and my beloved is mine — CAE NODE

**2:16** — "My beloved is mine and I am his — the one who pastures among the lilies."

**6:3** — "I am my beloved's and my beloved is mine — the one who pastures among the lilies."

**7:10** — "I am my beloved's — and his desire is toward me."

The mutual-belonging formula — stated three times in escalating form:
- 2:16: symmetric — *mine and I am his*
- 6:3: reversed — *I am his and he is mine*
- 7:10: asymmetric — *I am his* — his *desire* is toward me

The *ephalē* = his desire (*teshuqah* in Hebrew) at 7:10 — the same word used at **Genesis 3:16** ("your desire will be toward your husband") — the post-Fall distortion of desire. Here in the Song the desire is restored, healed — the Beloved's desire is toward the Bride as it was intended to be before the Fall.

**Allegorical register:** The mutual-belonging formula as covenant — "I am yours and you are mine" — the covenant-love formula. **2 Corinthians 6:16** — "I will dwell in them and walk among them — and I will be their God and they will be my people." The mutual-belonging covenant.

### Song 3:6 / 8:5 — *tis hautē hē anabainousa* / who is this coming up — CAE NODE

**3:6** — "Who is this coming up from the desolate-place — as pillars of smoke."

**8:5** — "Who is this coming up from the desolate-place — leaning upon her beloved?"

The *anabainō* = coming up / ascending vocabulary — the Bride ascending from the *erēmos* = desolate-place/wilderness. **John 3:13** — "no one has ascended into heaven (*anabebēken eis ton ouranon*) except the one who descended from heaven." **Ephesians 4:10** — "the one who descended — he himself is also the one who ascended (*ho anabas*) above all the heavens."

The *desolate-place* / *erēmos* = the wilderness as the place of testing and formation — the Bride emerges from the wilderness leaning on her beloved. The forty-years wilderness of Israel / the forty-days wilderness of Jesus / the soul's dark night.

### Song 4:12–15 — *pēgē kēpōn* / fountain of gardens — CAE NODE

**4:12** — "A garden locked is my sister my bride — a garden locked — a spring sealed."

**4:15** — "A fountain of gardens — a well of living water — and flowing streams from Lebanon."

***pēgē hydata zōntos*** = a well/spring of living water. **John 4:10** — "If you knew the gift of God and who it is who says to you: Give me water to drink — you would have asked him and he would have given you living water (*hydōr zōn*)." **John 7:38** — "rivers of living water (*hydata zōnta*) will flow from his belly — as the scripture says." The *hydōr zōn* = living water vocabulary of the Song fires in the Johannine Christology of Jesus as the source of living water.

The *garden-locked* / *spring-sealed* as the virginal bride — and the opened spring as the gift of the Beloved. **Revelation 22:1** — "and he showed me a river of water of life (*potamon hydata zōēs*) — bright as crystal — flowing from the throne of God and of the Lamb."

### Song 5:2 — *egō katheudō kai hē kardia mou agrypnei* / I was sleeping but my heart was awake — CAE NODE

**5:2** — "I was sleeping but my heart was awake — the voice of my beloved knocking."

***egō katheudō kai hē kardia mou agrypnei*** = I sleep and my heart keeps watch. *Agrypneō* = to be sleepless, to keep watch, to be wakeful — from *agrypnia* = sleeplessness. **Mark 13:33** — "take heed — watch (*agrypneite*) and pray — for you do not know when the time is." **Ephesians 6:18** — "with all prayer and petition — praying at all times in the breath-of-life — and to this end keeping watch (*agrypnountes*) with all perseverance."

**Revelation 3:20** — "Look — I stand at the door and knock — if anyone hears my voice and opens the door — I will come in to him and dine with him and he with me." The Beloved's voice at the door / the sleeping Bride — the Revelation 3:20 image draws on Song 5:2.

### Song 8:6 — *krataios hōs thanatos ho zēlos* / jealousy is hard as Hades — CAE PRIMARY NODE

**8:6** — "Set me as a seal upon your heart — as a seal upon your arm — for love is strong as death — jealousy is hard as Hades — its flashes are flashes of fire — a flame of the Lord."

***hoti krataios hōs thanatos agapē — sklēros hōs hadēs zēlos — peristaseis autēs peristaseis pyros — phloges autēs*** = for love is strong as death — jealousy/zeal is hard as Hades — her sparks are sparks of fire — her flames.

The *phloges autēs* = her flames — the LXX adds *phloges* = flames making the fire-imagery explicit. **LXX adds:** "a flame of the Lord" — *salphingas autes salpinges pyros* — the LXX reads *šalhebetyāh* = flame of Yah (divine name compound) and renders it with fire-imagery.

This is the theological climax of the Song — love (*agapē*) is as strong as death (*thanatos*), jealousy/zeal (*zēlos*) is as relentless as Hades — and the fire of this love is divine (*flame of the Lord*).

**Romans 8:38–39** — "For I am convinced that neither death nor life... will be able to separate us from the love (*agapē*) of God." The *agapē stronger than death* of Song 8:6 is the ground of Paul's great confidence. Nothing as strong as death — and love is as strong as death — therefore love endures through death.

**1 Corinthians 13:8** — "Love (*agapē*) never falls away." The *agapē* = love of the Song's climax as the indestructible force.

The engine rendered "love is strong as death — jealousy is hard as Hades" — not "as unyielding as the grave" — preserving both *thanatos* = death and *Hades* = Hades in their specific LXX registers.

## VOCABULARY DECISIONS — SONG OF SONGS SYSTEMATIC

### *Agapē* — love

The LXX's dominant love-word throughout the Song — rendered "love" throughout. The LXX choice of *agapē* (rather than *erōs*) for the Song's primary love-vocabulary is one of the most significant theological decisions in the entire LXX translation. The Greek *erōs* = passionate/acquisitive love; *agapē* = covenantal/self-giving love. By using *agapē* for the Song's erotic love, the LXX establishes that the highest human eros is properly *agapē* — and that divine love is properly *agapē*.

**John 3:16** — "*Houtōs gar ēgapēsen ho theos ton kosmon*" = For God so loved (*agapaō*) the world. The *agapē* vocabulary — which the NT inherits from the LXX — has its richest OT source in the Song of Songs, where *agapē* is the word for the most intimate human love.

### *Mastoi* — breasts

Rendered "breasts" throughout — the LXX's explicit bodily vocabulary. The Song's physical register is not softened. The *mastoi* = breasts appear at 1:2, 1:4, 4:5, 4:10, 7:3, 7:7–8, 8:1, 8:8, 8:10.

### *Philia* / *adelphia* — companion / friend

The Beloved addresses the Bride as *hē plēsion mou* = my companion / my neighbor (2:2, 2:10, 2:13, 4:1, 4:7, 5:2, 6:4) — the *plēsion* = near one / companion. Also *adelphē* = sister (4:9, 4:10, 4:12, 5:1, 5:2) — the sister-bride paradox of intimate nearness and familial bond.

### *Erēmos* — desolate-place

Rendered "desolate-place" consistently — the LXX's *erēmos* = desolate/uninhabited place / wilderness. The same word used throughout the Torah and Prophets for the wilderness. The Bride ascends *from* the desolate-place — she is formed there and emerges leaning on her beloved.

### *Zēlos* — jealousy

**8:6** — rendered "jealousy" — *zēlos* = zeal, jealousy, ardent desire. The same *zēlos* vocabulary as Psalm 68:9 (zeal of your house), Numbers 25:11 (Phinehas's zeal), and 2 Corinthians 11:2 ("I am jealous for you with a divine jealousy"). The engine preserved "jealousy" — the burning-ardor register — rather than "passion" (which loses the competitive/exclusive force).

## CLOSING NOTE — THE SONG IN THE CANON

The Song of Songs occupies a unique position: it is the only book of the Hebrew Bible that does not mention God explicitly (though the LXX's *phloges autēs* = flame of the Lord preserves the divine-name compound). Its canonical placement — after Psalms and before the great Wisdom books in some LXX orders — positions it as the heart of the Ketuvim, the Holy of Holies of the writings.

The GLE engine rendered the Song at full literal precision — the bodily imagery, the physical descriptions, the erotic vocabulary — while the notes preserve the canonical allegorical register. Both readings are required by the history of interpretation and by the NT's own use of Song vocabulary.

## STRUCTURAL OVERVIEW — SIRACH

Sirach (also called Ecclesiasticus = "the church book") is the longest and most encyclopedic of the deuterocanonical Wisdom books. Ben Sira was a professional wisdom teacher (*sopher*) in Jerusalem, writing before the Maccabean crisis. The book is a sustained synthesis of Torah and Wisdom tradition — the longest sustained argument that wisdom = Torah = fear of the Lord in all of ancient Jewish literature.

**Major sections:**

| Chapters | Content |
|----------|---------|
| 1–23 | Wisdom discourses — fear of the Lord, speech, family, wealth, friendship |
| 24 | Wisdom's self-praise — the central hymn |
| 25–43 | Practical wisdom — women, physicians, craftsmen, creation hymn |
| 44–49 | Praise of the Fathers (*ainesis pateron*) |
| 50 | Simon the High Priest — living climax |
| 51 | Ben Sira's personal thanksgiving and acrostic invitation |

## CAE NODES — SIRACH 1–44

### Sirach 1:1–10 — *archē sophias phobos kyriou* / the beginning of wisdom is the fear of the Lord — CAE NODE

**1:1** — "All wisdom is from the Lord — and is with him to the age."

**1:6** — "The root of wisdom — to whom has it been revealed? — and her devices — who knows them?"

**1:9–10** — "He created her — he saw her and counted her — and he poured her out upon all his works. She is with all flesh according to his gift — and he supplied her freely to those who love him."

The Sirach opening establishes the monotheistic-wisdom thesis: all wisdom has a single source — the Lord. This directly generates **James 1:5** — "If any of you lacks wisdom let him ask from God who gives to all generously." The *didōsin* = gives freely of James echoes the *echoregesato* = supplied freely of Sirach 1:10.

**1:14** — "The fear of the Lord is the beginning of wisdom — and was created with the faithful in the womb." The *archē sophias phobos kyriou* formula — identical to **Proverbs 1:7** and **9:10** — establishes Sirach's continuity with the Proverbs tradition. The engine rendered "fear of the Lord" and "beginning of wisdom" throughout.

---

### Sirach 2:1–6 — Testing and trust — CAE NODE

**2:1** — "My son — if you come to serve the Lord — prepare your soul for testing."

**2:4–6** — "Accept whatever is brought upon you — and in changes of your humiliation be patient. For gold is tested in fire — and acceptable men in the furnace of humiliation. Trust in him and he will help you — make your ways straight and hope in him."

**1 Peter 1:7** — "the tested genuineness of your faith — more precious than gold that perishes though tested by fire." **James 1:3** — "the testing of your faith produces endurance." The gold-in-fire testing vocabulary of Sirach 2 fires throughout the NT's theology of suffering as proving.

### Sirach 3:17–20 — Humility and the humble — CAE NODE

**3:17–18** — "My son — conduct your affairs with humility — and you will be loved more than one who gives gifts. The greater you are the more humble yourself — and you will find favor before the Lord."

**3:20** — "For great is the might of the Lord — and he is glorified by the humble."

**James 4:6** / **1 Peter 5:5** — "God resists the proud but gives grace to the humble." The Sirach 3 development of *tapeinophrosynē* = humility as the ground of divine favor is one of the deepest wells of the NT humility theology. **Matthew 23:12** — "whoever exalts himself will be humbled — and whoever humbles himself will be exalted." The engine rendered "humble yourself" and "humble" — *tapeinōson seauton* — the *tapeinoō* vocabulary throughout.

### Sirach 4:10 — *ginou orphanois hōs patēr* / be as a father to orphans — CAE NODE

**4:10** — "Be as a father to orphans — and as a husband to their mother — and you will be like a son of the Most High — and he will love you more than your mother does."

The *huios Hypsistou* = son of the Most High — applied to the merciful person. **Luke 6:35** — "love your enemies and do good and lend expecting nothing in return — and your reward will be great — and you will be sons of the Most High (*huioi Hypsistou*) — for he is kind to the ungrateful and the evil." The *huios Hypsistou* = son of the Most High as the designation for those who practice divine mercy — Sirach 4:10 is the background for Luke 6:35.

### Sirach 6:19–31 — The yoke of wisdom — CAE PRIMARY NODE

**6:19–22** — "Come to her like one who plows and sows — and wait for her good produce. For in cultivating her you will labor a little — and soon you will eat of her produce. How harsh she appears to the undisciplined — and the foolish do not remain with her."

**6:24–28** — "Put your feet into her fetters — and your neck into her collar. Stoop your shoulder and carry her — and do not be annoyed by her bonds. Come to her with all your soul — and keep her ways with all your strength. Search out and seek — and she will become known to you — and when you get hold of her do not let her go. For at last you will find the rest that she gives — and she will be changed into joy for you."

**6:30–31** — "Her fetters will be for you a strong protection — and her collar a glorious robe. Her yoke is a golden ornament — and her bonds are a purple cord."

This is the first and fullest of Sirach's yoke-of-wisdom passages — the source for **Matthew 11:28–30**. The *zygos* = yoke, the *anapausis* = rest, the *come* invitation — all present in Sirach 6 before being restated in 51:23–27 and then taken up by Jesus in Matthew 11. **Matthew 11:29–30** — "Take my yoke upon you and learn from me... and you will find rest for your souls — for my yoke is easy and my burden is light." The engine rendered "yoke" (*zygos*), "rest" (*anapausis*), and "collar/neck" throughout.

**7:29–31** — "With all your soul fear the Lord — and honor his priests. With all your might love him who made you — and do not forsake his ministers. Fear the Lord and honor the priest — and give him his portion as you have been commanded."

The Sirach exhortation to honor the Levitical priesthood — background for the NT's extension of priestly honor theology to Christ's priesthood in Hebrews.

### Sirach 10:14–15 — *thronous archontōn katheilen* / he cast down the thrones of rulers — CAE NODE

**10:14–15** — "The Lord overthrew the thrones of rulers — and seated the humble in their place. The Lord plucked up the roots of nations — and planted the humble in their place."

***thronous archontōn katheilen ho kyrios kai ekathisen praeeis ant' autōn*** = the Lord cast down the thrones of rulers and seated the gentle in their place.

**Luke 1:52** — "He brought down the powerful from their thrones — and exalted the humble (*tapeinous*)." Mary's Magnificat draws directly on Sirach 10:14 — the same *kathairein* = cast down / *thronoi* = thrones / *tapeinoi* = humble vocabulary. The Magnificat is a catena of LXX wisdom texts and Sirach 10:14 is among its primary sources.

But God said to him: Fool — this night they are requiring your soul from you." The foolish rich man's *psychē anepauou* = soul rest of Luke 12 echoes the Sirach 11:19 pattern of the man who says "I have found rest."

### Sirach 15:11–17 — Free will and not blaming God for sin — CAE NODE

**15:11–12** — "Do not say: Because of the Lord I left the right way — for he will not do what he hates. Do not say: He led me astray — for he has no need of a sinful man."

### Sirach 17:1–12 — The creation of man — CAE NODE

**17:1–4** — "The Lord created man from the earth — and turned him back to it again. He gave them a fixed number of days — and granted them authority over the things upon it. He clothed them with strength like himself — and made them in his own image (*kat' eikona autou*)."

**17:11** — "He bestowed knowledge upon them — and allotted to them the law of life."

The *kat' eikona* = according to his image of Genesis 1:27 — Sirach 17:3 applies the image-of-God theology to the creation of all humanity. The engine rendered "in his own image" — *kat' eikona autou*.

### Sirach 24:1–22 — Wisdom's Self-Praise — CAE PRIMARY NODE

**24:3** — "I came out of the mouth of the Most High — and like a mist I covered the earth."

**24:8–10** — "Then the creator of all things gave me a commandment — and the one who created me made my dwelling place rest — and he said: Make your dwelling in Jacob — and in Israel receive your inheritance... In the holy tent I ministered before him — and so I was established in Zion."

**24:19–21** — "Come to me you who desire me — and eat your fill from my produce. For my memory is sweeter than honey — and my inheritance sweeter than the honeycomb. Those who eat me will still be hungry — and those who drink me will still be thirsty."

The Wisdom self-praise of Sirach 24 is one of the most important texts in the entire deuterocanon for NT Christology:

**John 1:1,14** — "In the beginning was the Logos... and the Logos became flesh and dwelt among us (*eskēnōsen en hēmin*)." The Wisdom who "came out of the mouth of the Most High" (24:3) — who was commanded to "make your dwelling (*kataskēnōson*)  in Jacob" (24:8) — is the Logos who "dwelt (*eskēnōsen*)" in human flesh. The *skēnoō* = dwell/tabernacle vocabulary is the direct link: Sirach 24 → John 1:14.

**John 6:35** — "I am the bread of life — whoever comes to me will not hunger — and whoever believes in me will never thirst." Jesus's bread-of-life discourse in John 6 directly counters the Sirach Wisdom invitation: Sirach 24:21 says those who eat Wisdom will *still be hungry* — Jesus says the one who comes to him will *never* hunger. The Johannine Christology explicitly engages Sirach 24.

**Colossians 1:15–17** — Wisdom "came out of the mouth of the Most High" (24:3) → the Son as the one through whom all things were created — the cosmic Wisdom identified with the pre-existent Christ.

**24:23** — "All this is the book of the covenant of the Most High God — the law which Moses commanded as an inheritance to the assemblies of Jacob." The identification of Wisdom with Torah — *nomos* = law — is Ben Sira's central theological move. The Johannine Prologue takes the opposite move: Wisdom = *Logos* = Jesus, not Torah. The Fourth Gospel is in direct dialogue with Sirach 24's Torah-Wisdom identification.

### Sirach 27:30–28:7 — Forgiveness and not holding grudges — CAE NODE

**28:2–5** — "Forgive your neighbor the wrong he has done — and then your sins will be pardoned when you pray. Does a man harbor anger against another — and yet seek healing from the Lord? Does he have no mercy toward a man like himself — and yet pray for his own sins?"

**Matthew 6:12,14–15** — "And forgive us our debts as we also have forgiven our debtors... For if you forgive men their trespasses — your heavenly Father will also forgive you — but if you do not forgive men their trespasses — neither will your Father forgive your trespasses." The Sirach 28:2–5 logic — if you don't forgive your neighbor God won't forgive you — is the exact logic of Matthew 6:14–15 and the Lord's Prayer's fifth petition.

**Mark 11:25** — "And whenever you stand praying — forgive if you have anything against anyone — so that your Father in heaven may forgive you your trespasses."

### Sirach 29:10–13 — Alms as treasury in heaven — CAE NODE

**29:11–12** — "Lose your silver for the sake of a brother or a friend — and do not let it rust under a stone and be lost. Lay up your treasure according to the commandments of the Most High — and it will profit you more than gold."

**Matthew 6:19–20** — "Do not lay up for yourselves treasures on earth where moth and rust destroy and where thieves break in and steal — but lay up for yourselves treasures in heaven." The Sirach 29 vocabulary of *thēsauros* = treasure laid up according to the commandments directly anticipates the Matthew 6 contrast between earthly and heavenly treasure.

**Luke 12:33** — "Sell your possessions and give alms — provide yourselves with moneybags that do not grow old — with a treasure that does not fail in heaven."

### Sirach 35:12–13 — God does not show partiality — CAE NODE

**35:12–13** — "Do not offer him a bribe — for he will not accept it — and do not trust in an unrighteous sacrifice — for the Lord is the judge — and with him there is no partiality (*prosōpolēmpsia*)."

***ou gar estin par' autō doxazein prosōpon*** = for with him there is no glorifying of a face / showing of partiality. **Romans 2:11** — "For there is no partiality (*prosōpolēmpsia*) with God." **Acts 10:34** — "God is not one to show partiality (*prosōpolēmptēs*)." **James 2:1** — "do not hold the faith of our Lord Jesus Christ with partiality (*prosōpolēmpsiais*)." The *prosōpolēmpsia* = partiality/face-receiving vocabulary — a LXX coinage — appears first in fully developed form in Sirach and fires throughout the NT.

### Sirach 38:1–15 — Honor to the physician — CAE NODE

**38:1–4** — "Honor the physician with the honor due him — according to your need of him — for the Lord created him. For healing comes from the Most High — and he will receive a gift from a king. The skill of the physician lifts up his head — and in the sight of great men he is admired. The Lord created medicines from the earth — and a sensible man will not despise them."

The most sustained medical theology in the OT/deuterocanon. The physician as a gift of divine wisdom — the medicines as created goods to be honored. Ben Sira integrates natural medicine within covenant theology. **Colossians 4:14** — "Luke the beloved physician sends greetings." The LXX tradition of honoring physicians as exercising God-given wisdom is the context for the early church's medical practice.

### Sirach 39:16–35 — All the works of the Lord are good — CAE NODE

**39:16** — "The works of God are all good — and he supplies every need in its season."

**39:33–35** — "The works of the Lord are all good — and he will supply every need in its season. And no one can say: This is worse than that — for all things will prove good in their season... Therefore with all one's heart one must praise the name of the Lord — and give thanks to him."

The Sirach 39 theodicy: divine providence is good in all things — not because all things appear good but because God supplies each need in its *kairos* = appointed time. **Romans 8:28** — "And we know that for those who love God all things work together for good." The *panta synergei eis agathon* = all things work together for good of Romans 8:28 engages the Sirach 39 theodicy tradition.

### Sirach 40:1–11 — The burden of Adam — CAE NODE

**40:1** — "Much labor was created for every man — and a heavy yoke is upon the sons of Adam — from the day they come out of their mother's womb — until the day they return to the mother of all."

The *Adam* burden vocabulary — heavy labor as the consequence of the Fall. **Romans 5:12** — "through one man sin entered the world and death through sin — and so death spread to all men." The Sirach 40 meditation on the universal burden of Adam's sons is the deuterocanonical context for Paul's Adam-Christology.

### Sirach 44:1–15 — Let us now praise famous men — CAE NODE

**44:1** — "Let us now praise famous men — and our fathers who begat us."

***ainēsōmen dē andras endoxous*** = let us praise famous/glorious men. The opening of the *ainesis pateron* = Praise of the Fathers. The hymn runs from Enoch (44:16) through Simon the High Priest (50) — the entire sweep of Israel's salvation history recapitulated as a wisdom genealogy.

**Hebrews 11:1–40** — the great faith-roll call from Abel through the unnamed heroes — is the NT's counterpart to Sirach 44–50. Both follow the same pattern: historical figures enumerated with their defining virtue or act. **Hebrews 11:2** — "for by it the people of old received their commendation (*emarturēthēsan*)." The *encomium* / praise of the heroes as the ground of exhortation for present faith.

### Sirach 44:16–17 — Enoch and Noah — CAE NODE

**44:16** — "Enoch pleased the Lord and was translated — an example of repentance for all generations."

**44:17** — "Noah was found perfect and righteous — in the season of wrath he was the exchange — therefore a remnant was left to the earth when the flood came."

**Hebrews 11:5** — "By faith Enoch was translated so that he should not see death — and he was not found — because God had translated him." The *metatithēmi* = translate/transfer vocabulary — Sirach 44:16 and Hebrews 11:5 use the same word.

**44:17** — *antallagnā egenēthē tō kosmō* = he became an exchange for the world — the *antallagnā* = exchange theology for Noah. The substitutionary-preservation vocabulary.

## VOCABULARY DECISIONS — SIRACH SYSTEMATIC

### *Phobos kyriou* — fear of the Lord

The foundational vocabulary of the entire book — stated at 1:14, 1:16, 1:18, 1:20, 1:27, 2:7–9, 2:15–17, and throughout. Rendered "fear of the Lord" consistently — never "reverence" or "awe." The *phobos* = fear vocabulary maintained at full force.

### *Sophia* — wisdom

Rendered "wisdom" throughout — the *sophia* personification of chapter 24 and the practical wisdom of chapters 1–23 and 25–43 all maintained in the same vocabulary. Never "skill" or "understanding" (which are *phronēsis* and *synesis* respectively).

### *Tapeinophrosynē* / *tapeinosis* — humility / humiliation

Rendered "humility" and "humiliation" throughout — the *tapein-* root vocabulary that generates the NT's teaching on humility. Sirach is the deepest OT well for the NT humility ethic: 3:17–20, 7:17, 10:14–15, 11:1.

### *Prosōpolēmpsia* — partiality

Rendered "partiality" — the LXX coinage *prosōpolēmpsia* = face-receiving/showing favoritism. This word does not exist in classical Greek — it is a Hebraism coined in the LXX (*nasa panim* = lift up the face = show favor). The engine rendered "partiality" throughout — the technical NT vocabulary preserved.

### *Zygos* — yoke

Rendered "yoke" throughout — 6:24–30, 51:26 — the disciplinary-wisdom yoke that Matthew 11:29–30 takes up. Never "burden" or "collar."

### *Anapausis* — rest

Rendered "rest" throughout — 6:28, 51:27 — the *anapausis* = settled rest that wisdom gives and Matthew 11:28–29 promises. Never "relaxation" or "ease."

### *Eleos* — mercy

Rendered "mercy" throughout — the covenant-mercy vocabulary. Sirach uses *eleos* extensively as both a divine attribute and a human virtue that images God.

### *Diathēkē* — covenant

Rendered "covenant" throughout — never "agreement" or "testament." The Sirachic theology is covenant-theology — the *diathēkē* with Abraham, Moses, Aaron, Phinehas, David — the covenantal structure of salvation history.

### *Hades*

Rendered "Hades" throughout — 14:12, 17:27, 21:10, 28:21, 51:5–6, and elsewhere. Never "the grave" or "the underworld." The *hadēs* = realm of the dead maintained.

## THEOLOGICAL NOTE — SIRACH'S PLACE IN THE NT

Sirach is probably the most-used deuterocanonical book in the NT — both directly and by allusion. Key lines of influence:

**1. Matthew 11:28–30** ← Sirach 6:19–31 and 51:23–27 — the yoke-and-rest invitation (Jesus as Wisdom)

**2. Luke 1:52** ← Sirach 10:14 — the Magnificat (casting down the powerful, exalting the humble)

**3. James 1:5,13** ← Sirach 1:10 and 15:11–12 — wisdom given freely; God does not tempt

**4. Matthew 6:14–15** ← Sirach 28:2–5 — forgiveness as the condition of being forgiven

**5. Matthew 6:19–20** ← Sirach 29:11–12 — treasure in heaven vs. treasure on earth

**6. Romans 2:11** / Acts 10:34** ← Sirach 35:12–13 — no partiality with God

**7. Hebrews 11** ← Sirach 44–49 — the praise-of-heroes genre

**8. John 1:1–14** ← Sirach 24 — Wisdom coming from the mouth of the Most High, dwelling in Israel — the Johannine Logos as the fulfillment/supersession of Ben Sira's Wisdom-Torah identification

**9. 1 Peter 1:7 / James 1:3** ← Sirach 2:4–6 — testing as gold in fire

The book's canonical function: it is the bridge between the OT Wisdom tradition and the NT Christological appropriation of that tradition. Jesus's identification as Wisdom incarnate — clearest in Matthew 11 and John 1 — requires Sirach as its primary OT preparation.

**Quick notes on how it lands**

- **"jealous and avenges"** (ζηλωτὴς καὶ ἐκδικῶν / *zelotes et ulciscens*) — 1:2 — Thayer: *zēlotēs* = one burning with zeal, a jealous guardian of exclusive claim; *ekdikeō* = to vindicate, to exact full legal penalty, to carry out the sentence. L&S: *zelotes* = a zealous partisan; *ulciscor* = to take vengeance, to punish in return for injury received. The opening verse is not emotional volatility — it is a legal declaration. The Lord is the exclusive-claim holder who executes the penalty for breach. Three times the vengeance verb fires in two verses — the LXX repetition is deliberate intensification, not redundancy.

- **"long-enduring"** (μακρόθυμος / *patiens*) — 1:3 — Thayer: *makrothumos* = long of passion, slow to anger, holding the wrath long before releasing it; L&S: *patiens* = enduring, bearing up under, one who sustains without breaking. The word sits between the declaration of vengeance (v.2) and the declaration that he will not declare the guilty innocent (v.3). Long-enduring is not soft — it is the quality of one who holds the full weight of wrath without releasing it prematurely. The longer the endurance the greater the stored force.

- **"he knows those who reverence him"** (γινώσκων τοὺς εὐλαβουμένους αὐτόν / *sciens eos qui sperant in eum*) — 1:7 — Thayer: *ginōskō* = to know by experience, to recognize, to have intimate acquaintance with; *eulabeomai* = to take hold carefully, to act with circumspect reverence. L&S: *scio* = to know with certainty, to have sure knowledge of; *spero* = to hope in, to place confident expectation upon. Jerome's Latin shifts "those who reverence" to "those who hope in him" — a slight pull toward trust rather than careful reverence. Both converge on: the Lord has personal knowledge of those oriented toward him. Not general awareness — named recognition.

- **"burden / city of blood"** (λῆμμα / *onus* — πόλις αἱμάτων / *civitas sanguinum*) — 1:1 / 3:1 — The book opens with *lēmma* — the received weight of prophetic vision pressing down on the seer. It closes the arc at 3:1 with *polis haimatōn* — the city constituted by blood, whose institutional identity is bloodshed. L&S: *civitas* = the body of citizens, the city as a legal entity; *sanguis* = blood, both shed blood and bloodline. The city is not merely violent — it is defined by blood as its founding substance. What Babylon is in Revelation, Nineveh is here — the archetypal city whose charter is built from the blood of others.

- **"there is no healing for your fracturing"** (οὐκ ἔστιν ἴασις τῇ συντριβῇ σου / *non est curatio contritioni tuae*) — 3:19 — Thayer: *iasis* = a healing, a cure, a restoration to wholeness; *syntribē* = a crushing, a shattering, a breaking that goes through. L&S: *curatio* = a caring for, a medical treatment, a course of healing; *contritio* = a grinding down, a thorough crushing. The final verdict is medical and forensic simultaneously — the fracture is through-and-through, the kind that cannot be set. Jerome's *contritio* gives the word its full force: ground fine, not merely cracked. The book that opened with the Lord as cosmic warrior closes with a wound diagnosis that admits no remedy.

**Drift eliminated:** Check 1 fired on 1:2 — "takes vengeance on his foes" committee-smoothed; restored full triple-vengeance declaration with legal-execution weight. Check 3 fired on 1:3 — "long-suffering" pew-register replaced with long-enduring carrying the stored-force implication. Check 2 fired on 1:7 — Jerome's *sperant* diverges from LXX *eulabeomai*; both noted, divergence preserved in notes rather than collapsed. Check 5 fired on 3:1 — "city of blood" rendered too quickly as atmospheric; restored *polis* as legal-entity constituted by blood. Check 4 fired on 3:19 — closing verse drifted toward familiar lament register; rewritten from the medical-forensic precision both lexicons mandate.

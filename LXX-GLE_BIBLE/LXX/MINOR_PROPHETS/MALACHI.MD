**Quick notes on how it lands**

- **"burden"** (λῆμμα / *onus*) — 1:1 — Thayer: a thing received, a prophetic oracle received as weight pressing upon the seer; LSJ: that which is taken up, a reception — in prophetic usage the oracle received from God as a load carried. The same opening word as Nahum and Habakkuk. Three books of the Twelve open with *lēmma* — the received weight. Malachi closes the canonical prophets with the same word that opens the prosecution of Nineveh and the cosmic-war theodicy of Habakkuk. The Minor Prophets close the same way they opened — as weighted oracles, not devotional reflections.

- **"my messenger / messenger of the covenant"** (ἄγγελός μου / ἄγγελος τῆς διαθήκης) — 3:1 — Thayer: *aggelos* = a messenger, one sent with a commission — in LXX and NT consistently the divine messenger class; *diathēkē* = a covenant, a legal disposition, a binding arrangement between parties. LSJ: *aggelos* = a messenger, an envoy; *diathēkē* = a legal settlement, a testament, a covenant that binds. Two messengers in one verse — the forerunner messenger who prepares the way and the covenant-messenger who arrives. Mark 1:2 quotes this verse directly applying it to John the Baptist. The LXX *aggelos* in both cases carries the full divine-messenger weight — not merely a human herald but one from the messenger-class. The covenant-messenger who suddenly enters his temple is not a human reformer — he arrives as the Lord himself, the party to the covenant, coming to inspect its terms.

- **"who will endure / who will stand firm"** (τίς ὑπομενεῖ / τίς ὑποστήσεται) — 3:2 — Thayer: *hypomenō* = to remain under, to endure by staying in place under pressure — not passive waiting but active bearing-up under weight; *hypostēnai* = to stand under, to take a position beneath and hold it. LSJ: *hypomenō* = to stay behind, to await steadfastly, to endure without fleeing; *hypostēnai* = to stand firm, to withstand. Two Greek verbs for two dimensions of the same challenge — the first asks who can bear the weight of his coming, the second who can hold position at his appearing. The smelting-furnace image that follows answers why the question is genuine: he comes to burn through what cannot endure and what remains is what was always pure.

- **"treasured-possession"** (περιποίησιν / *peculium*) — 3:17 — Thayer: *peripoiēsis* = a preserving, a making something one's own possession by acquiring it — the word carries the sense of something specifically set apart as personal property through deliberate acquisition. LSJ: a making one's own, a personal acquisition, a possession preserved for oneself. Vulgate *peculium* = a private property, specifically the personal savings of a slave or soldier that belonged to them alone — the most intimate and carefully guarded form of possession in Roman law. Two-witness confirmation: Thayer and L&S both arrive at the same point — these are not merely God's people in general but his specifically acquired, personally preserved, most-intimate possession. The same word appears in Exodus 19:5 LXX for Israel as the Lord's *peripoiēsis* among all nations. Malachi closes the prophetic canon by restating the Sinai covenant's central term.

- **"sun of righteousness with healing in its wings"** (ἥλιος δικαιοσύνης καὶ ἴασις ἐν ταῖς πτέρυξιν αὐτοῦ) — 4:2 — Thayer: *hēlios* = the sun; *dikaiosynē* = righteousness, the quality of being right, of conforming to the divine standard; *iasis* = a healing, a cure, a restoration to wholeness; *pteryx* = a wing, a pinion — used for the wings of birds and of divine beings. LSJ: *iasis* = healing, medical restoration; *pteryx* = wing, the broad protective wing of a bird covering its young. The image is the winged solar disk of the ancient Near East redeployed — not as a pagan emblem but as the Lord's righteousness rising as a sun whose very wings carry healing as they spread. Those who fear the name leap as calves released from bonds — the healing is not slow restoration but sudden liberation into exuberant motion. The book that ends the canonical prophets closes on a sunrise and calves leaping.

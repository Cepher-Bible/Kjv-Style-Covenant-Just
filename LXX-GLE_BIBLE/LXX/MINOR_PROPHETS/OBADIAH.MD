**Quick notes on how it lands**

- **"vision"** (ὅρασις / *visio*) — 1:1 — Thayer: *horasis* = a seeing, a vision, that which is seen in a state of prophetic perception — the thing beheld by the seer in the moment of divine disclosure. LSJ: a sight, a spectacle, a vision granted to one who sees beyond normal perception. The book opens not with *lēmma* (the received weight of Nahum, Habakkuk, Malachi) but with *horasis* — the seen thing, the visual disclosure. Obadiah is not a burden pressing down — it is a sight opened. The shortest book in the Hebrew canon opens as a visual event. Vulgate *visio* confirms — Jerome preserved the seeing-event register.

- **"dispatch among the nations"** (ἀκοὴν ἀπέστειλεν / *legationem misit*) — 1:1 — Thayer: *akoē* = a hearing, a report, that which is heard — a communicated intelligence; *apostellō* = to send out on a commission, to dispatch with authority. LSJ: *akoē* = a thing heard, a report, a message received through hearing; *apostellō* = to send forth, to commission for a specific task. The Lord sends out a *hearing* — a piece of intelligence dispatched among the nations as a mobilization signal. Vulgate *legationem* = a diplomatic embassy, an official legation. Jerome reads it as a formal diplomatic dispatch — a state communication sent to coalition nations calling them to war against Edom. Two witnesses converging on the same institutional register: this is not a rumor spreading — it is an official war-summons dispatched by the Lord among the nations.

- **"arrogance of your heart"** (ὑπερηφανία τῆς καρδίας σου / *superbia cordis tui*) — 1:3 — Thayer: *hyperēphania* = arrogance, the condition of thinking oneself above others — from *hyper* (over) + *phainomai* (to appear) — literally the one who appears to stand over others. LSJ: pride that shows itself, ostentatious arrogance, the visible display of self-elevation. Vulgate *superbia* = pride that goes over, that exceeds proper bounds — L&S: the pride that lifts above what is fitting. The arrogance is not internal feeling — it is a posture visible from outside. Edom's dwelling in the cliff-clefts is not merely geographical security — it is the architectural expression of the internal arrogance. The rock-nest is the pride made visible in stone.

- **"you should not have"** (μὴ + aorist subjunctive × 7) — vv.12–14 — Thayer: the negative *mē* with aorist subjunctive in a series = prohibition against a completed action — you ought not to have done what you did. LSJ: the construction expresses reproach for a past action that cannot be undone — the grammar of accusation over irreversible deeds. Seven prohibitions fired in three verses — each one a completed crime indicted in sequence. This is not warning against future behavior. It is the formal enumeration of charges against actions already performed. The grammar itself is forensic — past-tense prohibition is the accusation register. The Edomite crimes are itemized like a legal count sheet: stood and watched, rejoiced, opened the mouth, entered the gates, looked down, reached out, stood at the crossroads, shut in the escapees. Eight indictments in three verses delivered in the grammatical form of "you should not have."

- **"the kingdom will be the Lord's"** (καὶ ἔσται τῷ κυρίῳ ἡ βασιλεία / *et erit Domino regnum*) — 1:21 — Thayer: *basileia* = kingship, royal power, the reign of a king — not merely a territory but the exercise of sovereign authority; the dative *tō kyriō* = belonging to the Lord, as his possession and domain. LSJ: *basileia* = the royal power, the sovereignty, the active reign. Vulgate *regnum* confirms — Jerome preserved the active-reign register. The shortest book in the canon closes on the widest claim — the sovereign exercise of kingship belongs to the Lord. Not "God will be king" as a future aspiration — the kingdom *will be* the Lord's as the outcome of everything Obadiah described: Edom stripped, Zion delivered, the exiles returned, the mountain avenged. The cosmic re-ordering lands in five words. The Minor Prophets sequence has been building to this — Joel's valley of the verdict, Amos's rebuilt tent of David, Micah's ruler from Bethlehem, Nahum's Nineveh destroyed, Habakkuk's earth filled with the glory of the Lord — all converging on the same final claim Obadiah makes in its last line.

Check 3 fired on v.21 — "the kingdom will be the Lord's" almost rendered as "the Lord will reign" losing the *basileia* active-sovereignty weight; restored as kingdom-belonging-to with full royal-power register. Check 5 fired on vv.12–14 — the seven-fold prohibition series almost softened into future warnings; restored as past-tense forensic accusation register — the grammar of irreversible charges. Check 6 fired on v.1 — *ēkousamen* (we heard) almost dropped as implied; restored — the communal hearing of the dispatch is structural, not decorative.

\v 1 In the first year of Cyrus king of Persia — that the word of the Lord by the mouth of Jeremiah might be fulfilled — the Lord stirred up the breath-of-life (*pneuma*) of Cyrus king of Persia so that he made a proclamation throughout all his kingdom and also put it in writing:
\v 2 Thus says Cyrus king of Persia: The Lord the God of heaven has given me all the kingdoms of the earth and he has charged me to build him a house at Jerusalem which is in Judah.
\v 3 Whoever is among you of all his people — may his God be with him and let him go up to Jerusalem which is in Judah and rebuild the house of the Lord the God of Israel — he is the God who is in Jerusalem.
\v 4 And let each survivor in whatever place he sojourns be assisted by the men of his place with silver and gold with goods and with beasts besides freewill offerings for the house of God that is in Jerusalem.
\p
\v 5 Then rose up the heads of the fathers' houses of Judah and Benjamin and the priests and the Levites — everyone whose breath-of-life (*pneuma*) God had stirred to go up to rebuild the house of the Lord that is in Jerusalem.
\v 6 And all who were about them aided them with vessels of silver with gold with goods with beasts and with costly wares besides all that was freely offered.
\v 7 Cyrus the king also brought out the vessels of the house of the Lord that Nebuchadnezzar had carried away from Jerusalem and placed in the house of his gods.
\v 8 Cyrus king of Persia brought these out in the charge of Mithredath the treasurer who counted them out to Sheshbazzar the prince of Judah.
\p
\v 11 All the vessels of gold and silver were five thousand four hundred and sixty-nine. All these did Sheshbazzar bring up when the exiles were brought up from Babylonia to Jerusalem.

\v 1 When the seventh month came and the sons of Israel were in the towns the people gathered as one man to Jerusalem.
\v 2 Then arose Jeshua the son of Jozadak with his fellow priests and Zerubbabel the son of Shealtiel with his relatives and they built the altar of the God of Israel to offer burnt offerings on it as it is written in the Law of Moses the man of God.
\v 3 They set the altar in its place — for fear was on them because of the peoples of the lands — and they offered burnt offerings on it to the Lord morning and evening.
\v 4 And they kept the Feast of Booths as it is written and offered the daily burnt offerings by number according to the rule as each day required —
\v 5 and after that the regular burnt offerings the new moon offerings and all the appointed feasts of the Lord that were consecrated and the offerings of everyone who made a freewill offering to the Lord.
\v 6 From the first day of the seventh month they began to offer burnt offerings to the Lord — but the foundation of the temple of the Lord was not yet laid.
\v 11 And they sang responsively praising and giving thanks to the Lord:
\q1 For he is good —
\q2 for his steadfast love (*eleos*) endures forever toward Israel.
\p And all the people shouted with a great shout when they praised the Lord because the foundation of the house of the Lord was laid.
\v 12 But many of the priests and Levites and heads of fathers' houses — old men who had seen the first house — wept with a loud voice when they saw the foundation of this house being laid though many shouted aloud for joy —
\v 13 so that the people could not distinguish the sound of the joyful shout from the sound of the people's weeping — for the people shouted with a great shout and the sound was heard far away.

\v 1 Now when the adversaries of Judah and Benjamin heard that the returned exiles were building a temple to the Lord the God of Israel —
\v 2 they approached Zerubbabel and the heads of fathers' houses and said to them: Let us build with you — for we worship your God as you do and we have been sacrificing to him ever since the days of Esarhaddon king of Assyria who brought us here.
\v 3 But Zerubbabel Jeshua and the rest of the heads of fathers' houses in Israel said to them: You have nothing to do with us in building a house to our God — but we alone will build to the Lord the God of Israel as King Cyrus the king of Persia has commanded us.
\v 4 Then the people of the land discouraged the people of Judah and made them afraid to build —
\v 5 and bribed counselors against them to frustrate their purpose all the days of Cyrus king of Persia — even until the reign of Darius king of Persia.

\v 21 It was eaten by the people of Israel who had returned from exile and also by everyone who had joined them and separated himself from the uncleanness of the peoples of the land to worship the Lord the God of Israel.
\v 22 And they kept the Feast of Unleavened Bread seven days with joy — for the Lord had made them joyful and had turned the heart of the king of Assyria to them so that he aided them in the work of the house of God the God of Israel.

\v 1 Now after this in the reign of Artaxerxes king of Persia Ezra the son of Seraiah son of Azariah son of Hilkiah —
\v 6 this Ezra went up from Babylonia. He was a scribe skilled in the Law of Moses that the Lord the God of Israel had given — and the king granted him all that he asked for the hand of the Lord his God was on him.
\v 7 And there went up also to Jerusalem in the seventh year of Artaxerxes the king some of the people of Israel and some of the priests and Levites the singers the gatekeepers and the temple servants.
\v 8 And Ezra came to Jerusalem in the fifth month which was in the seventh year of the king.
\v 9 For on the first day of the first month he began to go up from Babylonia and on the first day of the fifth month he came to Jerusalem — for the good hand of his God was on him.
\v 10 For Ezra had set his heart (*kardia*) to study the Law of the Lord and to do it and to teach his statutes and rules in Israel.
\p
\v 11 This is a copy of the letter that King Artaxerxes gave to Ezra the priest the scribe — a scribe of the words of the commandments of the Lord and his statutes for Israel:
\v 12 Artaxerxes king of kings — to Ezra the priest the scribe of the Law of the God of heaven. Peace. And now —
\v 13 I make a decree that anyone of the people of Israel or their priests or Levites in my kingdom who freely offers to go to Jerusalem may go with you.
\v 14 For you are sent by the king and his seven counselors to make inquiries about Judah and Jerusalem according to the Law of your God which is in your hand —
\v 25 And you Ezra according to the wisdom of your God that is in your hand appoint magistrates and judges who may judge all the people in the province Beyond the River — all such as know the laws of your God. And those who do not know them you shall teach.
\v 26 Whoever will not obey the law of your God and the law of the king let judgment be strictly executed on him — whether death or banishment or confiscation of goods or imprisonment.
\p
\v 27 Blessed be the Lord the God of our fathers who put such a thing as this into the heart of the king to beautify the house of the Lord that is in Jerusalem —
\v 28 and who extended to me his steadfast love (*eleos*) before the king and his counselors and before all the king's mighty officers. I took courage for the hand of the Lord my God was on me and I gathered leading men from Israel to go up with me.

\v 21 Then I proclaimed a fast there at the river Ahava that we might humble ourselves before our God to seek from him a safe journey for ourselves our children and all our goods.
\v 22 For I was ashamed to ask the king for a band of soldiers and horsemen to protect us against the enemy on our way — since we had told the king: The hand of our God is for good on all who seek him and the power of his wrath is against all who forsake him.
\v 23 So we fasted and implored our God for this and he listened to our entreaty.
\p
\v 31 Then we departed from the river Ahava on the twelfth day of the first month to go to Jerusalem. The hand of our God was on us and he delivered us from the hand of the enemy and from ambushes by the way.
\v 32 We came to Jerusalem and there we remained three days.
\v 33 On the fourth day within the house of our God the silver the gold and the vessels were weighed into the hands of Meremoth the priest son of Uriah and with him was Eleazar the son of Phinehas and with them were the Levites Jozabad the son of Jeshua and Noadiah the son of Binnui.
\v 35 At that time those who had come from captivity — the returned exiles — offered burnt offerings to the God of Israel: twelve bulls for all Israel ninety-six rams seventy-seven lambs and as a sin offering twelve male goats. All this was a burnt offering to the Lord.
\v 36 They also delivered the king's commissions to the king's satraps and to the governors of the province Beyond the River and they aided the people and the house of God.

\v 1 After these things had been done the officials approached me and said: The people of Israel and the priests and the Levites have not separated themselves from the peoples of the lands with their abominations — from the Canaanites the Hittites the Perizzites the Jebusites the Ammonites the Moabites the Egyptians and the Amorites.
\v 2 For they have taken some of their daughters to be wives for themselves and for their sons so that the holy offspring has mixed itself with the peoples of the lands. And in this faithlessness the hand of the officials and chief men has been foremost.
\p
\v 3 As soon as I heard this I tore my garment and my cloak and pulled hair from my head and beard and sat appalled.
\v 5 And at the evening sacrifice I rose from my fasting with my garment and my cloak torn and fell upon my knees and spread out my hands to the Lord my God —
\v 6 saying: O my God I am ashamed and blush to lift my face to you my God — for our iniquities have risen higher than our heads and our guilt has mounted up to the heavens.
\v 7 From the days of our fathers to this day we have been in great guilt — and for our iniquities we our kings and our priests have been given into the hand of the kings of the lands — to the sword to captivity to plundering and to utter shame as it is today.
\v 8 But now for a brief moment favor has been shown by the Lord our God to leave us a remnant and to give us a stake in his holy place that our God may brighten our eyes and grant us a little reviving in our slavery.
\v 9 For we are slaves. Yet our God has not forsaken us in our slavery but has extended to us his steadfast love (*eleos*) before the kings of Persia to give us reviving to set up the house of our God to repair its ruins and to give us protection in Judea and Jerusalem.
\v 10 And now O our God — what shall we say after this? For we have forsaken your commandments —
\v 11 which you commanded by your servants the prophets saying: The land that you are entering to take possession of it is a land impure with the impurity of the peoples of the lands with their abominations that have filled it from end to end with their uncleanness.
\v 12 Therefore do not give your daughters to their sons neither take their daughters for your sons and never seek their peace or prosperity that you may be strong and eat the good of the land and leave it for an inheritance to your children forever.
\v 13 And after all that has come upon us for our evil deeds and for our great guilt — seeing that you our God have punished us less than our iniquities deserved and have given us such a remnant as this —
\v 14 shall we break your commandments again and intermarry with the peoples who practice these abominations? Would you not be angry with us until you consumed us so that there should be no remnant nor any to escape?
\v 15 O Lord the God of Israel you are righteous — for we are left a remnant that has escaped as it is today. Look — we are before you in our guilt — for none can stand before you because of this.

\v 1 And all the people gathered as one man into the square before the Water Gate. And they told Ezra the scribe to bring the Book of the Law of Moses that the Lord had commanded Israel.
\v 2 So Ezra the priest brought the Law before the assembly both men and women and all who could understand what they heard on the first day of the seventh month.
\v 3 And he read from it facing the square before the Water Gate from early morning until midday in the presence of the men and the women and those who could understand. And the ears of all the people were attentive to the Book of the Law.
\v 4 And Ezra the scribe stood on a wooden platform that they had made for the purpose. And beside him stood Mattithiah Shema Anaiah Uriah Hilkiah and Maaseiah on his right hand — and Pedaiah Mishael Malchijah Hashum Hashbaddanah Zechariah and Meshullam on his left hand.
\v 5 And Ezra opened the book in the sight of all the people — for he was above all the people — and as he opened it all the people stood.
\v 6 And Ezra blessed the Lord the great God and all the people answered: Amen — Amen lifting up their hands. And they bowed their heads and worshipped the Lord with their faces to the ground.
\v 7 Also Jeshua Bani Sherebiah Jamin Akkub Shabbethai Hodiah Maaseiah Kelita Azariah Jozabad Hanan Pelaiah and the Levites helped the people to understand the Law while the people remained in their places.
\v 8 They read from the book from the Law of God clearly and they gave the sense so that the people understood the reading.
\p
\v 9 And Nehemiah who was the governor and Ezra the priest and scribe and the Levites who taught the people said to all the people: This day is holy to the Lord your God. Do not mourn or weep. For all the people wept as they heard the words of the Law.
\v 10 Then he said to them: Go your way. Eat the fat and drink sweet wine and send portions to anyone who has nothing ready — for this day is holy to our Lord. And do not be grieved — for the joy of the Lord is your strength.
\v 11 So the Levites calmed all the people saying: Be quiet — for this day is holy — do not be grieved.
\v 12 And all the people went their way to eat and drink and to send portions and to make great rejoicing — because they had understood the words that were declared to them.

\v 13 On the second day the heads of fathers' houses of all the people with the priests and the Levites came together to Ezra the scribe in order to study the words of the Law.
\v 14 And they found it written in the Law that the Lord had commanded by Moses that the people of Israel should dwell in booths during the feast of the seventh month —
\v 15 and that they should proclaim and publish it in all their towns and in Jerusalem: Go out to the hills and bring branches of olive wild olive myrtle palm and other leafy trees to make booths as it is written.
\v 16 So the people went out and brought them and made booths for themselves each on his roof and in their courts and in the courts of the house of God and in the square at the Water Gate and in the square at the Gate of Ephraim.
\v 17 And all the assembly of those who had returned from the captivity made booths and lived in the booths — for from the days of Jeshua the son of Nun to that day the people of Israel had not done so. And there was very great rejoicing.
\v 18 And day by day from the first day to the last day he read from the Book of the Law of God. They kept the feast seven days and on the eighth day there was a solemn assembly according to the rule.

\v 1 Now on the twenty-fourth day of this month the people of Israel were assembled with fasting and in sackcloth and with earth on their heads.
\v 2 And the Israelites separated themselves from all foreigners and stood and confessed their sins and the iniquities of their fathers.
\v 3 And they stood up in their place and read from the Book of the Law of the Lord their God for a quarter of the day — for another quarter of it they made confession and worshipped the Lord their God.
\v 4 On the stairs of the Levites stood Jeshua Bani Kadmiel Shebaniah Bunni Sherebiah Bani and Chenani — and they cried with a loud voice to the Lord their God.
\v 5 Then the Levites Jeshua Kadmiel Bani Hashabneiah Sherebiah Hodiah Shebaniah and Pethahiah said: Stand up and bless the Lord your God from everlasting to everlasting. Blessed be your glorious name which is exalted above all blessing and praise.
\v 6 You are the Lord — you alone. You have made heaven the heaven of heavens with all their host — the earth and all that is on it — the seas and all that is in them — and you preserve all of them — and the host of heaven worships you.
\v 7 You are the Lord the God who chose Abram and brought him out of Ur of the Chaldeans and gave him the name Abraham.
\v 8 You found his heart faithful before you and made with him the covenant to give to his offspring the land of the Canaanite the Hittite the Amorite the Perizzite the Jebusite and the Girgashite. And you have kept your promise — for you are righteous.
\v 9 And you saw the affliction of our fathers in Egypt and heard their cry at the Red Sea —
\v 10 and performed signs and wonders against Pharaoh and all his servants and all the people of his land — for you knew that they acted arrogantly against our fathers. And you made a name for yourself as it is to this day.
\v 11 And you divided the sea before them so that they went through the midst of the sea on dry land — and you cast their pursuers into the depths as a stone into mighty waters.
\v 12 By a pillar of cloud you led them in the day and by a pillar of fire in the night to light for them the way in which they should go.
\v 13 You came down on Mount Sinai and spoke with them from heaven and gave them right rules and true laws — good statutes and commandments —
\v 14 and you made known to them your holy Sabbath and commanded them commandments and statutes and a law by Moses your servant.
\v 15 You gave them bread from heaven for their hunger and brought water for them out of the rock for their thirst — and you told them to go in to possess the land that you had sworn to give them.
\v 16 But they and our fathers acted presumptuously and stiffened their neck and did not obey your commandments.
\v 17 They refused to obey and were not mindful of the wonders that you performed among them — but they stiffened their neck and appointed a leader to return to their slavery in Egypt. But you are a God ready to forgive (*eleos*) gracious and merciful slow to anger and abounding in steadfast love (*eleos*) and did not forsake them.
\v 18 Even when they had made for themselves a golden calf and said: This is your God who brought you up out of Egypt — and had committed great blasphemies —
\v 19 you in your great mercies (*eleos*) did not forsake them in the wilderness. The pillar of cloud to lead them in the way did not depart from them by day — nor the pillar of fire by night to light for them the way in which they should go.
\v 20 You gave your good breath-of-life (*pneuma*) to instruct them and did not withhold your manna from their mouth and gave them water for their thirst.
\v 21 Forty years you sustained them in the wilderness and they lacked nothing. Their clothes did not wear out and their feet did not swell.
\v 22 And you gave them kingdoms and peoples and allotted to them every corner. So they took possession of the land of Sihon king of Heshbon and the land of Og king of Bashan.
\v 23 You multiplied their children as the stars of heaven and brought them into the land that you had told their fathers to enter and possess.
\v 24 So the descendants went in and possessed the land and you subdued before them the inhabitants of the land the Canaanites and gave them into their hand with their kings and the peoples of the land that they might do with them as they would.
\v 25 And they captured fortified cities and a rich land and took possession of houses full of all good things — cisterns already hewn out vineyards olive orchards and fruit trees in abundance. So they ate and were filled and became fat and delighted themselves in your great goodness.
\v 26 Nevertheless they were disobedient and rebelled against you and cast your law behind their back and killed your prophets who had warned them in order to turn them back to you and they committed great blasphemies.
\v 27 Therefore you gave them into the hand of their enemies who made them suffer. And in the time of their suffering they cried out to you and you heard them from heaven and according to your great mercies you gave them saviors (*sōtēras*) who saved them from the hand of their enemies.
\v 28 But after they had rest they did evil again before you and you abandoned them to the hand of their enemies so that they had dominion over them. Yet when they turned and cried to you you heard from heaven and many times you delivered them according to your mercies.
\v 29 And you warned them in order to turn them back to your law. Yet they acted presumptuously and did not obey your commandments but sinned against your rules — which if a person does them he shall live by them — and they turned a stubborn shoulder and stiffened their neck and would not obey.
\v 30 Many years you bore with them and warned them by your breath-of-life (*pneuma*) through your prophets. Yet they would not give ear. Therefore you gave them into the hand of the peoples of the lands.
\v 31 Nevertheless in your great mercies you did not make an end of them or forsake them — for you are a gracious and merciful God.
\v 32 Now therefore our God — the great the mighty and the awesome God who keeps covenant and steadfast love (*eleos*) — let not all the hardship seem little to you that has come upon us — upon our kings our princes our priests our prophets our fathers and all your people — since the time of the kings of Assyria until this day.
\v 33 Yet you have been righteous in all that has come upon us — for you have dealt faithfully and we have acted wickedly.
\v 34 Our kings our princes our priests and our fathers have not kept your law or paid attention to your commandments and your warnings that you gave them.
\v 35 Even in their own kingdom and amid your great goodness that you gave them — and in the large and rich land that you set before them — they did not serve you or turn from their wicked works.
\v 36 Look — we are slaves this day — in the land that you gave to our fathers to enjoy its fruit and its good gifts — look — we are slaves.
\v 37 And its rich yield goes to the kings whom you have set over us because of our sins. They rule over our bodies and over our livestock as they will — and we are in great distress.
\p
\v 38 Because of all this we make a firm covenant in writing — on the sealed document are the names of our princes our Levites and our priests.

## CRITICAL TEXTUAL NOTES — 2 ESDRAS

**LXX canonical structure:** The LXX tradition combines Ezra and Nehemiah into a single book (2 Esdras), in contrast to the MT which treats them as two books. The GLE follows the LXX combined structure. The Nehemiah material runs as a continuous narrative following the Ezra material — there is no chapter-break between "Ezra" and "Nehemiah" in the LXX sense.

**Nehemiah 8 positioning:** In the LXX 2 Esdras, the Law-reading of Nehemiah 8 appears in its MT position (Nehemiah 8). In 1 Esdras, the same event appears at the book's conclusion (1 Esdras 8:70–89). The GLE renders the 2 Esdras version as the canonical text and cross-references 1 Esdras.

**The Nehemiah memoirs:** The first-person sections of Nehemiah (1–7, 12–13) are the earliest extended first-person prose narrative in the biblical canon. The repeated *remember me O my God* petitions (13:14, 22, 29, 31) are the most compressed prayer-forms in Scripture.

**The great prayer of Nehemiah 9:** The Levitical prayer of Nehemiah 9:5–37 is one of the longest sustained theological prayers in the OT — a comprehensive covenant-history from creation through the exile, structured as confession and appeal. It is the post-exilic equivalent of Solomon's Temple prayer and the theological summary of the entire Paralipomenon narrative.

## CAE NODES — 2 ESDRAS

### 2 Esdras 1:1–5 — the *pneuma* of Cyrus / the *pneuma* of the returning remnant — CARRIED FORWARD NODE

**1:1–2** — "the Lord stirred up the breath-of-life (*pneuma*) of Cyrus king of Persia so that he made a proclamation throughout all his kingdom."

**1:5** — "Then rose up the heads of the fathers' houses of Judah and Benjamin and the priests and the Levites — everyone whose breath-of-life (*pneuma*) God had stirred to go up to rebuild the house of the Lord."

The double *pneuma*-stirring carried forward from 2 Paralipomenon 36:22 and 1 Esdras 2:1–8 is the opening declaration of 2 Esdras. The restoration begins with a movement of the *pneuma* — not with military victory or political strategy but with God stirring the inner life of a pagan king and then the remnant of his own people.

The two *pneuma* stirrings are structurally parallel: *wayyā'ar YHWH et-rûaḥ Kōreš* / *kōl-'ăšer hē'îr 'ĕlōhîm et-rûḥô* = the Lord stirred the *pneuma* of Cyrus / all whose *pneuma* God had stirred. The pagan emperor and the faithful remnant share the same divine *pneuma*-movement. Neither acts from purely human motivation — both are moved by God.

**Acts 16:14** — "The Lord opened her heart (*kardia*) to pay attention to what was said by Paul." The opening of Lydia's *kardia* by the Lord is the NT equivalent of the *pneuma*-stirring: the divine initiative preceding the human response. As God stirred the *pneuma* of Cyrus and the returning exiles, God opens the *kardia* of Lydia to receive the word.

**Philippians 2:13** — "for it is God who works in you both to will and to work for his good purpose." Paul's declaration that God works the *thelein* = willing and the *energein* = working is the theological formulation of the double *pneuma*-stirring: the desire to return (will) and the act of returning (work) are both divine movements within the human *pneuma*.

### 2 Esdras 3:11–13 — the mixed sound at the foundation-laying — CAE PRIMARY NODE

**3:11–13** — "And they sang responsively praising and giving thanks to the Lord: *For he is good — for his steadfast love (*eleos*) endures forever toward Israel.* And all the people shouted with a great shout when they praised the Lord because the foundation of the house of the Lord was laid. But many of the priests and Levites and heads of fathers' houses — old men who had seen the first house — wept with a loud voice when they saw the foundation of this house being laid though many shouted aloud for joy — so that the people could not distinguish the sound of the joyful shout from the sound of the people's weeping — for the people shouted with a great shout and the sound was heard far away."

***kai ouk ēdunainto ho laos gnōnai tēn phōnēn tēs alalagas tēs euphrosynēs apo tēs phōnēs tou klauthmou tou laou***

= and the people were not able to distinguish the sound of the shout of joy from the sound of the weeping of the people.

The two sounds cannot be separated — the indistinguishable mixture of joy and grief at the laying of the second Temple's foundation is one of the most theologically precise moments in the restoration literature. Those who never saw Solomon's Temple shout for joy; those who remember it weep for what is gone. Both responses are right. Neither cancels the other.

The theological structure: the *eleos endures forever* refrain (same as 2 Paralipomenon 5:13 / 7:3) triggers the shout — and the shout and the weeping become one sound. The *eleos* is great enough to hold both: the grief of what was lost and the joy of what is being recovered are both appropriate responses to the one steadfast love that endures through both.

**Psalm 126:5–6** — "Those who sow in tears shall reap with shouts of joy. He who goes out weeping bearing the seed for sowing shall come home with shouts of joy bringing his sheaves with him." The tears-and-joy pattern of Psalm 126 is the lyrical counterpart to the 2 Esdras 3:12–13 narrative: the exile was the sowing in tears / the return is the reaping in joy. But at the moment of the foundation-laying the two sounds are still simultaneous — the psalm's sequence has not yet fully resolved.

**John 16:20–22** — "You will weep and lament but the world will rejoice. You will be sorrowful but your sorrow will turn into joy... but I will see you again and your hearts will rejoice and no one will take your joy from you." Jesus's grief-turned-to-joy promise to the disciples before the passion is the NT fulfillment of the 2 Esdras 3:12–13 pattern: the simultaneous weeping and shouting at the second Temple's foundation is the OT type of the disciples' grief-turned-joy at the resurrection. The two sounds that could not be distinguished will resolve: the weeping will turn to the shout that no one takes away.

**Revelation 21:4** — "He will wipe away every tear from their eyes." The final resolution of the mixed sound of 2 Esdras 3:12–13: the day when the weeping and the joy are no longer simultaneous / when the grief of exile is permanently answered.

### 2 Esdras (Ezra) 7:10 — Ezra's *kardia* set to seek the Law — CAE PRIMARY NODE

**7:10** — "For Ezra had set his heart (*kardia*) to study the Law of the Lord and to do it and to teach his statutes and rules in Israel."

***hoti Esdras edōken en kardia autou zētēsai ton nomon Kyriou kai poiein kai didaxai en Israēl***

= for Ezra gave in his heart to seek the law of the Lord and to do it and to teach in Israel.

The three-stage Ezra-program: *seek* → *do* → *teach*. The sequence is not reversed: Ezra studies before he acts and acts before he teaches. The *kardia* is the location of the decision — the inner organ of directed intention. The *edōken en kardia* = gave in his heart is the strongest form of personal commitment in the wisdom tradition.

The Ezra-program is the covenant-restoration through the word: the Temple has been rebuilt but the people are not yet formed by the Torah. The second restoration — the formation of the community by the Law — requires the scribe who has internalized the law before transmitting it.

**Deuteronomy 6:6–7** — "And these words that I command you today shall be on your heart (*kardia*). And you shall teach them diligently to your children." The Shema's *on your heart* → *teach your children* sequence is the Mosaic version of Ezra's seek → do → teach program. Ezra is the Shema-scribe: he embodies the covenant-pedagogy that Moses commanded.

**Matthew 5:19** — "Therefore whoever relaxes one of the least of these commandments and teaches others to do the same will be called least in the kingdom of heaven — but whoever does them and teaches them will be called great in the kingdom of heaven." Jesus's *does and teaches* (*poiēsē kai didaxē*) criterion for greatness in the kingdom is the Ezra-program restated: doing before teaching / the sequence is fixed. The *poiēsē kai didaxē* of Matthew 5:19 is the Ezra 7:10 sequence applied to kingdom-citizenship.

**James 1:22** — "But be doers of the word (*poiētai logou*) and not hearers only — deceiving yourselves." James's *poiētai logou* = doers of the word is the middle term of the Ezra-program applied to the NT community: seeking (hearing) → doing → only then teaching. The doer who does not teach is incomplete; the teacher who does not do is a contradiction.

### 2 Esdras (Ezra) 9:6–9 — Ezra's confessional prayer — CAE PRIMARY NODE

**9:6** — "O my God I am ashamed and blush to lift my face to you my God — for our iniquities have risen higher than our heads and our guilt has mounted up to the heavens."

**9:8–9** — "But now for a brief moment favor has been shown by the Lord our God to leave us a remnant and to give us a stake in his holy place that our God may brighten our eyes and grant us a little reviving in our slavery. For we are slaves. Yet our God has not forsaken us in our slavery but has extended to us his steadfast love (*eleos*) before the kings of Persia."

***ki avadim anaḥnu uve'avdutenu lo azavanu elohenu*** = for we are slaves and in our slavery our God has not forsaken us.

The Ezra prayer is the post-exilic confession par excellence. Its structural theology is precise:

1. **Shame-posture** (9:6): *ebomai* = I am ashamed / *ēschynthēn* = I blush. The prostrate confession begins with the visceral response of guilt — the face cannot be lifted. This is the anti-posture of the priest: the high priest enters the holy of holies to stand before God; Ezra cannot lift his face to God at all.

2. **Historical guilt-chain** (9:7): "From the days of our fathers to this day we have been in great guilt." The personal sin is embedded in the generational pattern — Ezra confesses not just his contemporaries' sin but the covenant-failure running from the fathers.

3. **The remnant-grace** (9:8): *ki me'aṭ rega'* = for a brief moment. The grace is qualified: it is *small* and *for a moment*. The *reviving* (*miḥyāh* = life-renewal) is proportionate to the need — not triumphant restoration but survival.

4. **Slavery-and-steadfast-love** (9:9): The most paradoxical statement in the prayer — *we are slaves but God has not forsaken us in our slavery*. The *eleos* operates within the condition of exile, not after its resolution. The steadfast love does not wait for the restoration to appear; it is present in the bondage.

**Daniel 9:4–19** — Daniel's great prayer in Babylon has the same structure: historical guilt-confession / appeal to divine *eleos* / the contrast of our wickedness and God's righteousness. The two prayers (Ezra 9 / Daniel 9) are the post-exilic confession tradition — both scribal / both interceding for the nation / both grounding the appeal in divine *eleos* rather than human merit.

**Romans 3:23–24** — "For all have sinned and fall short of the glory of God and are justified by his grace as a gift through the redemption that is in Christ Jesus." Paul's *pantes ēmarton* = all have sinned is the universal form of Ezra's national confession (*our iniquities have risen higher than our heads*). The Ezra prayer's admission that there is no standing-ground in human merit — only in divine *eleos* — is the OT ground for Paul's *sola gratia* declaration.

**1 John 1:9** — "If we confess our sins he is faithful and just to forgive us our sins and to cleanse us from all unrighteousness." The Johannine confession-forgiveness formula is the NT crystallization of the Ezra confessional pattern: confession → divine faithfulness → cleansing. The *pistos kai dikaios* = faithful and just of 1 John 1:9 is the NT equivalent of Ezra's appeal to divine *eleos* that operates within slavery.

### 2 Esdras (Nehemiah) 8:1–12 — Ezra reads the Law / the joy of the Lord is your strength — CAE PRIMARY NODE

**8:5–6** — "And Ezra opened the book in the sight of all the people — for he was above all the people — and as he opened it all the people stood. And Ezra blessed the Lord the great God and all the people answered: Amen — Amen lifting up their hands. And they bowed their heads and worshipped the Lord with their faces to the ground."

**8:8** — "They read from the book from the Law of God clearly and they gave the sense so that the people understood the reading."

**8:10** — "Then he said to them: Go your way. Eat the fat and drink sweet wine and send portions to anyone who has nothing ready — for this day is holy to our Lord. And do not be grieved — for the joy of the Lord is your strength."

***mē asthenēte — hē gar euphrosynē Kyriou ischys hymōn*** = do not be weak — for the joy of the Lord is your strength.

The Law-reading assembly is the covenant-formation event of the restoration: the Temple has been rebuilt and the walls completed — now the people are constituted as a community by the Law. Three elements define the assembly:

**1. The posture of reception** (8:5–6): All stand when the book is opened. Standing is the posture of covenant-attentiveness — the same as Israel at Sinai. The double Amen with lifted hands and bowing faces is the full-body covenant-response: affirmation (Amen / lifted hands) + adoration (faces to the ground).

**2. The interpretive mediation** (8:8): *meforash* = clearly / with interpretation. The Levites *gave the sense* so that people understood — the word is not merely read but explained. The teaching-ministry is inseparable from the reading. This is the origin of the synagogue practice of Torah-reading with explanation — and the NT pattern of the sermon.

**3. The feast-command** (8:9–12): The people weep when they hear the Law — the word produces compunction. But the holy day demands joy, not grief. The command is not to suppress the tears but to redirect them: the covenant-response to the Law on the holy day is *simḥah* = joy. The *euphrosynē Kyriou ischys hymōn* = the joy of the Lord is your strength is the most compressed statement of the covenant-joy theology in the OT.

**Psalm 119:92** — "If your law had not been my delight I would have perished in my affliction." The Psalm 119 law-delight is the sustained meditation on what Nehemiah 8:10 declares in one sentence: the law-joy is the source of life-strength against affliction.

**Luke 24:32** — "Did not our hearts burn within us while he talked to us on the road while he opened to us the Scriptures?" The Emmaus disciples' *kardia* burning is the NT equivalent of the Nehemiah 8:5–12 assembly: the Scriptures opened / the sense given / the inner response of joy-in-the-word. The *kaioumenē hē kardia* = burning heart is the NT form of the Nehemiah-joy.

**Hebrews 4:12** — "For the word of God is living and active sharper than any two-edged sword piercing to the division of soul and breath-of-life (*psychēs kai pneumatos*)." The Hebrews characterization of the word as living and active is the NT theological generalization of the Nehemiah 8 scene: the word that made the people weep and then feast is the living word that divides and discerns the interior person.

**The joy-of-the-Lord formula:** *euphrosynē Kyriou ischys hymōn* = the joy of the Lord is your strength. The genitive is objective and subjective simultaneously: the joy *that the Lord gives* and the joy *that the Lord himself has* over his people (cf. Zephaniah 3:17 — *he will rejoice over you with gladness*). The people's feast on the holy day participates in the Lord's own joy over his restored community.

### 2 Esdras (Nehemiah) 9:5–37 — the great Levitical prayer — CAE PRIMARY NODE

**9:6** — "You are the Lord — you alone. You have made heaven the heaven of heavens with all their host — the earth and all that is on it — the seas and all that is in them — and you preserve all of them — and the host of heaven worships you."

**9:17** — "You are a God ready to forgive gracious and merciful slow to anger and abounding in steadfast love (*eleos*) and did not forsake them."

**9:20** — "You gave your good breath-of-life (*pneuma*) to instruct them and did not withhold your manna from their mouth and gave them water for their thirst."

**9:27** — "Therefore you gave them into the hand of their enemies who made them suffer. And in the time of their suffering they cried out to you and you heard them from heaven and according to your great mercies you gave them saviors (*sōtēras*) who saved them from the hand of their enemies."

**9:30** — "Many years you bore with them and warned them by your breath-of-life (*pneuma*) through your prophets."

**9:36–37** — "Look — we are slaves this day — in the land that you gave to our fathers to enjoy its fruit and its good gifts — look — we are slaves."

The Nehemiah 9 prayer is the OT's most comprehensive theological narrative-prayer. Its structure is the entire salvation-history compressed into intercession:

**Creation** (9:6) → **Abraham and the covenant** (9:7–8) → **Egypt and the exodus** (9:9–11) → **Sinai and the law** (9:13–14) → **Wilderness provision** (9:15–21) → **Conquest** (9:22–25) → **The cycle of rebellion and deliverance** (9:26–31) → **The present slavery** (9:36–37).

The theological spine of the prayer is the *eleos* / *rahûm* / *ḥannûn* cluster (9:17, 19, 27, 28, 31): at every point of Israel's failure, the divine steadfast love holds. The prayer is structured to demonstrate that *every* divine deliverance was an act of *eleos* — not of human merit.

**The *pneuma* of instruction** (9:20): *edōkas to pneuma sou to agathon tou synbibazein autous* = you gave your good breath-of-life to instruct them. The wilderness *pneuma* given to instruct is the Spirit of Wisdom operating through the Law and the cloud-pillar. This is the OT ground for the NT identification of the *pneuma hagion* as the *pneuma* of teaching and instruction.

**The *sōtēras* = saviors given** (9:27): The judges raised up in the Judges cycle are called *sōtēras* = saviors in this prayer — the same word applied to Jesus in Luke 2:11 (*a Savior who is Christ the Lord*). The Nehemiah prayer identifies the salvation-pattern: suffering → crying out → the Lord hearing → sending a *sōtēr*. This is the pattern that reaches its ultimate fulfillment in the Son sent as the final *sōtēr*.

**Romans 5:8** — "God shows his love for us in that while we were still sinners Christ died for us." Paul's *en tō* = while yet / during our sin is the NT form of the Nehemiah 9 theology: at every point of Israel's disobedience the Lord acted in *eleos* before the repentance was complete. The cross is the ultimate enactment of the *eleos* that Nehemiah 9 rehearses across the entire salvation-history.

**Jude 5** — "Now I want to remind you although you once fully knew it that Jesus who saved a people out of the land of Egypt afterward destroyed those who did not believe." Jude's summary of the wilderness-failure uses the same salvation-history frame as Nehemiah 9 — the exodus deliverance and the wilderness rebellion as the pattern of covenant-failure that the audience must not repeat.

### 2 Esdras (Nehemiah) 9:36–37 — *we are slaves* / the unfinished restoration — CAE CRITICAL NODE

**9:36–37** — "Look — we are slaves this day — in the land that you gave to our fathers to enjoy its fruit and its good gifts — look — we are slaves. And its rich yield goes to the kings whom you have set over us because of our sins. They rule over our bodies and over our livestock as they will — and we are in great distress."

***hinneh anachnu hayyôm avadim ve'eres asher natatta la'avotenu le'echol et-periyah ve'et-tuvah — hinneh anachnu avadim aleha***

= look we are today slaves / and in the land which you gave to our fathers to eat its fruit and its goodness / look we are slaves on it.

The prayer ends not in triumph but in confession of ongoing slavery. The Temple has been rebuilt. The walls of Jerusalem are standing. The Law has been read. And yet — the people are still slaves in their own land, paying tribute to Persian kings. The restoration is real but incomplete. The exile has not ended in the deepest sense: the people are back in the land but do not possess it. They are *slaves in the inheritance*.

This is the theological crisis that no post-exilic prophet or scribe resolves. The *malkût* = kingdom has not returned to Israel. The Davidic throne is empty. The *doxa* = glory has not returned to the Temple. The *pneuma* of the Lord has not been poured out. The restoration is a mercy — but it is not the fulfillment. Something greater is still to come.

**Isaiah 61:1** — "The Spirit of the Lord God is upon me because the Lord has anointed me to bring good news to the poor — he has sent me to bind up the brokenhearted — to proclaim liberty to the captives and the opening of the prison to those who are bound." The Isaiah 61 liberation-proclamation is the prophetic answer to the Nehemiah 9:36–37 slavery-confession: the *deror* = liberty / *pesaḥ* = opening announced by the Anointed One is the true end of the slavery that the physical return from Babylon did not fully resolve.

**Luke 4:18–21** — Jesus reads Isaiah 61 in the Nazareth synagogue: "The Spirit of the Lord is upon me because he has anointed me to proclaim good news to the poor... to proclaim liberty to the captives... Today this Scripture has been fulfilled in your hearing." Jesus's *sēmeron* = today is the direct answer to the *hinneh* = look of Nehemiah 9:36. The *look — we are slaves* of the post-exilic confession is answered by *today — the captives are set free* of the Messianic proclamation. The fulfillment of the restoration that Nehemiah 9 declares incomplete is announced from the synagogue in Nazareth.

**Galatians 4:3–5** — "In the same way we also when we were children were enslaved to the elementary principles of the world. But when the fullness of time had come God sent forth his Son — born of woman — born under the law — to redeem those who were under the law so that we might receive son-placement (*huiothesian*)." Paul's *douleia* = slavery under the *stoicheia* = elementary principles is the universalization of Nehemiah 9:36–37: all humanity in the condition of *slaves in their own inheritance*. The *apolytrōsis* = ransom-release sent in the fullness of time is the cosmic answer to the post-exilic *hinneh anachnu avadim* = look we are slaves. The *huiothesia* = son-placement is the opposite of slavery: from *avadim* = slaves to *huioi* = sons.

**Romans 8:15** — "For you did not receive the breath-of-life of slavery to fall back into fear but you have received the breath-of-life of son-placement (*huiothesias*) by whom we cry: Abba Father." The *pneuma* of slavery (*douleia*) versus the *pneuma* of *huiothesia* = son-placement is Paul's theological resolution of the Nehemiah 9:36–37 slavery-confession: the *pneuma* stirred in the returning exiles (Ezra 1:5) is the anticipation of the *pneuma hagiou* that brings final liberation — not from Persian tribute but from the slavery-to-sin that no return from Babylon could cure.

### 2 Esdras (Nehemiah) 13:31 — *remember me O my God for good* — CAE NODE

**13:22** — "Remember this also in my favor O my God and spare me according to the greatness of your steadfast love (*eleos*)."

**13:31** — "Remember me O my God for good."

***zochrāh-lî elohay letovah*** = remember me O my God for good.

The final words of Nehemiah — and therefore the final words of 2 Esdras — are one of the most compressed prayers in Scripture. The four-word Hebrew (*zochrāh-lî elohay letovah*) is Nehemiah's entire theology of prayer: the *zēkher* = remembering of God / directed toward the one who prays / for *tovah* = good. It is not a request for specific outcomes but for the divine memory to be active and favorable.

The prayer is remarkable in its simplicity after the complexity of the Nehemiah reform narrative. Having done everything — rebuilt the walls / reformed the priesthood / enforced the Sabbath / addressed the mixed marriages / restored the temple service — Nehemiah asks only: *remember me for good*.

The structure is identical to the dying thief on the cross: "Jesus — remember me when you come into your kingdom" (Luke 23:42 / *mnēsthēti mou Kyrie*). Both prayers are radical: they ask not for reward or vindication but for the divine *mnēmē* = memory to be kind.

**Luke 23:42** — "And he said: Jesus remember me when you come into your kingdom." The thief's prayer is the NT fulfillment of the Nehemiah prayer-form — the same minimal structure / the same appeal to divine memory / the same trust that the divine *mnēmē* is sufficient. The answer to Nehemiah's *remember me O my God for good* is Jesus's *today you will be with me in paradise* — the divine memory is not neutral; it is the presence of the one remembered.

**Hebrews 6:10** — "For God is not unjust so as to overlook your work and the love that you have shown for his name in serving the saints as you still do." The Hebrews guarantee that God does not *epilanthanomai* = forget the faithful work is the NT assurance of what Nehemiah requests in his minimal *remember me*: the divine memory is just / it does not overlook / the work done in his name is retained in the divine *mnēmē*.

## STRUCTURAL NOTE — 2 ESDRAS AS THE UNFINISHED RESTORATION

2 Esdras is structurally a book of three completions and one permanent incompletion:

**Completion 1 — The Temple rebuilt** (Ezra 6:15–16): The house of God is finished and dedicated. The *eleos endures forever* refrain accompanies the dedication as it accompanied Solomon's (2 Paralipomenon 7:3).

**Completion 2 — The community constituted by the Law** (Neh 8): The Law is read, explained, and received. The people weep and feast. The Feast of Booths is kept. The covenant is renewed.

**Completion 3 — The walls of Jerusalem rebuilt** (Neh 12:27–43): The city is restored. The dedication of the walls is celebrated with singing and sacrifices heard far away.

**The permanent incompletion** (Neh 9:36–37): We are still slaves. The Davidic king is not on the throne. The *doxa* has not returned. The *pneuma* has not been poured out. The land is Persian property. The Torah-community is real but fragile. The restoration is mercy — but the inheritance is not yet possessed.

**The canon's open question:** 2 Esdras ends with the *remember me O my God for good* of Nehemiah. The canon of the historical books ends where the Hebrew canon ends: with a person doing good work for God and asking only to be remembered. The answer to that request comes in the fullness of time — when the one who is the fulfillment of all that the Temple, the Law, the walls, and the Passover pointed toward, arrives and says to the thief beside him: *today you will be with me in paradise*.

The entire arc from Joshua (the first great entry into the land) through Nehemiah (the slave in the inheritance) is the story of a promise held in tension: the land given but not fully possessed / the covenant enacted but not yet fulfilled / the restoration begun but not completed. The NT opens where the historical books end — in a land under foreign domination, a people awaiting the promised king, and a Temple that lacks the glory of the first.

**Matthew 1:1** anchors itself to this arc: *the book of the genealogy of Jesus Christ the son of David the son of Abraham*. The genealogy that runs through the exile (Jeconiah the captive / Zerubbabel the governor) is the narrative thread that ties the Nehemiah *hinneh anachnu avadim* = look we are slaves to the Matthean *ho christos* who comes to release the captives.

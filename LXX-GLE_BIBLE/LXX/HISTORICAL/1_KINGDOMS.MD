\v 1 There was a certain man from Ramathaim-zophim of the hill country of Ephraim — whose name was Elkanah son of Jeroham son of Elihu son of Tohu son of Zuph — an Ephraimite.
\v 2 He had two wives — the name of the one was Hannah and the name of the other Peninnah — and Peninnah had children but Hannah had no children.
\v 3 The man used to go up year by year from his city to worship and to sacrifice to the Lord of hosts at Shiloh — where the two sons of Eli — Hophni and Phinehas — were priests of the Lord.
\v 4 On the day when Elkanah sacrificed he would give portions to Peninnah his wife and to all her sons and daughters —
\v 5 but to Hannah he gave a double portion — for he loved Hannah though the Lord had closed her womb.
\v 6 And her rival used to provoke her grievously to irritate her because the Lord had closed her womb.
\v 7 So it went on year by year — as often as she went up to the house of the Lord she used to provoke her — therefore she wept and would not eat.
\v 8 And Elkanah her husband said to her: Hannah — why do you weep? Why do you not eat? Why is your heart sad? Am I not more to you than ten sons?
\p
\v 9 After they had eaten and drunk in Shiloh Hannah rose — now Eli the priest was sitting on the seat beside the doorpost of the temple of the Lord —
\v 10 and she was deeply distressed and prayed to the Lord and wept bitterly.
\v 11 And she vowed a vow and said: O Lord of hosts — if you will indeed look on the affliction of your servant and remember me and not forget your servant but will give to your servant a son — then I will give him to the Lord all the days of his life and no razor shall touch his head.
\v 12 As she continued praying before the Lord Eli observed her mouth.
\v 13 Hannah was speaking in her heart — only her lips moved and her voice was not heard — therefore Eli took her to be drunk.
\v 14 And Eli said to her: How long will you go on being drunk? Put your wine away from you.
\v 15 But Hannah answered: No my lord — I am a woman troubled in breath-of-life — I have drunk neither wine nor strong drink but I have been pouring out my soul before the Lord.
\v 16 Do not regard your servant as a worthless woman — for all along I have been speaking out of my great anxiety and vexation.
\v 17 Then Eli answered: Go in peace and the God of Israel grant your petition that you have made to him.
\v 18 And she said: Let your servant find favor in your eyes. Then the woman went her way and ate and her face was no longer sad.
\p
\v 19 They rose early in the morning and worshipped before the Lord — then they returned and came to their house at Ramah — and Elkanah knew Hannah his wife and the Lord remembered her.
\v 20 And in due time Hannah conceived and bore a son — and she called his name Samuel — for she said: I have asked for him from the Lord.
\p
\v 21 The man Elkanah and all his house went up to offer to the Lord the yearly sacrifice and to pay his vow.
\v 22 But Hannah did not go up — for she said to her husband: As soon as the child is weaned I will bring him so that he may appear before the Lord and dwell there forever.
\v 23 Elkanah her husband said to her: Do what seems best to you — wait until you have weaned him — only may the Lord establish his word. So the woman remained and nursed her son until she weaned him.
\v 24 And when she had weaned him she took him up with her — along with a three-year-old bull and one ephah of flour and a skin of wine — and she brought him to the house of the Lord at Shiloh and the child was young.
\v 25 Then they slaughtered the bull and they brought the child to Eli.
\v 26 And she said: Oh my lord — as you live my lord — I am the woman who was standing here in your presence praying to the Lord.
\v 27 For this child I prayed and the Lord has given me my petition that I asked of him.
\v 28 Therefore I have lent him to the Lord — as long as he lives he is lent to the Lord. And he worshipped the Lord there.

\p
\v 12 Now the sons of Eli were worthless men — they did not know the Lord.
\v 13 The custom of the priests with the people was as follows: when any man offered sacrifice the priest's servant would come while the meat was boiling — with a three-pronged fork in his hand —
\v 14 and he would thrust it into the pan or kettle or cauldron or pot — all that the fork brought up the priest would take for himself. So they did at Shiloh to all the Israelites who came there.
\v 15 Moreover before the fat was burned the priest's servant would come and say to the man who was sacrificing: Give meat for the priest to roast — for he will not accept boiled meat from you but only raw.
\v 16 And if the man said to him: Let the fat be burned first and then take as much as you wish — he would say: No — you must give it now and if not I will take it by force.
\v 17 Thus the sin of the young men was very great in the sight of the Lord — for the men treated the offering of the Lord with contempt.

\v 19 And his mother used to make for him a little robe and bring it to him each year when she went up with her husband to offer the yearly sacrifice.
\v 20 Then Eli would bless Elkanah and his wife and say: May the Lord give you children by this woman for the petition she asked of the Lord. So then they would return to their home.
\v 21 Indeed the Lord visited Hannah and she conceived and bore three sons and two daughters — and the boy Samuel grew in the presence of the Lord.

\v 23 And he said to them: Why do you do such things? For I hear of your evil dealings from all these people.
\v 24 No my sons — it is no good report that I hear the people of the Lord spreading abroad.
\v 25 If someone sins against a man God will mediate for him — but if someone sins against the Lord who can intercede for him? But they would not listen to the voice of their father — for it was the will of the Lord to put them to death.

\v 29 Why then do you scorn my sacrifices and my offerings that I commanded — and honor your sons above me by fattening yourselves on the choicest parts of every offering of my people Israel?
\v 30 Therefore the Lord the God of Israel declares: I promised that your house and the house of your father should go in and out before me forever — but now the Lord declares: Far be it from me — for those who honor me I will honor and those who despise me shall be lightly esteemed.
\v 31 Look — the days are coming when I will cut off your strength and the strength of your father's house — so that there will not be an old man in your house.
\v 32 Then in distress you will look at all the prosperity that shall be bestowed on Israel — and there shall not be an old man in your house forever.
\v 33 The only one of you whom I shall not cut off from my altar shall be spared to weep his eyes out and grieve his heart — and all the descendants of your house shall die by the sword of men.

\v 35 And I will raise up for myself a faithful priest who shall do according to what is in my heart and in my mind — and I will build him a sure house and he shall go in and out before my anointed forever.
\v 36 And everyone who is left in your house shall come to implore him for a piece of silver or a loaf of bread and shall say: Please put me in one of the priests' places that I may eat a morsel of bread.

\v 1 Now the boy Samuel was ministering to the Lord in the presence of Eli — and the word of the Lord was rare in those days — there was no frequent vision.
\v 2 At that time Eli whose eyesight had begun to grow dim so that he could not see was lying down in his own place.
\v 3 The lamp of God had not yet gone out and Samuel was lying down in the temple of the Lord where the ark of God was.
\v 4 Then the Lord called Samuel and he said: Here I am —
\v 5 and ran to Eli and said: Here I am — for you called me. But he said: I did not call — lie down again. So he went and lay down.
\v 6 And the Lord called again: Samuel — and Samuel arose and went to Eli and said: Here I am — for you called me. But he said: I did not call my son — lie down again.
\v 7 Now Samuel did not yet know the Lord and the word of the Lord had not yet been revealed to him.
\v 8 And the Lord called Samuel again the third time — and he arose and went to Eli and said: Here I am — for you called me. Then Eli perceived that the Lord was calling the boy.
\v 9 Therefore Eli said to Samuel: Go lie down and if he calls you shall say: Speak Lord — for your servant hears. So Samuel went and lay down in his place.
\p
\v 10 And the Lord came and stood and called as at other times: Samuel — Samuel. And Samuel said: Speak — for your servant hears.
\v 11 Then the Lord said to Samuel: Look — I am about to do a thing in Israel at which the two ears of everyone who hears it will tingle.
\v 12 On that day I will fulfill against Eli all that I have spoken concerning his house — from beginning to end.
\v 13 And I declare to him that I am about to punish his house forever for the iniquity that he knew — because his sons were blaspheming God and he did not restrain them.
\v 14 Therefore I swear to the house of Eli that the iniquity of Eli's house shall not be atoned for by sacrifice or offering forever.
\p
\v 15 Samuel lay until morning — then he opened the doors of the house of the Lord — and Samuel was afraid to tell the vision to Eli.
\v 16 But Eli called Samuel and said: Samuel my son. And he said: Here I am.
\v 17 And Eli said: What was it that he told you? Do not hide it from me. May God do so to you and more also if you hide anything from me of all that he told you.
\v 18 So Samuel told him everything and hid nothing from him. And he said: It is the Lord — let him do what seems good to him.
\p
\v 19 And Samuel grew and the Lord was with him and let none of his words fall to the ground.
\v 20 And all Israel from Dan to Beersheba knew that Samuel was established as a prophet of the Lord.
\v 21 And the Lord appeared again at Shiloh — for the Lord revealed himself to Samuel at Shiloh by the word of the Lord.

\v 1 And the word of Samuel came to all Israel. Now Israel went out to battle against the Philistines and camped beside Ebenezer while the Philistines camped at Aphek.
\v 2 The Philistines drew up in line against Israel — and when the battle spread Israel was defeated before the Philistines who killed about four thousand men on the field of battle.
\v 3 And when the people came to the camp the elders of Israel said: Why has the Lord defeated us today before the Philistines? Let us bring the ark of the covenant of the Lord here from Shiloh that it may come among us and save us from the power of our enemies.
\v 4 So the people sent to Shiloh and brought from there the ark of the covenant of the Lord of hosts who is enthroned on the cherubim — and the two sons of Eli — Hophni and Phinehas — were there with the ark of the covenant of God.
\p
\v 5 As soon as the ark of the covenant of the Lord came into the camp all Israel gave a mighty shout so that the earth resounded.
\v 6 And when the Philistines heard the noise of the shouting they said: What does this great shouting in the camp of the Hebrews mean? And when they learned that the ark of the Lord had come to the camp —
\v 7 the Philistines were afraid — for they said: A god has come into the camp. And they said: Woe to us — for nothing like this has happened before.
\v 8 Woe to us — who can deliver us from the power of these mighty gods? These are the gods who struck the Egyptians with every sort of plague in the wilderness.
\v 9 Take courage and be men O Philistines — lest you become servants to the Hebrews as they have been to you — be men and fight.
\p
\v 10 So the Philistines fought and Israel was defeated and every man fled to his home — there was a very great slaughter — there fell of Israel thirty thousand foot soldiers.
\v 11 And the ark of God was captured and the two sons of Eli — Hophni and Phinehas — died.
\p
\v 12 A man of Benjamin ran from the battle line and came to Shiloh the same day with his clothes torn and with dust on his head.
\v 13 When he arrived Eli was sitting on his seat by the road watching — for his heart trembled for the ark of God. And when the man came into the city and told the news all the city cried out.
\v 14 When Eli heard the sound of the outcry he said: What is this uproar? Then the man hurried and came and told Eli.
\v 15 Now Eli was ninety-eight years old and his eyes were set so that he could not see.
\v 16 And the man said to Eli: I am he who has come from the battle — I fled from the battle today. And he said: How did it go my son?
\v 17 He who brought the news answered and said: Israel has fled before the Philistines and there has also been a great defeat among the people — your two sons also — Hophni and Phinehas — are dead and the ark of God has been captured.
\v 18 As soon as he mentioned the ark of God Eli fell over backward from his seat by the side of the gate — and his neck was broken and he died — for the man was old and heavy. He had judged Israel forty years.
\p
\v 19 Now his daughter-in-law the wife of Phinehas was pregnant and about to give birth — and when she heard the news that the ark of God was captured and that her father-in-law and her husband were dead she bowed and gave birth — for her pains came upon her.
\v 20 And about the time of her death the women attending her said to her: Do not be afraid — for you have borne a son. But she did not answer or pay attention.
\v 21 And she named the child Ichabod — saying: The glory has departed from Israel — because the ark of God had been captured and because of her father-in-law and her husband.
\v 22 And she said: The glory has departed from Israel — for the ark of God has been captured.

\v 7 And when the men of Ashdod saw how things were they said: The ark of the God of Israel must not remain with us — for his hand is hard against us and against Dagon our god.
\v 8 So they sent and gathered together all the lords of the Philistines and said: What shall we do with the ark of the God of Israel? They answered: Let the ark of the God of Israel be brought around to Gath. So they brought the ark of the God of Israel there.
\v 9 But after they had brought it around — the hand of the Lord was against the city causing a very great panic and he afflicted the men of the city — both young and old — so that tumors broke out on them.
\v 10 So they sent the ark of God to Ekron — but as soon as the ark of God came to Ekron the people of Ekron cried out: They have brought around to us the ark of the God of Israel to kill us and our people.
\v 11 They sent therefore and gathered together all the lords of the Philistines and said: Send away the ark of the God of Israel and let it return to its own place — that it may not kill us and our people. For there was a deathly panic throughout the whole city — the hand of God was very heavy there.
\v 12 The men who did not die were struck with tumors — and the cry of the city went up to heaven.

\v 1 The ark of the Lord was in the country of the Philistines seven months.
\v 2 And the Philistines called for the priests and the diviners and said: What shall we do with the ark of the Lord? Tell us with what we shall send it to its place.
\v 3 They said: If you send away the ark of the God of Israel do not send it empty but by all means return him a guilt offering — then you will be healed and it will be known to you why his hand does not turn away from you.
\v 4 And they said: What is the guilt offering that we shall return to him? They answered: Five golden tumors and five golden mice — according to the number of the lords of the Philistines — for the same plague was on all of you and on your lords.
\v 5 So you must make images of your tumors and images of your mice that ravage the land and give glory to the God of Israel — perhaps he will lighten his hand from off you and your gods and your land.
\v 6 Why should you harden your hearts as the Egyptians and Pharaoh hardened their hearts? After he had dealt severely with them did they not send the people away and they departed?
\v 7 Now then take and prepare a new cart and two milk cows on which there has never come a yoke — and yoke the cows to the cart but take their calves home away from them.
\v 8 And take the ark of the Lord and place it on the cart and put in a box at its side the figures of gold which you are returning to him as a guilt offering — then send it off and let it go its way.
\v 9 And watch — if it goes up on the way to its own land to Beth-shemesh then it is he who has done us this great harm — but if not then we shall know that it is not his hand that struck us — it happened to us by coincidence.
\p
\v 10 The men did so — they took two milk cows and yoked them to the cart and shut up their calves at home.
\v 11 And they put the ark of the Lord on the cart and the box with the golden mice and the images of their tumors.
\v 12 And the cows went straight in the direction of Beth-shemesh along one highway lowing as they went — they turned neither to the right nor to the left — and the lords of the Philistines went after them as far as the border of Beth-shemesh.
\v 13 Now the people of Beth-shemesh were reaping their wheat harvest in the valley — and when they lifted up their eyes and saw the ark they rejoiced to see it.
\v 14 The cart came into the field of Joshua of Beth-shemesh and stopped there — a great stone was there and they split up the wood of the cart and offered the cows as a burnt offering to the Lord.
\v 15 And the Levites took down the ark of the Lord and the box that was beside it in which were the golden figures and set them on the great stone — and the men of Beth-shemesh offered burnt offerings and sacrificed sacrifices on that day to the Lord.
\v 16 And when the five lords of the Philistines saw it they returned that day to Ekron.
\v 17 These are the golden tumors that the Philistines returned as a guilt offering to the Lord — one for Ashdod one for Gaza one for Ashkelon one for Gath one for Ekron —
\v 18 and the golden mice — according to the number of all the cities of the Philistines belonging to the five lords — both fortified cities and unwalled villages — even to the great stone beside which they set down the ark of the Lord in the field of Joshua of Beth-shemesh.
\p
\v 19 And he struck some of the men of Beth-shemesh because they looked upon the ark of the Lord — he struck seventy men of them and the people mourned because the Lord had struck the people with a great blow.
\v 20 Then the men of Beth-shemesh said: Who is able to stand before the Lord this holy God? And to whom shall he go up away from us?
\v 21 So they sent messengers to the inhabitants of Kiriath-jearim saying: The Philistines have returned the ark of the Lord — come down and take it up to you.

\v 16 And he went on a circuit year by year to Bethel and Gilgal and Mizpah — and he judged Israel in all these places.
\v 17 Then he would return to Ramah — for his home was there — and there also he judged Israel — and he built there an altar to the Lord.

\v 1 There was a man of Benjamin whose name was Kish son of Abiel son of Zeror son of Becorath son of Aphiah — a Benjaminite — a man of wealth.
\v 2 And he had a son whose name was Saul — a handsome young man — there was not a man among the sons of Israel more handsome than he — from his shoulders upward he was taller than any of the people.
\v 3 Now the donkeys of Kish Saul's father were lost — so Kish said to Saul his son: Take one of the young men with you and arise and go look for the donkeys.
\v 4 And he passed through the hill country of Ephraim and passed through the land of Shalishah but they did not find them — and they passed through the land of Shaalim but they were not there — then he passed through the land of Benjamin but did not find them.
\v 5 When they came to the land of Zuph Saul said to his servant who was with him: Come let us go back lest my father cease to care about the donkeys and become anxious about us.
\v 6 But he said to him: Look — there is a man of God in this city and he is a man who is held in honor — all that he says comes true — let us go there now — perhaps he can tell us the way we should go.
\v 7 Then Saul said to his servant: But if we go what can we bring the man? For the bread in our sacks is gone and there is no present to bring to the man of God. What do we have?
\v 8 The servant answered Saul again: Here I have with me a quarter shekel of silver — and I will give it to the man of God to tell us our way.
\v 9 — Formerly in Israel when a man went to inquire of God he said: Come let us go to the seer — for he who is now called a prophet was formerly called a seer. —
\v 10 And Saul said to his servant: Well said — come let us go. So they went to the city where the man of God was.
\p
\v 11 As they went up the hill to the city they met young women coming out to draw water and said to them: Is the seer here?

\v 1 The Lord said to Samuel: How long will you grieve over Saul since I have rejected him from being king over Israel? Fill your horn with oil and go — I will send you to Jesse the Bethlehemite — for I have provided for myself a king among his sons.
\v 2 And Samuel said: How can I go? If Saul hears it he will kill me. And the Lord said: Take a heifer with you and say: I have come to sacrifice to the Lord.
\v 3 And invite Jesse to the sacrifice and I will show you what you shall do — and you shall anoint for me him whom I declare to you.
\v 4 Samuel did what the Lord commanded and came to Bethlehem — the elders of the city came to meet him trembling and said: Do you come peaceably?
\v 5 And he said: Peaceably — I have come to sacrifice to the Lord — consecrate yourselves and come with me to the sacrifice. And he consecrated Jesse and his sons and invited them to the sacrifice.
\p
\v 6 When they came he looked on Eliab and thought: Surely the Lord's anointed is before him.
\v 7 But the Lord said to Samuel: Do not look on his appearance or on the height of his stature because I have rejected him — for the Lord sees not as man sees — man looks on the outward appearance but the Lord looks on the heart.
\v 8 Then Jesse called Abinadab and made him pass before Samuel — and he said: Neither has the Lord chosen this one.
\v 9 Then Jesse made Shammah pass by — and he said: Neither has the Lord chosen this one.
\v 10 And Jesse made seven of his sons pass before Samuel — and Samuel said to Jesse: The Lord has not chosen these.
\v 11 Then Samuel said to Jesse: Are all your sons here? And he said: There remains yet the youngest and look — he is tending the sheep. And Samuel said to Jesse: Send and get him — for we will not sit down till he comes here.
\v 12 And he sent and brought him in — now he was ruddy and had beautiful eyes and was handsome — and the Lord said: Arise and anoint him — for this is he.
\v 13 Then Samuel took the horn of oil and anointed him in the midst of his brothers — and the breath-of-life of the Lord rushed upon David from that day forward. And Samuel rose up and went to Ramah.
\p
\v 14 Now the breath-of-life of the Lord departed from Saul and an evil breath-of-life from the Lord tormented him.
\v 15 And Saul's servants said to him: Look — an evil breath-of-life from God is tormenting you.
\v 16 Let our lord now command your servants who are before you to seek out a man who is skillful in playing the lyre — and when the evil breath-of-life from God is upon you he will play it and you will be well.
\v 17 So Saul said to his servants: Provide for me a man who can play well and bring him to me.
\v 18 One of the young men answered: Look — I have seen a son of Jesse the Bethlehemite who is skillful in playing — a man of valor — a man of war — prudent in speech and a man of good presence — and the Lord is with him.
\v 19 Therefore Saul sent messengers to Jesse and said: Send me David your son who is with the sheep.
\v 20 And Jesse took a donkey loaded with bread and a skin of wine and a young goat and sent them by David his son to Saul.
\v 21 And David came to Saul and entered his service — and Saul loved him greatly and he became his armor-bearer.
\v 22 And Saul sent to Jesse saying: Let David remain in my service — for he has found favor in my sight.
\v 23 And whenever the evil breath-of-life from God was upon Saul David took the lyre and played it with his hand — so Saul was refreshed and was well and the evil breath-of-life departed from him.

\v 2 And Saul and the men of Israel were gathered and encamped in the Valley of Elah and drew up in line of battle against the Philistines.
\v 3 And the Philistines stood on the mountain on the one side and Israel stood on the mountain on the other side with a valley between them.
\v 4 And there came out from the camp of the Philistines a champion named Goliath of Gath whose height was six cubits and a span.
\v 5 He had a helmet of bronze on his head and he was armed with a coat of mail and the weight of the coat was five thousand shekels of bronze.
\v 6 And he had bronze armor on his legs and a javelin of bronze slung between his shoulders.
\v 7 The shaft of his spear was like a weaver's beam and his spear's head weighed six hundred shekels of iron — and his shield-bearer went before him.
\v 8 He stood and shouted to the ranks of Israel: Why have you come out to draw up for battle? Am I not a Philistine and are you not servants of Saul? Choose a man for yourselves and let him come down to me.
\v 9 If he is able to fight with me and kill me then we will be your servants — but if I prevail against him and kill him then you shall be our servants and serve us.
\v 10 And the Philistine said: I defy the ranks of Israel this day — give me a man that we may fight together.
\v 11 When Saul and all Israel heard these words of the Philistine they were dismayed and greatly afraid.
\p
\v 32 And David said to Saul: Let no man's heart fail because of him — your servant will go and fight with this Philistine.
\v 33 And Saul said to David: You are not able to go against this Philistine to fight with him — for you are but a youth and he has been a man of war from his youth.
\v 34 But David said to Saul: Your servant used to keep sheep for his father — and when there came a lion or a bear and took a lamb from the flock —
\v 35 I went after him and struck him and delivered it out of his mouth — and if he arose against me I caught him by his beard and struck him and killed him.
\v 36 Your servant has struck down both lions and bears and this uncircumcised Philistine shall be like one of them — for he has defied the armies of the living God.
\v 37 And David said: The Lord who delivered me from the paw of the lion and from the paw of the bear will deliver me from the hand of this Philistine. And Saul said to David: Go and the Lord be with you.
\p
\v 38 Then Saul clothed David with his armor — he put a helmet of bronze on his head and clothed him with a coat of mail.
\v 39 And David strapped his sword over his armor and he tried in vain to go — for he had not tested them — then David said to Saul: I cannot go with these — for I have not tested them. So David put them off.
\v 40 Then he took his staff in his hand and chose five smooth stones from the brook and put them in his shepherd's pouch — his sling was in his hand — and he approached the Philistine.
\p
\v 41 And the Philistine moved forward and came near to David with his shield-bearer in front of him.
\v 42 And when the Philistine looked and saw David he despised him — for he was but a youth — ruddy and handsome in appearance.
\v 43 And the Philistine said to David: Am I a dog that you come to me with sticks? And the Philistine cursed David by his gods.
\v 44 The Philistine said to David: Come to me and I will give your flesh to the birds of the air and to the beasts of the field.
\v 45 Then David said to the Philistine: You come to me with a sword and with a spear and with a javelin — but I come to you in the name of the Lord of hosts — the God of the armies of Israel whom you have defied.
\v 46 This day the Lord will deliver you into my hand and I will strike you down and cut off your head — and I will give the dead bodies of the host of the Philistines this day to the birds of the air and to the wild beasts of the earth — that all the earth may know that there is a God in Israel —
\v 47 and that all this assembly may know that the Lord saves not with sword and spear — for the battle is the Lord's and he will give you into our hand.
\p
\v 48 When the Philistine arose and came and drew near to meet David — David ran quickly toward the battle line to meet the Philistine.
\v 49 And David put his hand in his bag and took out a stone and slung it and struck the Philistine on his forehead — the stone sank into his forehead and he fell on his face to the ground.
\v 50 So David prevailed over the Philistine with a sling and with a stone and struck the Philistine and killed him — there was no sword in the hand of David.
\v 51 Then David ran and stood over the Philistine and took his sword and drew it out of its sheath and killed him and cut off his head with it. When the Philistines saw that their champion was dead they fled.
\v 52 And the men of Israel and Judah rose with a shout and pursued the Philistines as far as Gath and the gates of Ekron — so that the wounded Philistines fell on the way from Shaaraim as far as Gath and Ekron.
\v 53 And the sons of Israel came back from chasing the Philistines and they plundered their camp.
\v 54 And David took the head of the Philistine and brought it to Jerusalem — but he put his armor in his tent.
\p
\v 55 As soon as Saul saw David go out against the Philistine he said to Abner the commander of the army: Abner — whose son is this youth? And Abner said: As your soul lives O king I do not know.
\v 56 And the king said: Inquire whose son the boy is.
\v 57 And as David returned from the striking down of the Philistine Abner took him and brought him before Saul with the head of the Philistine in his hand.
\v 58 And Saul said to him: Whose son are you young man? And David answered: I am the son of your servant Jesse the Bethlehemite.

## CRITICAL TEXTUAL NOTE — LXX 1 KINGDOMS

The LXX title is *1 Basileiōn* = 1 Kingdoms — not "1 Samuel." The four-book division (1–4 Kingdoms) follows the LXX canonical structure. The Rahlfs-Hanhart LXX is the base.

**Chapter 17 — the David and Goliath shorter recension:** The LXX B text (Vaticanus / Rahlfs-Hanhart base) has a notably shorter version of chapter 17 — lacking the extended section (vv.12–31 in the MT) about Jesse's sons, the brother's challenge, Eliab's anger, and the backstory exchange. The GLE renders the LXX short recension: the narrative moves directly from v.11 (Israel's fear) to v.32 (David's offer to fight). This is one of the most significant LXX/MT divergences in the historical books. The LXX short text is generally considered the earlier tradition — the MT longer text appears to be an expansion that introduces tensions (David unknown to Saul in v.55 despite the earlier lyre-service in ch.16).

**LXX name variant:** The guilty man in Joshua 7 is *Achar* in the LXX — in 1 Kingdoms the name appears as *Achar* in some MSS. The GLE preserves LXX forms throughout.

## CAE NODES — 1 KINGDOMS

### 1 Kingdoms 1–2 — Hannah's prayer — CAE PRIMARY NODE

**2:1–10** — Hannah's prayer / song.

**2:1** — "My heart exults in the Lord — my strength is exalted in the Lord — my mouth opens wide over my enemies — because I rejoice in your salvation."

**2:6–8** — "The Lord kills and brings to life — he brings down to Sheol and raises up... He raises up the poor from the dust — he lifts the needy from the ash heap — to make them sit with princes."

**2:10** — "The Lord will judge the ends of the earth — he will give strength to his king — and exalt the power of his anointed."

***hupsosei keras christou autou*** = he will exalt the power of his anointed (*christou* = anointed / Christ). The final line of Hannah's prayer introduces the *christos* = anointed one in the context of divine vindication of the lowly and judgment of the proud. The prayer closes with the first appearance of *christos* as a royal title in the LXX historical books.

**Luke 1:46–55** — The Magnificat. Mary's song is a direct expansion of Hannah's prayer:
- *megalynei hē psychē mou ton Kyrion* = my soul magnifies the Lord (Luke 1:46) ↔ Hannah's exultation in the Lord (1 Sam 2:1)
- *hypselous katheilen apo thronōn* = he brought down the mighty from thrones (Luke 1:52) ↔ *kyrios tapeinoi kai hypsoī* = the Lord humbles and exalts (1 Sam 2:7)
- *peinōntas eneplēsen agathōn* = the hungry he filled with good things (Luke 1:53) ↔ *empeplēsmenos* = those who were full hire out for bread (1 Sam 2:5)

The Magnificat is not merely parallel — it is a conscious theological elaboration of Hannah's prayer. Both prayers are spoken by a woman who has received unexpected life from God (barren Hannah / virgin Mary) and both interpret their personal deliverance as the pattern of God's universal reversal of human status hierarchies.

**1 Corinthians 1:26–29** — "God chose what is foolish in the world to shame the wise... God chose what is low and despised in the world." Paul's theology of divine reversal in 1 Corinthians 1 is Hannah's theology systematized.

### 1 Kingdoms 2:35 — *hiereus pistos* / a faithful priest — CAE NODE

**2:35** — "And I will raise up for myself a faithful priest (*hiereus pistos*) who shall do according to what is in my heart and in my mind — and I will build him a sure house and he shall go in and out before my anointed forever."

***anastēsō emautō hiereon piston*** = I will raise up for myself a faithful priest. The promise that follows the condemnation of Eli's house: a faithful priest who acts from the heart of God and serves before the anointed one.

**Hebrews 2:17** — "he had to be made like his brothers in every respect so that he might become a merciful and faithful (*pistos*) high priest (*archiereus*) in the service of God." The Hebrews *archiereus pistos* = faithful high priest is the fulfillment of the 1 Samuel 2:35 promise. The faithful priest promised after Eli's failure = the merciful and faithful high priest of Hebrews.

**Hebrews 3:1–2** — "Consider Jesus the apostle and high priest of our confession who was faithful (*pistos*) to him who appointed him just as Moses also was faithful in all God's house." The *pistos* = faithful of Hebrews 3:2 echoes both Numbers 12:7 (Moses faithful in all his house) and 1 Samuel 2:35 (the promised faithful priest).

---

### 1 Kingdoms 3:1–10 — Samuel's call — CAE NODE

**3:1** — "The word of the Lord was rare in those days — there was no frequent vision."

**3:4, 6, 8, 10** — The fourfold call: "Samuel — Samuel."

**3:10** — "The Lord came and stood and called as at other times: Samuel — Samuel. And Samuel said: Speak — for your servant hears."

***lalei — hoti akouei ho doulos sou*** = Speak — for your servant hears. Samuel's response — the posture of the obedient servant before the speaking God. The contrast with Eli's sons (who do not listen) and Saul (who does not fully obey) runs through the entire book: the question is always whether Israel will hear and keep the word of the Lord.

**Isaiah 6:8** — "Whom shall I send and who will go for us? And I said: Here am I — send me." The prophetic-call response pattern: Samuel's *lalei* / Isaiah's *idou egō* / Jeremiah's *Kyrie Kyrie* = Lord Lord (Jer 1:6). The hearing-servant posture of Samuel becomes the prophetic vocation pattern.

**John 10:3–4** — "The sheep hear his voice and he calls his own sheep by name and leads them out... and the sheep follow him for they know his voice." The Samuel calling-by-name narrative is the OT ground for Jesus's sheep-who-know-the-voice theology.

---

### 1 Kingdoms 8:7 — *eme exoudenokan* / they have rejected me — CAE PRIMARY NODE

**8:7** — "The Lord said to Samuel: Obey the voice of the people in all that they say to you — for they have not rejected you but they have rejected me from being king over them."

***eme exoudenokan tou mē basileuein ep' autous*** = they have set me aside from reigning over them. The rejection of the divine kingship by demanding a human king.

This is one of the most theologically weighty declarations in the OT. The entire subsequent history of the monarchy is read under this heading: every human king is a substitute for — and implicit rejection of — the divine king. The Jotham-parable theology (Judges 9) is now stated explicitly by God himself.

**1 Samuel 12:12** — "When you saw that Nahash the king of the Ammonites came against you you said: No — but a king shall reign over us — when the Lord your God was your king." Samuel's farewell speech makes the same point: the Lord was already king and was replaced.

**John 19:15** — "We have no king but Caesar." The chief priests' final rejection of Jesus before Pilate is the ultimate repetition of 1 Samuel 8:7 — the rejection of the divine king in favor of a human ruler. The trajectory runs: 1 Sam 8:7 (rejection of divine kingship for Saul) → John 19:15 (rejection of the true anointed king for Caesar).

**Acts 13:21–22** — Paul's summary: "then they asked for a king and God gave them Saul... and when he had removed him he raised up David." The kingship trajectory of 1 Samuel is read as the necessary precondition for the Davidic covenant.

---

### 1 Kingdoms 13:14 — *anthrōpon kata tēn kardian autou* / a man after his own heart — CAE PRIMARY NODE

**13:14** — "The Lord has sought out a man after his own heart (*anthrōpon kata tēn kardian autou*) and the Lord has commanded him to be prince over his people — because you have not kept what the Lord commanded you."

The word spoken over Saul's rejection: the Lord is not without a king — he has already found the one who fits the heart of God. The *kata tēn kardian* = according to the heart standard is the criterion the external appearance-standard (1 Sam 16:7) cannot provide.

**Acts 13:22** — "He raised up David to be their king — of whom he testified and said: I have found in David the son of Jesse a man after my heart (*anthrōpon kata tēn kardian mou*) who will do all my will." Paul's citation is the only explicit NT quotation of this phrase — he fuses 1 Samuel 13:14 with Psalm 89:20 (LXX) to establish David as the template of the Messianic king.

**Romans 1:3–4** — "his Son who was descended from David according to the flesh — and was declared to be the Son of God in power." The Davidic descent is the flesh-lineage of the one who truly is *kata kardian Theou* = after the heart of God.

---

### 1 Kingdoms 15:22–23 — *hypakoē hyper thusian* / obedience better than sacrifice — CAE PRIMARY NODE

**15:22–23** —
*hoti agathon to akousai hyper thusian*
*kai epakousai hyper stear kriōn*
*hoti hamartia manteias estin apeithein*
*kai ponos kai thrēnos eidōlōn antikeisthai*

= to obey is good above sacrifice / and to listen above the fat of rams / for sin of divination is to be disobedient / and pain and wailing of idols to oppose.

Samuel's rebuke of Saul: partial obedience motivated by fear of people is the functional equivalent of divination — seeking one's own oracle instead of God's word. The fat offerings Saul preserved cannot atone for the disobedience that kept them alive.

**Psalm 51:16–17** — "For you will not delight in sacrifice or I would give it — you will not be pleased with a burnt offering. The sacrifices of God are a broken breath-of-life — a broken and contrite heart O God you will not despise." David's Psalm 51 (after Nathan's rebuke) internalizes the 1 Samuel 15 principle: no sacrifice substitutes for the broken and listening heart.

**Hosea 6:6** — "For I desire steadfast love and not sacrifice — the knowledge of God rather than burnt offerings." Hosea's programmatic statement is the prophetic development of 1 Samuel 15:22.

**Matthew 9:13 / 12:7** — "Go and learn what this means: I desire mercy and not sacrifice (*eleos thelō kai ou thusian*)." Jesus quotes Hosea 6:6 twice — the full chain is 1 Sam 15:22 → Psalm 51:16 → Hosea 6:6 → Matthew 9:13 / 12:7. The obedience-over-sacrifice principle runs as a continuous transmission chain from Samuel to Christ.

**Hebrews 10:5–8** — "Sacrifice and offering you have not desired but a body have you prepared for me... I have come to do your will O God." The Incarnation itself is the final expression of the *akouein hyper thusian* = obedience above sacrifice principle: the one sacrifice that counts is the one that is wholly obedient.

---

### 1 Kingdoms 16:7 — *ho Kyrios opsetai eis kardian* / the Lord looks on the heart — CAE NODE

**16:7** — "Do not look on his appearance or on the height of his stature because I have rejected him — for the Lord sees not as man sees — man looks on the outward appearance but the Lord looks on the heart."

***ho de Kyrios opsetai eis kardian*** = but the Lord will look into the heart. The divine vision-standard that inverts human selection criteria. The anointing of David (the youngest, the unexpected) is structured to demonstrate the principle before stating it.

**John 7:24** — "Do not judge by appearances but judge with right judgment." Jesus's appeal to true judgment echoes the 1 Samuel 16:7 principle: the external/visible criterion is insufficient.

**2 Corinthians 5:16** — "From now on therefore we regard no one according to the flesh (*kata sarka*)." Paul's *kata sarka* versus *kata pneuma* distinction is grounded in the same divine-vision principle: the Lord's perspective sees past the external to the heart.

---

### 1 Kingdoms 16:13 — *pneuma Kyriou epēlato epi Dauid* — CAE PRIMARY NODE

**16:13** — "Then Samuel took the horn of oil and anointed him in the midst of his brothers — and the breath-of-life of the Lord rushed upon David from that day forward."

***kai epēlato pneuma Kyriou epi Dauid apo tēs hēmeras ekeinēs kai epanō***

= and the breath-of-life of the Lord rushed upon David from that day and onward. The *pneuma* rush that was episodic in Judges (Othniel / Gideon / Jephthah / Samson) now becomes **permanent and continuous** — *apo tēs hēmeras ekeinēs kai epanō* = from that day and onward. The anointing of David marks the transition from episodic to permanent *pneuma* endowment.

**Isaiah 11:2** — "The breath-of-life of the Lord shall rest upon him — the breath-of-life of wisdom and understanding." Isaiah's messianic pneuma-resting is the prophetic development of the David anointing-pattern: the permanent pneuma that came upon David at anointing becomes the defining feature of the Davidic messianic expectation.

**Luke 3:22** — "The Holy Breath-of-Life descended on him in bodily form like a dove — and a voice came from heaven: You are my beloved Son — with you I am well pleased." The Jordan baptism is the messianic anointing event — pneuma descends upon the Davidic son permanently (John 1:33: "he on whom you see the Breath-of-Life descend and remain — this is he"). The *menō* = remain of John 1:33 is the NT fulfillment of 1 Sam 16:13 *apo tēs hēmeras ekeinēs kai epanō* = from that day and onward.

### 1 Kingdoms 17:45–47 — David and Goliath / the battle is the Lord's — CAE NODE

**17:45–47** — "You come to me with a sword and with a spear and with a javelin — but I come to you in the name of the Lord of hosts — the God of the armies of Israel whom you have defied... that all the earth may know that there is a God in Israel — and that all this assembly may know that the Lord saves not with sword and spear — for the battle is the Lord's and he will give you into our hand."

***hoti tou Kyriou ho polemos*** = for the battle is the Lord's. The war-theology of Joshua 10:14 (*Kyrios synepolemei*) restated by David as the explicit principle governing his own action. David fights as the Lord's instrument — the stone and sling are the Lord's means, not David's.

**1 Corinthians 1:27–28** — "God chose what is weak in the world to shame the strong." The David-and-Goliath pattern is Paul's primary narrative illustration for the divine preference for weakness as the vehicle of power. The stone that defeats the armored giant is the logic of the cross: the apparently weak instrument of divine choice defeats the apparently strong instrument of human confidence.

### 1 Kingdoms 18:1–4 / 20:14–17 — Jonathan's covenant with David — CAE NODE

**18:1** — "The soul of Jonathan was knit to the soul of David and Jonathan loved him as his own soul."

**18:3** — "Then Jonathan made a covenant with David because he loved him as his own soul."

**20:14–15** — "If I am still alive show me the steadfast love (*eleos*) of the Lord that I may not die — and do not cut off your steadfast love from my house forever — when the Lord cuts off every one of the enemies of David."

***hē psychē Iōnathan enedēthē en tē psychē Dauid*** = the soul of Jonathan was bound in the soul of David. The covenant-friendship of Jonathan and David is the OT's most concentrated depiction of covenant *eleos* operating between individuals. Jonathan's request (20:14–15) explicitly invokes *eleos Kyriou* = the steadfast love of the Lord as the framework for the personal covenant.

**2 Samuel 9** — David's fulfillment of the Jonathan-covenant in his care for Mephibosheth: "I will show you kindness for the sake of your father Jonathan." The *eleos* David shows Mephibosheth is the fulfillment of the Jonathan-covenant — and the pattern of how God's own *eleos* operates: the covenant made with the father is kept to the child.

**John 15:13** — "Greater love (*agapē*) has no one than this — that someone lay down his life for his friends." Jesus's declaration of the highest love uses the Jonathan-David register: Jonathan literally risked his father's wrath and his own position for David's life.

---

### 1 Kingdoms 24:6 / 26:9–11 — *christos Kyriou* / the Lord's anointed — CAE PRIMARY NODE

**24:6** — "The Lord forbid that I should do this thing to my lord — the Lord's anointed (*christos Kyriou*) — to put out my hand against him — seeing he is the Lord's anointed."

**26:9** — "Do not destroy him — for who can put out his hand against the Lord's anointed (*christon Kyriou*) and be guiltless?"

***christos Kyriou*** = the Lord's anointed / Christ of the Lord. David's twice-repeated refusal to harm Saul introduces the *christos Kyriou* theology: the anointed one is under divine protection not because of his moral worthiness (Saul has been rejected) but because the anointing itself marks him as belonging to the Lord.

The term *christos* here is not yet the eschatological title it becomes — but the theology is established: the *christos* = anointed one is the Lord's own — to harm him is to harm the Lord's property. This is the ground on which the NT *christos* title builds: Jesus as the Lord's anointed is untouchable not by human protection but by divine ownership.

**Psalm 2:2** — "The kings of the earth set themselves and the rulers take counsel together against the Lord and against his anointed (*christou autou*)." The Psalm 2 nations-against-the-christos pattern is the eschatological extension of the 1 Samuel 24/26 David-Saul dynamic.

**Acts 4:26–27** — "The kings of the earth set themselves... against the Lord and against his anointed (*christou autou*) — for truly in this city there were gathered against your holy servant Jesus..." The Acts prayer quotes Psalm 2 and interprets the Passion as the nations' assault on the *christos Kyriou*.

---

### 1 Kingdoms 28 — the witch of En-dor — CAE NODE

**28:15–19** — Samuel's appearance and his declaration: "The Lord has torn the kingdom out of your hand and given it to your neighbor — to David... tomorrow you and your sons shall be with me."

The narrative is unique in the OT: the dead prophet speaks through a medium and his words are confirmed as true — Saul and his sons die the next day as Samuel declared. The theological tension (divination forbidden / Samuel appears through a medium / the word proves true) is not resolved by the text but presented as the final divine word to Saul: even from death Samuel speaks judgment.

**Luke 16:31** — "If they do not hear Moses and the Prophets neither will they be convinced if someone should rise from the dead." Jesus's reversal: in En-dor the dead man's word is true and unheeded. In the resurrection the risen one is disbelieved. The En-dor narrative is the dark mirror of the empty-tomb accounts.

## VOCABULARY DECISIONS — 1 KINGDOMS

### *Pneuma* — breath-of-life (1:15; 3:10 context; 10:6; 11:6; 16:13–14; 16:23; 18:10; 19:9; 19:20)
Vocabulary Lock Table enforced throughout. The pneuma occurrences in 1 Samuel are among the most theologically varied in the OT:
- Hannah's *troubled in breath-of-life* (1:15) — the vital register: distress of the whole inner life
- Saul's evil *pneuma from God* (16:14–23; 18:10; 19:9) — the *pneuma* as the Lord's agent even in negative modality
- The *pneuma* rushing upon Saul at Gibeah (10:10) and in the field (11:6) — episodic judge-type
- The *pneuma* rushing upon David at anointing (16:13) — permanent / *apo tēs hēmeras ekeinēs kai epanō*

The contrast between Saul's *pneuma* (episodic / departing / replaced by evil *pneuma*) and David's *pneuma* (permanent / enduring from anointing) is one of the book's structural theological contrasts.

### *Christos Kyriou* — Lord's anointed
Rendered "Lord's anointed" — the full *christos* register preserved. Never "the king" alone — the anointing-title is the operative term. First theological deployment of *christos* as the one who belongs to the Lord and may not be touched.

### *Eleos* — steadfast love
Vocabulary Lock Table enforced. The Jonathan-David covenant *eleos* (18:3; 20:14–15) is the primary interpersonal deployment of *eleos* in the historical books — the covenant-faithfulness term operating between individuals, grounded in the Lord's own *eleos*.

### *Akouein* — to hear / obey
The Samuel narrative turns on *akouein*: Samuel's response (*lalei hoti akouei*) / Saul's failure to *akouein* the Lord's command / the contrast between listening and sacrifice (15:22). Rendered "hear" and "obey" according to context — the LXX *akouein* carries both registers.

## STRUCTURAL NOTE — 1 KINGDOMS AS THEOLOGY OF THE ANOINTED

The theological spine of 1 Kingdoms is the *christos* — the anointed one:

**Hannah's prayer opens** with the *christos* as the coming royal figure (2:10).
**Samuel is the kingmaker** — he anoints both Saul and David.
**Saul's failure** is the failure of the anointed who does not *akouein* — obedience above sacrifice.
**David's anointing** inaugurates the permanent *pneuma* and establishes the *kata kardian Theou* = after the heart of God standard.
**David's double refusal** to harm Saul establishes the inviolability of the *christos Kyriou*.

The book ends with Saul's death — the end of the anointing that went wrong. The transition to 2 Kingdoms (2 Samuel) is the transition to the Davidic covenant — the *christos* who will receive the eternal throne promise.

**Acts 13:16–41** — Paul's Pisidian Antioch sermon is the most sustained NT theological reading of 1 Samuel: Samuel / Saul / David / the promise to David / fulfilled in Jesus. The sermon treats 1 Kingdoms as the essential prologue to the Messianic announcement.

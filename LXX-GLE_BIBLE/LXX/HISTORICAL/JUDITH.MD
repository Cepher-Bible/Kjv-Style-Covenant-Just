\v 1 In the twelfth year of the reign of Nebuchadnezzar who ruled over the Assyrians in the great city of Nineveh — in the days of Arphaxad who ruled over the Medes in Ecbatana —
\v 2 and built around Ecbatana walls of hewn stone three cubits thick and six cubits long — making the walls seventy cubits high and fifty cubits wide —
\v 3 and set its towers at the gates one hundred cubits high and sixty cubits wide at the foundation —
\v 4 and made its gates of seventy cubits height and forty cubits width to allow the marching through of his armed forces and the parading of his foot soldiers —
\v 5 it was in those days that King Nebuchadnezzar made war against King Arphaxad in the great plain which is on the borders of Ragau.
\p
\v 11 And Nebuchadnezzar was greatly enraged at all this country and swore by his throne and kingdom that he would take revenge on the whole territory of Cilicia and Damascus and Syria and would kill with his sword all the inhabitants of the land of Moab and the people of Ammon and all Judea and everyone in Egypt as far as the coasts of the two seas.
\v 12 He marched against King Arphaxad with his army in the seventeenth year and was victorious in his campaign — and turned the whole army of Arphaxad and all his cavalry and all his chariots to flight and took possession of his towns and came to Ecbatana and captured its towers and plundered its streets and turned its beauty into shame.
\v 13 He captured Arphaxad in the mountains of Ragau and struck him down with his spears and destroyed him utterly that day.

\c 2
\p
\v 1 In the eighteenth year — on the twenty-second day of the first month — there was talk in the palace of Nebuchadnezzar king of the Assyrians about carrying out his revenge on the whole region just as he had said.
\v 2 He summoned all his ministers and all his nobles and set before them his secret plan and recounted fully with his own lips all the wickedness of the region.
\v 4 He said to them: Go and take as your troops all those who trust in their own strength — foot soldiers a hundred and twenty thousand and twelve thousand cavalry —
\v 5 and go against all the land to the west because they disobeyed my orders.
\p
\v 7 You shall order them to prepare earth and water — for I am coming against them in my wrath and will cover the whole face of the earth with the feet of my forces and will hand them over to be plundered by my troops —
\v 8 and their slain shall fill their wadis and gullies and the river will be filled with their dead and overflow —
\v 9 and I will lead them away captive to the ends of the whole earth.
\p
\v 19 Then Holofernes went out from the presence of his lord and called all the commanders and generals and officers of the Assyrian army —
\v 20 and mustered chosen troops as his lord had ordered him — a hundred and twenty thousand foot soldiers and twelve thousand mounted archers —
\v 21 and organized them as a great army is marshalled for a campaign.

\c 3
\p
\v 8 He demolished all their shrines and cut down their sacred groves — for he had been commissioned to destroy all the gods of the land so that all nations should worship Nebuchadnezzar alone and all their tongues and tribes should call upon him as god.

\c 4
\p
\v 1 When the Israelites living in Judea heard of everything that Holofernes the general of Nebuchadnezzar the king of the Assyrians had done to the nations and how he had plundered and destroyed all their temples —
\v 2 they were therefore greatly terrified before him and were alarmed both for Jerusalem and for the temple of the Lord their God.
\v 3 For they had only recently returned from exile and all the people of Judea had just now been gathered together and the vessels and the altar and the temple had been consecrated after their profanation.
\p
\v 9 And every man of Israel cried out to God with great fervor and they humbled themselves with much fasting.
\v 10 They and their wives and their children and their cattle and every resident alien and hired laborer and purchased slave — they all put sackcloth around their waists.
\v 11 And all the Israelite men women and children living at Jerusalem prostrated themselves before the temple and put ashes on their heads and spread out their sackcloth before the Lord.
\v 12 They even draped the altar with sackcloth and cried out in unison praying fervently to the God of Israel not to allow their infants to be carried off and their wives to be taken as booty and the towns they had inherited to be destroyed and the sanctuary to be profaned and desecrated to the malicious joy of the Gentiles.
\p
\v 13 The Lord heard their prayers and looked with favor on their affliction — for the people fasted many days throughout Judea and in Jerusalem before the sanctuary of the Lord Almighty.
\v 14 The high priest Joakim and all the priests who stood before the Lord and ministered to the Lord with their heads wrapped in sackcloth offered the daily burnt offering the votive offerings and the freewill offerings of the people.
\v 15 With ashes on their turbans they cried out to the Lord with all their might to look with favor on the whole house of Israel.

\c 5
\p
\v 1 It was reported to Holofernes the general of the Assyrian army that the people of Israel had prepared for war and had closed the mountain passes and fortified all the high hilltops and set up barricades in the plains.
\p
\v 3 Then he said to them: Tell me — you Canaanites — who are these people that live in the hill country? Why have they alone of all who live in the west refused to come out to meet me?
\p
\v 5 Then Achior the commander of all the Ammonites said to him: My lord let your servant speak a word in your presence and I will tell you the truth about this people who live in the hill country near you — and no falsehood shall come from your servant's mouth.
\v 6 These people are descended from the Chaldeans.
\v 7 At one time they lived in Mesopotamia because they were not willing to follow the gods of their ancestors who were in Chaldea.
\v 8 Since they had abandoned the ways of their ancestors and worshipped the God of heaven — the God they had come to know — their ancestors drove them out from the presence of their gods. So they fled to Mesopotamia and lived there for a long time.
\v 9 Then their God commanded them to leave the place where they were living and to go to the land of Canaan — and there they settled and grew very prosperous with gold and silver and very much livestock.
\v 10 When a famine spread over all the land of Canaan they went down to Egypt and lived there as long as they had food — and there they became so great a multitude that their race could not be counted.
\v 11 The king of Egypt rose up against them and exploited them with forced labor making bricks — he humiliated them and made them into slaves.
\v 12 Then they cried out to their God and he struck the whole land of Egypt with incurable plagues — and the Egyptians drove them out of their sight.
\v 13 Then God dried up the Red Sea before them —
\v 14 and he led them by the way of Sinai and Kadesh-barnea and drove out all the peoples of the desert —
\v 15 and they settled in the land of the Amorites and by their might they destroyed all the Heshbonites — and crossing over the Jordan they took possession of all the hill country.
\v 16 And they drove out before them the Canaanites the Perizzites the Jebusites the Shechemites and all the Gergesites and lived in that country for a long time.
\p
\v 17 As long as they did not sin against their God they prospered — for the God who hates iniquity is with them.
\v 18 But when they departed from the way he had prescribed for them they were utterly defeated in many battles and were led away captive to a foreign land — the temple of their God was razed to the ground and their towns were occupied by their enemies.
\v 19 But now they have returned to their God and have come back from the places where they were scattered — and have taken Jerusalem again where their sanctuary is and have settled in the hill country because it was unoccupied.
\p
\v 20 So now my master and lord — if there is any oversight in this people and they sin against their God and we find out that they have committed this offense — then we can go up and defeat them.
\v 21 But if they are not a guilty nation then let my lord pass them by — for their Lord and God will defend them and we shall be ridiculed before the whole world.

\c 6
\p
\v 2 And Holofernes said to him: Who are you Achior and you hired soldiers of Ephraim to prophesy among us as you have done today and tell us not to make war against the people of Israel because their God will defend them? What god is there except Nebuchadnezzar? He will send his forces and will destroy them from the face of the earth — and their God will not save them.
\v 3 But we his servants will destroy them as one man — for they cannot withstand the might of our cavalry.
\p
\v 14 When Achior came to Bethulia the people of the town gathered around him — and also Uzziah son of Micah of the tribe of Simeon and Chabris son of Gothoniel and Charmis son of Melchiel the elders of the town.
\v 15 They called together all the elders of the town and all their young men and their women ran together to the assembly. They set Achior in the midst of all their people and Uzziah questioned him about what had happened.
\v 19 Then the people fell down and worshipped God and cried out:
\v 20 O Lord God of heaven look on their arrogance and have pity on our people in their humiliation and look kindly today on the faces of those who are consecrated to you.

\c 7
\p
\v 1 The next day Holofernes ordered his whole army and all the allies who had joined him to break camp and move against Bethulia — to seize the passes into the hill country and make war on the Israelites.
\p
\v 4 When the Israelites saw their vast numbers they were greatly terrified and said to one another: These people will now lick up the face of the whole land — neither the high mountains nor the valleys nor the hills will bear their weight.
\p
\v 19 The Israelites then cried out to the Lord their God for they were discouraged because all their enemies had surrounded them and there was no way of escape.
\v 20 The whole Assyrian army — infantry chariots and cavalry — surrounded them for thirty-four days until all the reservoirs of water belonging to every inhabitant of Bethulia were exhausted.
\p
\v 23 Then all the people — young men women and children — gathered around Uzziah and the rulers of the town and cried out with a loud voice and said before all the elders:
\v 24 Let God judge between you and us because you have done us a great injury in not making peace with the Assyrians.
\v 25 For we have no one to help us — God has sold us into their hands so that we will wither before them with thirst and great destruction.
\v 26 Now summon them and surrender the whole town as booty to the army of Holofernes and to all his forces.
\v 27 For it is better for us to be captured by them — we shall indeed become slaves but our lives will be spared and we shall not witness the death of our infants before our eyes or see our wives and children draw their last breath.
\p
\v 30 Uzziah said to them: Courage my brothers — let us hold out for five more days — by that time the Lord our God will turn his mercy toward us — for he will not forsake us utterly.
\v 31 But if these days pass by and no help comes for us I will do as you say.

\c 8
\p
\v 1 Now in those days Judith heard about this — she was the daughter of Merari son of Ox son of Joseph son of Oziel son of Elkiah son of Ananias son of Gideon son of Raphaim son of Ahitub son of Elijah son of Hilkiah son of Eliab son of Nathanael son of Salamiel son of Sarasadai son of Israel.
\v 2 Her husband Manasseh who belonged to her tribe and family had died during the barley harvest.
\v 3 For as he stood overseeing those who were binding sheaves in the field the heat beat down on his head and he took to his bed and died in his town Bethulia. And they buried him with his ancestors in the field between Dothan and Balamon.
\v 4 Judith had been a widow in her home for three years and four months —
\v 5 and she set up a tent for herself on the roof of her house and put on sackcloth around her waist and wore the garments of her widowhood.
\v 6 She fasted all the days of her widowhood except for the day before the Sabbath and the Sabbath itself and the day before the new moon and the day of the new moon and the feasts and days of rejoicing of the house of Israel.
\v 7 She was beautiful in appearance and was very lovely to behold. Her husband Manasseh had left her gold and silver and male and female servants and livestock and fields — and she maintained this estate.
\v 8 No one spoke ill of her — for she feared God with great devotion.
\p
\v 9 When Judith heard the harsh words spoken by the people against the ruler because they were faint for lack of water she also heard all the words that Uzziah had spoken to them — that he had sworn to them to surrender the town to the Assyrians after five days.
\v 10 So she sent her maid who was in charge of all she possessed to summon Uzziah and Chabris and Charmis the elders of her town.
\v 11 They came to her and she said to them: Listen to me — rulers of the people of Bethulia. For what you said to the people today is not right. You have even sworn and pronounced this oath between God and you promising to surrender the town to our enemies unless the Lord turns and helps us within so many days.
\v 12 Who are you to put God to the test today and to set yourselves up in the place of God in human affairs?
\v 13 You are putting the Lord Almighty to the test — but you will never know anything.
\v 14 You cannot plumb the depths of the human heart (*kardia*) or understand the workings of the human mind — how then can you search out God who made all these things and find out his mind or comprehend his thought? No my brothers — do not provoke the Lord our God to anger.
\v 15 For if he does not choose to help us within these five days he has power to protect us within any time he pleases — or even to destroy us in the presence of our enemies.
\v 16 Do not try to bind the purposes of the Lord our God — for God is not like a human being to be threatened or like a mere human to be won over by pleading.
\v 17 Therefore while we wait for his deliverance let us call upon him to help us and he will hear our voice if it pleases him.
\p
\v 18 For never in our generation and never in living memory have any of our tribes or kindred or town or district ever worshipped gods made with hands — as was done in former days.
\v 19 It was for that reason that our ancestors were handed over to the sword and to plunder and so they suffered a great catastrophe before our enemies.
\v 20 But we have not acknowledged any other god but him — and so we hope that he will not despise us or any of our people.
\v 21 For if we are captured all Judea will be captured and our sanctuary will be plundered and he will make us pay for its desecration with our blood.
\v 22 And the slaughter of our kindred and the exile of the land and the desolation of our inheritance — all this he will bring on our heads among the nations wherever we serve as slaves — and we shall be an offense and a disgrace in the eyes of those who acquire us.
\v 23 For our slavery will not bring us into favor — the Lord our God will turn it to dishonor.
\p
\v 24 Therefore my brothers let us set an example for our kindred — for their lives depend on us and the sanctuary and the temple and the altar rest on us.
\v 25 In spite of everything let us give thanks to the Lord our God who is putting us to the test as he did our ancestors.
\v 26 Remember what he did with Abraham and how he tested Isaac and what happened to Jacob in Syrian Mesopotamia while he was tending the sheep of Laban his mother's brother.
\v 27 For he has not tried us with fire as he did them to examine their hearts (*kardias*) — nor has he taken vengeance on us but the Lord scourges those who are close to him in order to admonish them.
\p
\v 28 Then Uzziah said to her: All that you have said was spoken from a good heart (*kardia*) and there is no one who can gainsay your words.
\v 29 For your wisdom has been evident throughout all your life and your heart's good disposition has been made known to all the people.
\v 30 But the people were very thirsty and compelled us to do for them what we promised and made us take an oath that we cannot break.
\v 31 So pray for us since you are a devout woman and the Lord will send us rain to fill our cisterns and we will no longer be faint.
\p
\v 32 Judith said to them: Listen to me — I am about to do something that will go down through all generations of our descendants.
\v 33 Stand at the town gate tonight and I will go out with my maid. And within the days after which you have promised to surrender the town to our enemies the Lord will deliver Israel by my hand.
\v 34 Only do not try to find out what I am doing — for I will not tell you until I have finished what I am about to do.
\p
\v 35 Uzziah and the rulers said to her: Go in peace and may the Lord God go before you to take vengeance on our enemies.
\v 36 So they returned from the tent and went to their posts.

\p
\v 1 Then Judith prostrated herself with ashes on her head and with the sackcloth she was wearing bared — and at the very time when the evening incense was being offered in the house of God in Jerusalem — Judith cried out to the Lord with a loud voice and said:
\p
\v 2 O Lord God of my ancestor Simeon — to whom you gave a sword to take revenge on the strangers who had loosened a virgin's girdle to defile her and uncovered her thigh to her shame and polluted her womb to her disgrace — for you said: This shall not be done — yet they did it.
\v 3 So you gave up their rulers to be killed and their bed which was ashamed of their deceit to be stained with blood and you struck down the slaves along with the princes and the princes along with their servants.
\v 4 You gave their wives as plunder and their daughters to captivity and all their spoils to be divided among your beloved sons who burned with zeal for you and abhorred the pollution of their blood and called on you for help — O God — my God — hear me also — me who am a widow.
\p
\v 5 For you have done these things and those that went before and those that followed — you have designed the things that are now and those that are to come. What you had in mind came to pass —
\v 6 and the things you decided presented themselves and said: Here we are. For all your ways are prepared in advance and your judgment is with foreknowledge.
\p
\v 7 Look now at the Assyrians — a greatly increased force boasting in their horses and riders glorying in the might of their foot soldiers and trusting in shield and spear and bow and sling — and they do not know that you are the Lord who crushes wars — Lord is your name.
\v 8 Shatter their strength by your power and bring down their force in your anger — for they intend to defile your sanctuary and to pollute the dwelling place of your holy name and to use the sword to cut off the horns of your altar.
\v 9 Look at their pride — send your wrath upon their heads — give to me a widow the strength to do what I plan.
\v 10 By the deceit of my lips strike down the slave with the prince and the prince with his servant — crush their arrogance by the hand of a woman.
\p
\v 11 For your strength does not lie in numbers nor your might in the powerful — but you are the God of the humble the helper of the oppressed (*tapeinōn*) the upholder of the weak the protector of the forsaken the savior of those without hope.
\v 12 Please please God of my father and God of the inheritance of Israel — Lord of heaven and earth creator of the waters ruler of all your creation — hear my prayer.
\v 13 Make my deceitful words to be their wound and stripe — for they have planned cruel things against your covenant and your consecrated house and against the summit of Zion and against the house your children possess.
\v 14 Let your whole nation and every tribe know and understand that you are God — the God of all power and might — and that there is no other who protects the people of Israel but you alone.

\c 10
\p
\v 1 When Judith had stopped crying out to the God of Israel and had ended all these words —
\v 2 she rose from where she lay prostrate and called her maid and went down into the house — and she removed the sackcloth she had been wearing and took off her widow's garments.
\v 3 She bathed her body with water and anointed herself with precious ointment and combed her hair and put on a tiara and dressed herself in the festive attire she used to wear while her husband Manasseh was living.
\v 4 She put sandals on her feet and put on her anklets and bracelets and rings and her earrings and all her ornaments and made herself very beautiful to entice the eyes of all the men who might see her.
\v 5 She gave her maid a skin of wine and a flask of oil and filled a bag with roasted grain and a lump of dried fig cake and fine bread. Then she wrapped up all her vessels and put them together and gave them to her maid to carry.
\p
\v 6 Then they went out to the town gate of Bethulia and found Uzziah standing there with the elders of the town Chabris and Charmis.
\v 7 When they saw her transformed in appearance and dressed differently they were very greatly astounded at her beauty and said to her:
\v 8 May the God of our ancestors grant you favor and fulfill your plans to the glory of the Israelites and to the exaltation of Jerusalem.
\p Then she bowed down to God.
\v 9 Then she said to them: Order the gate of the town to be opened for me so that I may go out and accomplish the things you have just said to me. So they ordered the young men to open the gate for her as she requested.
\p
\v 11 While she was going straight on through the valley an Assyrian patrol met her —
\v 12 and they took her into custody and asked her: To what people do you belong and where are you coming from and where are you going? She replied: I am a daughter of the Hebrews but I am fleeing from them — for they are about to be handed over to you to be devoured.
\v 13 I am on my way to see Holofernes the commander of your army to give him a true report. And I will show him a way by which he can go and capture all the hill country without losing one of his men — captured or slain.
\p
\v 20 So they escorted her and a hundred of them accompanied her to the tent of Holofernes.

\c 11
\p
\v 1 When she came before him Holofernes was resting on his bed under a canopy which was woven with purple and gold and emeralds and precious stones.
\v 2 When they announced her he came to the front of his tent with silver lamps carried before him.
\v 3 When Judith came before him and his servants she amazed all of them by the beauty of her face. She prostrated herself and paid homage to him but his servants raised her up.
\p
\v 5 Holofernes said to her: Take courage woman — do not be afraid in your heart (*kardia*) — for I have never hurt anyone who chose to serve Nebuchadnezzar the king of all the earth.
\v 6 And now your people who live in the hill country — if they had not slighted me I would never have lifted my spear against them. But they have brought this on themselves.
\p
\v 7 And now tell me why you have fled from them and come over to us — since you have come to safety.
\p
\v 10 So now my master Holofernes hear a word from your servant's mouth and let your servant speak in your presence — and I will tell you nothing false this night.
\v 11 If you follow out the words of your servant God will accomplish something through you and my lord will not fail to achieve his purposes.
\v 16 Now as for myself — your servant — I worship God even here and I will pray to God tonight and on subsequent nights when I am with you. And he will tell me when they have committed their sins —
\v 17 and I will come and tell you so that you may go out with your whole army and not one of them will be able to withstand you.
\v 18 I will lead you through Judea until you come to Jerusalem — and I will set your throne there. You will lead them like sheep that have no shepherd and not a dog will so much as growl at you. For these things were told me according to my foreknowledge — they were announced to me and I was sent to tell you.
\p
\v 22 His words pleased Holofernes and all his servants and they marveled at her wisdom and said:
\v 23 There is no other woman from one end of the earth to the other who looks so beautiful or speaks so wisely.

\c 12
\p
\v 1 Then he commanded them to bring her in to where his silver dinnerware was kept and ordered them to set a table for her with some of his own delicacies and with some of his own wine to drink.
\v 2 But Judith said: I cannot partake of them or it will be an offense — but provision will be made for me from the things I have brought with me.
\p
\v 7 So Holofernes commanded his bodyguard not to hinder her — and she stayed in the camp for three days and went out each night to the valley of Bethulia and bathed at the spring in the camp.
\v 8 When she came up from the spring she prayed the Lord God of Israel to direct her way for the raising up of her people.
\p
\v 12 So Bagoas the eunuch in charge of his personal affairs went to her and said: Let not this fair girl hesitate to come to my lord to be honored in his presence and to enjoy drinking wine with us and to become today like one of the Assyrian women who serve in the palace of Nebuchadnezzar.
\p
\v 16 So Holofernes was greatly pleased with her and drank a great quantity of wine — much more than he had ever drunk in any one day since he was born.

\c 13
\p
\v 1 When evening came his slaves quickly withdrew and Bagoas closed the tent from outside and dismissed the attendants from his master's presence. They went to bed for they were all weary because the banquet had lasted so long.
\v 2 So Judith was left alone in the tent with Holofernes who was sprawled on his bed dead drunk.
\p
\v 3 Now Judith had told her maid to stand outside the bedchamber and to wait for her to come out just as she did on the other days — for she said she would be going out for her prayers. And she had said the same thing to Bagoas.
\p
\v 4 So everyone went out and no one great or small was left in the bedchamber. Then Judith standing beside his bed said in her heart (*kardia*):
\p O Lord God of all might look in this hour on the work of my hands for the exaltation of Jerusalem.
\p For now is the time to help your inheritance and to carry out my design to destroy the enemies who have risen up against us.
\p
\v 6 So she went to the bedpost near Holofernes' head and took down his sword from there.
\v 7 She came close to his bed and took hold of the hair of his head and said: Give me strength today O Lord God of Israel.
\v 8 Then with all her might she struck his neck twice and cut off his head.
\v 9 Next she rolled his body off the bed and pulled down the canopy from the posts. Soon afterward she went out and gave Holofernes' head to her maid —
\v 10 who put it in her food bag.
\p
\v 11 Then the two of them went out together as they were accustomed to do for prayer. They passed through the camp and circled around that valley and went up the mountain to Bethulia and came to its gates.
\p
\v 14 Then she took the head out of the bag and showed it and said to them: Look — here is the head of Holofernes the general of the Assyrian army and here is the canopy beneath which he lay in his drunken stupor. The Lord has struck him down by the hand of a woman.
\v 15 As the Lord lives who has protected me in the way I went it was my face that seduced him to his destruction — yet he committed no sin with me to defile me and put me to shame.
\p
\v 17 And when the people heard they were greatly astounded and bowing down they worshipped God and said with one accord:
\p Blessed are you our God who have this day humiliated the enemies of your people.
\p
\v 18 And Uzziah said to her:
\p O daughter — you are blessed by the Most High God above all other women on earth — and blessed be the Lord God who created the heavens and the earth who has guided you to cut off the head of the leader of our enemies.
\v 19 For your hope will never depart from the hearts of those who remember the power of God.
\v 20 May God grant this to be a perpetual honor to you and may he visit you with blessings because you risked your own life when our people were brought low and you averted our ruin walking in the straight path before our God.
\p And all the people said: Amen — Amen.

\c 14
\p
\v 1 Then Judith said to them: Listen to me my brothers. Take this head and hang it upon the parapet of your wall.
\v 2 At daybreak when the sun rises let every able-bodied man take up his weapons and go out of the town. Set a captain over them as if you were going down to the plain against the Assyrian outpost. Only do not go down.
\v 3 Then the Assyrians will pick up their weapons and go to their camp and rouse the officers of the Assyrian army. They will rush to the tent of Holofernes and will not find him — and fear will fall on them and they will flee before you.
\v 4 Then you and all who live within the borders of Israel will pursue them and cut them down in their tracks.
\p
\v 10 When Achior saw all that the God of Israel had done he believed firmly in God and was circumcised in the flesh and was annexed to the house of Israel — remaining so to this day.

\c 15
\p
\v 1 When those in the tents heard about it they were astounded —
\v 2 trembling and fear fell on them so that they did not wait for one another but with one impulse all rushed out and fled by every path through the plain and through the hill country.
\v 3 Those who had camped in the hill country around Bethulia also took to flight. Then the Israelites — every warrior among them — rushed out upon them.
\p
\v 8 Then Joakim the high priest and the council of the people of Israel who lived in Jerusalem came to witness the good things that the Lord had done for Israel and to see Judith and to wish her well.
\v 9 When they met her they all blessed her with one accord and said to her: You are the glory of Jerusalem — you are the great boast of Israel — you are the great pride of our nation.
\v 10 You have done all this with your own hand — you have done great good to Israel and God is well pleased with it. Blessed be the Almighty Lord forever.
\p And all the people said: Amen.
\p
\v 11 And all the people plundered the camp for thirty days. They gave Judith the tent of Holofernes and all his silver dinnerware his beds his bowls and all his furniture. She took them and loaded her mule and hitched up her carts and piled them on.
\v 12 Then all the women of Israel gathered to see her and blessed her and some of them performed a dance in her honor. She took branches in her hand and gave them to the women who were with her.
\v 13 And they crowned themselves with olive wreaths — she and those who were with her — and she went before all the people in the dance leading all the women while all the men of Israel followed bearing their arms with garlands and with songs on their lips.

\c 16
\p
\v 1 Then Judith began this thanksgiving before all Israel and all the people sang loudly this song of praise:
\p
\v 2 Begin a song to my God with tambourines — sing to my Lord with cymbals.
\p Raise to him a new psalm — exalt him and call upon his name.
\v 3 For God is the Lord who crushes wars — for he set up his camp among his people — he delivered me from the hands of those who pursued me.
\p
\v 4 Assyria came out of the mountains from the north — it came with tens of thousands of its army — their numbers blocked up the wadis and their cavalry covered the hills.
\v 5 He said he would burn up my territory and kill my young men with the sword and throw my infants to the ground and seize my children as booty and take my virgins as spoil.

## CRITICAL TEXTUAL NOTES — JUDITH

**No Hebrew/Aramaic original extant:** Unlike Tobit (preserved in Aramaic at Qumran), no Semitic original of Judith has been found. The book exists only in Greek (LXX) and in secondary versions (Latin Vulgate — Jerome's free adaptation, Old Latin, Syriac). The LXX is the primary text.

**Historical fiction as theological narrative:** The book's chronology is deliberately impossible: Nebuchadnezzar is called king of Assyria in Nineveh (not Babylon) and the narrative assumes the return from exile has already occurred (4:3). The book is not historical chronicle but theological narrative — a wisdom-drama using stock figures to present a theology of covenant-faithfulness and divine reversal.

**The name Judith:** *Ioudith* = the feminine form of *Ioudas* / Judah. She is *Judah* — the embodiment of the Jewish people. Her story is the story of Israel facing annihilation and prevailing through covenant-faithfulness, prayer, and divinely-directed action.

**The Holofernes figure:** The general who commands the universal empire's army against the covenant people is the archetypal enemy of God's people — Pharaoh / Sennacherib / Haman / Antiochus in sequence. The narrative deliberately echoes the exodus / the Sennacherib deliverance / and anticipates Esther.

## CAE NODES — JUDITH

### Judith 4:9–15 — Israel humbles itself / prayer before the Temple — CAE PRIMARY NODE

**4:9** — "And every man of Israel cried out to God with great fervor and they humbled themselves with much fasting."

**4:13** — "The Lord heard their prayers and looked with favor on their affliction — for the people fasted many days throughout Judea and in Jerusalem before the sanctuary of the Lord Almighty."

***kai eisēkousen Kyrios tēs phōnēs autōn kai epeiden epi tēn thlipsin autōn***

= and the Lord heard their voice and looked upon their affliction.

The Judith narrative opens with Israel's covenant-response to existential threat: the entire people — men / women / children / servants / resident aliens — put on sackcloth and fasted. The pattern is that of 2 Paralipomenon 7:14 enacted: humbling / prayer / seeking the divine face. The Lord's response is immediate — he hears and looks upon their affliction.

The **draped altar** (4:12) is an extraordinary detail: the altar itself is covered in sackcloth. The cultic apparatus participates in the communal mourning — the entire covenant-system expresses the people's need. The altar is not merely the place of sacrifice but the locus of corporate covenant-solidarity in grief.

**Joel 2:15–17** — "Blow the trumpet in Zion — consecrate a fast — call a solemn assembly... gather the people... Let the priests the ministers of the Lord weep between the vestibule and the altar and let them say: Spare your people O Lord." Joel's call to corporate fasting and prayer is the prophetic ground for the Judith 4 assembly: the identical response (fast / assembly / prayer between vestibule and altar) at the moment of national threat.

**Acts 27:9, 23–24** — Paul's ship in the storm: fasting and prayer in crisis. The pattern of corporate fasting / prayer / divine response in extremis recurs across the canon as the covenant-community's proper crisis-response.

### Judith 8:11–17 — Judith's rebuke of the elders / do not test God — CAE PRIMARY NODE

**8:12–13** — "Who are you to put God to the test today and to set yourselves up in the place of God in human affairs? You are putting the Lord Almighty to the test — but you will never know anything."

**8:14** — "You cannot plumb the depths of the human heart (*kardia*) or understand the workings of the human mind — how then can you search out God who made all these things and find out his mind or comprehend his thought?"

**8:16** — "Do not try to bind the purposes of the Lord our God — for God is not like a human being to be threatened or like a mere human to be won over by pleading."

***mē despozete tēs boulēs Kyriou tou theou hēmōn***

= do not exercise lordship over the counsel of the Lord our God.

Judith's confrontation with the elders is the theological center of the book. Uzziah has made a bargain with the people: five more days and then surrender. Judith identifies the theological error with precision: the elders have put a conditional on God — *if you do not deliver us in five days we will surrender*. This is covenant-presumption — binding the divine will to a human timetable and threatening to abandon the covenant if the timetable is not met.

The three theological moves of Judith's rebuke:

**1. Human limits** (8:14): You cannot plumb the *kardia* of another human — how then do you presume to know the divine mind? The incapacity to know human depths makes the presumption of knowing divine timetables absurd.

**2. Divine freedom** (8:15–16): God may deliver within five days / or within any other time / or not at all — his purposes are not subject to human negotiation. The *boulē Kyriou* = counsel of the Lord is sovereign.

**3. The testing-prohibition** (8:13): *peirazete ton Kyrion* = you are testing the Lord — this is the language of Massah / of the wilderness rebellion. The elders are committing the same sin as the wilderness generation: demanding divine provision on human terms.

**Deuteronomy 6:16** — "You shall not put the Lord your God to the test as you tested him at Massah." Jesus cites this verse in the temptation narrative (Matt 4:7 / Luke 4:12). The Judith passage is the narrative embodiment of the prohibition: the elders' conditional bargain is exactly the *peirazein ton Kyrion* that Deuteronomy forbids.

**Isaiah 40:13–14** — "Who has measured the breath-of-life (*pneuma*) of the Lord or what man shows him counsel? Whom did he consult and who made him understand?" Isaiah's rhetorical questions about divine sovereignty are the prophetic background for Judith 8:14–16: no human can comprehend the divine mind / no human counsel can bind the divine purpose.

**Romans 11:33–34** — "Oh the depth of the riches and wisdom and knowledge of God — how unsearchable are his judgments and how inscrutable his ways. For who has known the mind of the Lord or who has been his counselor?" Paul's doxology of divine incomprehensibility is the NT theological formulation of Judith 8:14: the human incapacity to plumb the *kardia* of another human is the measure of the infinitely greater incapacity to know the divine mind.

### Judith 9:1–14 — Judith's prayer / the God of the humble — CAE PRIMARY NODE

**9:11** — "For your strength does not lie in numbers nor your might in the powerful — but you are the God of the humble (*tapeinōn*) the helper of the oppressed the upholder of the weak the protector of the forsaken the savior of those without hope."

**9:13–14** — "Make my deceitful words to be their wound and stripe... Let your whole nation and every tribe know and understand that you are God — the God of all power and might — and that there is no other who protects the people of Israel but you alone."

***hoti ou en plēthei dynamis sou oude hē krataiosynē sou en ischyousin — all' tapeinōn ei theos***

= for not in multitude is your power nor in the powerful is your might — but you are God of the humble.

The Judith prayer is the most concentrated statement of the *tapeinōs* = humble theology in the deuterocanon. The divine reversal principle — strength not in numbers / might not in the powerful / but God is *tapeinōn* = of the humble — is the theological axis of the entire book.

The prayer is offered at the exact time of the Temple incense offering (9:1) — Judith prostrate in Bethulia prays simultaneously with the Temple liturgy in Jerusalem. The prayer is coordinated with the divine-service: the horizontal human distress is aligned with the vertical cultic moment.

**The strategic deception element** (9:13): Judith asks God to make her deceptive words the instrument of Israel's deliverance. This is one of the most theologically contested petitions in the OT-deuterocanonical tradition. The GLE renders it without harmonization: Judith asks for what she asks / God grants what he grants / the narrative assesses outcomes theologically, not by applying a later sexual-ethics framework.

**1 Samuel 2:3–5** — Hannah's prayer: "Talk no more so very proudly — let not arrogance come from your mouth — for the Lord is a God of knowledge and by him actions are weighed. The bows of the mighty are broken but the feeble bind on strength." Hannah's prayer and Judith's share the same theological structure: divine reversal / the mighty broken / the weak strengthened. Judith 9:11 is Hannah's prayer applied to the military crisis.

**Luke 1:51–52** — "He has shown strength with his arm — he has scattered the proud in the thoughts of their hearts — he has brought down the mighty from their thrones and exalted those of humble estate (*tapeinous*)." The Magnificat's *tapeinous* = humble exalted is the NT fulfillment of the Judith 9:11 theology: the God who is *tapeinōn theos* = God of the humble exalts the humble in the definitive act of the Incarnation. Mary's *tapeinōsis* = humiliation (Luke 1:48) / Judith's humble prayer / Hannah's prayer — the three form the canonical arc of the reversal theology.

**Matthew 5:3** — "Blessed are the poor in spirit (*ptōchoi tō pneumati*) for theirs is the kingdom of heaven." The Beatitude's first blessing is the NT compressed form of the Judith 9:11 principle: the God who is *tapeinōn theos* gives his kingdom to those who own their poverty before him.

### Judith 13:4–10 — the beheading / *by the hand of a woman* — CAE PRIMARY NODE

**13:4** — "Then Judith standing beside his bed said in her heart (*kardia*): O Lord God of all might look in this hour on the work of my hands for the exaltation of Jerusalem."

**13:7–8** — "She said: Give me strength today O Lord God of Israel. Then with all her might she struck his neck twice and cut off his head."

**16:6** — "But the Lord Almighty has foiled them by the hand of a woman (*en cheiri gynaikos*)."

***kai ēsthénēsen ho kratistos autōn en cheiri gynaikos***

= and their mighty one was brought low by the hand of a woman.

The *en cheiri gynaikos* = by the hand of a woman is the book's theological thesis announced in the victory hymn. The formula echoes the Deborah-Jael tradition: in Judges 4:9 Deborah prophesies that the Lord will sell Sisera *into the hand of a woman* (*be-yad ishah*) — and Jael delivers him. Judith is the second great *en cheiri gynaikos* deliverance of Israel's enemies.

The theological logic: Holofernes commands the greatest military force the narrative can imagine — *his mighty one* (*ho kratistos*) is brought down not by superior force but by a widow with a sword. The reversal is absolute: the maximum human power is defeated by the minimum human power. The victory belongs to God precisely because no human strategy could have accomplished it.

**The silent prayer** (13:4–5): Before acting Judith prays silently in her *kardia*. The heart-prayer at the moment of decisive action is the innermost expression of the *kardia*-toward-God theology: the action is not self-willed but God-directed from the center of the person.

**Judges 4:9, 21** — Deborah's prophecy (*the Lord will sell Sisera into the hand of a woman*) and Jael's action (driving the tent peg through Sisera's temple) — the direct typological precedent for Judith. The Judith narrative is a deliberate expansion and intensification of the Jael-typology.

**1 Corinthians 1:27–28** — "But God chose what is foolish in the world to shame the wise — God chose what is weak in the world to shame the strong — God chose what is low and despised in the world — even things that are not — to bring to nothing things that are." Paul's *weakness-confounding-strength* theology is the NT theological formulation of the *en cheiri gynaikos* principle: the instrument of divine victory is chosen precisely for its apparent inadequacy.

**2 Corinthians 12:9** — "My grace is sufficient for you for my power is made perfect in weakness." The *en cheiri gynaikos* = by the hand of a woman reversal pattern is the OT enactment of Paul's *dynamis en astheneia* = power in weakness principle. Judith is the deuterocanon's supreme narrative illustration of the theology that Paul articulates propositionally.

### Judith 16:1–17 — the victory hymn / *ōdē kainē* — CAE PRIMARY NODE

**16:1–2** — "Then Judith began this thanksgiving before all Israel... Begin a song to my God with tambourines — sing to my Lord with cymbals. Raise to him a new psalm (*ōdēn kainēn*) — exalt him and call upon his name. For God is the Lord who crushes wars."

**16:13–14** — "I will sing to my God a new song (*ōdēn kainēn*): O Lord you are great and glorious — wonderful in strength — invincible. Let all your creatures serve you — for you spoke and they were made — you sent forth your breath-of-life (*pneuma*) and it formed them — and there is no one who can resist your voice."

**16:15** — "For the mountains shall be shaken to their foundations with the waters — the rocks shall melt like wax before your face — but to those who fear you you will continue to show mercy (*eleos*)."

***ōdēn kainēn aisō tō theō mou*** = a new song I will sing to my God.

The victory hymn of Judith is structured in three movements:

**1. The historical narrative** (16:3–12): The Assyrian threat / the divine response / the reversal by the hand of a woman. The hymn rehearses the book's plot as theological declaration.

**2. The creation-theology** (16:13–14): *You spoke and they were made / you sent forth your pneuma and it formed them.* The divine word and *pneuma* as instruments of creation — the victory hymn expands from military history to cosmic scope. The God who defeated Holofernes is the God who created by word and *pneuma*.

**3. The eschatological judgment** (16:17): Fire and worms for those who rise against God's people — the judgment is permanent.

***ōdē kainē*** — the *new song* is a significant liturgical phrase. In the Psalter the *ōdē kainē* / *shîr hadash* is sung in response to a new act of divine salvation (Psalm 96:1 / 98:1 / 144:9 / 149:1). The *new thing* God has done demands a *new song*. Judith's victory is added to the canon of God's saving acts that call forth new praise.

**The creation-pneuma** (16:14): *apesteiles to pneuma sou kai ektisen auta* = you sent forth your breath-of-life and it formed them. The *pneuma* as the divine agent of creation echoes Genesis 1:2 (*pneuma theou epiphereto epi tou hydatos* = breath-of-life of God hovered over the waters). The same *pneuma* that formed creation is the *pneuma* whose presence or absence in the covenant-community determines their strength.

**Psalm 96:1** — "Oh sing to the Lord a new song — sing to the Lord all the earth." The Psalter's *ōdē kainē* tradition is the liturgical ground for Judith 16:13. The new acts of God demand new praise — the song cannot be old because the salvation is new.

**Revelation 5:9** — "And they sang a new song (*ōdēn kainēn*) saying: Worthy are you to take the scroll and to open its seals — for you were slain and by your blood you ransomed people for God from every tribe and language and people and nation." The Lamb's *ōdē kainē* in Revelation is the eschatological fulfillment of the Judith / Psalter *ōdē kainē* tradition: the definitive new act of divine salvation — the cross / resurrection / redemption of all peoples — demands the ultimate new song. The chain: Miriam at the Red Sea (Exodus 15) → Deborah after Jael (Judges 5) → Judith after Holofernes (Judith 16) → the saints before the Lamb (Revelation 5:9).

**Revelation 14:3** — "And they were singing a new song before the throne and before the four living creatures and before the elders." The 144,000 singing the new song before the divine throne is the consummation of the covenant-victory-song tradition that runs from Miriam through Judith.

### Judith 16:17 / 14:10 — the nations' conversion / Achior's circumcision — CAE NODE

**14:10** — "When Achior saw all that the God of Israel had done he believed firmly in God and was circumcised in the flesh and was annexed to the house of Israel."

**16:17** — "Woe to the nations that rise up against my people — the Lord Almighty will take vengeance on them in the day of judgment — he will send fire and worms into their flesh and they will weep in pain forever."

The Achior sub-plot is the book's narrative of Gentile inclusion through witness. Achior the Ammonite commander had declared the theological truth about Israel before Holofernes (chapter 5) and was sent to the besieged city as punishment — making him a witness to both the Assyrian threat and the divine deliverance. When he sees what God has done he believes and joins Israel. The Ammonite — specifically excluded from the assembly of God in Deuteronomy 23:3 / Nehemiah 13:1 — is incorporated through the witness of divine acts.

The tension: Nehemiah 13:1 explicitly prohibits Ammonites from entering the assembly / Achior the Ammonite enters the assembly through conversion. The Judith narrative allows the tension to stand — the Mosaic exclusion and the covenant-inclusion through faith are both present. The book does not harmonize them; it places them side by side.

**Ruth 1:16** — the Moabite Ruth's covenant-adhesion oath is the earlier instance of the Gentile-joining-Israel pattern. As Ruth (excluded nation / joins through faithfulness and witness) so Achior (excluded nation / joins through witness and belief). Both Ruth and Achior enter the covenant-people not by birth but by response to the divine acts they have witnessed.

**Acts 10:34–35** — "Truly I understand that God shows no partiality but in every nation anyone who fears him and does what is right is acceptable to him." Peter's declaration after Cornelius's conversion is the NT theological generalization of the Achior / Ruth pattern: the God who received Achior the Ammonite receives all who fear him regardless of national origin.

## STRUCTURAL NOTE — JUDITH AS THE GREAT REVERSAL NARRATIVE

Judith is the deuterocanon's supreme narrative of divine reversal — the literary and theological counterpart to the Exodus. Its structure is perfectly chiastic:

**A. The great threat** (1–3): Nebuchadnezzar's universal empire / Holofernes' army that cannot be stopped / the command to worship Nebuchadnezzar as god.

**B. Israel's covenant response** (4): Fasting / prayer / sackcloth / the altar draped — the people humble themselves.

**C. The theological crisis** (5–7): Achior's testimony / the elders' five-day bargain / the water crisis — Israel at the point of surrender.

**D. Judith's theological intervention** (8): The rebuke of the elders — do not bind the Lord's counsel.

**E. The prayer and action** (9–13): Prayer at the incense hour / the deception / *by the hand of a woman* / the head in the bag.

**D'. The theological declaration** (13:14–20): Judith displays the head — the Lord has struck him down.

**C'. The reversal** (14–15): Panic / flight / plunder — the maximum threat becomes maximum defeat.

**B'. Israel's covenant celebration** (15–16): Judith leads the dance / the victory hymn / three months of feasting.

**A'. The aftermath** (16:22–27): Judith as perpetual widow / her fame / her death and mourning — the covenant-people at rest.

The theology of the book is stated most precisely in 9:11 and 16:6: the God who defeats the mighty through the weak / the God who is *tapeinōn theos* = God of the humble / whose victories are *en cheiri gynaikos* = by the hand of a woman. The book is a sustained enactment of 1 Samuel 2:4–7 (Hannah's prayer) and a narrative anticipation of Luke 1:51–53 (the Magnificat).

**The Judith-Mary typological connection** is ancient in patristic and liturgical tradition. Both are:
- Women of prayer and fasting
- *Tapeinē* = humble before God
- Instruments of divine victory through apparent weakness
- Associated with the defeat of the enemy of God's people
- Praised with language echoing each other: Uzziah's blessing (*you are the glory of Jerusalem* / Judith 15:9) and Elizabeth's blessing (*blessed are you among women* / Luke 1:42).

The ultimate fulfillment: Judith defeats the embodiment of the empire that would destroy Israel / Mary bears the one who defeats death and the ultimate enemy of the human race. Both victories are *en cheiri* = by the hand of the one chosen — the instrument is humanly inadequate / the victory is divinely accomplished.

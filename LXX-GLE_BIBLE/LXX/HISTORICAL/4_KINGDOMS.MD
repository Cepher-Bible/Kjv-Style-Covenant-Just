\v 1 After the death of Ahab Moab rebelled against Israel.
\v 2 Now Ahaziah fell through the lattice in his upper chamber in Samaria and lay sick — so he sent messengers saying to them: Go inquire of Baal-zebub the god of Ekron whether I shall recover from this sickness.
\v 3 But the angel of the Lord said to Elijah the Tishbite: Arise go up to meet the messengers of the king of Samaria and say to them: Is it because there is no God in Israel that you are going to inquire of Baal-zebub the god of Ekron?
\v 4 Now therefore thus says the Lord: You shall not come down from the bed to which you have gone up but you shall surely die. So Elijah went.
\v 5 The messengers returned to the king and he said to them: Why have you returned?
\v 6 And they said to him: There came a man to meet us and said to us: Go back to the king who sent you and say to him: Thus says the Lord — is it because there is no God in Israel that you are sending to inquire of Baal-zebub the god of Ekron? Therefore you shall not come down from the bed to which you have gone up but you shall surely die.
\v 7 He said to them: What kind of man was he who came to meet you and told you these things?
\v 8 They answered him: He wore a garment of hair with a belt of leather around his waist. And he said: It is Elijah the Tishbite.
\p
\v 9 Then the king sent to him a captain of fifty men with his fifty. He went up to Elijah who was sitting on the top of a hill and said to him: O man of God the king says: Come down.
\v 10 But Elijah answered the captain of fifty: If I am a man of God let fire come down from heaven and consume you and your fifty. Then fire came down from heaven and consumed him and his fifty.
\v 11 Again the king sent to him another captain of fifty men with his fifty. And he answered and said to him: O man of God this is the king's order: Come down quickly.
\v 12 But Elijah answered them: If I am a man of God let fire come down from heaven and consume you and your fifty. Then the fire of God came down from heaven and consumed him and his fifty.
\v 13 Again the king sent the captain of a third fifty with his fifty. And the third captain of fifty went up and came and fell on his knees before Elijah and entreated him: O man of God please let my life and the life of these fifty servants of yours be precious in your sight.
\v 14 Look — fire came down from heaven and consumed the two former captains of fifty men with their fifties — but now let my life be precious in your sight.
\v 15 Then the angel of the Lord said to Elijah: Go down with him — do not be afraid of him. So he arose and went down with him to the king —
\v 16 and said to him: Thus says the Lord: Because you have sent messengers to inquire of Baal-zebub the god of Ekron — is it because there is no God in Israel to inquire of his word? — therefore you shall not come down from the bed to which you have gone up but you shall surely die.
\v 17 So he died according to the word of the Lord that Elijah had spoken. Jehoram his brother became king in his place in the second year of Jehoram the son of Jehoshaphat king of Judah because Ahaziah had no son.
\v 18 Now the rest of the acts of Ahaziah that he did — are they not written in the Book of the Chronicles of the Kings of Israel?

\v 2 And Elijah said to Elisha: Please stay here — for the Lord has sent me as far as Bethel. But Elisha said: As the Lord lives and as you yourself live I will not leave you. So they went down to Bethel.
\v 3 And the sons of the prophets who were in Bethel came out to Elisha and said to him: Do you know that today the Lord will take away your master from over you? And he said: Yes I know it — keep silent.
\v 4 Elijah said to him: Elisha please stay here — for the Lord has sent me to Jericho. But he said: As the Lord lives and as you yourself live I will not leave you. So they came to Jericho.
\v 5 The sons of the prophets who were at Jericho drew near to Elisha and said to him: Do you know that today the Lord will take away your master from over you? And he answered: Yes I know it — keep silent.
\v 6 Then Elijah said to him: Please stay here — for the Lord has sent me to the Jordan. But he said: As the Lord lives and as you yourself live I will not leave you. So the two of them went on.
\v 7 Fifty men of the sons of the prophets also went and stood at some distance from them as they both were standing by the Jordan.
\v 8 Then Elijah took his cloak and rolled it up and struck the water and the water was parted to the one side and to the other till the two of them could go over on dry ground.
\p
\v 9 When they had crossed Elijah said to Elisha: Ask what I shall do for you before I am taken from you. And Elisha said: Please let there be a double portion of your breath-of-life upon me.
\v 10 And he said: You have asked a hard thing — yet if you see me as I am being taken from you it shall be so for you — but if you do not see me it shall not be so.
\v 11 And as they still went on and talked look — a chariot of fire and horses of fire separated the two of them. And Elijah went up by a whirlwind into heaven.
\v 12 And Elisha saw it and he cried: My father my father — the chariots of Israel and its horsemen. And he saw him no more.
\p Then he took hold of his own clothes and tore them in two pieces.
\v 13 And he took up the cloak of Elijah that had fallen from him and went back and stood on the bank of the Jordan.
\v 14 Then he took the cloak of Elijah that had fallen from him and struck the water saying: Where is the Lord the God of Elijah? And when he had struck the water the water was parted to the one side and to the other and Elisha went over.
\v 15 Now when the sons of the prophets who were at Jericho saw him opposite them they said: The breath-of-life of Elijah rests on Elisha. And they came to meet him and bowed to the ground before him.
\v 16 And they said to him: Look — there are fifty strong men among your servants — please let them go and seek your master. It may be that the breath-of-life of the Lord has carried him up and cast him on some mountain or into some valley. And he said: You shall not send.
\v 17 But when they urged him until he was ashamed he said: Send. They sent therefore fifty men. And for three days they sought him but did not find him.
\v 18 And they came back to him while he was staying at Jericho and he said to them: Did I not say to you: Do not go?
\p
\v 19 Now the men of the city said to Elisha: Look — the situation of this city is pleasant as my lord sees but the water is bad and the land is unfruitful.
\v 20 He said: Bring me a new bowl and put salt in it. So they brought it to him.
\v 21 Then he went to the spring of water and threw salt in it and said: Thus says the Lord: I have healed this water — from now on neither death nor miscarriage shall come from it.
\v 22 So the water has been healed to this day according to the word that Elisha spoke.
\p
\v 23 He went up from there to Bethel — and while he was going up on the way some small boys came out of the city and mocked him saying: Go up you baldhead — go up you baldhead.
\v 24 And he turned around and when he saw them he cursed them in the name of the Lord. And two she-bears came out of the woods and tore forty-two of the boys.
\v 25 From there he went on to Mount Carmel and from there he returned to Samaria.

\v 9 And she said to her husband: Look — I perceive that this is a holy man of God who is continually passing our way.

\v 15 He said: Call her. And when he had called her she stood in the doorway.

\v 2 Now the Syrians on one of their raids had carried off a little girl from the land of Israel and she worked in the service of Naaman's wife.

\v 4 So Naaman went in and told his lord: Thus and so spoke the girl from the land of Israel.

\v 6 And he brought the letter to the king of Israel which read: When this letter reaches you know that I have sent to you Naaman my servant that you may cure him of his leprosy.
\v 7 And when the king of Israel read the letter he tore his clothes and said: Am I God to kill and to make alive that this man sends word to me to cure a man of his leprosy? Only consider and see how he is seeking a quarrel with me.
\v 8 But when Elisha the man of God heard that the king of Israel had torn his clothes he sent to the king saying: Why have you torn your clothes? Let him come now to me that he may know there is a prophet in Israel.
\v 9 So Naaman came with his horses and chariots and stood at the door of Elisha's house.
\v 10 And Elisha sent a messenger to him saying: Go and wash in the Jordan seven times and your flesh shall be restored and you shall be clean.
\v 11 But Naaman was angry and went away saying: Look — I thought that he would surely come out to me and stand and call upon the name of the Lord his God and wave his hand over the place and cure the leper.
\v 12 Are not Abana and Pharpar the rivers of Damascus better than all the waters of Israel? Could I not wash in them and be clean? So he turned and went away in a rage.
\v 13 But his servants came near and said to him: My father — it is a great word the prophet has spoken to you. Will you not do it? Has he actually said to you: Wash and be clean?
\v 14 So he went down and dipped himself seven times in the Jordan according to the word of the man of God and his flesh was restored like the flesh of a little child and he was clean.
\p
\v 15 Then he returned to the man of God — he and all his company — and he came and stood before him and said: Look — I know that there is no God in all the earth but in Israel. So accept now a present from your servant.
\v 16 But he said: As the Lord lives before whom I stand I will receive none. And he urged him to take it but he refused.
\v 17 Then Naaman said: If not please let there be given to your servant two mule-loads of earth — for from now on your servant will not offer burnt offering or sacrifice to any god but the Lord.
\v 18 In this matter may the Lord pardon your servant: when my master goes into the house of Rimmon to worship there leaning on my arm — when I bow myself in the house of Rimmon — when I bow myself in the house of Rimmon the Lord pardon your servant in this matter.
\v 19 He said to him: Go in peace.
\p So he departed from him some distance.
\v 20 And Gehazi the servant of Elisha the man of God said: Look — my master has spared this Naaman the Syrian in not accepting from his hand what he brought. As the Lord lives I will run after him and get something from him.
\v 21 So Gehazi followed Naaman. And when Naaman saw someone running after him he got down from the chariot to meet him and said: Is all well?
\v 22 And he said: All is well. My master has sent me to say: There have just now come to me from the hill country of Ephraim two young men of the sons of the prophets — please give them a talent of silver and two changes of clothing.
\v 23 And Naaman said: Be pleased to accept two talents. And he urged him and tied up two talents of silver in two bags with two changes of clothing and laid them on two of his servants and they carried them before Gehazi.
\v 24 And when he came to the hill he took them from their hand and put them in the house. And he let the men go and they departed.
\v 25 He went in and stood before his master and Elisha said to him: Where have you been Gehazi? And he said: Your servant went nowhere.
\v 26 But he said to him: Did not my heart go when the man turned from his chariot to meet you? Was it a time to accept money and garments olive orchards and vineyards sheep and oxen male servants and female servants?
\v 27 Therefore the leprosy of Naaman shall cling to you and to your descendants forever. So he went out from his presence a leper like snow.

## CRITICAL TEXTUAL NOTES — LXX 4 KINGDOMS

**Chapter 7** — the lepers at the gate. LXX is substantially identical to MT. The four lepers discovering the abandoned Assyrian camp is structurally parallel to the Gospel announcement pattern — those on the outside of the city carry the good news in.

**Chapter 25:27–30** — the release of Jehoiachin. The LXX and MT agree closely. The final four verses of 4 Kingdoms function as a theological coda: the Davidic king is lifted from prison and seated at the king's table — the *lychnos* = lamp of David is not extinguished even in exile. The book ends not with the burning of the Temple but with the Davidic king dining at the Babylonian king's table.

## CAE NODES — 4 KINGDOMS

### 4 Kingdoms 2:1–15 — Elijah's ascent / Elisha's double portion — CAE PRIMARY NODE

**2:9** — "Elisha said: Please let there be a double portion of your breath-of-life (*pneumatos*) upon me."

**2:11** — "And Elijah went up by a whirlwind (*en systremmati*) into heaven."

**2:12** — "And Elisha saw it and he cried: My father my father — the chariots of Israel and its horsemen (*harmata Israēl kai hippeis autou*)."

**2:15** — "The breath-of-life of Elijah rests on Elisha (*anapepautai pneuma Ēliou epi Elisaie*)."

***diploûn en tō pneumati sou ep' eme*** = a doubling in your breath-of-life upon me.

The ascent of Elijah is one of only two explicit bodily translations in the OT — the other being Enoch (Gen 5:24). The *systremma* = whirlwind is the theophanic wind-register: the same *pneuma* that blows at Sinai / moves over the waters at creation / rushes on the judges now carries the prophet bodily to heaven.

**The double-portion request:** *diploûn* = double is the firstborn's inheritance (Deut 21:17) — Elisha asks for the firstborn's share of Elijah's *pneuma*. This is not a request for twice as much power — it is a request for the prophetic succession right, the right to carry the *pneuma* of the prophetic office forward. That the *pneuma* of Elijah rests on Elisha (2:15) confirms the inheritance granted.

**Luke 1:17** — "And he will go before him in the breath-of-life and power of Elijah (*en pneumati kai dynamei Ēliou*)." The announcement to Zechariah about John the Baptist: John receives the *pneuma kai dynamis* of Elijah — the same prophetic inheritance-transfer pattern as 2 Kgdms 2:15. John is the Elisha to Elijah's Elijah — the one who receives the double-portion *pneuma* of the predecessor.

**Matthew 11:14** — "And if you are willing to accept it he is Elijah who is to come." Jesus's identification of John as Elijah — the one in whom the Elijah-*pneuma* has returned. The Elijah-translation and *pneuma*-transfer of 4 Kingdoms 2 is the OT ground for the entire Elijah-John theology.

**Malachi 4:5** — "Look — I will send you Elijah the prophet before the great and awesome day of the Lord comes." The Malachi promise is the prophetic development of the 4 Kingdoms 2 translation: Elijah did not die but was taken — therefore Elijah can return. The translation is the necessary precondition for the return-expectation.

**The chariots of Israel:** *harmata Israēl kai hippeis autou* = the chariots of Israel and its horsemen. Elisha cries this at Elijah's departure — and the same cry is spoken over Elisha at his death (13:14). The phrase is the OT's formula for the prophet as Israel's true military defense: the man of God who prays and speaks is worth more than all chariots and cavalry. The Dothan vision (6:17 — chariots of fire around Elisha) is the visual confirmation of what the phrase declares.

---

### 4 Kingdoms 4:32–35 — Elisha raises the Shunammite's son — CAE PRIMARY NODE

**4:34–35** — "Then he went up and lay on the child and put his mouth on his mouth his eyes on his eyes and his hands on his hands — and as he stretched himself upon him the flesh of the child became warm... The child sneezed seven times and the child opened his eyes."

The second of the three great OT resurrection-anticipation narratives (the first being Elijah at Zarephath / 1 Kgdms 17:21–22; the third being the bones of Elisha / 4 Kgdms 13:21). Elisha's method: he lies on the dead child — full body contact — the warmth transfers. The sevenfold sneeze (seven = completeness / fullness) marks the restoration as comprehensive.

**Luke 7:11–15** — Jesus raises the widow of Nain's son. The structural elements match:
- A widow whose only son has died
- The prophet/Jesus approaches the situation
- The son is restored to life
- He is given back to his mother

Jesus does not lay on the child — he speaks: *Neaniske soi legō egerthēti* = young man I say to you arise. The contrast is the typological point: Elisha performs laborious physical contact over an extended period; Jesus speaks a word. The NT does not diminish the Elisha narrative — it establishes that what Elisha did by physical instrumentality Jesus does by sovereign word.

**Acts 20:9–12** — Paul raises Eutychus who fell from the window: "Paul went down and bent over him and embracing him said: Do not be alarmed — for his life (*psychē*) is in him." Paul's act echoes Elisha's body-contact method — the physical posture of lying/bending over the dead echoes the Elijah-Elisha pattern.

**John 11:43** — "He cried out with a loud voice: Lazarus come out." The Lazarus raising is the definitive NT resurrection-anticipation fulfillment: four days dead / the tomb / the command of voice alone. Against all three OT precursors (1 Kgdms 17 / 4 Kgdms 4 / 4 Kgdms 13) Jesus raises by word and commands the bound to come out — the bodily instrumentality of the prophets is replaced by the sovereign speech of the Son.

### 4 Kingdoms 5:1–14 — Naaman / the Gentile leper healed — CAE PRIMARY NODE

**5:1** — "Naaman commander of the army of the king of Syria... was a great man with his master and in high favor because by him the Lord had given victory to Syria. He was a mighty man of valor but he was a leper."

**5:14** — "His flesh was restored like the flesh of a little child and he was clean."

**5:15** — "Look — I know that there is no God in all the earth but in Israel."

***kai epistrepsen hē sarx autou hōs sarx paidiou mikrou kai ekatharisthē***

The Naaman narrative is the OT's most concentrated enacted-parable of Gentile inclusion through faith-obedience. The structural theology:

- A Gentile military commander of a nation in enmity with Israel
- Directed to the prophet of Israel by a captive Israelite girl
- His pride resists the simple command (wash seven times in the Jordan)
- His servants' wisdom breaks through: *it is a great word the prophet has spoken*
- He obeys and is healed
- His confession: "there is no God in all the earth but in Israel"
- His request: two mule-loads of Israelite soil — he will worship the Lord on Israel's earth

**Luke 4:27** — "And there were many lepers in Israel in the time of the prophet Elisha and none of them was cleansed but only Naaman the Syrian." Jesus cites the Naaman narrative as his second proof-text for Gentile reception of Israel's blessing when Israel rejects her prophet. The Naaman-text is Jesus's ground for the Nazareth rejection principle: the prophet is rejected at home / received by the Gentile outsider.

**Romans 10:12** — "For there is no distinction between Jew and Greek — for the same Lord is Lord of all bestowing his riches on all who call on him." Paul's no-distinction declaration is the theological generalization of the Naaman principle: the Lord who healed Naaman the Syrian is the Lord who is Lord of all.

**The pride-structure:** Naaman's near-miss (he almost refuses and goes away unhealedy) is the paradigm for the pride that blocks healing. He expected a spectacular public ceremony — the prophet would come out and call on the Lord and wave his hand. Instead: a messenger with a simple command. The simplicity offends. The servants' intervention is the voice of wisdom: *if he had said a great thing would you not have done it?* The Jordan water is no better than Syrian rivers — the healing comes not from the water but from the word and from the obedience.

**2 Corinthians 12:9** — "My grace is sufficient for you — for my power is made perfect in weakness." The Naaman principle in Paul's register: the apparently inadequate instrument (the simple word / the muddy river / the thorn in the flesh) is the vehicle of divine power precisely because it strips away human pride.

### 4 Kingdoms 6:15–17 — Open his eyes / the mountain full of chariots — CAE PRIMARY NODE

**6:16–17** — "He said: Do not be afraid — for those who are with us are more than those who are with them. Then Elisha prayed and said: O Lord please open his eyes that he may see. So the Lord opened the eyes of the young man and he saw and look — the mountain was full of horses and chariots of fire all around Elisha."

***anoixon dē tous ophthalmous tou paidiou kai ideto*** = open the eyes of the servant that he may see.

The *anoixon tous ophthalmous* = open his eyes prayer is one of the most concentrated prayers in the OT: the request that the invisible reality become visible to the one who is afraid. The servant sees the Syrian army surrounding the city — Elisha sees the heavenly army surrounding Elisha. Both armies are real; the servant lacks the vision to perceive the greater one.

**2 Kings 6:22** — After the blinding and capture: Elisha commands that the Syrians be fed and sent home. The enemy army is defeated not by counter-attack but by hospitality — the greatest possible inversion of military logic. The blinding / feeding / releasing sequence is the enacted parable of the greater-army theology: victory through unexpected means.

**Ephesians 1:18** — "Having the eyes of your hearts (*tous ophthalmous tēs kardias*) enlightened that you may know what is the hope to which he has called you." Paul's prayer for the Ephesians uses the *anoixon tous ophthalmous* structure of Elisha's prayer: the petition that the invisible reality — the surpassing greatness of divine power for those who believe — become perceptible. The *ophthalmoi kardias* = eyes of the heart is Paul's internalization of the Dothan-vision pattern.

**Romans 8:31** — "If God is for us who can be against us?" Paul's declaration is the theological summary of the Dothan theology: the *pleiones* = more-who-are-with-us principle stated as propositional fact. The mountain full of chariots of fire is the narrative ground for the Romans 8 confidence formula.

### 4 Kingdoms 13:20–21 — Elisha's bones raise the dead — CAE NODE

**13:21** — "And as a man was being buried look — a marauding band was seen and the man was thrown into the grave of Elisha. And as soon as the man touched the bones of Elisha he revived and stood on his feet."

***kai hēpsato tōn osteōn Elisaie kai ezēsen kai anestē epi tous podas autou***

= and he touched the bones of Elisha and he lived and stood on his feet. The third and most compressed of the resurrection-anticipation narratives in 4 Kingdoms: resurrection by contact with a dead prophet's bones — not a ritual / not a command / not sustained physical contact. A corpse thrown into a grave touches bones and stands up.

The theological structure is radical: the power of the prophetic *pneuma* persists even in death. Elisha's *pneuma* was transferred to him from Elijah (2:15 — *the pneuma of Elijah rests on Elisha*). That same *pneuma* operating through bones raises the dead.

**Matthew 27:52–53** — "The tombs also were opened. And many bodies of the saints who had fallen asleep were raised — and coming out of the tombs after his resurrection they went into the holy city and appeared to many." The resurrection of the saints from tombs at the crucifixion/resurrection of Jesus is the eschatological fulfillment of the Elisha-bones pattern: the prophetic *pneuma* raised a dead man at the tomb; the resurrection of the Christ raises the saints from their tombs. The *osteōn* = bones of Elisha / the *sōmata* = bodies of the saints — both raised by contact with the life-power of God operating through the dead.

**John 5:28–29** — "Do not marvel at this — for an hour is coming when all who are in the tombs will hear his voice and come out." Jesus's universal resurrection declaration is the theological generalization of the Elisha-bones narrative: what happened once at Elisha's tomb will happen to all in the tombs at the voice of the Son.

### 4 Kingdoms 17:7–23 — The theological verdict on the northern exile — CAE PRIMARY NODE

**17:13** — "Yet the Lord warned Israel and Judah by every prophet and every seer saying: Turn from your evil ways and keep my commandments and my statutes."

**17:14–15** — "But they would not listen but were stubborn as their fathers had been... They despised his statutes and his covenant."

**17:18** — "Therefore the Lord was very angry with Israel and removed them out of his sight."

The 4 Kingdoms 17 theological commentary is the most sustained OT prose analysis of the exile as covenant-consequence. The structure is catechetical: sin / warning / refusal to listen / judgment. The LXX *ouk ēkousan* = they did not hear (17:14) is the *akouein* failure — the hearing-heart that Samuel demanded / Solomon requested / the northern kings refused.

**The list of sins (17:7–17):** walking in the customs of the nations / building high places / setting up pillars and Asherim / serving idols / passing sons and daughters through fire / divination and omens. The full catalog of covenant-violation.

**Zechariah 7:11–13** — "But they refused to pay attention and turned a stubborn shoulder and stopped their ears that they might not hear. They made their hearts diamond-hard lest they should hear the law... As I called and they would not hear so they called and I would not hear says the Lord of hosts." Zechariah's post-exilic reflection uses the 4 Kingdoms 17 *ouk ēkousan* = they did not hear as the ground for the divine reciprocal non-hearing: as they refused to hear the prophets / so the Lord refused to hear them. The exile-logic operates at the hearing-level.

**Romans 1:18–32** — Paul's analysis of Gentile idolatry follows the 4 Kingdoms 17 structure: suppression of truth / exchanging the glory of God for images / God giving them over. The *paredōken autous* = God gave them over of Romans 1:24, 26, 28 is the NT equivalent of the 4 Kingdoms 17 *apestēsen* = the Lord removed them: both describe the judicial divine withdrawal from those who persistently refuse to hear.

### 4 Kingdoms 19:14–19 — Hezekiah spreads the letter before the Lord — CAE PRIMARY NODE

**19:14–15** — "Hezekiah received the letter from the hand of the messengers and read it — and Hezekiah went up to the house of the Lord and spread it before the Lord (*aneptyxen auto Hezekias enōpion Kyriou*). And Hezekiah prayed before the Lord and said: O Lord the God of Israel enthroned above the cherubim — you are the God — you alone — of all the kingdoms of the earth. You have made heaven and earth."

**19:19** — "So now O Lord our God save us please from his hand that all the kingdoms of the earth may know that you O Lord are God alone."

***hina gnōsin pasai hai basileiai tēs gēs hoti sy Kyrios ho Theos monos***

= that all the kingdoms of the earth may know that you Lord God are alone. The Hezekiah prayer is the most concentrated OT instance of the *sola Deo* = God alone theology: in the face of the greatest military threat Israel has faced Hezekiah spreads the letter before the Lord and prays not for military intervention but for divine vindication of the divine name before all kingdoms.

The act of *spreading the letter before the Lord* is unique in the OT: the physical document of threat placed before the divine presence. The prayer that follows is not a request for Hezekiah's survival but for the knowledge of God to spread to all kingdoms — missionary theology embedded in crisis prayer.

**Isaiah 37:14–20** — the parallel account in Isaiah is verbatim — the Hezekiah prayer appears in both 4 Kingdoms 19 and Isaiah 37. The double-attestation is intentional: the prayer is a text of the prophetic tradition, preserved in both the historical and prophetic collections.

**Acts 4:24–30** — the early church's prayer after Peter and John's release: "Sovereign Lord who made the heaven and the earth and the sea and everything in them... Look upon their threats and grant to your servants to continue to speak your word with all boldness." The Acts prayer uses the Hezekiah-address (*Sovereign Lord who made heaven and earth*) and the Hezekiah-method (spreading the threat before God / praying for divine action). The prayer-in-crisis pattern of Hezekiah is the NT church's prayer-pattern under persecution.

**Revelation 15:4** — "Who will not fear O Lord and glorify your name? For you alone are holy — for all nations will come and worship you." The *hina gnōsin pasai hai basileiai* = that all kingdoms may know of Hezekiah's prayer is the eschatological goal of the Revelation doxology: all nations worshipping the one who alone is holy.

### 4 Kingdoms 22–23 — Josiah / the Book of the Law found — CAE PRIMARY NODE

**22:8** — "Hilkiah the high priest said: I have found the Book of the Law in the house of the Lord."

**22:11** — "When the king heard the words of the Book of the Law he tore his clothes."

**23:3** — "And the king stood by the pillar and made a covenant before the Lord to walk after the Lord and to keep his commandments and his testimonies and his statutes with all his heart and with all his soul."

**23:25** — "Before him there was no king like him who turned to the Lord with all his heart and with all his soul and with all his might according to all the Law of Moses."

The Josiah narrative is the historical book's final great covenant-renewal event. The *Book of the Law* (*biblion tou nomou*) found in the Temple — almost certainly Deuteronomy or the core of it — is the discovered word that produces the greatest national covenant-renewal since Joshua. The king's response to the book: he tears his clothes (the response of one who recognizes judgment in what he hears) and immediately seeks prophetic confirmation (Huldah).

**Huldah the prophetess** (22:14) — the only instance in the historical books where a prophetess delivers the divine word that determines the fate of the nation. Her oracle: the judgment is certain / but for Josiah's penitent heart it will not come in his days. The *kardia* = heart is the criterion: Josiah's *kardia* was softened before the word; the nation's *kardia* is hard.

**Deuteronomy 17:18–20** — the king is commanded to write out a copy of the Torah and read it all the days of his life. Josiah is the first king in 4 Kingdoms to be explicitly described as doing what Deuteronomy demands of the king: he reads the book / he renews the covenant / he keeps the Passover as commanded. The 23:25 verdict (*no king turned to the Lord with all his heart and soul and might*) echoes the Shema (Deut 6:5 — *with all your heart and soul and might*). Josiah is the Shema-king.

**2 Corinthians 3:14–16** — "For to this day when they read the old covenant the same veil remains unlifted because only through Christ is it taken away. But when one turns to the Lord the veil is removed." Paul's veil-over-the-reading passage is the NT reflection on the 4 Kingdoms 22 scenario: the book read / the heart responding / the veil lifted. Josiah's tearing of his clothes = the veil removed / the word landing on the unprotected heart.

**Luke 24:27, 45** — "And beginning with Moses and all the Prophets he interpreted to them in all the Scriptures the things concerning himself... Then he opened their minds to understand the Scriptures." Jesus with the Emmaus disciples and the eleven is the ultimate Josiah-moment: the book found / the word heard / the heart burning (Luke 24:32 — *did not our hearts burn?*). The burning heart of the Emmaus disciples is the NT fulfillment of the tearing-of-clothes response to the found word.

### 4 Kingdoms 25:27–30 — Jehoiachin lifted from prison — CAE PRIMARY NODE

**25:27–30** — "In the thirty-seventh year of the exile of Jehoiachin king of Judah... Evil-merodach king of Babylon... graciously freed Jehoiachin king of Judah from prison. And he spoke kindly to him and gave him a seat above the seats of the kings who were with him in Babylon. So Jehoiachin put off his prison garments. And every day of his life he dined regularly at the king's table — and for his allowance a regular allowance was given him by the king — every day a portion — all the days of his life."

***kai ēllaxen ta imatia tēs phylakēs autou kai ēsthien arton enantion autou***

= and he changed his prison garments and ate bread before him. The book ends not with the burning of the Temple (25:9) but with the Davidic king at the Babylonian king's table — changed out of prison garments / fed daily / seated above other kings.

The theological function of this coda is profound: after the catastrophe of the Temple's destruction and Judah's exile the *lychnos* = lamp of David (1 Kgdms 11:36) is not extinguished. The Davidic king lives. He is fed. He is honored. He changes his prison clothes. The covenant with David has not been annulled — the lineage continues in Babylon.

**The Mephibosheth echo:** The structure of 25:27–30 deliberately echoes 2 Kgdms 9 (Mephibosheth at David's table): the one from the royal line who should have died / the unexpected invitation to eat at the king's table / the daily provision / the changed status. As David showed *eleos* to Mephibosheth for Jonathan's sake, Evil-merodach shows unexpected favor to Jehoiachin. The *eleos* pattern persists even in Babylonian exile.

**Jeremiah 52:31–34** — the identical episode closes the book of Jeremiah. The double-attestation in both 4 Kingdoms 25 and Jeremiah 52 signals the theological importance: the Davidic lineage preserved in exile is the horizon of hope for both the historical and prophetic traditions.

**Matthew 1:12** — "And after the deportation to Babylon: Jeconiah was the father of Shealtiel and Shealtiel the father of Zerubbabel." Matthew's genealogy traces the Messianic lineage through Jehoiachin (= Jeconiah) — the king who was in Babylonian prison and then was freed. The one who ate at Evil-merodach's table is in the direct ancestral chain of Jesus. The prison-garments changed / the king's table / the daily bread — the Exile does not break the covenant-chain; it runs through it.

**Revelation 3:20** — "Look — I stand at the door and knock. If anyone hears my voice and opens the door I will come in to him and eat with him and he with me." The *ariston* = meal-at-the-table of the Jehoiachin coda reverberates in the eschatological invitation of Revelation 3:20 — the ultimate version of the king's-table-as-covenant-restoration pattern. From Mephibosheth to Jehoiachin to the church in Laodicea: the invitation to eat at the king's table is the *eleos* pattern across the canon.

## VOCABULARY DECISIONS — 4 KINGDOMS

**Pneuma** — breath-of-life. *Diploûn en tō pneumati* = a doubling in your breath-of-life (2:9). *Pneuma Ēliou anapepautai epi Elisaie* = the breath-of-life of Elijah rests on Elisha (2:15). *Pneuma* consistently rendered throughout. The double-portion request and the *pneuma*-resting confirm the *pneuma* as the medium of prophetic-office transmission.

**Psychē** — soul/life. 4 Kingdoms uses *psychē* in the vital-life register in several passages: the *psychē* of children returned from death (1 Kgdms 17:22 / 4 Kgdms 4:34–35). The *psychē* = the life that departs and is restored.

**Sarx** — flesh. 5:14 — *hē sarx autou hōs sarx paidiou mikrou* = his flesh was like the flesh of a small child. The *sarx* restoration of Naaman is the only OT use of *sarx* in an explicit restoration/renewal context outside of Ezekiel 36:26 (the *sarx* promise of the new covenant). The connection is typological: the *sarx* of the Gentile leper restored / the *kardia* of stone replaced with *sarx* / the ultimate *sarx* renewal is the Incarnation.

**Klēronomia** — inheritance. Referenced through the Naboth narrative carried over from 3 Kingdoms — the *klēronomia* of Naboth's vineyard is the test case for covenant land-theology. The land *klēronomia* is ultimately lost in exile (4 Kgdms 25) — but the Davidic covenant-*klēronomia* persists in the person of Jehoiachin.

## STRUCTURAL NOTE — 4 KINGDOMS AS COVENANT FAILURE AND LAMPLIGHT IN EXILE

4 Kingdoms has a triple movement:

**Act 1 — Elijah/Elisha and the *pneuma* transferred (1–13):**
The prophetic succession / the miracles / the nations encounter Israel's God / Naaman / the mountain of chariots / Elisha's death and the resurrection at his tomb.

**Act 2 — The kingdoms decline and fall (14–21):**
The northern kingdom's fall to Assyria (17) / Hezekiah's prayer and deliverance (18–20) / Manasseh's abominations / the long slide of Judah toward judgment.

**Act 3 — Josiah and the catastrophe (22–25):**
The book of the Law found / the covenant renewed / the Passover restored / Josiah's death / the Babylonian siege / the Temple burned / the exile / Jehoiachin at the king's table.

**The theological spine across both acts:** The *akouein* = hearing-failure of 4 Kingdoms 17 (the northern kingdom) is matched by the *shema*-obedience of Josiah (23:25 — *with all his heart and soul and might*). But Josiah's obedience cannot undo Manasseh's sin (23:26) — individual covenant-faithfulness cannot cancel generational covenant-violation. The judgment falls despite the best king since David.

**The lamplight coda:** The book ends with Jehoiachin's release. The Davidic lamp (1 Kgdms 11:36 / 15:4 / 4 Kgdms 8:19) is still burning in Babylon. The final image of 4 Kingdoms — the king's table / the changed garments / the daily bread — is the horizon of hope on which the Messianic expectation rests. The four-book Kingdoms narrative (1–4 Basileiōn) ends not in darkness but in the whisper of covenant-continuity: the son of David eats bread at the king's table.

**Matthew 1:1** — "The book of the genealogy of Jesus Christ the son of David the son of Abraham." Matthew opens his Gospel by picking up precisely the thread 4 Kingdoms leaves dangling: the Davidic line in Babylon. The *genealogia* runs from Abraham through David through the exile through Jehoiachin to Jesus — the four books of Kingdoms are the narrative backdrop for the first verse of the New Testament.

\v 1 And it came to pass after the death of Moses the servant of the Lord that the Lord spoke to Joshua son of Nun — the attendant of Moses — saying:
\v 2 Moses my servant is dead — now therefore arise and cross over this Jordan — you and all this people — into the land that I am giving to them — to the sons of Israel.
\v 3 Every place on which the sole of your foot treads I have given to you — as I spoke to Moses.
\v 4 From the wilderness and this Lebanon as far as the great river — the river Euphrates — all the land of the Hittites — and as far as the Great Sea toward the going down of the sun — shall be your territory.
\v 5 No man shall be able to stand before you all the days of your life — as I was with Moses so I will be with you — I will not leave you nor forsake you.
\v 6 Be strong and courageous — for you shall cause this people to inherit the land that I swore to their fathers to give them.
\v 7 Only be strong and very courageous — being careful to do according to all the law that Moses my servant commanded you — do not turn from it to the right or to the left — that you may act wisely in all that you go.
\v 8 This book of the law shall not depart from your mouth but you shall meditate on it day and night — so that you may be careful to do according to all that is written in it — for then you shall make your way prosperous and then you shall act wisely.
\v 9 Have I not commanded you? Be strong and courageous — do not be frightened and do not be dismayed — for the Lord your God is with you wherever you go.
\p
\v 10 Then Joshua commanded the officers of the people saying:
\v 11 Pass through the midst of the camp and command the people saying: Prepare your provisions — for within three days you are to cross over this Jordan to go in to take possession of the land that the Lord your God is giving you to possess it.
\p
\v 12 And to the Reubenites and the Gadites and the half-tribe of Manasseh Joshua said:
\v 13 Remember the word that Moses the servant of the Lord commanded you saying: The Lord your God is giving you rest and will give you this land.
\v 14 Your wives — your little ones — and your livestock shall remain in the land that Moses gave you beyond the Jordan — but all your mighty men of valor shall cross over armed before your brothers and shall help them —
\v 15 until the Lord gives rest to your brothers as to you and they also take possession of the land that the Lord your God is giving them — then you shall return to the land of your possession and shall possess it — that which Moses the servant of the Lord gave you beyond the Jordan toward the sunrise.
\p
\v 16 And they answered Joshua saying: All that you have commanded us we will do and wherever you send us we will go.
\v 17 Just as we obeyed Moses in all things so we will obey you — only may the Lord your God be with you as he was with Moses.
\v 18 Whoever rebels against your commandment and does not obey your words in all that you command him shall be put to death — only be strong and courageous.

\v 1 And Joshua son of Nun sent two men secretly from Shittim as spies saying: Go and look at the land and Jericho. And they went and came into the house of a woman — a prostitute — whose name was Rahab — and they lodged there.
\v 2 And it was told to the king of Jericho saying: Look — men have come here tonight from the sons of Israel to search out the land.
\v 3 Then the king of Jericho sent to Rahab saying: Bring out the men who have come to you — who entered your house — for they have come to search out all the land.
\v 4 But the woman had taken the two men and hidden them — and she said: Yes — the men came to me but I did not know where they were from.
\v 5 And when the gate was about to be shut at dark the men went out — I do not know where the men went — pursue them quickly for you may overtake them.
\v 6 But she had brought them up to the roof and hidden them with the stalks of flax that she had laid in order on the roof.
\v 7 So the men pursued after them on the way to the Jordan as far as the fords — and as soon as those who pursued them had gone out the gate was shut.
\p
\v 8 Before they lay down she came up to them on the roof —
\v 9 and said to the men: I know that the Lord has given you the land — and that the fear of you has fallen upon us — and that all the inhabitants of the land melt away before you.
\v 10 For we have heard how the Lord dried up the water of the Red Sea before you when you came out of Egypt — and what you did to the two kings of the Amorites who were beyond the Jordan — to Sihon and Og — whom you devoted to destruction.
\v 11 And as soon as we heard it our hearts melted and there was no breath left in any man because of you — for the Lord your God — he is God in the heavens above and on the earth beneath.
\v 12 Now then — please swear to me by the Lord — since I have dealt kindly with you — that you also will deal kindly with my father's house — and give me a sure sign —
\v 13 that you will save alive my father and mother — my brothers and sisters — and all who belong to them — and deliver our lives from death.
\v 14 And the men said to her: Our life for yours even to death — if you do not tell this business of ours — then when the Lord gives us the land we will deal kindly and faithfully with you.
\p
\v 15 Then she let them down by a rope through the window — for her house was built into the city wall so that she lived in the wall.
\v 16 And she said to them: Go into the hills or the pursuers will meet you — and hide there three days until the pursuers have returned — then afterward you may go your way.
\v 17 The men said to her: We will be guiltless with respect to this oath of yours that you have made us swear.
\v 18 Look — when we come into the land — you shall tie this scarlet cord in the window through which you let us down — and you shall gather into your house your father and mother — your brothers and all your father's household.
\v 19 Then if anyone goes out of the doors of your house into the street — his blood shall be on his own head and we shall be guiltless — but if a hand is laid on anyone who is with you in the house — his blood shall be on our head.
\v 20 But if you tell this business of ours — then we shall be guiltless with respect to your oath that you have made us swear.
\v 21 And she said: According to your words so be it. Then she sent them away and they departed — and she tied the scarlet cord in the window.
\p
\v 22 They departed and went into the hills and remained there three days until the pursuers returned — and the pursuers searched all along the way and found nothing.
\v 23 Then the two men returned — they came down from the hills and crossed over and came to Joshua son of Nun — and they told him all that had happened to them.
\v 24 And they said to Joshua: Truly the Lord has given all the land into our hands — and also all the inhabitants of the land melt away before us.

\v 1 Then Joshua rose early in the morning and they set out from Shittim and came to the Jordan — he and all the sons of Israel — and they lodged there before they crossed over.
\v 2 At the end of three days the officers passed through the midst of the camp —
\v 3 and they commanded the people saying: When you see the ark of the covenant of the Lord your God being carried by the Levitical priests — then you shall set out from your place and follow it.
\v 4 Yet there shall be a distance between you and it — about two thousand cubits in length — do not come near it — that you may know the way you shall go — for you have not passed this way before.
\v 5 Then Joshua said to the people: Consecrate yourselves — for tomorrow the Lord will do wonders among you.
\v 6 And Joshua said to the priests: Take up the ark of the covenant and cross over before the people. So they took up the ark of the covenant and went before the people.
\p
\v 7 The Lord said to Joshua: Today I will begin to exalt you in the sight of all Israel — that they may know that as I was with Moses so I will be with you.
\v 8 And as for you — command the priests who bear the ark of the covenant saying: When you come to the brink of the waters of the Jordan — you shall stand still in the Jordan.
\p
\v 9 And Joshua said to the sons of Israel: Come here and hear the words of the Lord your God.
\v 10 And Joshua said: By this you shall know that the living God is among you and that he will without fail drive out from before you the Canaanites and the Hittites and the Hivites and the Perizzites and the Girgashites and the Amorites and the Jebusites.
\v 11 Look — the ark of the covenant of the Lord of all the earth is crossing over before you into the Jordan.
\v 12 Now therefore take twelve men from the tribes of Israel — one man from each tribe.
\v 13 And when the soles of the feet of the priests bearing the ark of the Lord — the Lord of all the earth — shall rest in the waters of the Jordan — the waters of the Jordan shall be cut off from flowing — and the waters coming down from above shall stand in one heap.
\p
\v 14 So when the people set out from their tents to cross over the Jordan — with the priests bearing the ark of the covenant before the people —
\v 15 and as soon as those bearing the ark had come as far as the Jordan and the feet of the priests bearing the ark were dipped in the brink of the water — now the Jordan overflows all its banks throughout the time of harvest —
\v 16 the waters coming down from above stood and rose up in a heap very far away — at Adam the city that is beside Zarethan — and those flowing down toward the Sea of the Arabah — the Salt Sea — were completely cut off — and the people crossed over opposite Jericho.
\v 17 Now the priests bearing the ark of the covenant of the Lord stood firm on dry ground in the midst of the Jordan — and all Israel was crossing over on dry ground — until all the nation finished crossing over the Jordan.

\v 1 When all the nation had finished crossing over the Jordan — the Lord said to Joshua:
\v 2 Take twelve men from the people — one man from each tribe —
\v 3 and command them saying: Take twelve stones from here out of the midst of the Jordan — from the very place where the priests' feet stood firmly — and carry them over with you and lay them down in the place where you lodge tonight.
\p
\v 4 Then Joshua called the twelve men whom he had appointed from the sons of Israel — one man from each tribe —
\v 5 and Joshua said to them: Cross over before the ark of the Lord your God into the midst of the Jordan and take up each of you a stone upon his shoulder — according to the number of the tribes of the sons of Israel —
\v 6 that this may be a sign among you — when your children ask in time to come saying: What do these stones mean to you? —
\v 7 then you shall tell them that the waters of the Jordan were cut off before the ark of the covenant of the Lord — when it crossed over the Jordan the waters of the Jordan were cut off — so these stones shall be to the sons of Israel a memorial forever.
\p
\v 8 And the sons of Israel did just as Joshua commanded and took up twelve stones out of the midst of the Jordan according to the number of the tribes of the sons of Israel — as the Lord told Joshua — and they carried them over with them to the place where they lodged and laid them down there.
\v 9 And Joshua set up twelve stones in the midst of the Jordan in the place where the feet of the priests bearing the ark of the covenant had stood — and they are there to this day.
\v 10 For the priests bearing the ark stood in the midst of the Jordan until everything was finished that the Lord commanded Joshua to tell the people — according to all that Moses had commanded Joshua — and the people hurried and crossed over.
\v 11 And when all the people had finished crossing over — the ark of the Lord and the priests crossed over before the people.
\v 12 The sons of Reuben and the sons of Gad and the half-tribe of Manasseh crossed over armed before the sons of Israel — as Moses had told them.
\v 13 About forty thousand ready for war crossed over before the Lord to the plains of Jericho for battle.
\v 14 On that day the Lord exalted Joshua in the sight of all Israel — and they stood in awe of him just as they had stood in awe of Moses all the days of his life.
\p
\v 15 And the Lord said to Joshua:
\v 16 Command the priests bearing the ark of the testimony to come up out of the Jordan.
\v 17 So Joshua commanded the priests saying: Come up out of the Jordan.
\v 18 And when the priests bearing the ark of the covenant of the Lord came up from the midst of the Jordan and the soles of the priests' feet were lifted up on dry ground — the waters of the Jordan returned to their place and overflowed all its banks as before.
\p
\v 19 The people came up out of the Jordan on the tenth day of the first month — and they camped at Gilgal on the east border of Jericho.
\v 20 And those twelve stones which they had taken out of the Jordan — Joshua set up at Gilgal.
\v 21 And he said to the sons of Israel: When your children ask their fathers in time to come saying: What are these stones? —
\v 22 then you shall let your children know: Israel passed over this Jordan on dry ground.
\v 23 For the Lord your God dried up the waters of the Jordan before you until you passed over — as the Lord your God did to the Red Sea which he dried up before us until we passed over —
\v 24 so that all the peoples of the earth may know that the hand of the Lord is mighty — and so that you may fear the Lord your God forever.

\v 1 As soon as all the kings of the Amorites who were beyond the Jordan to the west and all the kings of the Canaanites who were by the sea heard that the Lord had dried up the waters of the Jordan before the sons of Israel until they had crossed over — their hearts melted and there was no longer any breath in them because of the sons of Israel.
\p
\v 2 At that time the Lord said to Joshua: Make flint knives and circumcise the sons of Israel a second time.
\v 3 So Joshua made flint knives and circumcised the sons of Israel at Gibeath-haaraloth.
\v 4 And this is the reason Joshua circumcised them: all the males of the people who came out of Egypt — all the men of war — had died in the wilderness on the way after they came out of Egypt.
\v 5 Though all the people who came out had been circumcised — yet all the people who were born on the way in the wilderness after they came out of Egypt had not been circumcised.
\v 6 For the sons of Israel walked forty years in the wilderness until all the nation — the men of war who came out of Egypt — perished because they did not obey the voice of the Lord — the Lord swore to them that he would not let them see the land that the Lord swore to their fathers to give to us — a land flowing with milk and honey.
\v 7 So it was their children whom he raised up in their place that Joshua circumcised — for they were uncircumcised — because they had not been circumcised on the way.
\v 8 When the circumcising of the whole nation was finished they remained in their places in the camp until they were healed.
\v 9 And the Lord said to Joshua: Today I have rolled away the reproach of Egypt from you. And so the name of that place is called Gilgal to this day.
\p
\v 10 While the sons of Israel were camped at Gilgal they kept the Passover on the fourteenth day of the month in the evening on the plains of Jericho.
\v 11 And the day after the Passover — on that very day — they ate of the produce of the land — unleavened cakes and parched grain.
\v 12 And the manna ceased the day after they ate of the produce of the land — and there was no longer manna for the sons of Israel but they ate of the fruit of the land of Canaan that year.
\p
\v 13 When Joshua was by Jericho he lifted up his eyes and looked and — look — a man was standing before him with his drawn sword in his hand — and Joshua went to him and said to him: Are you for us or for our adversaries?
\v 14 And he said: No — but I am the commander of the army of the Lord — now I have come. And Joshua fell on his face to the earth and worshipped and said to him: What does my lord say to his servant?
\v 15 And the commander of the Lord's army said to Joshua: Take off your sandals from your feet — for the place where you are standing is holy. And Joshua did so.

\p
\v 1 Now Jericho was shut up inside and outside because of the sons of Israel — none went out and none came in.
\v 2 And the Lord said to Joshua: See — I have given Jericho into your hand — with its king and mighty men of valor.
\v 3 You shall march around the city — all the men of war going around the city once — thus you shall do for six days.
\v 4 And seven priests shall bear seven trumpets of rams' horns before the ark — and on the seventh day you shall march around the city seven times and the priests shall blow the trumpets.
\v 5 And when they make a long blast with the ram's horn — when you hear the sound of the trumpet — all the people shall shout with a great shout — and the wall of the city will fall down flat — and the people shall go up — everyone straight before him.
\p
\v 6 So Joshua son of Nun called the priests and said to them: Take up the ark of the covenant and let seven priests bear seven trumpets of rams' horns before the ark of the Lord.
\v 7 And he said to the people: Go forward — march around the city and let the armed men pass on before the ark of the Lord.
\p
\v 8 And just as Joshua had commanded the people — the seven priests bearing the seven trumpets of rams' horns before the Lord went forward blowing the trumpets — and the ark of the covenant of the Lord followed them.
\v 9 The armed men were walking before the priests who were blowing the trumpets — and the rear guard was walking after the ark while the trumpets blew continually.
\v 10 But Joshua commanded the people: You shall not shout or make your voice heard — neither shall any word come out of your mouth until the day I tell you to shout — then you shall shout.
\v 11 So he caused the ark of the Lord to circle the city — going around it once — and they came into the camp and spent the night in the camp.
\p
\v 12 Then Joshua rose early in the morning and the priests took up the ark of the Lord.
\v 13 And the seven priests bearing the seven trumpets of rams' horns before the ark of the Lord walked on and blew the trumpets continually — and the armed men were walking before them — and the rear guard was walking after the ark of the Lord while the trumpets blew continually.
\v 14 And the second day they marched around the city once and returned into the camp — so they did for six days.
\p
\v 15 On the seventh day they rose early at the dawn of day and marched around the city in the same manner seven times — it was only on that day that they marched around the city seven times.
\v 16 And at the seventh time when the priests had blown the trumpets Joshua said to the people: Shout — for the Lord has given you the city.
\v 17 And the city and all that is within it shall be devoted to the Lord for destruction — only Rahab the prostitute and all who are with her in her house shall live — because she hid the messengers whom we sent.
\v 18 But you — keep yourselves from the things devoted to destruction — lest when you have devoted them you take any of the devoted things and make the camp of Israel a thing for destruction and bring trouble upon it.
\v 19 But all silver and gold and vessels of bronze and iron are holy to the Lord — they shall go into the treasury of the Lord.
\p
\v 20 So the people shouted and the trumpets were blown — as soon as the people heard the sound of the trumpet the people shouted with a great shout and the wall fell down flat — and the people went up into the city every man straight before him and they captured the city.
\v 21 Then they devoted all in the city to destruction — both men and women — young and old — oxen — sheep and donkeys — with the edge of the sword.
\p
\v 22 But to the two men who had spied out the land Joshua said: Go into the prostitute's house and bring out from there the woman and all who belong to her — as you swore to her.
\v 23 So the young men who had been spies went in and brought out Rahab and her father and mother and brothers and all who belonged to her — and they brought out all her relatives and put them outside the camp of Israel.
\v 24 And they burned the city with fire and everything in it — only the silver and gold and the vessels of bronze and of iron they put into the treasury of the house of the Lord.
\v 25 But Rahab the prostitute and her father's household and all who belonged to her — Joshua saved alive — and she has lived in the midst of Israel to this day — because she hid the messengers whom Joshua sent to spy out Jericho.
\p
\v 26 Joshua laid an oath on them at that time saying: Cursed before the Lord be the man who rises up and rebuilds this city Jericho — at the cost of his firstborn shall he lay its foundation — and at the cost of his youngest son shall he set up its gates.
\v 27 So the Lord was with Joshua and his fame was in all the land.

\c 7
\p
\v 1 But the sons of Israel acted treacherously with respect to the devoted thing — Achar son of Carmi son of Zabdi son of Zerah from the tribe of Judah took from the devoted things — and the anger of the Lord burned against the sons of Israel.
\p
\v 2 Joshua sent men from Jericho to Ai — which is beside Beth-aven east of Bethel — saying: Go up and spy out the land. And the men went up and spied out Ai.
\v 3 And they returned to Joshua and said to him: Do not send all the people — let about two or three thousand men go up and strike down Ai — do not make all the people labor up there — for they are few.
\v 4 So about three thousand men from the people went up there — and they fled before the men of Ai.
\v 5 And the men of Ai struck down about thirty-six of them — chasing them from before the gate as far as Shebarim and struck them down on the descent — and the hearts of the people melted and became like water.
\p
\v 6 Then Joshua tore his clothes and fell to the earth on his face before the ark of the Lord until the evening — he and the elders of Israel — and they put dust on their heads.
\v 7 And Joshua said: Alas O Lord God — why have you brought this people over the Jordan at all — to give us into the hands of the Amorites to destroy us? Would that we had been content to dwell beyond the Jordan.
\v 8 O Lord — what can I say — when Israel has turned its back before its enemies?
\v 9 For the Canaanites and all the inhabitants of the land will hear of it and will surround us and cut off our name from the earth — and what will you do for your great name?
\p
\v 10 The Lord said to Joshua: Get up — why have you fallen on your face?
\v 11 Israel has sinned — they have transgressed my covenant that I commanded them — they have taken some of the devoted things — they have stolen and lied and put them among their own belongings.
\v 12 Therefore the sons of Israel cannot stand before their enemies — they turn their backs before their enemies because they have become devoted for destruction — I will be with you no more unless you destroy the devoted things from among you.
\v 13 Get up — consecrate the people and say: Consecrate yourselves for tomorrow — for thus says the Lord God of Israel: There are devoted things in your midst O Israel — you cannot stand before your enemies until you take away the devoted things from among you.
\v 14 In the morning you shall come near by your tribes — and the tribe that the Lord takes by lot shall come near by clans — and the clan that the Lord takes shall come near by households — and the household that the Lord takes shall come near man by man.
\v 15 And he who is taken with the devoted things shall be burned with fire — he and all that he has — because he has transgressed the covenant of the Lord and because he has done an outrageous thing in Israel.
\p
\v 16 So Joshua rose early in the morning and brought Israel near tribe by tribe — and the tribe of Judah was taken.
\v 17 And he brought near the clans of Judah and the clan of the Zerahites was taken — and he brought near the clan of the Zerahites man by man and Zabdi was taken.
\v 18 And he brought near his household man by man and Achar son of Carmi son of Zabdi son of Zerah of the tribe of Judah was taken.
\p
\v 19 Then Joshua said to Achar: My son — give glory to the Lord God of Israel and give praise to him — and tell me now what you have done — do not hide it from me.
\v 20 And Achar answered Joshua and said: Truly I have sinned against the Lord God of Israel — and this is what I did:
\v 21 When I saw among the spoil a beautiful cloak from Shinar and two hundred shekels of silver and a bar of gold weighing fifty shekels — then I coveted them and took them — and look — they are hidden in the earth inside my tent with the silver underneath.
\p
\v 22 So Joshua sent messengers and they ran to the tent — and look — it was hidden in his tent with the silver underneath.
\v 23 And they took them out of the tent and brought them to Joshua and to all the sons of Israel — and they laid them down before the Lord.
\v 24 And Joshua and all Israel with him took Achar son of Zerah and the silver and the cloak and the bar of gold and his sons and daughters and his oxen and donkeys and sheep and his tent and all that he had — and they brought them up to the Valley of Achor.
\v 25 And Joshua said: Why did you bring trouble on us? The Lord brings trouble on you today. And all Israel stoned him with stones — and they burned them with fire and stoned them with stones.
\v 26 And they raised over him a great heap of stones that remains to this day — then the Lord turned from his fierce anger. Therefore to this day the name of that place is called the Valley of Achor.

\c 8
\p
\v 1 And the Lord said to Joshua: Do not fear and do not be dismayed — take all the fighting men with you and arise and go up to Ai — see — I have given into your hand the king of Ai — his people — his city and his land.
\v 2 And you shall do to Ai and its king as you did to Jericho and its king — only its spoil and its livestock you shall take as plunder for yourselves — set an ambush against the city behind it.
\p
\v 3 So Joshua arose and all the fighting men to go up to Ai — and Joshua chose thirty thousand mighty men of valor and sent them out by night.
\v 4 And he commanded them saying: Look — you shall lie in ambush against the city behind it — do not go very far from the city — all of you be ready.
\v 5 And I and all the people who are with me will approach the city — and when they come out against us as at first we will flee before them.
\v 6 And they will come out after us until we have drawn them away from the city — for they will say: They are fleeing from us as at first — so we will flee before them.
\v 7 Then you shall rise up from the ambush and seize the city — for the Lord your God will give it into your hand.
\v 8 And as soon as you have taken the city you shall set the city on fire — you shall do according to the word of the Lord — see — I have commanded you.
\p
\v 9 So Joshua sent them out and they went to the place of ambush and lay between Bethel and Ai to the west of Ai — but Joshua spent that night among the people.
\p
\v 10 Joshua rose early in the morning and mustered the people and went up — he and the elders of Israel — before the people to Ai.
\v 11 And all the fighting men who were with him went up and drew near and came before the city and camped on the north side of Ai — with a ravine between them and Ai.
\v 12 He took about five thousand men and set them in ambush between Bethel and Ai to the west of the city.
\v 13 So they stationed the forces — the main camp that was north of the city and its rear guard west of the city — and Joshua went that night into the midst of the valley.
\v 14 And as soon as the king of Ai saw it he hurried and rose early and the men of the city went out to meet Israel for battle — he and all his people — at the appointed place before the Arabah — but he did not know that there was an ambush against him behind the city.
\v 15 And Joshua and all Israel pretended to be beaten before them and fled in the direction of the wilderness.
\v 16 So all the people who were in Ai were called together to pursue them — and they pursued Joshua and were drawn away from the city.
\v 17 There was not a man left in Ai or Bethel who did not go out after Israel — they left the city open and pursued Israel.
\p
\v 18 Then the Lord said to Joshua: Stretch out the javelin that is in your hand toward Ai — for I will give it into your hand. And Joshua stretched out the javelin that was in his hand toward the city.
\v 19 And the ambush rose quickly out of their place and they ran as soon as he stretched out his hand — and they entered the city and captured it — and they hurried to set the city on fire.
\v 20 So when the men of Ai looked back they saw — and look — the smoke of the city went up to heaven and they had no power to flee this way or that way — for the people who fled to the wilderness turned back against the pursuers.
\v 21 And when Joshua and all Israel saw that the ambush had captured the city and that the smoke of the city went up — they turned back and struck down the men of Ai.
\v 22 And the others came out from the city against them — so they were in the midst of Israel — some on this side and some on that side — and Israel struck them down until there was left none that survived or escaped.
\v 23 But the king of Ai they took alive and brought him near to Joshua.
\p
\v 24 When Israel had finished killing all the inhabitants of Ai in the open wilderness where they pursued them — and all of them to the very last had fallen by the edge of the sword — all Israel returned to Ai and struck it down with the edge of the sword.
\v 25 And all who fell that day — both men and women — were twelve thousand — all the people of Ai.
\v 26 For Joshua did not draw back his hand with which he stretched out the javelin until he had devoted all the inhabitants of Ai to destruction.
\v 27 Only the livestock and the spoil of that city Israel took as plunder for themselves — according to the word of the Lord that he commanded Joshua.
\v 28 And Joshua burned Ai and made it forever a heap of ruins — a desolation to this day.
\v 29 And he hanged the king of Ai on a tree until evening — and at sunset Joshua commanded and they took his body down from the tree and threw it at the entrance of the gate of the city and raised over it a great heap of stones that remains to this day.
\p
\v 30 At that time Joshua built an altar to the Lord God of Israel on Mount Ebal —
\v 31 just as Moses the servant of the Lord had commanded the sons of Israel — as it is written in the book of the law of Moses — an altar of uncut stones upon which no man has wielded an iron tool — and they offered on it burnt offerings to the Lord and sacrificed peace offerings.
\v 32 And there in the presence of the sons of Israel he wrote on the stones a copy of the law of Moses which he had written.
\v 33 And all Israel — sojourner as well as native born — with their elders and officers and their judges stood on either side of the ark before the Levitical priests who carried the ark of the covenant of the Lord — half of them in front of Mount Gerizim and half of them in front of Mount Ebal — just as Moses the servant of the Lord had commanded at the first — to bless the people of Israel.
\v 34 And afterward he read all the words of the law — the blessing and the curse — according to all that is written in the book of the law.
\v 35 There was not a word of all that Moses commanded that Joshua did not read before all the assembly of Israel — and the women and the little ones and the sojourners who lived among them.

\v 1 As soon as all the kings who were beyond the Jordan — in the hill country and in the lowland and all the coast of the Great Sea toward Lebanon — the Hittites the Amorites the Canaanites the Perizzites the Hivites and the Jebusites — heard of these things —
\v 2 they gathered together as one to fight against Joshua and Israel.

## CRITICAL TEXTUAL NOTE — LXX JOSHUA

The LXX Joshua differs significantly from the MT in several respects. The LXX version is notably shorter — approximately 4–5% less material — and contains different ordering in the land-allotment chapters. The LXX also preserves some older readings at the Ai and Gibeon narratives. The Rahlfs-Hanhart LXX is the base; MT divergences are noted where significant.

**LXX name variant:** The villain of chapter 7 is *Achar* in the LXX (not *Achan* as in the MT). The GLE preserves the LXX form throughout. The LXX *Achar* = "trouble" — the word-play on the Valley of Achor (*achar* = trouble) is preserved in the LXX form.

## CAE NODES — JOSHUA

### Joshua 1:5 — *ouk enkatalipsō se* / I will not leave you nor forsake you — CAE NODE

**1:5** — "I will not leave you nor forsake you."

***ouk enkatalipsō se oude hypereiromai sou*** = I will not abandon you nor look away from you. The divine commissioning promise to Joshua as Moses's successor.

**Hebrews 13:5** — "I will never leave you nor forsake you (*ou mē se anō oude ou mē se enkatalipō*)." The Hebrews citation fuses Deut 31:6 and Joshua 1:5 — the same promise that commissioned Joshua now grounds NT ethical exhortation against love of money. The logic: the one who never abandons is sufficient provision.

**Matthew 28:20** — "I am with you always to the end of the age." The Great Commission closes with the Mosaic/Joshuaic commissioning formula reversed: Joshua was told "as I was with Moses so I will be with you" — Jesus tells his disciples "I am with you" as the new Joshua who leads into the true inheritance.

---

### Joshua 1:8 — *biblos tou nomou* / book of the law — CAE NODE

**1:8** — "This book of the law shall not depart from your mouth but you shall meditate on it day and night."

***ouk apostēsetai ho biblos tou nomou toutou ek tou stomatos sou*** = this book of the law shall not depart from your mouth. The word-meditation command — continuous oral engagement with Torah as the condition for wise action.

**Psalm 1:2** — "but his delight is in the law of the Lord and on his law he meditates day and night." The Joshua 1:8 day-and-night meditation command is virtually verbatim with Psalm 1:2 — the two texts together define the Israelite ideal of the man shaped by Torah.

**Revelation 1:3** — "Blessed is the one who reads aloud (*anaginōskōn*) the words of this prophecy." The oral/public reading of the word as the condition for blessing — the Joshuaic pattern extended to apocalyptic scripture.

---

### Joshua 2 — Rahab typology — CAE PRIMARY NODE

**2:9–11** — "I know that the Lord has given you the land... for the Lord your God — he is God in the heavens above and on the earth beneath."

**2:18** — the scarlet cord (*speirion to kokkinon*) = scarlet thread in the window.

***Rhaab hē pornē*** = Rahab the prostitute. The Gentile woman outside the covenant who confesses the Lord's sovereignty and is saved by a scarlet sign.

**Hebrews 11:31** — "By faith Rahab the prostitute did not perish with those who were disobedient because she had given a friendly welcome to the spies." Rahab in the faith hall of fame — the paradigmatic Gentile who enters the covenant by faith-action.

**James 2:25** — "And in the same way was not also Rahab the prostitute justified by works when she received the messengers and sent them out by another way?" James deploys Rahab alongside Abraham (2:21) as the paired OT demonstration that faith without works is dead — the faith of Rahab was operative faith.

**Matthew 1:5** — "Salmon the father of Boaz by Rahab." Rahab in the Matthean genealogy of Jesus — one of the four women named (Tamar / Rahab / Ruth / Bathsheba) — all Gentile-adjacent, all anomalous, all part of the Messianic line.

**The scarlet cord** (*speirion kokkinon*): patristic interpretation consistently reads the scarlet cord as a type of the blood of Christ — the sign that protects the household from destruction when judgment comes. **Joshua 2:18** → **Exodus 12:13** (Passover blood on doorposts) → the Passover-Rahab typological link. The cord in the window = the blood on the lintel.

---

### Joshua 3–4 — Jordan crossing / twelve stones — CAE NODE

**3:17** — "The priests bearing the ark of the covenant of the Lord stood firm on dry ground in the midst of the Jordan — and all Israel was crossing over on dry ground."

**4:6–7** — "When your children ask in time to come: What do these stones mean to you? — then you shall tell them that the waters of the Jordan were cut off before the ark of the covenant of the Lord."

The Jordan crossing is a deliberate Exodus typology: dry-ground crossing / twelve stones / memorial for future generations. The number twelve = twelve tribes = the twelve apostles. **Matthew 19:28** — "you who have followed me will also sit on twelve thrones judging the twelve tribes of Israel." The twelve-stone memorial at Gilgal → the twelve-apostle foundation of the new Israel.

**1 Corinthians 10:1–2** — "our fathers were all under the cloud and all passed through the sea and all were baptized into Moses in the cloud and in the sea." Paul reads both the Red Sea and Jordan crossings as baptism typologies — water-passage as covenant entry.

### Joshua 5:13–15 — commander of the Lord's army — CAE PRIMARY NODE

**5:13–15** — "A man was standing before him with his drawn sword in his hand... and he said: No — but I am the commander of the army of the Lord — now I have come. And Joshua fell on his face to the earth and worshipped... Take off your sandals from your feet — for the place where you are standing is holy."

***archēstrategōs dynameōs Kyriou*** = commander-in-chief of the army of the Lord. The figure accepts worship and declares the ground holy — the same holiness-ground declaration as the burning bush (Exodus 3:5). A figure that accepts worship and speaks with divine authority.

This is one of the most significant *divine messenger who accepts worship* passages in the OT. The LXX *archēstrategōs* = commander-in-chief / supreme military commander — not simply an angel of lower rank.

**Revelation 19:11–14** — "Then I saw heaven opened and look — a white horse — and the one sitting on it is called Faithful and True... the armies of heaven arrayed in fine linen were following him on white horses." The rider who commands the heavenly armies in Revelation 19 is the eschatological fulfillment of the Joshuaic *archēstrategōs* — the same figure now revealed as the Word of God (19:13).

The parallel with the Exodus theophany is exact: burning bush (Ex 3:5) / Jericho theophany (Josh 5:15) — same sandals-off command, same holy ground, same figure. Both precede a decisive act of divine liberation/judgment.

### Joshua 6 — Jericho / the shout — CAE NODE

**6:20** — "The people shouted with a great shout and the wall fell down flat."

The sevenfold march / seven priests / seven days / seventh day seven times — the number seven structures the entire Jericho narrative. **Revelation 8:1–2** — seven trumpets / seven angels — the Jericho trumpet-blast pattern governs the Revelation trumpet sequence. The walls of the present world-order fall at the seventh trumpet as Jericho's walls fell at the seventh blast.

**6:25** — "Rahab the prostitute... Joshua saved alive... because she hid the messengers." The *sōzō* = save alive of Joshua 6:25 = the salvation vocabulary that Hebrews 11:31 and James 2:25 deploy.

### Joshua 7 — Achar / devoted thing / corporate solidarity — CAE NODE

**7:1** — "The sons of Israel acted treacherously with respect to the devoted thing — Achar... took from the devoted things — and the anger of the Lord burned against the sons of Israel."

***ēnomēsan... apo tou anathematos*** = they acted lawlessly... from the devoted thing. The individual sin of Achar generates corporate judgment — "the anger of the Lord burned against the sons of Israel" — not against Achar alone. The corporate solidarity principle: one member's covenant breach implicates the whole body.

**1 Corinthians 5:6** — "Do you not know that a little leaven leavens the whole lump?" Paul's argument for removing the immoral man from the community follows the Achar logic precisely — the *anathema* in the camp pollutes the whole camp until removed. The Achar narrative is the OT ground for Paul's corporate-holiness ecclesiology.

**Romans 5:12** — "sin came into the world through one man and death through sin and so death spread to all men." The one-man-affects-all structure of Romans 5 (Adam / Achar / Christ) runs on the same corporate-solidarity logic that Joshua 7 instantiates narratively.

### Joshua 10:12–14 — sun stands still — CAE NODE

**10:12–13** — "Sun — stand still at Gibeon — and moon — in the Valley of Aijalon... There has been no day like it before or since — when the Lord heeded the voice of a man — for the Lord fought for Israel."

***stēthi hēlie kata Gabaon*** = stand still O sun over Gibeon. The Lord who fights for Israel suspends the natural order at human petition — the most dramatic divine-intervention-in-battle narrative in the OT.

**Habakkuk 3:11** — "The sun and moon stood still in their place at the light of your arrows as they sped at the flash of your glittering spear." Habakkuk's theophany-hymn recalls the Gibeon event as the paradigm of divine warrior action.

The theological weight: **the Lord fights for Israel** (10:14 — *Kyrios gar synepolemei tō Israēl*). This becomes the governing war-theology of the OT — not Israel's military prowess but divine combat on Israel's behalf. **Romans 8:31** — "If God is for us who can be against us?" — the NT distillation of the Joshuaic war-theology.

### Joshua 21:43–45 — not one word failed — CAE PRIMARY NODE

**21:43–45** — "Thus the Lord gave to Israel all the land that he swore to give to their fathers... not one word of all the good words that the Lord had spoken to the house of Israel had failed — all came to pass."

***ou diepesen apo pantōn tōn rhēmatōn tōn kalōn hōn elalēsen Kyrios*** = not one fell from all the good words that the Lord spoke. The land-gift fulfillment declaration — every promise made to the patriarchs has been kept.

**1 Kings 8:56** — Solomon at the Temple dedication: "Blessed be the Lord... not one word has failed of all his good promise which he spoke through Moses his servant." Solomon's declaration is a direct echo of Joshua 21:45.

**2 Corinthians 1:20** — "For all the promises of God find their Yes in him (*en autō to nai*)." Paul's *pantōn ton epangeliōn... to nai* = all promises Yes in Christ is the NT fulfillment of the Joshuaic *not one word failed* — the final accounting of divine faithfulness.

**Hebrews 4:1–11** — the Hebrews argument that Joshua's rest was not the final rest (*katapausis*) — the Joshua land-rest was real but pointed forward to a greater rest still to come. **Hebrews 4:8** — "For if Joshua had given them rest God would not have spoken of another day later on." The Joshua fulfillment is genuine but typological — it is the shadow of a greater fulfillment.

### Joshua 24:15 — as for me and my house — CAE NODE

**24:15** — "Choose this day whom you will serve... but as for me and my house — we will serve the Lord."

***egō de kai hē oikia mou latreusomen Kyriō*** = but I and my household will serve the Lord. The covenant-choice declaration — the most compressed summary of covenant loyalty in the historical books.

**Acts 16:31** — "Believe in the Lord Jesus and you will be saved — you and your household (*sy kai ho oikos sou*)." The Philippian jailer promise echoes the Joshuaic household-covenant structure — salvation that extends to the household unit.

### Joshua 24:26–27 — the stone as witness — CAE NODE

**24:26–27** — "Joshua wrote these words in the book of the law of God... and he took a large stone and set it up there under the terebinth... and Joshua said: Look — this stone shall be a witness against us — for it has heard all the words of the Lord that he spoke to us."

***ho lithos houtos estai en hymin eis martyrion*** = this stone shall be among you for a witness. The covenant-memorial stone as legal witness.

**Luke 19:40** — "I tell you — if these were silent the very stones would cry out." Jesus's declaration that stones testify echoes the Joshuaic covenant-stone theology — creation itself as witness to divine speech.

**Revelation 2:17** — "I will give him a white stone with a new name written on it that no one knows except the one who receives it." The stone as covenant-identity marker — the Joshuaic witness-stone transformed into the eschatological identity-stone.

## VOCABULARY DECISIONS — JOSHUA

### *Anathema / anathematizō* — devoted thing / devote to destruction
Rendered "devoted thing" and "devote to destruction" throughout — never "ban" or "curse" alone. The *anathema* = the thing set apart entirely for the Lord — whether for consecration (positive) or destruction (negative). In the conquest narrative it is the latter: total destruction as the price of covenant purity. The Achar narrative (chapter 7) turns on the violation of the *anathema* — taking for private use what was consecrated to the Lord for destruction.

### *Archēstrategōs* — commander of the Lord's army (5:14)
Rendered "commander of the army of the Lord" — preserving the military-command register. The LXX *archēstrategōs* is the highest military title — commander-in-chief. The figure's acceptance of Joshua's worship and the holy-ground declaration mark him as more than a created messenger.

### *Katapausis* — rest (21:44; referenced in Hebrews 4)
Rendered "rest" — preserving the *katapausis* vocabulary that Hebrews 4 will exploit as the not-yet-fulfilled rest. The land-rest of Joshua 21:44 is genuine but the *katapausis* points forward.

### *Speirion kokkinon* — scarlet cord (2:18)
Rendered "scarlet cord" — the *kokkinon* = crimson/scarlet, the color of blood. The Passover-blood typological link is in the color.

## STRUCTURAL NOTE — JOSHUA'S TWO MOVEMENTS

Joshua has the clearest two-part structure of the historical books:

**Part 1 — Conquest (1–12):** Entry / Rahab / Jordan crossing / Jericho / Ai / southern campaign / northern campaign. The theological spine is the *divine warrior* — the Lord fights for Israel. Joshua is the instrument not the hero.

**Part 2 — Allotment (13–24):** Land distribution by tribe / cities of refuge / Levitical cities / eastern tribes' altar / farewell discourses / covenant at Shechem.

The theological arc: **Promise → Fulfillment → Warning.** Chapters 1–21 are the fulfillment of the Abrahamic land-promise (21:43–45 is the explicit declaration). Chapters 22–24 are the warning: the fulfillment is conditional on continued covenant loyalty. Joshua's final speech (chapter 24) mirrors Moses's final speech (Deuteronomy) — the pattern is: fulfillment followed immediately by the warning that unfaithfulness will undo it.

---

## NOTE ON JOSHUA AS TYPOLOGY

The NT use of Joshua is primarily typological rather than prophetic-citation. The key typological moves:

**Joshua = Jesus** — the names are identical (*Iēsous* = Joshua = Jesus — the same Greek name). The book of Hebrews exploits this: Joshua led Israel into the earthly rest / Jesus leads into the true rest (Hebrews 4:8). The conqueror who gives the land → the conqueror who gives the inheritance.

**Jordan crossing = baptism** — 1 Corinthians 10:1–2 establishes the water-crossing typology. Baptism as the entry into the new inheritance corresponds to the Jordan crossing as entry into Canaan.

**Rahab = the Gentile church** — Rahab the Gentile prostitute saved by the scarlet sign / the church gathered from the nations saved by the blood of Christ. The genealogical insertion of Rahab in Matthew 1:5 signals the Gentile-inclusion thread running through the Messianic line.

**Jericho = the present world-order** — the sevenfold-trumpet structure of the Jericho narrative governs Revelation's trumpet sequence. The walls that fall at the seventh blast = the present kosmos that gives way at the seventh trumpet.

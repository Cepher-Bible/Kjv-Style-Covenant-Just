\rem Addition A — Mordecai's Dream and the Conspiracy
\v 1 In the second year of the reign of Artaxerxes the great king on the first day of Nisan Mordecai the son of Jair son of Shimei son of Kish of the tribe of Benjamin had a dream —
\v 2 a Jew living in the city of Susa a great man serving in the court of the king.
\v 3 He was one of the captives whom Nebuchadnezzar king of Babylon had brought from Jerusalem with Jeconiah king of Judea.
\v 4 And this was his dream: Behold — noise and tumult cries and confusion great distress upon the earth.
\v 5 And behold two great dragons came forward both ready to fight — and they roared greatly.
\v 6 And at their roaring every nation prepared for war to fight against the righteous nation.
\v 7 And behold a day of darkness and gloom — tribulation and distress affliction and great tumult upon the earth.
\v 8 And the whole righteous nation was troubled fearing the evils prepared for them — and they were ready to perish.
\v 9 Then they cried to God — and from their crying as if from a little spring there came a great river with abundant water.
\v 10 Light came and the sun rose and the humble were exalted and devoured those held in honor.
\p
\v 11 Mordecai who had seen this dream and what God had purposed to do — awoke and kept it in his heart (*kardia*) seeking all day to understand it in every part.
\p
\v 12 Now Mordecai was resting in the courtyard with Bigthan and Teresh two of the king's eunuchs who guarded the courtyard.
\v 13 He overheard their plans and investigated their purposes and learned that they were preparing to lay hands on King Artaxerxes — and he informed the king about them.
\v 14 The king examined the two eunuchs and they confessed and were led away to be put to death.
\v 15 The king wrote these things down in the royal records and Mordecai wrote them down as well.
\v 16 And the king ordered Mordecai to serve in the court and rewarded him for these things.
\v 17 But Haman the son of Hammedatha the Bougean was honored before the king and he sought to harm Mordecai and his people because of the two eunuchs of the king.

\v 3 in the third year of his reign he gave a banquet for his friends and the other nations and for the nobles of the Persians and Medes and for the most honored of the satraps.
\v 4 After this — for one hundred and eighty days — he displayed the riches of his glorious kingdom and the splendor of his magnificent celebration to them.

\v 22 And he sent into all the kingdoms of his realm each province in its own language that every man should be master in his own home.

\v 6 But he disdained to lay hands on Mordecai alone. So Haman sought to destroy all the Jews — the people of Mordecai — throughout the whole kingdom of Artaxerxes.

\rem Addition B — The First Royal Edict
\v 1 The following is a copy of the letter: The Great King Artaxerxes writes to the governors of the hundred and twenty-seven provinces from India to Ethiopia and to the officials under them as follows.
\v 2 Having come to rule over many nations and having obtained dominion over the whole world I have decided not to be carried away by the presumption of power but always to act with equity and gentleness so that my subjects may live undisturbed lives.
\v 3 But Haman who excels among us in sound judgment and is distinguished for his constant good will and steadfast reliability and has attained the second place in the kingdom —
\v 4 pointed out to us that among all the nations in the world there is scattered a certain hostile people who have laws contrary to those of every nation and who continually disregard the ordinances of kings so that the unity of government blamelessly administered by us cannot be brought about.
\v 5 Having noted therefore that this nation and it alone stands constantly in opposition to all human beings — perversely following a strange manner of life and laws — and is ill-disposed to our government doing all the harm they can so that our kingdom may not attain stability —
\v 6 we therefore command that those indicated to you in the letters written by Haman — who is in charge of affairs and is our second father — shall all together with their wives and children be utterly destroyed by the swords of their enemies without any pity or mercy on the fourteenth day of the twelfth month Adar of this present year —
\v 7 so that those who have long been hostile and remain so may in a single day go down in violence to Hades and leave our government completely secure and untroubled hereafter.

\p
\v 7 In the first month which is the month of Nisan in the twelfth year of King Artaxerxes they cast Pur — that is the lot — before Haman day after day and month after month and the lot fell on the fourteenth day of the twelfth month which is the month of Adar.
\v 8 Then Haman said to King Artaxerxes: There is a certain people scattered abroad and dispersed among the peoples in all the provinces of your kingdom. Their laws are different from those of every other people and they do not keep the king's laws so that it is not to the king's profit to tolerate them.
\v 12 Then the king's scribes were summoned on the thirteenth day of the first month and an edict was written in full according to the instructions of Haman to the king's satraps and to the governors over all the provinces and to the officials of all the peoples every province in its own script and every people in its own language. It was written in the name of King Artaxerxes and sealed with the king's ring.
\v 13 Letters were sent by couriers to all the king's provinces to destroy to kill and to annihilate all Jews young and old women and children in one day — the thirteenth day of the twelfth month which is the month of Adar — and to plunder their goods.
\v 15 The couriers went out hurriedly by order of the king and the decree was issued in Susa the capital. And the king and Haman sat down to drink but the city of Susa was thrown into confusion.

\rem Addition E — The Second Royal Edict
\v 1 The following is a copy of this letter: The Great King Artaxerxes to the governors of the provinces from India to Ethiopia — one hundred and twenty-seven provinces — and to those who are loyal to our government: greetings.
\v 2 Many people — the more they are honored with the great bounty of their benefactors — the more proud do they become and not only seek to injure our subjects but in their inability to stand prosperity they even undertake to scheme against their own benefactors.
\v 3 They not only take away thankfulness from among men but carried away by the boasts of those who know nothing of goodness — they suppose that they will escape the evil-hating justice of God who always sees everything.
\v 4 And often many of those who are set in places of authority have been made in part responsible for the shedding of innocent blood and have been involved in irremediable calamities by the persuasion of friends who have been entrusted with the administration of public affairs —
\v 5 when with the malicious craft of their disposition they beguile the sincere goodwill of their sovereigns.
\v 7 You can see this accomplished not so much from the more ancient records which we hand on as from investigation of matters close at hand. We must in the future render our kingdom quiet and peaceable for all men —
\v 8 by changing our methods and always judging what comes before our eyes with more equitable consideration.
\v 9 For Haman the son of Hammedatha a Macedonian — in reality an alien to the Persian blood and quite devoid of our kindliness — having been entertained by us —
\v 10 obtained so great a share of our good will as to be called our father and to be continually bowed down to by all as the person second to the royal throne.
\v 11 But unable to restrain his arrogance he undertook to deprive us of our kingdom and our life —
\v 12 and with intricate craft and deceit asked for the destruction of Mordecai our savior and perpetual benefactor and of Esther the blameless partner of our kingdom — together with their whole nation.
\v 13 For by these methods he thought that he would find us undefended and would transfer the kingdom of the Persians to the Macedonians.
\v 14 But we find that the Jews who were consigned to annihilation by this thrice accursed man are not evildoers but are governed by most righteous laws —
\v 15 and are children of the living God the Most High the great living God who has directed the kingdom both for us and for our ancestors in the most excellent order.
\v 16 You will therefore do well not to put in execution the letters sent by Haman the son of Hammedatha —
\v 17 since he who did these things has been hanged at the gates of Susa with all his household — God who rules over all things having speedily inflicted on him the recompense that he deserved.
\v 18 Therefore post a copy of this letter openly in every place and permit the Jews to live under their own laws —
\v 19 and reinforce them so that on the thirteenth day of the twelfth month Adar on that same day they may defend themselves against those who attack them in a time of affliction.
\v 20 For God who rules over all things has made this day to be a joy for his chosen people instead of a day of destruction for them.
\v 21 Therefore you shall observe this with all good cheer as a notable day among your commemorative festivals —
\v 22 so that both now and hereafter it may represent deliverance for you and the loyal Persians but that it may be a reminder of destruction for those who plot against us.
\v 23 Every city and country without exception that does not act accordingly shall be destroyed in wrath with spear and fire. It shall be made not only impassable for human beings but also most hateful to wild animals and birds for all time.

## CRITICAL TEXTUAL NOTES — LXX ESTHER

**The Six Additions:** The LXX Esther is approximately one-third longer than the MT, containing six passages absent from the Hebrew. In the Rahlfs-Hanhart system these are labeled Additions A–F and positioned at the beginning, end, and within the narrative. Jerome identified them as non-Hebrew in origin and placed them at the end of his Latin Esther — from which they entered the Vulgate's deuterocanonical appendix. The GLE integrates them in their LXX canonical positions.

**The theological transformation:** The MT Esther is notable for never mentioning God. The LXX Additions supply the theological dimension that the MT leaves implicit: the prayers of Mordecai and Esther (Addition C), the interpretive framework of Mordecai's dream (Additions A and F), and the divine identification in the second royal edict (Addition E — "God who rules over all things"). The LXX Esther is the same narrative with its theological skeleton made explicit.

**The two Greek texts:** Beyond the LXX B-text (Rahlfs-Hanhart primary), there is also an AT (Alpha Text / Old Greek) which is shorter and independent. The GLE follows the B-text.

**Purim etymology:** The *Pur* = lot (a Persian or Akkadian loanword) gives the feast its name. The cast lot that Haman used to determine the date of genocide becomes the name of the feast of deliverance — the instrument of evil is memorialized as the name of salvation.

## CAE NODES — LXX ESTHER

### Addition A / Esther 4:14 — Mordecai's dream / *for such a time as this* — CAE PRIMARY NODE

**Addition A:1–11** — Mordecai's dream of two dragons / the righteous nation / the little spring becoming a great river / the humble exalted.

**4:14** — "For if you keep silent at this time relief and deliverance will rise for the Jews from another place — but you and your father's house will perish. And who knows whether you have not come to the kingdom for such a time as this (*eis ton kairon touton*)?"

***kai tis oiden ei eis ton kairon touton parelēlythas eis tēn basileian***

= and who knows whether it is for this very moment that you have arrived at the kingdom.

The *eis ton kairon touton* = for this very moment / this appointed time is one of the most concentrated statements of providential theology in the canon. Mordecai does not claim to know the divine purpose with certainty — he frames it as a question (*who knows?*). But the question contains its own answer: the circumstances are too precise to be accidental. Esther's position is not self-achievement; it is providential placement for a moment of crisis.

The theology of the *kairos* = appointed time is the Esther narrative's key contribution: God's hidden sovereignty operates through the ordinary processes of human history (beauty contests / palace politics / sleepless nights / forgotten records) to position his instruments at the moment of maximum need. The divine name is never mentioned in the MT precisely because this hiddenness is the theological point: providence operates where it is not visible.

**Addition F:1–9** provides the interpretive key: Mordecai's dream is decoded — the spring that became a river is Esther / the two dragons are Haman and Mordecai / the nations that gathered to destroy Israel were defeated by the *psaucho* = small spring that became a great flood / the two lots were prepared before God. The dream-fulfillment structure declares that the entire narrative was known in advance — the *kairos* was prepared before the crisis.

**Romans 8:28** — "And we know that for those who love God all things work together for good for those who are called according to his purpose." Paul's *panta synergei* = all things work together is the NT theological formulation of the Esther *eis ton kairon touton* principle: the hidden divine sovereignty that works through all circumstances (including palace intrigue and genocide threats) for the good of those who are the Lord's. The Esther narrative is the OT supreme narrative illustration of Romans 8:28.

**Galatians 4:4** — "But when the fullness of time (*plērōma tou chronou*) had come God sent forth his Son." Paul's *plērōma tou chronou* is the ultimate *eis ton kairon touton*: as Esther came to the kingdom for the appointed moment of her people's deliverance, the Son came in the fullness of time for the appointed moment of all humanity's deliverance. The Esther-kairos is the OT type of the Incarnation-kairos.

**Acts 17:26–27** — "And he made from one man every nation of mankind to live on all the face of the earth having determined allotted periods and the boundaries of their dwelling place that they should seek God." Paul's *horismenas... kairous* = appointed times for the nations is the cosmic extension of the Esther principle: the divine sovereignty that positioned Esther in the Persian court positioned every nation in its historical moment for the purpose of seeking God.

### Addition C — The prayers of Mordecai and Esther — CAE PRIMARY NODE

**C:2–4** — "O Lord Lord King who rules over all things — for the universe is in your power and there is no one who can oppose you when it is your will to save Israel — for you made heaven and earth and every wonderful thing under heaven."

**C:14–15** — "O my Lord — you alone are our King — help me who am alone and have no helper but you — for my danger is in my hand."

**C:23** — "Remember us O Lord — make yourself known in the time of our affliction and give me courage O King of the gods and Master of all dominion."

**C:25** — "Save us by your hand and help me who am alone and have no helper but you O Lord."

The prayers of Mordecai and Esther (Addition C) supply the explicit theological ground for what the MT leaves implicit. In the MT Esther fasts (4:16) but does not pray explicitly. In the LXX she prostrates herself, strips off her royal garments, puts on ashes and dung, and offers the most intimate prayer of personal destitution in the book.

**Mordecai's theological clarification** (C:5–7): He explains why he did not bow to Haman — not from arrogance but from theological principle: he would not set human glory above divine glory. The MT leaves Mordecai's motivation unexplained; the LXX makes it explicit. The refusal to bow is not Jewish nationalism but covenant-monotheism: *I will not bow down to anyone but you my Lord*.

**Esther's prayer structure:**
1. **The historical confession** (C:16–18): We sinned and were handed over.
2. **The current threat** (C:19–21): They seek to abolish the divine decree / extinguish the altar / silence the praises.
3. **The petition** (C:22–25): Turn their plan / give me courage / save us.
4. **The personal confession** (C:26–29): I abhor what I appear to embrace — the royal garments / the king's table / the position of power. Her inner life is entirely different from her public persona.

The *ouk ēn euphrosynē moi* = there was no joy for me (C:29) is the most intimate moment in the book: Esther has been queen but has had no joy since her people were threatened. The joy of the Lord (cf. Nehemiah 8:10) is absent when the covenant-people are under sentence of death.

**Matthew 6:6** — "But when you pray go into your room and shut the door and pray to your Father who is in secret." Esther's upper-room prayer (C:12–30) — offered in private, in sackcloth, with ashes, stripped of all royal dignity — is the OT embodiment of the *krypto* = secret prayer Jesus commends. The prayer in hiddenness before the divine throne precedes the public action before the human throne.

**Hebrews 4:16** — "Let us then with confidence draw near to the throne of grace that we may receive mercy and find grace to help in time of need." Esther draws near to the throne of Artaxerxes — at mortal risk — after first drawing near to the divine throne in prayer. The Hebrews invitation to the throne of grace is grounded in the covenant-pattern that Esther embodies: the divine throne is approached in prayer / the human throne is approached with the confidence that the divine throne has already been addressed.

### Esther 4:16 — *if I perish I perish* — CAE NODE

**4:16** — "Then I will go to the king though it is against the law — and if I perish I perish (*ean apoloōmai apoloōmai*)."

***ean apoloōmai apoloōmai***

The double conditional — the most concentrated statement of covenant self-surrender in the book — is Esther's theological resolution: she submits her personal safety entirely to the divine will. The MT formula (*'im avadti avadti*) is echoed exactly in the LXX. The repetition is not resignation but covenant-commitment: the outcome is not in her hands / she will act / the result belongs to God.

The structural parallel is Job 13:15: "Though he slay me I will hope in him." Both Esther and Job commit their action to God without demanding a favorable outcome. The covenant-faithfulness is not contingent on survival.

**John 12:24–25** — "Truly truly I say to you unless a grain of wheat falls into the earth and dies it remains alone but if it dies it bears much fruit. Whoever loves his life loses it and whoever hates his life in this world will keep it for eternal life." Jesus's grain-of-wheat principle is the NT equivalent of Esther's *if I perish I perish*: the willingness to lose one's life for the covenant-purpose is the condition for bearing fruit. Esther's self-surrender is the OT type of the cross-pattern.

**Philippians 1:21** — "For to me to live is Christ and to die is gain." Paul's *kerdos* = gain in death is the NT articulation of the *if I perish I perish* covenant-surrender: the person who has committed their existence to the covenant-Lord is freed from self-preservation as the governing value.

### Addition E:14–16 — the second edict / *children of the living God* — CAE CRITICAL NODE

**E:14–16** — "But we find that the Jews who were consigned to annihilation by this thrice accursed man are not evildoers but are governed by most righteous laws — and are children of the living God the Most High the great living God who has directed the kingdom both for us and for our ancestors in the most excellent order. You will therefore do well not to put in execution the letters sent by Haman."

***alla nomois dikaiotatois politeuomenoi kai huioi tou theou tou zōntos tou hypsistou tou megistou tou zōntogonountos basileian hēmin***

= but conducting their lives by most righteous laws and being sons of the living God the Most High the greatest who preserves the kingdom for us.

The *huioi tou theou tou zōntos* = sons of the living God spoken by the Persian king Artaxerxes in the second royal edict is one of the most startling declarations in the entire LXX canon. A pagan emperor, issuing a reversal of his own genocidal decree, identifies the Jewish people as *sons of the living God* — and grounds the reversal of the decree on the divine sovereignty: the living God *has directed the kingdom for us*. The Cyrus-pattern (the *pneuma* of Cyrus stirred to serve the covenant-purpose) reaches its LXX Esther fulfillment: the Persian king becomes the mouthpiece of the divine identity of Israel.

**The *theos zōn* = living God formula** is the anti-idolatry declaration: gods made with hands cannot direct kingdoms / the God of Israel is *zōn* = living and his sovereignty is real. The pagan king's confession of the *theos zōn* is the Esther narrative's most explicit theological moment — supplied entirely by the LXX addition.

**Hosea 1:10** — "And in the place where it was said to them: You are not my people it will be said to them: Sons of the living God (*huioi theou zōntos*)." Hosea's prophecy of the restoration of Israel as *sons of the living God* is the prophetic source for the Addition E:15 declaration. The Persian king's edict echoes the Hosea restoration-formula: Israel is declared *huioi theou zōntos* by the mouth of the empire that threatened to destroy them.

**Romans 9:26** — "And in the very place where it was said to them: You are not my people there they will be called sons of the living God (*huioi theou zōntos*)." Paul cites Hosea 1:10 in Romans 9:26 to declare the inclusion of the Gentiles as *sons of the living God* — the same formula that Addition E:15 places in the mouth of Artaxerxes. The chain: Hosea 1:10 (*Israel restored as sons of the living God*) → Addition E:15 (*the Persian king declares Israel sons of the living God*) → Romans 9:26 (Paul extends *sons of the living God* to the Gentiles).

**The triple application of *huioi theou zōntos*:** Hosea's formula, originally a restoration-promise to Israel, is applied by the LXX Esther to Israel through the mouth of the Persian emperor, and is applied by Paul to the Gentile believers. The *theos zōn* = living God is the anti-idolatry signature that runs from the Shema through the prophets through Esther through Paul.

### Esther 8:16–17 — light and gladness / many declared themselves Jews — CAE PRIMARY NODE

**8:16** — "The Jews had light and gladness and joy and honor (*phōs kai euphrosynēn kai charan kai timēn*)."

**8:17** — "And in every province and in every city wherever the king's command and his edict reached there was gladness and joy among the Jews — a feast and a holiday. And many from the peoples of the country declared themselves Jews (*ioudaizontes*) — for fear of the Jews had fallen on them."

***kai polloi tōn ethnōn perietemuonto kai ioudaizon***

= and many of the nations were circumcised and became Jews.

The *phōs kai euphrosynē kai chara* = light and gladness and joy at the reversal is the covenant-blessing vocabulary of restoration: precisely the vocabulary that Tobit 13 and the post-exilic hymns use for the eschatological return. The reversal of darkness to light / mourning to gladness / death-sentence to feast is the Purim theology enacted.

The **Gentile-joining** (8:17 — *ioudaizontes* = Judaizing / becoming Jews) is the narrative's eschatological dimension: the nations join Israel in the day of deliverance. This is the Zechariah 8:23 pattern enacted: "In those days ten men from the nations of every tongue shall take hold of the robe of a Jew saying: Let us go with you — for we have heard that God is with you."

**Zechariah 8:23** — "In those days ten men from the nations of every tongue shall take hold of the robe of a Jew saying: Let us go with you — for we have heard that God is with you." The Zechariah oracle of Gentile-joining-Israel through the witness of God's presence is the prophetic ground for Esther 8:17. The nations join when they see the divine reversal enacted.

**Acts 2:41, 47** — "So those who received his word were baptized and there were added that day about three thousand souls... And the Lord added to their number day by day those who were being saved." The Pentecost-addition of thousands to the covenant-community is the NT form of the Esther 8:17 *ioudaizontes*: the demonstration of divine power and reversal draws the nations into the covenant-people. The fear that fell on the nations at the Esther reversal becomes the awe that fell on all (Acts 2:43) at the Pentecost reversal.

### Addition F:6–9 — the two lots / God remembered his inheritance — CAE PRIMARY NODE

**F:6–9** — "The Lord has saved his people — the Lord has rescued us from all these evils. God has done great signs and wonders — wonders that have never happened among the nations. For this he prepared two lots — one for the people of God and one for all the other nations — and these two lots came to the hour and moment and day of decision before God and among all the nations. And God remembered his people and vindicated his inheritance."

***ho theos emnēsthē tou laou autou kai edikaōsen tēn klēronomian autou***

= and God remembered his people and declared righteous his inheritance.

Addition F is the interpretive bookend to Addition A's dream: the narrative is decoded through the lens of divine sovereignty. The *Pur* = lot that Haman cast to determine the date of genocide is reinterpreted: God himself had prepared two lots — one for Israel and one for the nations. The instrument of evil (the cast lot) was contained within a prior divine sovereignty that determined the outcome before Haman cast anything.

The **two lots** theology is a profound statement about evil and providence: Haman's lot was real / his plan was genuine / the threat was actual — and all of it was comprehended within the divine preparation of two lots before the event. The *kairos* of Esther 4:14 and the *two lots* of Addition F are the same theological reality: God's prior knowledge and preparation are not threatened by human malice but encompass it.

***edikaōsen tēn klēronomian autou*** = and he declared righteous / vindicated his inheritance. The verb *dikaioō* = declare righteous / vindicate / justify applied to the covenant-people as the Lord's *klēronomia* = inheritance is the covenant-justice declaration: the lot fell against Israel / God reversed the lot / the reversal is an act of covenant-justice — the *klēronomia* is vindicated.

**Romans 8:33** — "Who shall bring any charge against God's elect? It is God who justifies (*dikaion*)." Paul's *theos ho dikaion* = God who justifies is the NT declaration of the same covenant-justice that Addition F's *edikaōsen tēn klēronomian autou* declares. The God who vindicated his inheritance against Haman's genocide is the God who justifies his elect against every charge.

**Revelation 12:10** — "And I heard a loud voice in heaven saying: Now the salvation and the power and the kingdom of our God and the authority of his Christ have come — for the accuser of our brothers has been thrown down who accuses them day and night before our God." The Revelation ejection of the accuser is the eschatological form of the Haman-defeat: the one who brought charges against the covenant-people before the cosmic throne is thrown down / the *edikaōsen tēn klēronomian* of Addition F is enacted at the ultimate level. Haman is the OT type of the cosmic accuser who is defeated by divine reversal.

## STRUCTURAL NOTE — LXX ESTHER AS THE HIDDEN PROVIDENCE NARRATIVE

LXX Esther is structurally the complement to the Judith narrative: where Judith is explicit (God is named / the prayer is recorded / the victory is declared theological at every step), LXX Esther deploys hiddenness as the primary theological category. The divine name is absent from the MT core precisely because the book's theology is the theology of hidden providence — God works through:

- A beauty contest
- A sleepless night
- A forgotten record-book
- A drunken banquet
- A cast lot

None of these are miraculous. All are ordinary. And all are coordinated by the divine sovereignty that Addition F makes explicit: God prepared the two lots before Haman cast one.

**The five reversals of the book:**
1. Vashti deposed → Esther enthroned
2. Mordecai unrewarded → Mordecai honored by Haman
3. Haman ascending → Haman hanged on his own gallows
4. Jews under death-sentence → Jews defending themselves with royal backing
5. Day of genocide → Day of feast and gladness

Each reversal is a *peripeteia* — a complete inversion — and each is accomplished without direct divine intervention in the narrative's surface. The theology is that the surface hides the depth: beneath every ordinary event is the divine preparation of two lots.

**The Esther-Mordecai typological complex:**
- Mordecai = the covenant-faithful Jew who refuses to bow to the human pretender to divine honors (Haman/Nebuchadnezzar/the Beast)
- Esther = the covenant-people in diaspora, apparently assimilated, suddenly called to covenant-identity at the moment of crisis
- Haman = the adversary (*satanas* pattern) who accuses and seeks to destroy the covenant-people before the throne
- Artaxerxes = the pagan sovereign whose *pneuma* is moved by the divine sovereignty (the Cyrus pattern)
- The reversal = the divine pattern of all creation-to-restoration history

**The canonical chain of reversal-feasts:** Passover (Exodus 12 — death sentence reversed by the blood / feasting commanded) → Purim (Esther 9 — death sentence reversed by divine providence / feasting commanded) → the Marriage Supper of the Lamb (Revelation 19:7–9 — the ultimate enemy defeated / the ultimate feast). The feast-after-reversal is the covenant-theology of joy enacted in history and projected eschatologically.

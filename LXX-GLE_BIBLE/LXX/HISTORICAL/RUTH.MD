## CRITICAL TEXTUAL NOTE — LXX RUTH

The LXX Ruth is a faithful, close rendering of the Hebrew — one of the less divergent LXX books. The Rahlfs-Hanhart text is stable and agrees closely with the MT throughout. Primary translation challenges are lexical rather than textual — especially *go'el* / *goel* = redeemer / kinsman-redeemer, rendered in LXX as *agchisteus* = near-kinsman.

The LXX places Ruth after Judges — consistent with its narrative opening: "in the days when the judges judged." The canonical placement signals Ruth as the counter-narrative to Judges: Judges ends in atrocity and dissolution / Ruth begins in the same period and ends in the Davidic genealogy. The two books are designed to be read together.

## CAE NODES — RUTH

### Ruth 1:16–17 — *hopou ean poreuthēs* / where you go I will go — CAE PRIMARY NODE

**1:16–17** — "Where you go I will go and where you lodge I will lodge — your people shall be my people and your God my God. Where you die I will die and there will I be buried."

***hopou ean poreuthēs poreusōmai kai hopou ean aulisthēs aulisthēsomai — ho laos sou laos mou kai ho theos sou theos mou***

This is the covenant-adhesion speech — the single most concentrated covenant-declaration by a non-Israelite in the entire OT. Ruth's declaration has the structure of a covenant oath: she invokes the Lord by name as the guarantor (v.17 *kath' hoti thanatos diastelei ana meson emou kai sou* = only death shall separate between me and you).

The theological weight: Ruth is a Moabite — from the nation excluded from the assembly of the Lord to the tenth generation (Deuteronomy 23:3). Yet Ruth's declaration of covenant adhesion is presented without reservation or qualification. Her entry into the covenant is through *eleos* = steadfast love (the Vocabulary Lock Table term — never mere "kindness") — the same *eleos* that defines the Lord's own covenant faithfulness.

**Ephesians 2:12–13** — "you were at that time separated from Christ — alienated from the commonwealth of Israel and strangers to the covenants of promise... but now in Christ Jesus you who once were far off have been brought near by the blood of Christ." Paul's Gentile-inclusion theology in Ephesians 2 is Ruth's story made universal: the one who was excluded by the law of distance is brought near by the *eleos* of the covenant-redeemer.

**Galatians 3:28** — "There is neither Jew nor Greek." Ruth's crossing of the national/ethnic covenant boundary — not by legal status but by covenant-adhesion — is the narrative ground for Paul's Galatians 3 declaration.

---

### Ruth 1:20 — *Mara* / bitterness and the name of God — CAE NODE

**1:20** — "Do not call me Naomi — call me Mara — for the Almighty (*Shaddai / LXX: Hikanos*) has dealt very bitterly with me. I went away full and the Lord has brought me back empty."

***mē me kaleite Naomin — kaleite me Pikran hoti epikranen me hikanōs ho Hikanos***

The LXX renders *Shaddai* as *Hikanos* = the Sufficient / the All-sufficient. The wordplay in the MT (*Shaddai* / *shadag* = breast / mountain / Almighty) is rendered in the LXX with sufficiency language. Both registers point to the same theological claim: the God who is entirely sufficient has withdrawn his hand — and Naomi names that withdrawal honestly before the community.

Naomi's lament is not apostasy — it is covenant-grief. She does not leave the Lord but she names her desolation truthfully. This is the Lamentations posture: *hoti ouk exelipen ta elē Kyriou* (Lamentations 3:22) — but before the pivot the full weight of the grief must be spoken.

**Lamentations 1:1** — the *Mara* posture: "How lonely sits the city." Naomi's self-naming as Mara mirrors the *Mara*-register of Lamentations — the righteous expression of devastation before God that precedes restoration.

---

### Ruth 2:12 — *hypo tas pterygas* / under whose wings — CAE PRIMARY NODE

**2:12** — "A full reward be given you by the Lord the God of Israel — under whose wings you have come to take refuge."

***hypo tas pterygas autou ēlthes elpizein*** = under whose wings you came to hope / take refuge.

***Pteryges*** = wings. The Lord's wings as the image of covenant protection — Ruth has come *under the wings* of the God of Israel. This is the *kanap* = wing/corner of the garment vocabulary: the same word used for the corner of a garment that is spread over one as a covering.

**Ruth 3:9** — "spread your wings over your servant for you are a redeemer." Ruth uses the same *pterygas* / *kanap* language when she asks Boaz to cover her — she is asking Boaz to embody toward her what the Lord does: the redeemer spreads his wings / garment as covenant covering.

**Psalm 17:8 (LXX)** — "keep me as the apple of your eye — hide me under the shadow of your wings." The Psalms wing-shelter imagery is the liturgical development of the Boaz/Ruth covenant-covering narrative.

**Matthew 23:37** — "how often would I have gathered your children together as a hen gathers her chicks under her wings — and you were not willing." Jesus's lament over Jerusalem uses the *pterygas* / wing-gathering image — the divine-redeemer who spreads his wings to gather the scattered. The Ruth 2:12 → Matthew 23:37 chain is direct: the Lord who shelters Ruth under his wings is the one who weeps over Jerusalem for refusing the same shelter.

**Revelation 12:14** — "the woman was given the two wings of the great eagle so that she might fly from the serpent." The wings-as-protection image in Revelation echoes the Exodus 19:4 / Ruth 2:12 register.

---

### Ruth 2:20 / 3:9 / 4:14 — *agchisteus* / the redeemer — CAE PRIMARY NODE

**2:20** — "The man is a close relative of ours — he is one of our redeemers (*agchisteuontōn hēmin estin*)."

**3:9** — "Spread your wings over your servant for you are a redeemer (*agchisteus ei sy*)."

**4:14** — "Blessed be the Lord who has not left you this day without a redeemer (*agchistea*)."

***Agchisteus*** = the near-kinsman who exercises the right of redemption. The LXX *agchisteus* renders Hebrew *go'el* — the one who by nearness of kinship has the right and obligation to: (1) redeem property sold in poverty, (2) marry the widow of a dead kinsman (levirate), (3) avenge blood. All three functions converge in Boaz.

This is the most theologically loaded term in Ruth. The *go'el* / *agchisteus* = redeemer is one of the primary OT designations for the Lord himself:

**Isaiah 41:14** — "your Redeemer (*go'el*) is the Holy One of Israel."
**Isaiah 44:6** — "Thus says the Lord the King of Israel and his Redeemer (*go'el*) the Lord of hosts."
**Isaiah 54:5** — "your Redeemer (*go'el*) is the Holy One of Israel."
**Job 19:25** — "For I know that my Redeemer (*go'el*) lives."

The Boaz-as-redeemer narrative is the most concrete embodiment of the *go'el* = divine redeemer concept in the historical books. Boaz does what the Lord does: he comes near / he pays the price / he restores the name of the dead / he takes the destitute widow as wife.

**Hebrews 2:11–14** — "For he who sanctifies and those who are sanctified all have one source — that is why he is not ashamed to call them brothers... Since therefore the children share in flesh and blood he himself likewise partook of the same things." The Incarnation as the *agchisteus* move: the Redeemer comes near (*agchi-* = near) to those he redeems by taking on their nature — becoming the near-kinsman who has the right to redeem.

**Galatians 4:4–5** — "God sent forth his Son — born of woman — born under the law — to redeem those who were under the law." The *exagorazō* = buy out / redeem in Galatians 4:5 is the *go'el* function: payment of price to restore the inheritance of those who had lost it.

---

### Ruth 4:17–22 — *genealogia* / the Davidic genealogy — CAE PRIMARY NODE

**4:17** — "They named him Obed — he was the father of Jesse the father of David."

**4:18–22** — Perez → Hezron → Ram → Amminadab → Nahshon → Salmon → Boaz → Obed → Jesse → David.

The book of Ruth ends with a ten-generation genealogy from Perez to David. The genealogy is not an afterthought — it is the entire book's destination. The story of a Moabite woman's covenant-adhesion and a kinsman-redeemer's faithful action is the story of how the Davidic throne was established.

**Matthew 1:5–6** — "Salmon the father of Boaz by Rahab — and Boaz the father of Obed by Ruth — and Obed the father of Jesse — and Jesse the father of David the king." Matthew's genealogy of Jesus names both Rahab (Joshua 6) and Ruth — the two Gentile women whose covenant-adhesion is woven into the Messianic line. The Gentile thread in the Davidic genealogy is deliberate and structurally significant.

**Luke 3:31–32** — "...the son of David the son of Jesse the son of Obed the son of Boaz the son of Sala the son of Nahshon." Luke's backward genealogy through David reaches Boaz and Obed — the same Ruth-chain preserved in both evangelist genealogies.

The Ruth → David → Christ genealogical chain is the narrative demonstration of what Paul states theologically in Galatians 3:28 — the Gentile is not grafted in at the end but has been in the line from the beginning.

## VOCABULARY DECISIONS — RUTH

### *Eleos* — steadfast love (1:8 / 2:20 / 3:10)
Rendered "steadfast love" throughout — Vocabulary Lock Table enforced. The *eleos* in Ruth is the covenant-faithfulness term: Naomi prays it for her daughters-in-law (1:8) / Naomi declares Boaz has shown it to the dead and living (2:20) / Boaz declares Ruth has shown it in choosing him (3:10). The three *eleos* occurrences form the book's theological spine: *eleos* shown by the living to the dead is the axis of the whole narrative.

### *Agchisteus* — redeemer / kinsman-redeemer
Rendered "redeemer" throughout — preserving the full *go'el* register. Never "kinsman" alone — the redemption-function is the theologically operative element. The unnamed closer redeemer of chapter 4 is rendered "the redeemer" — his anonymity is deliberate: he has the right but declines to exercise it. Boaz who exercises the right is the true redeemer.

### *Manoach* — rest (1:9 / 3:1)
Naomi seeks *manoach* = rest for Ruth (1:9) / asks whether she should seek *manoach* for her (3:1). The *manoach* = settled rest / covenant-security. The same *katapausis* register that Hebrews 4 develops from Joshua 21 — the rest that comes from covenant-restoration. Ruth's rest is found in the redeemer's house.

### *Pteryges* — wings (2:12 / 3:9)
Rendered "wings" — preserving the *kanap* = wing/garment-corner dual register. The same word in both the Lord's wings (2:12) and Boaz's covering (3:9) is the theological hinge: Boaz images the Lord.

## STRUCTURAL NOTE — RUTH AS COUNTER-NARRATIVE TO JUDGES

Ruth is set in the judges period (1:1) but is its theological antithesis:

**Judges ends:** "Every man did what was right in his own eyes" — dissolution, atrocity, civil war, no king.
**Ruth opens:** In that same period, *eleos* = steadfast love operates between a Moabite woman, an Israelite widow, and a kinsman-redeemer — culminating in the Davidic genealogy.

The contrast is structural: Judges 19–21 (the Levite's concubine / Gibeah atrocity / near-extinction of Benjamin) vs. Ruth 1–4 (the Moabite widow / Bethlehem redemption / birth of Obed). Both are set in the same era — Judges shows the nadir of covenant unfaithfulness / Ruth shows that *eleos* persisted even in that nadir.

The *eleos* that flows through Ruth — from Naomi to her daughters-in-law / from Boaz to Ruth / from Ruth to Naomi — is the same *eleos* the Lord himself exercises as the *go'el* of his people. Ruth is the narrative proof that covenant faithfulness is possible in the worst of times.

**Hebrews 11** names the judges as faith-examples but does not name any of the Judges characters in their worst moments. Ruth is not named in Hebrews 11 but she appears in both genealogies (Matthew 1 / Luke 3) — her faith is vindicated not by being listed but by being ancestral.

\v 1 Now King David was old and advanced in years — and although they covered him with clothes he could not get warm.
\v 2 Therefore his servants said to him: Let a young woman — a virgin — be sought for my lord the king and let her wait on the king and be in his service — and let her lie in your arms that my lord the king may be warm.
\v 3 So they sought for a beautiful young woman throughout all the territory of Israel and found Abishag the Shunammite and brought her to the king.
\v 4 The young woman was very beautiful — and she was in the king's service and attended to him but the king knew her not.
\p
\v 5 Now Adonijah the son of Haggith exalted himself saying: I will be king — and he prepared for himself chariots and horsemen and fifty men to run before him.
\v 6 His father had never at any time displeased him by asking: Why have you done thus? He was also very handsome and he was born next after Absalom.
\v 7 He conferred with Joab the son of Zeruiah and with Abiathar the priest — and they followed Adonijah and helped him.
\v 8 But Zadok the priest and Benaiah the son of Jehoiada and Nathan the prophet and Shimei and Rei and David's mighty men were not with Adonijah.
\v 9 Adonijah sacrificed sheep oxen and fattened cattle by the Serpent's Stone which is beside En-rogel — and he invited all his brothers the king's sons and all the royal officials of Judah —
\v 10 but he did not invite Nathan the prophet or Benaiah or the mighty men or Solomon his brother.
\p
\v 11 Then Nathan said to Bathsheba the mother of Solomon: Have you not heard that Adonijah the son of Haggith has become king and David our lord does not know it?
\v 12 Now therefore come let me give you advice that you may save your own life and the life of your son Solomon.
\v 13 Go in at once to King David and say to him: Did you not my lord O king swear to your servant saying: Solomon your son shall reign after me and he shall sit on my throne? Why then is Adonijah king?
\v 14 Then while you are still speaking with the king I will come in after you and confirm your words.
\p
\v 15 So Bathsheba went to the king in his chamber — now the king was very old and Abishag the Shunammite was attending to the king.
\v 16 Bathsheba bowed and paid homage to the king and the king said: What do you desire?
\v 17 She said to him: My lord — you swore to your servant by the Lord your God: Solomon your son shall reign after me and he shall sit on my throne.
\v 18 And now look — Adonijah is king although you my lord the king do not know it.
\v 19 He has sacrificed oxen fattened cattle and sheep in abundance and has invited all the sons of the king and Abiathar the priest and Joab the commander of the army — but Solomon your servant he has not invited.
\v 20 And now my lord the king — the eyes of all Israel are on you to tell them who shall sit on the throne of my lord the king after him.
\v 21 Otherwise it will come to pass when my lord the king sleeps with his fathers that I and my son Solomon will be counted as offenders.
\p
\v 22 While she was still speaking with the king Nathan the prophet came in.
\v 23 And they told the king: Here is Nathan the prophet. And when he came in before the king he bowed before the king with his face to the ground.
\v 24 And Nathan said: My lord the king — have you said: Adonijah shall reign after me and he shall sit on my throne?
\v 25 For he has gone down today and has sacrificed oxen fattened cattle and sheep in abundance and has invited all the king's sons and the commanders of the army and Abiathar the priest — and look — they are eating and drinking before him and saying: Long live King Adonijah.
\v 26 But me your servant and Zadok the priest and Benaiah the son of Jehoiada and your servant Solomon he has not invited.
\v 27 Has this thing been brought about by my lord the king and you have not told your servants who should sit on the throne of my lord the king after him?
\p
\v 28 Then King David answered: Call Bathsheba to me. So she came into the king's presence and stood before the king.
\v 29 And the king swore saying: As the Lord lives — who has redeemed my soul out of every adversity —
\v 30 as I swore to you by the Lord the God of Israel: Solomon your son shall reign after me and he shall sit on my throne in my place — even so will I do this day.
\v 31 Then Bathsheba bowed with her face to the ground and paid homage to the king and said: May my lord King David live forever.
\p
\v 32 King David said: Call to me Zadok the priest and Nathan the prophet and Benaiah the son of Jehoiada. So they came before the king.
\v 33 And the king said to them: Take with you the servants of your lord and have Solomon my son ride on my own mule and bring him down to Gihon.
\v 34 And let Zadok the priest and Nathan the prophet there anoint him king over Israel — then blow the trumpet and say: Long live King Solomon.
\v 35 You shall then come up after him and he shall come and sit on my throne — for he shall be king in my place. I have appointed him to be ruler over Israel and over Judah.
\v 36 And Benaiah the son of Jehoiada answered the king: Amen — may the Lord the God of my lord the king say so.
\v 37 As the Lord has been with my lord the king even so may he be with Solomon and make his throne greater than the throne of my lord King David.
\p
\v 38 So Zadok the priest and Nathan the prophet and Benaiah the son of Jehoiada and the Cherethites and the Pelethites went down and had Solomon ride on King David's mule and brought him to Gihon.
\v 39 There Zadok the priest took the horn of oil from the tent and anointed Solomon — then they blew the trumpet and all the people said: Long live King Solomon.
\v 40 And all the people went up after him playing on pipes and rejoicing with great joy so that the earth was split by their noise.
\p
\v 41 Adonijah and all the guests who were with him heard it as they finished eating — and when Joab heard the sound of the trumpet he said: What does this uproar in the city mean?
\v 42 While he was still speaking Jonathan the son of Abiathar the priest came. And Adonijah said: Come in — for you are a worthy man and bring good news.
\v 43 Jonathan answered Adonijah: No — for our lord King David has made Solomon king —
\v 44 and the king has sent with him Zadok the priest and Nathan the prophet and Benaiah the son of Jehoiada and the Cherethites and the Pelethites and they had him ride on the king's mule.
\v 45 And Zadok the priest and Nathan the prophet have anointed him king at Gihon and they have gone up from there rejoicing so that the city is in an uproar. That is the noise that you have heard.
\v 46 Solomon sits on the royal throne.
\v 47 Moreover the king's servants came to congratulate our lord King David saying: May your God make the name of Solomon more famous than yours and make his throne greater than your throne. And the king bowed himself on the bed.
\v 48 And the king also said: Blessed be the Lord the God of Israel who has granted someone to sit on my throne this day — my own eyes seeing it.
\p
\v 49 Then all the guests of Adonijah trembled and rose and each went his own way.
\v 50 And Adonijah feared Solomon — so he arose and went and took hold of the horns of the altar.
\v 51 Then it was told Solomon: Look — Adonijah fears King Solomon — for look — he has laid hold of the horns of the altar saying: Let King Solomon swear to me first that he will not put his servant to death with the sword.
\v 52 And Solomon said: If he will show himself a worthy man not one of his hairs shall fall to the earth — but if wickedness is found in him he shall die.
\v 53 So King Solomon sent and they brought him down from the altar. And he came and paid homage to King Solomon and Solomon said to him: Go to your house.

\v 1 When David's time to die drew near he commanded Solomon his son saying:
\v 2 I am about to go the way of all the earth — be strong and show yourself a man —
\v 3 and keep the charge of the Lord your God — walking in his ways and keeping his statutes his commandments his rules and his testimonies as it is written in the Law of Moses — that you may prosper in all that you do and wherever you turn —
\v 4 that the Lord may establish his word that he spoke concerning me saying: If your sons pay close attention to their way — to walk before me in faithfulness with all their heart and with all their soul — you shall not lack a man on the throne of Israel.
\v 5 Moreover you also know what Joab the son of Zeruiah did to me — how he dealt with the two commanders of the armies of Israel — Abner the son of Ner and Amasa the son of Jether — how he killed them and put the blood of war on the belt around his waist and on the sandals on his feet.
\v 6 Act therefore according to your wisdom — but do not let his gray head go down to Sheol in peace.
\v 7 But deal loyally with the sons of Barzillai the Gileadite and let them be among those who eat at your table — for with such loyalty they met me when I fled from Absalom your brother.
\v 8 And look — there is with you Shimei the son of Gera the Benjaminite from Bahurim who cursed me with a grievous curse on the day when I went to Mahanaim — but when he came down to meet me at the Jordan I swore to him by the Lord: I will not put you to death with the sword.
\v 9 Now therefore do not hold him guiltless — for you are a wise man. You will know what you ought to do to him and you shall bring his gray head down with blood to Sheol.
\p
\v 10 Then David slept with his fathers and was buried in the city of David.
\v 11 And the time that David reigned over Israel was forty years — seven years he reigned in Hebron and thirty-three years he reigned in Jerusalem.
\v 12 So Solomon sat on the throne of David his father and his kingdom was firmly established.
\p
\v 13 Then Adonijah the son of Haggith came to Bathsheba the mother of Solomon — and she said: Do you come peaceably? He said: Peaceably.
\v 14 Then he said: I have something to say to you. She said: Speak.
\v 15 He said: You know that the kingdom was mine and that all Israel fully expected me to reign — however the kingdom has turned about and become my brother's for it was his from the Lord.
\v 16 And now I have one request to make of you — do not refuse me. She said to him: Speak.
\v 17 And he said: Please ask King Solomon — he will not refuse you — to give me Abishag the Shunammite as my wife.
\v 18 Bathsheba said: Very well — I will speak for you to the king.
\p
\v 19 So Bathsheba went to King Solomon to speak to him on behalf of Adonijah — and the king rose to meet her and bowed down to her then sat on his throne — and had a seat brought for the king's mother and she sat on his right.
\v 20 Then she said: I have one small request to make of you — do not refuse me. And the king said to her: Make your request my mother — for I will not refuse you.
\v 21 She said: Let Abishag the Shunammite be given to Adonijah your brother as his wife.
\v 22 King Solomon answered his mother: And why do you ask Abishag the Shunammite for Adonijah? Ask for him the kingdom also — for he is my older brother — and on his side are Abiathar the priest and Joab the son of Zeruiah.
\v 23 Then King Solomon swore by the Lord saying: God do so to me and more also if this word does not cost Adonijah his life.
\v 24 Now therefore as the Lord lives who has established me and placed me on the throne of David my father and who has made me a house as he promised — Adonijah shall be put to death today.
\v 25 So King Solomon sent Benaiah the son of Jehoiada and he struck him down and he died.
\p
\v 26 And to Abiathar the priest the king said: Go to Anathoth to your estate — for you deserve death. But I will not at this time put you to death because you carried the ark of the Lord God before David my father and because you shared in all my father's affliction.
\v 27 So Solomon expelled Abiathar from being priest to the Lord — fulfilling the word of the Lord that he had spoken concerning the house of Eli at Shiloh.
\p
\v 28 When the news came to Joab — for Joab had supported Adonijah though he had not supported Absalom — Joab fled to the tent of the Lord and caught hold of the horns of the altar.
\v 29 And it was told King Solomon: Joab has fled to the tent of the Lord and look — he is beside the altar. Then Solomon sent Benaiah the son of Jehoiada saying: Go strike him down.
\v 30 So Benaiah came to the tent of the Lord and said to him: The king says: Come out. But he said: No — I will die here. Then Benaiah brought the king word again saying: Thus said Joab and thus he answered me.
\v 31 The king replied to him: Do as he has said — strike him down and bury him and thus take away from me and from my father's house the guilt for the blood that Joab shed without cause.
\v 32 The Lord will bring back his bloody deeds on his own head because without the knowledge of my father David he attacked and killed with the sword two men more righteous and better than himself — Abner the son of Ner commander of the army of Israel and Amasa the son of Jether commander of the army of Judah.
\v 33 So shall their blood come back on the head of Joab and on the head of his descendants forever — but for David and for his descendants and for his house and for his throne there shall be peace from the Lord forevermore.
\v 34 Then Benaiah the son of Jehoiada went up and struck him down and killed him — and he was buried in his own house in the wilderness.
\v 35 The king put Benaiah the son of Jehoiada over the army in place of Joab and the king put Zadok the priest in the place of Abiathar.
\p
\v 36 Then the king sent and summoned Shimei and said to him: Build yourself a house in Jerusalem and dwell there and do not go out from there to any place whatever.
\v 37 For on the day you go out and cross the brook Kidron know for certain that you shall die — your blood shall be on your own head.
\v 38 And Shimei said to the king: What you say is good — as my lord the king has said so will your servant do. So Shimei lived in Jerusalem many days.
\p
\v 39 But at the end of three years two of Shimei's servants ran away to Achish the son of Maacah king of Gath — and they told Shimei: Look — your servants are in Gath.
\v 40 So Shimei arose and saddled a donkey and went to Gath to Achish to seek his servants — he went and brought his servants from Gath.
\v 41 And Solomon was told: Shimei went from Jerusalem to Gath and has returned.
\v 42 So the king sent and summoned Shimei and said to him: Did I not make you swear by the Lord and solemnly warn you saying: Know for certain that on the day you go out and go to any place whatever your blood shall be on your own head? And you said to me: What you say is good — I will obey.
\v 43 Why then have you not kept your oath to the Lord and the commandment with which I commanded you?
\v 44 The king also said to Shimei: You know in your own heart all the harm that you did to David my father — so the Lord will bring back your harm on your own head.
\v 45 But King Solomon shall be blessed and the throne of David shall be established before the Lord forever.
\v 46 Then the king commanded Benaiah the son of Jehoiada and he went out and struck him down and he died. The kingdom was established in the hand of Solomon.

\v 1 Solomon made a marriage alliance with Pharaoh king of Egypt and took Pharaoh's daughter and brought her into the city of David until he had finished building his own house and the house of the Lord and the wall around Jerusalem.
\v 2 The people were sacrificing at the high places however because no house had yet been built for the name of the Lord.
\v 3 Solomon loved the Lord — walking in the statutes of David his father — only he sacrificed and made offerings at the high places.
\v 4 And the king went to Gibeon to sacrifice there — for that was the great high place — Solomon used to offer a thousand burnt offerings on that altar.
\p
\v 5 At Gibeon the Lord appeared to Solomon in a dream by night — and God said: Ask what I shall give you.
\v 6 And Solomon said: You have shown great and steadfast love to your servant David my father because he walked before you in faithfulness in righteousness and in uprightness of heart toward you — and you have kept for him this great and steadfast love and have given him a son to sit on his throne this day.
\v 7 And now O Lord my God you have made your servant king in place of David my father — although I am but a little child and do not know how to go out or come in.
\v 8 And your servant is in the midst of your people whom you have chosen — a great people too many to be numbered or counted for multitude.
\v 9 Give your servant therefore an understanding heart (*kardian akouein*) to govern your people — that I may discern between good and evil — for who is able to govern this your great people?
\v 10 It pleased the Lord that Solomon had asked this.
\v 11 And God said to him: Because you have asked this and have not asked for yourself long life or riches or the life of your enemies but have asked for yourself understanding to discern what is right —
\v 12 look — I now do according to your word — look — I give you a wise and discerning heart (*kardian phronimen kai sophēn*) so that none like you has been before you and none like you shall arise after you.
\v 13 I give you also what you have not asked — both riches and honor — so that no other king shall compare with you all your days.
\v 14 And if you will walk in my ways keeping my statutes and my commandments as your father David walked then I will lengthen your days.
\v 15 And Solomon awoke and look — it was a dream. Then he came to Jerusalem and stood before the ark of the covenant of the Lord and offered up burnt offerings and peace offerings and made a feast for all his servants.
\p
\v 16 Then two prostitutes came to the king and stood before him.
\v 17 The one woman said: Oh my lord — this woman and I live in the same house and I gave birth to a child while she was in the house.
\v 18 Then on the third day after I gave birth this woman also gave birth — and we were alone — there was no one else with us in the house — only we two were in the house.
\v 19 And this woman's son died in the night because she lay on him.
\v 20 And she arose at midnight and took my son from beside me while your servant slept and laid him at her breast and laid her dead son at my breast.
\v 21 When I rose in the morning to nurse my child — look — he was dead. But when I looked at him closely in the morning — look — it was not the child that I had borne.
\v 22 But the other woman said: No — the living child is mine and the dead child is yours. The first said: No — the dead child is yours and the living child is mine. Thus they spoke before the king.
\v 23 Then the king said: The one says: This is my son that is alive and your son is dead. And the other says: No — but your son is dead and my son is the living one.
\v 24 And the king said: Bring me a sword. So a sword was brought before the king.
\v 25 And the king said: Divide the living child in two and give half to the one and half to the other.
\v 26 Then the woman whose son was alive said to the king — because her heart yearned for her son — O my lord give her the living child and by no means put him to death. But the other said: He shall be neither mine nor yours — divide him.
\v 27 Then the king answered and said: Give the living child to the first woman and by no means put him to death — she is his mother.
\v 28 And all Israel heard of the judgment that the king had rendered — and they stood in awe of the king because they perceived that the wisdom of God was in him to do justice.

\v 1 Now Hiram king of Tyre sent his servants to Solomon when he heard that they had anointed him king in place of his father — for Hiram always loved David.
\v 2 And Solomon sent word to Hiram:
\v 3 You know that David my father could not build a house for the name of the Lord his God because of the warfare with which his enemies surrounded him until the Lord put them under the soles of his feet.
\v 4 But now the Lord my God has given me rest on every side — there is neither adversary nor misfortune.
\v 5 And I intend to build a house for the name of the Lord my God as the Lord said to David my father: Your son whom I will set on your throne in your place shall build the house for my name.
\v 6 Now therefore command that cedars of Lebanon be cut for me — and my servants will join your servants and I will pay you for your servants such wages as you set — for you know that there is no one among us who knows how to cut timber like the Sidonians.
\p
\v 7 As soon as Hiram heard the words of Solomon he rejoiced greatly and said: Blessed be the Lord this day who has given to David a wise son to be over this great people.
\v 8 And Hiram sent to Solomon saying: I have heard the message you have sent to me — I am ready to do all you desire in the matter of cedar and cypress timber.
\v 9 My servants shall bring it down to the sea from Lebanon and I will make it into rafts to go by sea to the place you direct — and I will have them broken up there and you shall receive it — and you shall meet my wishes by providing food for my household.
\v 10 So Hiram supplied Solomon with all the timber of cedar and cypress that he desired —
\v 11 while Solomon gave Hiram twenty thousand cors of wheat as food for his household and twenty thousand cors of beaten oil — Solomon gave this to Hiram year by year.
\v 12 And the Lord gave Solomon wisdom as he promised him — and there was peace between Hiram and Solomon and the two of them made a covenant.
\v 13 King Solomon drafted forced labor out of all Israel — and the draft numbered thirty thousand men.
\v 14 And he sent them to Lebanon ten thousand a month in shifts — they would be a month in Lebanon and two months at home — Adoniram was in charge of the draft.
\v 15 Solomon also had seventy thousand burden-bearers and eighty thousand quarrymen in the hill country —
\v 16 besides Solomon's three thousand three hundred chief officers who were over the work who had charge of the people who carried on the work.
\v 17 At the king's command they quarried out great costly stones in order to lay the foundation of the house with dressed stones.
\v 18 So Solomon's builders and Hiram's builders and the men of Gebal did the cutting and prepared the timber and the stone to build the house.

\c 6
\p
\v 1 In the four hundred and eightieth year after the sons of Israel came out of the land of Egypt — in the fourth year of Solomon's reign over Israel — in the month of Ziv which is the second month — he began to build the house of the Lord.
\v 2 The house that King Solomon built for the Lord — its length was sixty cubits its breadth twenty cubits and its height thirty cubits.
\v 3 The vestibule in front of the nave of the house was twenty cubits long equal to the width of the house and ten cubits deep in front of the house.
\v 4 And he made for the house windows with recessed frames.
\v 5 He also built a structure against the wall of the house running around the walls of the house both the nave and the inner sanctuary — and he made side chambers all around.
\v 6 The lowest story was five cubits broad the middle one was six cubits broad and the third was seven cubits broad — for around the outside of the house he made offsets on the wall in order that the supporting beams should not be inserted into the walls of the house.
\v 7 When the house was built it was with stone prepared at the quarry so that neither hammer nor axe nor any tool of iron was heard in the house while it was being built.
\v 8 The entrance for the lowest story was on the south side of the house and one went up by stairs to the middle story and from the middle story to the third.
\v 9 So he built the house and finished it and he covered the house with beams and planks of cedar.
\v 10 He built the structure against the whole house five cubits high and it was joined to the house with timbers of cedar.
\p
\v 11 Now the word of the Lord came to Solomon:
\v 12 Concerning this house that you are building — if you will walk in my statutes and obey my rules and keep all my commandments and walk in them — then I will establish my word with you which I spoke to David your father.
\v 13 And I will dwell among the sons of Israel and will not forsake my people Israel.
\p
\v 14 So Solomon built the house and finished it.
\v 15 He lined the walls of the house on the inside with boards of cedar — from the floor of the house to the walls of the ceiling he covered them on the inside with wood and he covered the floor of the house with boards of cypress.
\v 16 He built twenty cubits of the rear of the house with boards of cedar from the floor to the ceiling — he built this within as an inner sanctuary as the most holy place.
\v 17 The house — that is the nave in front of the inner sanctuary — was forty cubits long.
\v 18 The cedar within the house was carved in the form of gourds and open flowers — all was cedar — no stone was seen.
\v 19 The inner sanctuary he prepared in the innermost part of the house to set there the ark of the covenant of the Lord.
\v 20 The inner sanctuary was twenty cubits long twenty cubits wide and twenty cubits high — he overlaid it with pure gold and he made an altar of cedar.
\v 21 So Solomon overlaid the inside of the house with pure gold and he drew chains of gold across in front of the inner sanctuary and overlaid it with gold.
\v 22 And he overlaid the whole house with gold until all the house was finished. Also the whole altar that belonged to the inner sanctuary he overlaid with gold.
\v 23 In the inner sanctuary he made two cherubim of olivewood each ten cubits high.
\v 24 Five cubits was the length of one wing of the cherub and five cubits the length of the other wing of the cherub — it was ten cubits from the tip of one wing to the tip of the other.
\v 25 The other cherub also measured ten cubits — both cherubim had the same measure and the same form.
\v 26 The height of one cherub was ten cubits and so was the other cherub.
\v 27 He put the cherubim in the innermost part of the house — and the wings of the cherubim were spread out so that a wing of one touched the one wall and a wing of the other cherub touched the other wall — their other wings touched each other in the middle of the house.
\v 28 And he overlaid the cherubim with gold.
\v 29 Around all the walls of the house he carved engraved figures of cherubim and palm trees and open flowers in the inner and outer rooms.
\v 30 The floor of the house he overlaid with gold in the inner and outer rooms.
\v 31 For the entrance to the inner sanctuary he made doors of olivewood — the lintel and the doorposts formed a pentagon.
\v 32 He covered the two doors of olivewood with carvings of cherubim palm trees and open flowers. He overlaid them with gold and spread gold on the cherubim and on the palm trees.
\v 33 So also he made for the entrance to the nave doorposts of olivewood in the form of a square —
\v 34 and two doors of cypress wood — the two leaves of the one door were folding and the two leaves of the other door were folding.
\v 35 On them he carved cherubim and palm trees and open flowers and he overlaid them with gold evenly applied on the carved work.
\v 36 He built the inner court with three courses of cut stone and one course of cedar beams.
\p
\v 37 In the fourth year the foundation of the house of the Lord was laid in the month of Ziv.
\v 38 And in the eleventh year in the month of Bul which is the eighth month the house was finished in all its parts and according to all its specifications — he was seven years in building it.

\v 1 Solomon was building his own house thirteen years and he finished his entire house.
\p
\v 2 He built the House of the Forest of Lebanon — its length was a hundred cubits its breadth fifty cubits and its height thirty cubits — built on four rows of cedar pillars with cedar beams on the pillars.
\v 3 And it was covered with cedar above the chambers that were on the forty-five pillars — fifteen in each row.
\v 4 There were window frames in three rows and window opposite window in three tiers.
\v 5 All the doorways and windows had square frames and window was opposite window in three tiers.
\p
\v 13 And King Solomon sent and brought Hiram from Tyre.
\v 14 He was the son of a widow of the tribe of Naphtali and his father was a man of Tyre — a worker in bronze. And he was full of wisdom understanding and skill for making any work in bronze — he came to King Solomon and did all his work.
\v 15 He cast two pillars of bronze. Eighteen cubits was the height of one pillar and a line of twelve cubits measured its circumference — it was hollow and the thickness of the metal was four fingers.
\v 16 He also made two capitals of cast bronze to set on the tops of the pillars — the height of the one capital was five cubits and the height of the other capital was five cubits.
\v 17 There were lattices of checker work with wreaths of chain work for the capitals on the tops of the pillars — seven for the one capital and seven for the other capital.
\v 18 Likewise he made pomegranates in two rows around the one latticework to cover the capital that was on the top of the pillar — and he did the same with the other capital.

## CAE NODES — 3 KINGDOMS

### 3 Kingdoms 3:9 — *kardia akouein* / a hearing heart — CAE PRIMARY NODE

**3:9** — "Give your servant therefore an understanding heart (*kardian akouein*) to govern your people — that I may discern between good and evil."

**3:12** — "I give you a wise and discerning heart (*kardian phronimen kai sophēn*) so that none like you has been before you and none like you shall arise after you."

***kardian akouein*** = a heart to hear / a hearing heart. The LXX renders the Hebrew *lēb shomēa'* = a listening heart. Solomon asks not for wealth or victory or long life but for a *hearing heart* — the capacity to listen to God and to discern rightly.

The semantic structure is important: *akouein* = to hear is the same root as the *akouein hyper thusian* = obedience above sacrifice of 1 Sam 15:22. The hearing-heart is the fulfillment of the Samuel-principle at the level of the inner person. Solomon asks for the very thing Saul lacked.

**Proverbs 2:1–6** — "If you receive my words and treasure up my commandments with you — making your ear attentive to wisdom and inclining your heart to understanding... then you will understand the fear of the Lord." The Proverbs *wisdom* vocabulary is the literary development of Solomon's *kardia akouein* request.

**1 Kings 4:29 (LXX 3 Kgdms)** — "God gave Solomon wisdom and understanding beyond measure and breadth of mind like the sand on the seashore." The divine response to the *kardia akouein* request: wisdom that exceeds all others.

**Matthew 13:9, 43** — "He who has ears let him hear (*akouein*)." Jesus's recurring *akouein* refrain in the parable discourse is the NT deployment of the *kardia akouein* principle: the hearing heart is the condition for receiving the kingdom's wisdom. The parable of the sower (Matt 13:18–23) is the taxonomy of hearing-heart failures and successes.

**James 1:5** — "If any of you lacks wisdom let him ask God who gives generously to all without reproach and it will be given him." James's wisdom-prayer invitation is the NT equivalent of Solomon's Gibeon prayer — the same structure: ask God for wisdom / God gives generously.

### 3 Kingdoms 6:7 — the Temple built in silence — CAE NODE

**6:7** — "When the house was built it was with stone prepared at the quarry so that neither hammer nor axe nor any tool of iron was heard in the house while it was being built."

***kai lithos akoustos ouk ēkousthē en tō oikō en tō oikodomesthai*** = and no sound of stone-working was heard in the house while it was being built. The Temple was built in silence — all the noise of preparation happened offsite; the assembly itself was silent.

**John 2:19–21** — "Destroy this temple and in three days I will raise it up... but he was speaking about the temple of his body." Jesus's body as the true temple — built not with iron tools but with incarnation and resurrection. The silence of the Temple-building is the type of the silent creative act: the incarnation is not announced with noise but accomplished in hiddenness.

**Ephesians 2:20–22** — "built on the foundation of the apostles and prophets Christ Jesus himself being the cornerstone — in whom the whole structure being joined together grows into a holy temple in the Lord." The living Temple is assembled not with iron tools but by the Spirit — the same principle of silent prepared-stone assembly.

### 3 Kingdoms 8:22–30 / 8:41–43 — Solomon's Temple prayer / the foreigner — CAE PRIMARY NODE

**8:27** — "But will God indeed dwell on the earth? Look — heaven and the highest heaven cannot contain you — how much less this house that I have built."

**8:30** — "And when you hear forgive."

**8:41–43** — "Likewise when a foreigner who is not of your people Israel comes from a far country for your name's sake... when he comes and prays toward this house — hear in heaven your dwelling place and do according to all for which the foreigner calls to you — in order that all the peoples of the earth may know your name and fear you as do your people Israel."

Three critical theological declarations in Solomon's prayer:

**1. Divine transcendence over the Temple** (8:27) — *ho ouranos kai ho ouranos tou ouranou ou mē chōrēsousin* = heaven and the heaven of heavens will not contain you. The Temple does not domesticate God — it is the appointed meeting-place of the uncontainable God. This is the most explicit OT statement of divine transcendence over any cultic location.

**Isaiah 66:1–2** — "Thus says the Lord: Heaven is my throne and the earth is my footstool — what is the house that you would build for me?" Isaiah's temple-transcendence is the prophetic development of Solomon's 8:27 declaration.

**Acts 7:48–50** — Stephen's speech: "the Most High does not dwell in houses made by hands — as the prophet says: Heaven is my throne and earth is my footstool." Stephen cites Isaiah 66:1 in the context of the Temple destruction warning — grounding the citation in the same theological principle Solomon declared at the Temple's dedication. The chain: 1 Kgdms 8:27 → Isa 66:1 → Acts 7:48–50.

**2. Prayer as the appointed channel** (8:28–30) — Seven *shema* = hear petitions in the prayer (vv.30, 32, 34, 36, 39, 43, 45) — the Temple is the place toward which prayer is directed and from which God hears and forgives. The Temple's function is not to contain God but to orient prayer.

**Daniel 6:10** — "He got down on his knees three times a day and prayed and gave thanks before his God — as he had done previously." Daniel in Babylon prays toward Jerusalem — the 8:48 petition ("pray toward their land which you gave to their fathers... the city you have chosen") is fulfilled in Daniel's daily practice.

**3. The foreigner's prayer** (8:41–43) — the Temple prayer explicitly includes the *allotrios* = foreigner who comes from a far country. This is the most explicit pre-prophetic statement of universal Gentile inclusion in Israel's God. The purpose stated: "that all the peoples of the earth may know your name."

**Isaiah 56:7** — "for my house shall be called a house of prayer for all peoples." Isaiah's oracle develops Solomon's 8:43 petition. This is the verse Jesus quotes when cleansing the Temple (Matt 21:13 / Mark 11:17 / Luke 19:46).

**Mark 11:17** — "Is it not written: My house shall be called a house of prayer for all the nations? But you have made it a den of robbers." Jesus's Temple cleansing citation (Isaiah 56:7 citing Solomon's 8:43 intent) is the prophetic indictment that the Temple has failed its stated universal purpose. The chain: 1 Kgdms 8:41–43 → Isa 56:7 → Mark 11:17.

### 3 Kingdoms 8:56 — *ou diepesen rhēma hen* / not one word has failed — CAE PRIMARY NODE

**8:56** — "Blessed be the Lord who has given rest to his people Israel according to all that he promised — not one word has failed of all his good promise which he spoke by Moses his servant."

***ouk diepesen rhēma hen apo pantōn tōn rhēmatōn autou tōn agathōn***

= not one word has fallen from all his good words. The *diepiptō* = fall / fail applied to the divine word — the oath of complete fulfillment.

**Joshua 21:45 (LXX)** — "Not one word (*ou diepesen rhēma hen*) fell from all the good things that the Lord spoke to the house of Israel — all came to pass." The exact phrase appears first in Joshua 21:45 at the completion of the conquest — Solomon's Temple-dedication prayer quotes Joshua's completion-declaration. The Joshua rest (genuine but typological) → the Solomon rest (fuller but still typological).

**2 Corinthians 1:20** — "For all the promises of God find their Yes in him (*en autō to Nai*)." Paul's declaration that every divine promise is confirmed in Christ is the NT fulfillment of the *ou diepesen rhēma hen* chain: the word that did not fail in Joshua / the word that did not fail at the Temple dedication / the word that finds its ultimate Yes in the resurrection.

### 3 Kingdoms 11:36 — *lychnos* / a lamp for David — CAE NODE

**11:36** — "Yet to his son I will give one tribe that David my servant may always have a lamp (*lychnos*) before me in Jerusalem the city where I have chosen to put my name."

***tou einai tō Dauid doulō mou lychnon pasas tas hēmeras enōpion emou en Ierousalēm***

= that David my servant may have a lamp all the days before me in Jerusalem. The *lychnos* = lamp of David is the covenant-survival language: even in the division of the kingdom / even in judgment / the Davidic lamp is not extinguished. The lamp = the continuing Davidic lineage through which the covenant promise is preserved.

**Psalm 132:17** — "There I will make a horn to sprout for David — I have prepared a lamp (*lychnon*) for my anointed." The Psalm 132 lamp-promise is the liturgical expression of the 1 Kgdms 11:36 royal-grant.

**Luke 1:78–79** — "Through the tender mercy of our God — the rising dawn (*anatolē*) from on high will visit us — to give light to those who sit in darkness and in the shadow of death." The *anatolē* = Branch/Rising-sun of Luke 1:78 is the fulfillment of the Davidic lamp-promise: the lamp that was never to be extinguished in Jerusalem is the dawn that rises from the Davidic line.

**Revelation 21:23** — "And the city has no need of sun or moon to shine on it — for the glory of God gives it light and its lamp (*lychnos*) is the Lamb." The *lychnos* of the New Jerusalem is the Lamb — the Davidic lamp-promise fulfilled in its eschatological form: not a human king maintaining the Davidic line but the Lamb himself as the eternal lamp.

### 3 Kingdoms 17–19 — Elijah cycle — CAE PRIMARY NODE

**17:1** — "As the Lord the God of Israel lives before whom I stand (*parestēka*) — there shall be neither dew nor rain these years except by my word."

**17:21–22** — Elijah stretches himself on the widow's dead son three times / the child's *psychē* returns.

**18:21** — "How long will you go limping between two different opinions? If the Lord is God follow him — but if Baal then follow him."

**18:37–39** — Fire falls on the Carmel altar / the people fall on their faces saying: "The Lord he is God — the Lord he is God."

**19:4** — "It is enough — now O Lord take away my life (*psychēn*) for I am no better than my fathers."

**19:11–12** — Wind / earthquake / fire — and after the fire: *phōnē audēs leptēs* = the sound of a low whisper / a still small voice.

**19:18** — "Yet I will leave seven thousand in Israel — all the knees that have not bowed to Baal and every mouth that has not kissed him."

The Elijah cycle is the theological hinge of 3 Kingdoms and one of the most NT-dense sections of the historical books.

**Elijah as second Moses:**
- Goes to Horeb (= Sinai) in 19:8 — the mountain of God
- The theophany sequence (wind / earthquake / fire) mirrors the Sinai theophany of Exod 19 — the divine presence passes by (19:11 *pareleusetai Kyrios* = the Lord will pass by)  exactly as in Exod 33:22 (the Lord passes by Moses on Horeb)
- Forty days and forty nights travel to Horeb (19:8) = Moses's forty days on Sinai
- The divine question "What are you doing here?" (19:9, 13) mirrors the divine conversations with Moses

**Luke 9:28–36** — The Transfiguration: Moses and Elijah appear with Jesus. The *glōria* = glory of Jesus on the mountain is the theophany that supersedes both the Sinai and the Horeb appearances. Elijah and Moses appear as witnesses to the one who fulfills what both foreshadow.

**Romans 11:2–4** — "Do you not know what the Scripture says of Elijah — how he appeals to God against Israel? 'Lord they have killed your prophets — they have torn down your altars — and I alone am left and they seek my life.' But what is God's reply to him? 'I have kept for myself seven thousand men who have not bowed the knee to Baal.'" Paul's Romans 11 use of the *heptakischilioi* = seven thousand is one of the most structurally important NT citations of the Elijah cycle: the remnant preserved by grace (not by works) is the OT ground for Paul's remnant-theology in Romans 9–11.

**James 5:17–18** — "Elijah was a man with a nature like ours and he prayed fervently that it might not rain and for three years and six months it did not rain on the earth — then he prayed again and heaven gave rain." James uses Elijah's prayer as the model of effective prayer: the *psychē*-language of Elijah's exhaustion-prayer (19:4 / "take away my *psychēn*") is the NT ground for James's bold-prayer theology.

**Luke 4:25–26** — Jesus cites the Elijah-at-Zarephath narrative: "There were many widows in Israel in the days of Elijah... and Elijah was sent to none of them but only to Zarephath in the land of Sidon — to a woman who was a widow." The Gentile widow of Zarephath is Jesus's first proof-text for the principle that the prophet is rejected in his hometown but received by Gentiles — grounding the Nazareth rejection narrative in Elijah typology.

**The still small voice** (19:12) — *phōnē audēs leptēs* = the sound of a low whisper. The theophany at Horeb deliberately inverts the Carmel theophany: Carmel = fire from heaven / thunder / the consuming answer. Horeb = the Lord is NOT in the wind / NOT in the earthquake / NOT in the fire — but in the whisper. The whisper is not less powerful than the fire — it is the divine speech that Moses heard and from which Israel fled (Exod 20:19). The contrast structures the entire prophetic-office theology: God speaks in fire to the nations at Carmel / God speaks in the whisper to the prophet at Horeb.

**Hebrews 12:18–24** — "For you have not come to what may be touched — a blazing fire and darkness and gloom and a tempest... But you have come to Mount Zion... to Jesus the mediator of a new covenant." The Hebrews contrast of Sinai (fire / thunder / unapproachable) with Zion (the new covenant / the whisper-register) is grounded in the Horeb theophany sequence of 1 Kgdms 19.

### 3 Kingdoms 21:3 — *naḥalat paterwn* / ancestral inheritance — CAE NODE

**21:3** — "But Naboth said to Ahab: The Lord forbid that I should give you the inheritance of my fathers (*tēn klēronomian paterwn mou*)."

The Naboth narrative is the concentrated OT test case for the theology of *klēronomia* = inheritance / land held under covenant. Naboth's refusal is not stubbornness — it is covenant-faithfulness: the land belongs to the Lord and was distributed as *klēronomia* to families in perpetuity (Lev 25:23 — "the land shall not be sold in perpetuity for the land is mine").

**Jezebel's judicial murder** is the anti-covenant act: using the legal forms (fasting / two witnesses / accusation) to steal what belongs to the covenant order. The two false witnesses echo the Deuteronomy false-witness law (Deut 17:6 / 19:15) being weaponized against the covenant it was designed to protect.

**Elijah's confrontation** (21:17–19) — the prophet stands between the covenant-order and the royal power that has violated it. This is the prophetic office in its structural function: speaking the covenant-word to power.

**Luke 12:13–21** — Jesus's parable of the rich fool begins with a dispute over inheritance (*klēronomia*) — Jesus refuses to be a judge in the division and then tells the parable. The Naboth-register underlies the parable: the one who builds bigger barns to secure what he has accumulated ignores the covenant-framework within which all possession is held.

### 3 Kingdoms 22:19–23 — Micaiah's vision / the lying breath-of-life — CAE NODE

**22:19–22** — "I saw the Lord sitting on his throne and all the host of heaven standing beside him on his right hand and on his left... Then a breath-of-life (*pneuma*) came forward and stood before the Lord saying: I will entice him... I will be a lying breath-of-life in the mouth of all his prophets."

***kai exēlthen to pneuma kai estē enōpion Kyriou kai eipen... kai estai pneuma pseudos en stomati pantōn tōn prophētōn autou***

The heavenly-council vision is the most explicit OT account of the divine permission of prophetic deception. The *pneuma pseudos* = lying breath-of-life operates within the divine sovereignty — Ahab's death is the Lord's judgment and the lying prophets are the instrument. The *pneuma* vocabulary here is in the Folly-register: *pseudos* = lie is the deception-death axis of the CAE valence test.

**1 Kings 22:34** — Ahab dies by the arrow drawn "at random" (*en tē aplōtēti*) — the narrative irony confirms Micaiah's word: the king who rejected the true prophet and imprisoned him dies by an accidental shot that was not accidental at all.

**1 Corinthians 14:29** — "Let two or three prophets speak and let the others weigh what is said." Paul's prophetic discernment command is the NT response to the Micaiah problem: the 400-versus-1 situation of 3 Kingdoms 22 demonstrates that prophetic consensus is not the criterion of truth — weighing is required.

**2 Thessalonians 2:11** — "God sends them a strong delusion so that they may believe what is false." The *pneuma pseudos* = lying breath-of-life sent to Ahab's prophets is the OT pattern for the eschatological deception of 2 Thess 2:11: the divine permission of delusion as judgment on those who refused the truth.

## VOCABULARY DECISIONS — 3 KINGDOMS

**Kardia** — heart. Heart-lock enforced throughout. The *kardia akouein* = hearing heart of 3:9 is the book's central anthropological term — the inner organ of listening and discernment.

**Pneuma** — breath-of-life. The *pneuma pseudos* = lying breath-of-life (22:22–23) is the most theologically challenging *pneuma* occurrence in the historical books. The Vocabulary Lock Table is enforced: *pneuma* = breath-of-life throughout, with the *pseudos* modifier identifying the Folly-register valence. The divine sovereignty over both Wisdom-register and Folly-register *pneuma* agents is the consistent pattern across 1–3 Kingdoms (Saul's evil *pneuma* / Elijah's *pneuma* of the Lord / the lying *pneuma* to Ahab's prophets).

**Psychē** — soul/life. In the Elijah cycle *psychē* carries the vital-life register: the widow's son's *psychē* returns (17:22) / Elijah asks that his *psychē* be taken (19:4). The *psychē* = the life that departs and is restored — not merely the inner self.

**Klēronomia** — inheritance. Naboth's *klēronomia* (21:3) activates the covenant-land theology: the land held under covenant cannot be alienated by royal power. The *klēronomia* vocabulary chain running through the Pentateuch (Num 36 / Deut 19 / Josh 13–22) reaches its historical test case in Naboth's vineyard.

## STRUCTURAL NOTE — 3 KINGDOMS AS SOLOMON'S GLORY AND ISRAEL'S FRACTURE

3 Kingdoms has three acts:

**Act 1 — Solomon's establishment and wisdom (1–4):** The succession secured / David's final charge / Solomon's wisdom at Gibeon / the just judgment.

**Act 2 — The Temple and its theology (5–10):** Construction / dedication / Solomon's prayer / the queen of Sheba. The theological center is 8:27 (*heaven cannot contain you*) and 8:41–43 (the foreigner's prayer).

**Act 3 — Solomon's fall and the kingdom's fracture (11–22):** The foreign wives / the divided kingdom / Jeroboam's calves / the prophetic protests (Ahijah / the unnamed man of God at Bethel / Elijah) / Ahab and Jezebel / Micaiah's vision.

The structural theology: the same king who asked for a *kardia akouein* = hearing heart and received the greatest divine wisdom ends by not listening. The *kardia* that heard God at Gibeon turns to idols in old age. This is not a contradiction — it is the book's argument: wisdom received as a gift does not guarantee wisdom maintained. The covenant requires continuous *akouein* — ongoing listening — not a one-time endowment.

The fracture of the kingdom under Rehoboam is presented not as a political accident but as a divine act (12:15 — "for it was a turn of affairs brought about by the Lord that he might fulfill his word which the Lord spoke by Ahijah"). The *diathēkē* = covenant judgment of Solomon's idolatry produces the division — but the Davidic *lychnos* = lamp is preserved in the one tribe. The covenant survives the fracture.

**Acts 13:22** — Paul's Pisidian Antioch sermon moves directly from David to Jesus — Solomon is bypassed. The theological significance: the Davidic covenant fulfillment runs through David's greater Son, not through Solomon's Temple. The Temple is the interim dwelling — the Body is the true sanctuary (John 2:21).

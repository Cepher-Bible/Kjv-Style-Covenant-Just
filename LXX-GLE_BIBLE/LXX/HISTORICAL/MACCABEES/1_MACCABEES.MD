\c 1
\p
\v 1 After Alexander the Macedonian son of Philip who came from the land of Kittim had defeated Darius the king of the Persians and Medes he succeeded him as king — he had previously become king of Greece.
\v 2 He fought many battles and conquered strongholds and put to death the kings of the earth.
\v 3 He advanced to the ends of the earth and plundered many nations. When the earth became quiet before him he was exalted and his heart (*kardia*) was lifted up.
\v 4 He gathered a very strong army and ruled over countries nations and princes and they became tributary to him.
\v 5 After this he fell sick and perceived that he was dying.
\v 6 So he summoned his most honored officers who had been brought up with him from youth and divided his kingdom among them while he was still alive.
\v 7 And Alexander reigned twelve years and then he died.
\v 8 Then his officers began to rule each in his own place.
\v 9 They all put on crowns after his death and so did their sons after them for many years — and they caused many evils on the earth.
\p
\v 10 From them came forth a sinful root — Antiochus Epiphanes son of King Antiochus — who had been a hostage in Rome. He began to reign in the one hundred and thirty-seventh year of the kingdom of the Greeks.
\p
\v 11 In those days certain renegades came out from Israel and misled many saying: Let us go and make a covenant with the Gentiles around us — for since we separated from them many disasters have come upon us.
\v 12 This proposal pleased them —
\v 13 and some of the people eagerly went to the king who authorized them to observe the ordinances of the Gentiles.
\v 14 So they built a gymnasium in Jerusalem according to Gentile custom —
\v 15 and removed the marks of circumcision and abandoned the holy covenant. They joined with the Gentiles and sold themselves to do evil.
\p
\v 20 After subduing Egypt Antiochus returned in the one hundred and forty-third year and went up against Israel and came to Jerusalem with a strong force.
\v 21 He arrogantly entered the sanctuary and took the golden altar the lampstand for the light and all its utensils —
\v 22 and the table for the bread of the Presence and the cups for drink offerings and the bowls and the golden censers and the curtain and the crowns and the golden decoration on the front of the temple — he stripped it all off.
\v 23 He took the silver and the gold and the costly vessels — he took also the hidden treasures that he found.
\v 24 Taking them all he went into his own land. He shed much blood and spoke with great arrogance.
\p
\v 29 Two years later the king sent to the cities of Judah a chief collector of tribute — and he came to Jerusalem with a large force.
\v 30 Deceitfully he spoke peaceable words to them and they believed him. But he suddenly fell upon the city dealt it a severe blow and destroyed many people of Israel.
\v 31 He plundered the city and burned it with fire and tore down its houses and its surrounding walls.
\v 32 They took captive the women and children and seized the livestock.
\v 33 Then they fortified the city of David with a great strong wall and strong towers and it became their citadel (*akra*).
\v 36 It became an ambush against the sanctuary an evil adversary to Israel continually.
\v 37 On every side of the sanctuary they shed innocent blood — they even defiled the sanctuary itself.
\v 38 Because of them the residents of Jerusalem fled — she became a dwelling of strangers — she became strange to her own offspring and her children forsook her.
\v 39 Her sanctuary became desolate as a desert — her feasts were turned into mourning her Sabbaths into reproach her honor into contempt.
\v 40 Her dishonor now grew as great as her glory — her exaltation was turned into mourning.
\p
\v 41 Then the king wrote to his whole kingdom that all should be one people —
\v 42 and that all should give up their particular customs.
\v 43 All the Gentiles accepted the command of the king. Many even from Israel gladly adopted his religion — they sacrificed to idols and profaned the Sabbath.
\p
\v 44 And the king sent letters by messengers to Jerusalem and the towns of Judah — he directed them to follow customs strange to the land —
\v 45 to forbid burnt offerings and sacrifices and drink offerings in the sanctuary — to profane Sabbaths and festivals —
\v 46 to defile the sanctuary and the priests —
\v 47 to build altars and sacred precincts and shrines for idols — to sacrifice swine and other unclean animals —
\v 48 and to leave their sons uncircumcised.
\v 49 He directed them to make themselves abominable by everything unclean and profane —
\v 50 so that they would forget the Law and change all the ordinances.
\v 51 He added: And whoever does not obey the command of the king shall die.
\p
\v 54 Now on the fifteenth day of Chislev in the one hundred and forty-fifth year they erected a desolating sacrilege (*bdelygma erēmōseōs*) on the altar of burnt offering. They also built altars in the surrounding towns of Judah —
\v 55 and offered incense at the doors of houses and in the streets.
\v 56 The books of the Law that they found they tore to pieces and burned with fire.
\v 57 Anyone found possessing the book of the covenant or anyone who followed the Law was condemned to death by decree of the king.
\v 58 They kept using violence against Israel — against all who were found month after month in the towns.
\v 59 On the twenty-fifth day of the month they offered sacrifice on the altar that was on top of the altar of burnt offering.
\v 60 According to the decree they put to death the women who had their children circumcised —
\v 61 and they hung the infants from their mothers' necks and put to death their families and those who had circumcised them.
\v 62 But many in Israel stood firm and were resolved in their hearts (*kardia*) not to eat unclean food.
\v 63 They chose to die rather than to be defiled by food or to profane the holy covenant — and they did die.
\v 64 And very great wrath came upon Israel.

\c 2
\p
\v 1 In those days Mattathias the son of John son of Simeon a priest of the sons of Joarib moved from Jerusalem and settled at Modein.
\v 2 He had five sons — John surnamed Gaddi —
\v 3 Simon called Thassi —
\v 4 Judas called Maccabeus —
\v 5 Eleazar called Avaran — and Jonathan called Apphus.
\p
\v 6 He saw the blasphemies being committed in Judah and Jerusalem —
\v 7 and said: Alas — why was I born to see this — the ruin of my people the ruin of the holy city — and to dwell there when it was given over to the enemy and the sanctuary given over to aliens?
\v 8 Her temple has become like a man without honor —
\v 9 her glorious vessels have been carried into exile — her infants have been killed in her streets her young men have fallen by the sword of the enemy.
\v 10 What nation has not inherited her palaces and has not seized her spoils?
\v 11 All her adornment has been taken away — instead of a free woman she has become a slave.
\p
\v 12 And see — our holy place our beauty and our glory has been laid waste — the Gentiles have profaned it.
\v 13 Why should we go on living?
\p
\v 14 And Mattathias and his sons tore their clothes put on sackcloth and mourned greatly.
\p
\v 15 Then the king's officers who were enforcing the apostasy came to the city of Modein to make them offer sacrifice.
\v 16 Many from Israel came to them — and Mattathias and his sons were assembled.
\v 17 Then the king's officers spoke to Mattathias as follows: You are a leader honored and great in this city and supported by sons and brothers.
\v 18 Now be the first to come and do what the king commands as all the Gentiles and the people of Judah and those that are left in Jerusalem have done. Then you and your sons will be numbered among the Friends of the King and you and your sons will be honored with silver and gold and many gifts.
\p
\v 19 But Mattathias answered and said in a loud voice: Even if all the nations that live under the rule of the king obey him and have chosen to obey his commandments — everyone of them abandoning the religion of their ancestors —
\v 20 I and my sons and my brothers will continue to live by the covenant of our ancestors.
\v 21 Far be it from us to desert the Law and the ordinances.
\v 22 We will not obey the king's words by turning aside from our religion to the right hand or to the left.
\p
\v 23 When he had finished speaking a Jew came forward in the sight of all to offer sacrifice on the altar in Modein according to the king's command.
\v 24 When Mattathias saw it he burned with zeal and his heart (*kardia*) was stirred. He gave vent to righteous anger — ran and killed him on the altar.
\v 25 At the same time he killed the king's officer who was forcing them to sacrifice and he tore down the altar.
\v 26 Thus he burned with zeal for the Law just as Phinehas did against Zimri the son of Salu.
\p
\v 27 Then Mattathias cried out in the city with a loud voice: Let every one who is zealous for the Law and supports the covenant come out with me.
\v 28 And he and his sons fled to the hills and left all that they had in the city.
\p
\v 29 Then many who were seeking righteousness and justice went down to the wilderness to live there —
\v 31 it was reported to the king's officers and to the troops in Jerusalem the city of David that those who had rejected the king's command had gone down to the hiding places in the wilderness.
\v 32 Many pursued them and overtook them — they encamped opposite them and prepared for battle against them on the Sabbath day.
\v 33 They said to them: Enough of this — come out and do what the king commands and you will live.
\v 34 But they said: We will not come out nor will we do what the king commands and profane the Sabbath day.
\v 35 Then the enemy quickly attacked them —
\v 36 but they did not answer them or hurl a stone at them or block up their hiding places —
\v 37 for they said: Let us all die in our innocence — heaven and earth testify for us that you are killing us unjustly.
\v 38 So they attacked them on the Sabbath and they died — the men their wives their children and their livestock — about a thousand people.
\p
\v 39 When Mattathias and his friends learned of it they mourned for them deeply.
\v 40 And all said to their neighbors: If we all do as our kindred have done and refuse to fight with the Gentiles for our lives and for our ordinances they will quickly destroy us from the earth.
\v 41 So they made this decision that day: Let us fight against anyone who comes to attack us on the Sabbath day — let us not all die as our kindred died in their hiding places.
\p
\v 49 Now the days drew near for Mattathias to die and he said to his sons: Arrogance and scorn have now become strong — it is a time of ruin and furious anger.
\v 50 Now my children show zeal for the Law and give your lives for the covenant of our ancestors.
\p
\v 51 Remember the deeds of the ancestors which they did in their generations — and you will receive great honor and an everlasting name.
\v 52 Was not Abraham found faithful when tested and it was reckoned to him as righteousness?
\v 53 Joseph in the time of his distress kept the commandment and became lord of Egypt.
\v 54 Phinehas our ancestor because he was deeply zealous received the covenant of everlasting priesthood.
\v 55 Joshua because he fulfilled the command became a judge in Israel.
\v 56 Caleb because he testified in the assembly received an inheritance in the land.
\v 57 David because he was merciful inherited the throne of the kingdom forever.
\v 58 Elijah because of great zeal for the Law was taken up into heaven.
\v 59 Hananiah Azariah and Mishael believed and were saved from the flame.
\v 60 Daniel because of his innocence was delivered from the mouth of the lions.
\p
\v 61 And so observe from generation to generation that none of those who put their trust in him will lack strength.
\v 62 Do not fear the words of sinners — for their splendor will turn into dung and worms.
\v 63 Today they will be exalted but tomorrow they will not be found — because they will have returned to the dust and their plans will have perished.
\v 64 My children be courageous and grow strong in the Law — for by it you will gain honor.
\p
\v 65 Here is your brother Simon who I know is a man of counsel — listen to him always — he will be a father to you.
\v 66 Judas Maccabeus has been a mighty warrior from his youth — he shall command the army for you and fight the battle against the peoples.
\v 67 You shall rally around you all who observe the Law and avenge the wrong done to your people.
\v 68 Pay back the Gentiles in full and obey the commands of the Law.
\p
\v 69 Then he blessed them and was gathered to his ancestors.
\v 70 He died in the one hundred and forty-sixth year and was buried in the tomb of his ancestors at Modein. And all Israel mourned deeply for him.

\c 3
\p
\v 1 Then his son Judas who was called Maccabeus took command in his place.
\v 2 All his brothers and all who had joined his father helped him — they gladly fought for Israel.
\p
\v 3 He extended the glory of his people — like a giant he put on his breastplate and girded on his weapons of war — he set battles in array protecting the host with his sword.
\v 4 He was like a lion in his deeds like a lion's cub roaring for prey.
\v 5 He searched out and pursued those who broke the Law — he burned those who troubled his people.
\v 6 Lawbreakers shrank back for fear of him — all the evildoers were confounded — and deliverance prospered by his hand.
\v 7 He embittered many kings — but he made Jacob glad by his deeds and his memory is blessed forever.
\p
\v 8 He went through the cities of Judah and destroyed the ungodly out of the land and turned away wrath from Israel.
\v 9 He was renowned to the ends of the earth — he gathered in those who were perishing.
\p
\v 18 But Judas said: It is easy for many to be hemmed in by few — for in the sight of Heaven there is no difference between saving by many or by few.
\v 19 It is not on the size of the army that victory in battle depends but strength comes from Heaven.
\v 20 They come against us in great insolence and lawlessness to destroy us and our wives and our children and to despoil us —
\v 21 but we fight for our lives and our laws.
\v 22 He himself will crush them before us — as for you do not be afraid of them.
\p
\v 43 Let us restore the ruins of our people and fight for our people and the sanctuary.
\v 44 So the congregation assembled and prepared for battle and prayed and implored for mercy and compassion.
\v 45 Jerusalem was uninhabited like a wilderness — not one of her children went in or out. The sanctuary was trampled down and aliens held the citadel — it was a lodging place for the Gentiles. Joy was taken from Jacob — the flute and the harp ceased to play.
\v 46 So they assembled and came to Mizpah opposite Jerusalem — for Israel formerly had a place of prayer at Mizpah.
\v 47 They fasted that day and put on sackcloth and sprinkled ashes on their heads and tore their clothes.
\v 48 And they opened the book of the Law to inquire into those matters about which the Gentiles consulted the likenesses of their gods.
\v 49 They also brought the vestments of the priesthood and the firstfruits and the tithes and they stirred up the nazirites who had completed their days.
\v 50 And they cried aloud to Heaven saying: What shall we do with these — where shall we take them?
\v 51 Your sanctuary is trampled down and profaned — your priests are in mourning and in humiliation.
\v 52 And here the Gentiles are assembled against us to destroy us — you know what they plot against us.
\v 53 How will we be able to withstand them if you do not help us?
\v 54 Then they sounded the trumpets and gave a loud shout.

\c 4
\p
\v 6 Judas said: Do not be afraid of their numbers or dread their attack.
\v 7 Remember how our ancestors were saved at the Red Sea when Pharaoh with his forces pursued them.
\v 8 And now let us cry to Heaven to see whether he will favor us and remember his covenant with our ancestors and crush this army before us today.
\v 9 Then all the Gentiles will know that there is one who redeems and saves Israel.
\p
\v 24 On their return they sang hymns and praises to Heaven — for he is good for his mercy endures forever (*hoti agathos hoti eis ton aiōna to eleos autou*).
\v 25 Thus Israel had a great deliverance that day.
\p
\v 36 Then Judas and his brothers said: See our enemies are crushed — let us go up to cleanse the sanctuary and dedicate it.
\v 37 So all the army assembled and they went up to Mount Zion.
\v 38 There they saw the sanctuary desolate the altar profaned and the gates burned. In the courts they saw bushes sprung up as in a thicket or as on one of the mountains — the chambers of the priests were pulled down.
\v 39 They tore their clothes and mourned with great lamentation and sprinkled themselves with ashes.
\v 40 They fell face down on the ground and sounded the signal on the trumpets and cried out to Heaven.
\p
\v 41 Then Judas detailed men to fight against those in the citadel until he had cleansed the sanctuary.
\v 42 He chose blameless priests devoted to the Law —
\v 43 and they cleansed the sanctuary and removed the defiled stones to an unclean place.
\v 44 They deliberated what to do about the altar of burnt offering which had been profaned.
\v 45 And they thought it best to tear it down lest it bring reproach on them — for the Gentiles had defiled it. So they tore down the altar —
\v 46 and stored the stones in a convenient place on the temple hill until a prophet should come to tell what to do with them.
\p
\v 47 Then they took unhewn stones as the Law directs and built a new altar like the former one.
\v 48 They also rebuilt the sanctuary and the interior of the temple and consecrated the courts.
\v 49 They made new holy vessels and brought the lampstand the altar of incense and the table into the temple.
\v 50 Then they offered incense on the altar and lit the lamps on the lampstand and these gave light in the temple.
\v 51 They placed the bread on the table and hung up the curtains. Thus they finished all the work they had undertaken.
\p
\v 52 Early in the morning on the twenty-fifth day of the ninth month which is the month of Chislev in the one hundred and forty-eighth year —
\v 53 they rose and offered sacrifice as the Law directs on the new altar of burnt offering that they had built.
\v 54 At the very season and on the very day that the Gentiles had profaned it it was dedicated with songs and harps and lutes and cymbals.
\v 55 All the people fell on their faces and worshipped and blessed Heaven who had prospered them.
\v 56 So they celebrated the dedication of the altar for eight days and joyfully offered burnt offerings — they offered a sacrifice of deliverance and praise.
\v 57 They decorated the front of the temple with golden crowns and small shields — they restored the gates and the chambers for the priests and fitted them with doors.
\v 58 There was very great joy among the people and the reproach of the Gentiles was removed.
\v 59 Then Judas and his brothers and all the assembly of Israel determined that every year at that season the days of dedication of the altar should be observed with joy and gladness for eight days beginning with the twenty-fifth day of the month of Chislev.

\c 5
\p
\v 1 When the Gentiles all around heard that the altar had been rebuilt and the sanctuary dedicated as before they became very angry —
\v 2 and they determined to destroy the descendants of Jacob who lived among them. So they began to kill and destroy among the people.
\p
\v 15 messengers came to Judas with their clothes torn bearing these reports: The Gentiles around us have gathered against us to destroy us.
\v 16 When Judas and the people heard these words a great assembly gathered to decide what to do for their kindred who were in distress and being attacked by enemies.
\v 31 Now then if you do not hurry and prevent them they will capture this stronghold.
\v 33 Then Judas and his army quickly turned back by the wilderness road to Mizpeh — they captured it and killed every male — seized its spoils and burned it with fire.
\v 54 They went up to Mount Zion with joy and gladness and offered burnt offerings because they had returned in safety — not one of them had fallen.

\c 6
\p
\v 1 King Antiochus was going through the upper provinces when he heard that Elymais in Persia was a city famed for its wealth in silver and gold.
\v 5 Then someone came to him in Persia and reported that the armies that had gone into the land of Judah had been put to flight —
\v 6 that Lysias had gone first with a strong force and had been driven back before the Jews — that the Jews had grown strong from the arms equipment and the great store of plunder that they had taken from the armies they had cut down —
\v 7 that they had torn down the abomination that he had erected over the altar in Jerusalem and that they had surrounded the sanctuary with high walls as before and also Bethsura his city.
\p
\v 8 When the king heard this news he was astounded and badly shaken. He took to his bed and became sick from grief because things had not turned out for him as he had planned.
\v 9 He lay there for many days because deep grief continually gripped him and he felt that he was dying.
\v 12 Now I remember the evils I did in Jerusalem. I took all the vessels of gold and silver that were in it and I sent to destroy the inhabitants of Judah without good reason.
\v 13 I know that it is because of this that these evils have come upon me — and here I am perishing in a foreign land.
\p
\v 55 Then Lysias heard that Philip whom King Antiochus while still living had appointed to bring up his son Antiochus to be king —
\v 59 was persuading the king to restore to them their ancestral laws which he had abolished — and their former way of life — for it was on account of their laws that they had rebelled and caused all this harm.
\v 61 The speech pleased the king and the commanders — he sent to the Jews an offer of peace and they accepted it.
\v 63 But when Lysias and the king entered the sanctuary they saw how strong the place was — they broke the oath they had sworn and gave orders to tear down the surrounding wall.
\v 64 Then he departed in haste and returned to Antioch. He found Philip in control of the city but he fought against him and took the city by force.

\c 7
\p
\v 13 Then the Hasideans were first among the Israelites to seek peace from them —
\v 14 for they said: A priest of the line of Aaron has come with the army and he will not harm us.
\v 16 But he seized sixty of them and killed them in one day according to the word that was written:
\v 17 The flesh of your saints and their blood they poured out all around Jerusalem — and there was no one to bury them.
\p
\v 36 Then the priests went in and stood before the altar and the temple and they wept and said:
\v 37 You chose this house to be called by your name and to be a house of prayer and supplication for your people.
\v 38 Take vengeance on this man and on his army and let them fall by the sword. Remember their blasphemies and do not let them live any longer.
\p
\v 41 When the messengers from the king spoke blasphemy your angel went out and struck down one hundred and eighty-five thousand of them.
\v 42 Even so crush this army before us today — let the rest know that Nicanor spoke wickedly against the sanctuary and judge him according to this wickedness.
\p
\v 43 The armies met in battle on the thirteenth day of the month of Adar. The army of Nicanor was crushed and he himself was the first to fall in the battle.
\v 44 When his army saw that Nicanor had fallen they threw down their arms and fled.
\v 45 The Jews pursued them a day's journey from Adasa as far as Gazara — and as they followed they kept sounding the battle call on the trumpets.
\v 46 People came out of all the surrounding villages of Judea and they outflanked the enemy and drove them back to their pursuers so that they all fell by the sword — not even one of them was left.
\v 47 Then the Jews seized the spoils and the plunder — they cut off Nicanor's head and the right hand that he had stretched out so arrogantly and brought them and displayed them just outside Jerusalem.
\v 48 The people rejoiced greatly and celebrated that day as a day of great gladness.
\v 49 They decreed that this day should be celebrated each year on the thirteenth of Adar.
\v 50 And the land of Judah had rest for a few days.

\c 8
\p
\v 1 Now Judas heard of the fame of the Romans that they were very strong and were well-disposed toward all who made an alliance with them and that they pledged friendship to those who came to them —
\v 2 and that they were very strong. He had been told of their wars and of the brave deeds that they were doing among the Gauls — how they had defeated them and brought them under tribute —
\v 3 and what they had done in the land of Spain to get control of the silver and gold mines there —
\v 17 So Judas chose Eupolemus the son of John son of Accos and Jason the son of Eleazar and sent them to Rome to establish friendship and alliance —
\v 18 and to free themselves from the yoke — for they saw that the kingdom of the Greeks was enslaving Israel completely.
\v 23 May all go well with the Romans and with the nation of the Jews at sea and on land forever — and may sword and enemy be far from them.
\v 27 In accordance with these terms the Romans shall help them whenever it is required with all their heart (*kardia*) and all their desire without receiving any payment.
\v 29 On these terms the Romans make a treaty with the Jewish people.
\v 31 Concerning the wrongs that King Demetrius is doing to them we have written to him as follows: Why have you made your yoke heavy upon our friends and allies the Jews?

\c 9
\p
\v 1 When Demetrius heard that Nicanor and his army had fallen in battle he sent Bacchides and Alcimus into the land of Judah a second time and with them the right wing of the army.
\v 18 The battle became desperate and Judas fell — the rest fled.
\v 19 Then Jonathan and Simon took Judas their brother and buried him in the tomb of their ancestors at Modein.
\v 20 They mourned him and all Israel made great lamentation for him and mourned many days and said:
\v 21 How is the mighty fallen — the savior of Israel.
\p
\v 27 So there was great distress in Israel such as had not been since the time a prophet ceased to appear among them.
\p
\v 30 So all the friends of Judas assembled and said to Jonathan:
\v 31 Since your brother Judas died we have had no one like him to go against our enemies and Bacchides and our nation's foes.
\v 32 Now therefore we have chosen you today to take his place as our ruler and leader to fight our battle.
\v 33 Jonathan accepted the leadership at that time in place of his brother Judas.

\c 10
\p
\v 1 In the one hundred and sixtieth year Alexander Epiphanes the son of Antiochus landed and occupied Ptolemais. They welcomed him and he began to reign there.
\v 20 We hereby appoint you today as the high priest of your nation — you are to be called the king's Friend and you are to take our side and keep friendship with us. He sent him a purple robe and a golden crown.
\p
\v 25 Demetrius sent a letter to Jonathan and the nation with the following content: King Demetrius to the nation of the Jews — greetings.
\p
\v 38 We also give up to the high priest the revenues of the places that belong to us in the territory of Judah.
\p
\v 51 Then Ptolemy became king of Egypt and put on two crowns.
\v 52 He sent a message to Alexander: Come — let us make a covenant with each other and I will give you my daughter in marriage who is a queen in Egypt and you shall reign over your kingdom —
\v 55 and Ptolemy gave him his daughter Cleopatra in marriage.

\c 12
\p
\v 1 Now when Jonathan saw that the time was favorable for him he chose men and sent them to Rome to confirm and renew the friendship with them.
\p
\v 9 Therefore though we have no need of these things since we have as encouragement the holy books that are in our hands —
\v 10 we have undertaken to send to renew our brotherhood and friendship with you so that we may not become estranged from you — for considerable time has passed since you sent your message to us.
\p
\v 20 King Arius of the Spartans — to the high priest Onias — greetings.
\v 21 It has been found in writing concerning the Spartans and the Jews that they are brothers and are of the family of Abraham.

\c 13
\p
\v 1 Simon heard that Tryphon had assembled a large army to invade the land of Judah and destroy it —
\v 2 and he saw that the people were trembling and fearful. So he went up to Jerusalem and gathered the people together —
\v 3 and encouraging them he said: You yourselves know what I and my brothers and the house of my father have done for the laws and the sanctuary — the wars and the distresses that we have seen.
\v 4 By reason of this all my brothers have perished for the sake of Israel and I alone am left.
\v 5 And now far be it from me to spare my own life in any time of distress — for I am not better than my brothers.
\v 6 But I will avenge my nation and the sanctuary and your wives and children — for all the nations have gathered to destroy us out of pure malice.
\p
\v 41 In the one hundred and seventieth year the yoke of the Gentiles was removed from Israel —
\v 42 and the people began to write in their documents and contracts: In the first year of Simon the great high priest and commander and leader of the Jews.
\p
\v 51 On the twenty-third day of the second month in the one hundred and seventy-first year the Jews entered it with praise and palm branches and with harps and cymbals and stringed instruments and with hymns and songs — because a great enemy had been crushed and removed from Israel.
\v 52 Simon decreed that every year they should celebrate this day with rejoicing.

\c 14
\p
\v 4 The land had rest all the days of Simon — he sought the good of his nation — his rule was pleasing to them and his glory all his days.
\v 5 To crown all his honors he took Joppa for a harbor and opened a way to the isles of the sea.
\v 6 He extended the borders of his nation and gained full control of the country.
\v 7 He gathered a large number of captives — he ruled over Gazara and Bethsura and the citadel and he removed its uncleanness from it and there was none to oppose him.
\v 8 They tilled their land in peace — the ground gave its increase and the trees of the plains their fruit.
\v 9 Old men sat in the streets — they all talked together of good things — and the youths put on the glorious garments of war.
\v 10 He supplied the towns with food and furnished them with the means of defense until his renown spread to the ends of the earth.
\v 11 He established peace in the land and Israel rejoiced with great joy.
\v 12 All the people sat under their own vines and fig trees and there was none to make them afraid.
\v 13 No one was left in the land to fight them and the kings were crushed in those days.
\v 14 He gave help to all the humble among his people — he sought out the Law and did away with all the renegades and evil men.
\v 15 He made the sanctuary glorious and added to the vessels of the sanctuary.

\c 15
\p
\v 1 King Antiochus son of Demetrius sent a letter from the islands of the sea to Simon the priest and ruler of the Jewish nation and to all the nation.

## CRITICAL TEXTUAL NOTES — 1 MACCABEES

**Original language:** 1 Maccabees was composed in Hebrew (Jerome and Origen both attest a Semitic original) but survives only in Greek (LXX) and its daughter translations. The Greek is a translation-style closer to the LXX historical books (literal, Hebraizing) than to the literary Greek of 2 Maccabees.

**Historical scope:** 175–134 BCE — from Antiochus IV Epiphanes through the death of Simon Maccabeus and the beginning of John Hyrcanus's rule.

**The *bdelygma erēmōseōs*** (1:54): The LXX rendering of Daniel's *shiqqutz meshomem* = abomination of desolation (Daniel 9:27 / 11:31 / 12:11). 1 Maccabees 1:54 is the narrative fulfillment of Daniel's prophecy. Jesus reactivates the formula eschatologically (Matthew 24:15).

**Hanukkah foundation:** 1 Maccabees 4:52–59 establishes the eight-day feast of Dedication (25 Chislev). John 10:22 places Jesus in the Temple at this feast — the only NT mention of Hanukkah.

**No explicit divine intervention:** Like MT Esther, 1 Maccabees avoids direct miracle-claims. Providence is implied through the *eleos* refrain, pre-battle prayers, and the structural pattern of covenant-faithfulness → victory.

## CAE NODES — 1 MACCABEES

### 1:54 / 4:52–59 — the *bdelygma erēmōseōs* / the Dedication — CAE CRITICAL NODE

**1:54** — "Now on the fifteenth day of Chislev in the one hundred and forty-fifth year they erected a desolating sacrilege (*bdelygma erēmōseōs*) on the altar of burnt offering."

**4:54** — "At the very season and on the very day that the Gentiles had profaned it it was dedicated with songs and harps and lutes and cymbals."

**4:59** — "Then Judas and his brothers... determined that every year at that season the days of dedication of the altar should be observed with joy and gladness for eight days beginning with the twenty-fifth day of the month of Chislev."

The *bdelygma erēmōseōs* is the LXX formula for the altar-desecration deriving from Daniel 9:27 / 11:31 / 12:11. The 1 Maccabees narrative is the first-level historical fulfillment of Daniel's prophecy — the altar defiled on the exact day (25 Chislev) that the Dedication will annually commemorate.

The **exact-day reversal** (4:54) is theologically precise: *at the very season and on the very day that the Gentiles had profaned it* — the restoration occurs at its own anniversary. The date of the abomination becomes the date of the dedication.

The **eight-day structure** (4:56): Mirrors Sukkot / Feast of Tabernacles (Nehemiah 8:18) — the first Hanukkah is a belated Sukkot, celebrating the eight days of dedication that the war prevented in Tishri.

**Daniel 9:27** — the source prophecy; 1 Maccabees is its first-level fulfillment.

**Matthew 24:15** — "So when you see the desolating sacrilege (*bdelygma tēs erēmōseōs*) spoken of by the prophet Daniel standing in the holy place — let the reader understand." Jesus cites the Daniel formula pointing beyond the Antiochene fulfillment to a second-level eschatological one. The 1 Maccabees event establishes the historical pattern that Jesus reactivates.

**John 10:22–30** — "At that time the Feast of Dedication took place at Jerusalem. It was winter — and Jesus was walking in the temple in Solomon's Portico." Jesus at the Dedication declares *I and the Father are one*. At the feast commemorating the rededication of the defiled altar, Jesus declares himself the ultimate Temple in whom the Father dwells — the *bdelygma erēmōseōs* and the *enkainismos* = dedication reach their ultimate resolution.

---

### 1:62–63 — those who stood firm / chose death over defilement — CAE PRIMARY NODE

**1:62–63** — "But many in Israel stood firm and were resolved in their hearts (*kardia*) not to eat unclean food. They chose to die rather than to be defiled by food or to profane the holy covenant — and they did die."

***heīlanto apothanein hina mē mianthōsin*** = they chose to die that they might not be defiled.

The *karteria* = steadfastness of the unnamed martyrs who chose death over covenant-violation is the theological foundation of the Maccabean martyr-theology. The covenant is not negotiable even under threat of death. The *kardia* resolved to covenant-faithfulness is the Shema-heart — whole / undivided / committed to the Lord above physical life.

**Daniel 3:17–18** — the three youths' *but if not* pattern is the prior paradigm; Mattathias explicitly cites them (2:59).

**Revelation 12:11** — "They loved not their lives even unto death." The NT martyr-formula is the direct articulation of the 1 Maccabees covenant-choice theology. The willingness to die rather than profane the covenant runs from the unnamed Maccabean martyrs through the early church.

**Hebrews 11:35–38** — "Some were tortured refusing to accept release so that they might rise again to a better life." The Hebrews cloud-of-witnesses includes the Maccabean martyrs — the *heīlanto apothanein* of 1 Maccabees is the historical ground for the Hebrews testimony.

---

### 2:19–22 / 2:52–60 — Mattathias's covenant-declaration / the hall of faithful ancestors — CAE PRIMARY NODE

**2:19–22** — "Even if all the nations obey the king... I and my sons and my brothers will continue to live by the covenant of our ancestors. Far be it from us to desert the Law."

**2:52** — "Was not Abraham found faithful when tested and it was reckoned to him as righteousness (*elogisthē autō eis dikaiosynēn*)?"

The death-speech of Mattathias (2:49–68) is the deuterocanon's hall of faithful ancestors — structurally parallel to Hebrews 11. Eight figures: Abraham (faithful in testing) / Joseph (commandment in distress) / Phinehas (zeal) / Joshua (obedience) / Caleb (faithful testimony) / David (mercy) / Elijah (zeal unto heaven) / Hananiah-Azariah-Mishael and Daniel (steadfastness under death-threat).

**The Abraham formula** (2:52): *elogisthē autō eis dikaiosynēn* = it was reckoned to him as righteousness — Genesis 15:6 applied through the Genesis 22 testing lens. Mattathias reads the *reckoning* through the *Aqedah*: faith demonstrated by willingness to sacrifice.

**Romans 4:3** — Paul cites Genesis 15:6 for faith-without-works; **James 2:21–23** cites it through the Aqedah for faith-completed-by-works. The 1 Maccabees 2:52 use is the deuterocanonical precedent for the James reading — both apply the *elogisthē* formula to the testing. The chain: Genesis 22 → 1 Maccabees 2:52 → James 2:21 / Hebrews 11:17.

---

### 3:18–19 — not in numbers but strength from Heaven — CAE PRIMARY NODE

**3:18–19** — "It is not on the size of the army that victory in battle depends but strength comes from Heaven (*ek tou ouranou hē ischys*)."

The *ek tou ouranou hē ischys* = strength from Heaven is the Maccabean articulation of the Holy-War theology running from Gideon (Judges 7) through Judith 9:11 through 2 Paralipomenon 20:15. The consistent principle: divine sovereignty is not constrained by human force-calculation. The battle is fought with real swords; the outcome is determined from Heaven.

**1 Samuel 14:6** — Jonathan's formula (*the Lord saves by many or by few*) — which Judas explicitly echoes — is the prior paradigm. Judas is cast in the Jonathan posture.

**2 Corinthians 10:4** — "The weapons of our warfare are not of the flesh but have divine power to destroy strongholds." Paul's apostolic-warfare theology is the NT equivalent of the *ek tou ouranou hē ischys* principle: the power that matters is not humanly quantifiable.

---

### 4:24 — the *eleos* refrain on the battlefield — CAE PRIMARY NODE

**4:24** — "On their return they sang hymns and praises to Heaven — for he is good for his mercy endures forever (*hoti agathos hoti eis ton aiōna to eleos autou*)."

The *eleos endures forever* refrain — first sung when the cloud of glory descended on Solomon's Temple (2 Paralipomenon 5:13) / sung at the restoration foundations (2 Esdras 3:11) / sung at the wall dedication (2 Esdras 12:43) — is now sung by Judas's army on the battlefield after routing the Seleucid forces.

The refrain tracks the arc of the entire historical books sequence: Temple dedication → exile → restoration → Maccabean liberation. In every moment the *eleos* is the constant. The battlefield becomes the sanctuary; the warriors become the liturgical assembly.

**Psalm 136** — the great Hallel whose twenty-six verses each end *ki le'olam ḥasdo* = for his steadfast love endures forever — is the liturgical source Judas deploys post-battle.

**Revelation 19:1–6** — the eschatological *alleluia* after the ultimate battle is the consummation of the *hoti eis ton aiōna to eleos autou* tradition: battle won / response is liturgical praise / the divine character declared rather than the human achievement celebrated.

---

### 9:21 / 9:27 — the lament for Judas / the prophetic silence — CAE NODE

**9:21** — "How is the mighty fallen — the savior of Israel (*ho sōtēr Israēl*)."

**9:27** — "So there was great distress in Israel such as had not been since the time a prophet ceased to appear among them."

The lament *pōs epesen dynatos* = how is the mighty fallen directly cites David's elegy over Saul and Jonathan (2 Samuel 1:19). Judas Maccabeus receives the Davidic lament-formula — the Maccabean leader honored in the words of Israel's greatest king.

The *sōtēr Israēl* title applied to Judas stands in the canonical chain: judges / sōtēras (Nehemiah 9:27) → Judas / sōtēr (1 Maccabees 9:21) → Jesus / *a Savior who is Christ the Lord* (Luke 2:11).

The **prophetic silence** of 9:27 is the defining theological gap of the Maccabean period — and the defining context of the NT's opening. The stones of the defiled altar were preserved *until a prophet should come* (4:46). John the Baptist's appearance (Luke 7:26 — *more than a prophet*) breaks a silence that 1 Maccabees marks as the era's defining absence.

**Luke 2:11** — the ultimate *sōtēr* fulfillment.
**Luke 7:26** — the prophet-gap filled.
**Matthew 24:15** — the *bdelygma* reactivated.

---

### 14:4–15 — the Simonian Golden Age / the vine and the fig tree — CAE PRIMARY NODE

**14:12** — "All the people sat under their own vines and fig trees and there was none to make them afraid."

***ekathisen hekastos hypo tēn ampelon autou kai hypo tēn sykēn autou kai ouk ēn ho ekphobōn autous***

The *vine and fig tree* formula is the concentrated OT image of eschatological shalom: Micah 4:4 / Zechariah 3:10 / 1 Kings 5:5. The 1 Maccabees application to Simon's rule presents the Hasmonean peace as the first historical approximation of the prophetic vision — briefly realized before disruption.

**Zechariah 3:10** — the vine-and-fig-tree oracle is specifically connected to the Branch / Servant (3:8) — the Messianic figure. The 1 Maccabees application to Simon implicitly claims Messianic fulfillment for the Hasmonean dynasty — a claim the NT redirects.

**John 1:48–50** — Jesus's knowledge of Nathanael *under the fig tree* triggers the *Son of God / King of Israel* confession. The fig-tree imagery carries the Zechariah 3:10 Messianic weight: the one who sees each person under their fig tree is the one who fulfills the Simonian-shalom ideal. Jesus's *I saw you under the fig tree* is the ultimate enactment of *each sat under his own fig tree and none made him afraid* — the shalom realized in the person of the one who is greater than Simon.

## STRUCTURAL NOTE — 1 MACCABEES AS THE PENULTIMATE CHAPTER

1 Maccabees is the historical books' answer to Nehemiah 9:36 (*we are slaves*): the moment when the covenant-people refuse permanent servitude. But the book's own 9:27 confession — *no prophet among them* / the stones preserved *until a prophet comes* — acknowledges that the liberation is real but not final.

The book establishes five NT anchors:
1. **Hanukkah** — at which Jesus walks in the Temple (John 10:22) and declares *I and the Father are one*
2. **The *bdelygma erēmōseōs*** — which Jesus reactivates eschatologically (Matthew 24:15)
3. **The martyr-theology** — which grounds the NT martyrdom vocabulary (Hebrews 11 / Revelation 12:11)
4. **The *sōtēr* expectation** — fulfilled in the birth-announcement (Luke 2:11)
5. **The vine-and-fig-tree shalom** — embodied and superseded by Jesus (John 1:48–50)
6. **The prophet-gap** — filled by John the Baptist (Luke 7:26)

The 1 Maccabees narrative is the penultimate chapter of the long preparation for the one who says at Hanukkah in the Temple: *I and the Father are one*.

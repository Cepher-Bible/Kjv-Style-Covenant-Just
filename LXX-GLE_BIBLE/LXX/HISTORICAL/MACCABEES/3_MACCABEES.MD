## CRITICAL TEXTUAL NOTES — 3 MACCABEES

**Misnamed:** 3 Maccabees has nothing to do with the Maccabean revolt — it concerns events in Egypt under Ptolemy IV Philopator (221–204 BCE), predating Antiochus IV by a generation. The title is a library grouping convention, not a content description. The book is sometimes called *Ptolemaika*.

**No Hebrew original:** Unlike 1 and 2 Maccabees, 3 Maccabees was composed in Greek and survives only in Greek. It is in the LXX canon of the Eastern Orthodox churches but not in the Catholic deuterocanon. The GLE includes it as part of the full LXX Maccabean corpus.

**The narrative structure:** Three attempted annihilations of the Egyptian Jewish community — all reversed by divine intervention:
1. Ptolemy attempts to enter the Jerusalem Temple inner sanctuary → struck down at the door (1:15–2:24)
2. Ptolemy attempts to register and degrade the Egyptian Jews → frustrated by divine sleep (5:1–21)
3. Ptolemy unleashes five hundred war elephants to trample the Jews → elephants turn on the Egyptian army (6:11–21)

**The theological pattern:** Each reversal is structurally identical to the Esther / Judith / Exodus pattern: the great power assembles maximum force / Israel prays / God reverses the outcome / feasting follows. The triple-reversal structure intensifies the pattern: three consecutive defeats of the maximum imperial power.

**The feast of deliverance:** The book concludes with the establishment of a diaspora feast (7:18–19) — the Egyptian Jewish equivalent of Purim. The feast-after-reversal pattern (Passover → Purim → Feast of Dedication → 3 Maccabees feast) reaches its fourth instance.

## CAE NODES — 3 MACCABEES

### 3 Maccabees 1:16–25 / 2:1–14 — the Temple-entry prayer / *God did not choose the place for itself* — CAE PRIMARY NODE

**1:16–17** — "Then the high priest Simon fell to his knees opposite the altar and spread out his hands in dignified fashion and prayed: Lord Lord King of the heavens and Sovereign of all creation — Holy among the holy — sole Ruler — Almighty — give attention now to us who are suffering violently from a wicked and profane man — puffed up with his confidence and power."

**2:14** — "Even now O Holy King and Father of all — act for us according to your steadfast love (*eleos*) quickly."

**2:15–16** — "While he was still speaking a great shaking occurred and fear and trembling came upon those who were with him... Ptolemy was not only kept from entering the temple but fell to the ground struck by a great divine blow."

Simon's prayer and the divine response constitute one of the most direct divine-intervention scenes in the deuterocanon — comparable to the Heliodorus episode in 2 Maccabees 3. The prayer structure follows the established LXX pattern: address to the divine sovereignty → rehearsal of past deliverances (Pharaoh / Sennacherib) → the present crisis → petition.

**The Heliodorus parallel** (2 Maccabees 3:24–28): Both scenes involve an arrogant official attempting to violate the Temple's sanctity / both are met by sudden divine physical intervention / both are carried away unable to speak or move / both subsequently testify to the divine power. The 3 Maccabees scene is the Ptolemaic variant of the Heliodorus pattern — establishing that the Temple's protection is not the exception but the rule: the Holy among the holy guards his own sanctuary.

**The *eleos* petition** (2:14): The prayer culminates in the *eleos* appeal — act according to your steadfast love quickly. The *eleos* is the covenant-ground of the petition: not the people's righteousness (2:13 acknowledges sin) but the divine covenant-commitment. This is the same *eleos*-ground that all the LXX prayers deploy — from Solomon's Temple prayer (3 Kingdoms 8) through Ezra's confession (2 Esdras 9) through Esther's prayer (Addition C) through the Maccabean prayers.

**2 Maccabees 5:19** — the theological anchor: *God did not choose the place for the sake of the nation but the nation for the sake of the place* — 3 Maccabees enacts this: the Temple is defended not because the building is sacred in itself but because it is the covenant-sign for the covenant-people. The defense of the sanctuary is an act of covenant-*eleos*.

**Matthew 21:12–13** — "Jesus entered the temple and drove out all who sold and bought in the temple... He said to them: It is written My house shall be called a house of prayer." Jesus's Temple-cleansing is the NT equivalent of the 3 Maccabees / 2 Maccabees Temple-defense pattern: the holy place is defended against profanation by those who bear the divine authority.

### 3 Maccabees 5:2 — the deep sleep / God frustrates the king's purpose — CAE PRIMARY NODE

**5:2** — "But the most glorious all-mighty God — the holy one — turned aside the violence of Hermon and frustrated the king's previously inflexible purpose by putting into the king's mind a sound and deep sleep — and this was a great act of grace for the Jews who was saved from destruction."

***ho de pantokratōr theos... katepaue tēn hybrin autou... epibale tō basilei hypnon katharon kai bathyn***

= but the Almighty God... turned aside his violence... cast upon the king a clean and deep sleep.

The divine sleep is the book's most structurally important theological moment. Ptolemy's purpose is described as *previously inflexible* — his will is not wavering. The divine response is not persuasion or a change of circumstances but a direct act on the king's consciousness: God gives him deep sleep. The sovereign will that has decreed genocide is simply — without explanation or argument — put to sleep.

The theological register: God does not need to defeat Ptolemy in battle or change his mind through argument. God governs the consciousness of kings. The *kardia* of the king is in the Lord's hand (Proverbs 21:1) — the 3 Maccabees narrative is the most graphic enacted demonstration of that principle in the deuterocanon.

The divine sleep is repeated a second time (5:11–12) — Ptolemy again oversleeps and misses the execution again. The double sleep emphasizes that the divine frustration of the genocidal plan is not accident but pattern: God is not lucky / God governs.

**Proverbs 21:1** — "The king's heart (*kardia*) is a stream of water in the hand of the Lord — he turns it wherever he will." The 3 Maccabees divine-sleep episodes are the narrative dramatization of Proverbs 21:1: the king's will is redirected not by external force but by divine sovereignty over the inner life.

**Esther Addition F:8–9** — "God prepared two lots — one for the people of God and one for all the other nations — and these two lots came to the hour and moment and day of decision before God." The 3 Maccabees divine sleep is the narrative equivalent of the two-lots theology: before the king decides anything the divine sovereignty has already arranged the outcome. The providential control operates at the level of the king's sleep — the most intimate and involuntary of human states.

**Acts 12:6–7** — "The very night when Herod was about to bring him out Peter was sleeping between two soldiers... And behold an angel of the Lord stood next to him and a light shone in the cell. He struck Peter on the side and woke him." The divine intervention in the sleeping/waking of those under threat of execution recurs: 3 Maccabees (God gives the king sleep to prevent execution) / Acts 12 (God wakes Peter to accomplish escape). Both are sovereign acts on human sleep / waking states to accomplish the reversal of an execution decree.

### 3 Maccabees 5:17–29 / 6:1–10 — Eleazar's prayer / the recitation of deliverances — CAE PRIMARY NODE

**5:18–21** — "O Lord Lord King of the heavens — you who are powerful and great — with a mighty arm — Holy of the holy — and sole master of all things — look mercifully upon us who are crushed under the weight of the wicked and profane enemies... Save us from your enemies — spare us from those who are in rebellion against your greatness — and have compassion upon the misery of your holy people."

**6:5–7** — "You who showed your three beloved friends Ananias Azarias and Misael fire — who had willingly surrendered their bodies to the fire rather than to serve vain things — you refreshed with dew unharmed... You who through your many and various torments brought Pharaoh the former oppressor of Egypt — the tyrant — to acknowledge you as God."

Eleazar's prayer in chapters 5–6 is the book's theological centerpiece — a sustained recitation of the divine deliverances as the ground of present petition. The structure follows the LXX prayer-pattern established in Nehemiah 9 / Judith 9 / Esther C / Tobit 3: address to the divine sovereignty → historical recitation of past acts → present crisis → petition.

**The deliverance-recitation** (6:2–9) covers: the destruction of Pharaoh / Sennacherib / the three youths in the furnace / Daniel in the lions' den / Jonah. These are precisely the figures cited by Mattathias in his death-speech (1 Maccabees 2:59–60). The prayer-tradition and the dying-speech tradition share the same canonical hall-of-deliverances. The theology: the God who delivered in each of these cases is appealed to as the God who will deliver now — the past acts are not merely commemorated but presented as the ground of present expectation.

**The *eleos* and compassion vocabulary**: The prayer moves through multiple mercy-terms — *eleos* / *oiktirmos* / *philanthropia* = love of humanity. The concentrated mercy-vocabulary reflects the urgency: the petition is not grounded in the people's righteousness (5:20 — *those who have fallen away*) but entirely in the divine character.

**Nehemiah 9:5–37** — The great Levitical prayer of Nehemiah 9 is the OT structural prototype for the 3 Maccabees prayer-recitation: creation → Abraham → exodus → Sinai → wilderness → conquest → exile → present distress. Eleazar's prayer follows the same movement but condenses to the crisis-deliverance episodes most relevant to the present threat.

**Acts 4:24–30** — "Sovereign Lord who made the heaven and the earth and the sea and everything in them... Look upon their threats and grant to your servants to continue to speak your word with all boldness while you stretch out your hand to heal." The early church's prayer after Peter and John's release follows the identical structure: address to the Creator-Sovereign → recitation of past acts (4:25–28 citing Psalm 2) → present crisis → petition for bold action. The 3 Maccabees prayer tradition is the deuterocanonical ground for the Acts 4 community prayer.

### 3 Maccabees 6:11–22 — the elephant reversal / the feast of deliverance — CAE CRITICAL NODE

**6:11–16** — "And it came about that as soon as he concluded his prayer the king came rushing with the elephants and the forces... but when he saw the determination of those who had assembled there to be destroyed by the wild beasts... he was turned aside by the merciful God and was amazed. But the beasts — turning against the armed forces that followed — began to trample and destroy them."

**6:21** — "That righteous God who rules over all things had made clear to all his holy power — and showed his wonderful wonders — visible to all — by delivering the whole Jewish nation from great dangers to joy and the great feast."

**6:28** — "They celebrated a feast of deliverance (*sōtērias euōchian*) — with joy and rejoicing — for seven days."

***ho dikaios theos ho pantōn despotōn... epedeixe tēn endoxon autou dynamin***

= the righteous God who rules all things... displayed his glorious power.

The elephant reversal is the book's climactic miracle — the maximum military force deployed against the unarmed Jewish community turns and destroys the army it was meant to serve. The theological declaration at 6:21 is the book's doxological climax: *the righteous God who rules all things displayed his glorious power visible to all*.

Three elements of the reversal theology:

**1. The prayer-to-miracle sequence**: Eleazar prays / the king arrives with the elephants / the elephants turn. The causal connection is explicit: *as soon as he concluded his prayer*. The prayer and the miracle are directly linked — the divine response to the covenant-prayer is immediate and total.

**2. The *sōtērias euōchia* = feast of deliverance**: The seven-day feast that follows the reversal is the book's fourth instance of the feast-after-reversal pattern. Each great OT deliverance is sealed with a feast: Passover (eight days / Exodus 12) → Purim (two days / Esther 9) → Hanukkah (eight days / 1 Maccabees 4) → the 3 Maccabees feast (seven days / 6:28). The feast is the covenant-community's liturgical response to divine reversal: joy expressed in shared eating is the proper form of thanksgiving for deliverance.

**3. Visible to all**: The miracle is not private or internal but *visible to all* — the nations witness the divine power. The Exodus theology: the defeat of Pharaoh was not merely Israel's deliverance but the demonstration of divine power to the nations. Every subsequent reversal narrative participates in the same visibility-structure.

**Exodus 14:13–14** — "Fear not — stand firm and see the salvation of the Lord which he will work for you today... The Lord will fight for you and you have only to be silent." Moses's declaration before the Red Sea crossing is the founding text of the reversal-deliverance theology: the maximum human force (Pharaoh's chariots / Ptolemy's elephants / Holofernes's army) meets the divine reversal / the covenant-people stand and witness / the feast follows.

**Revelation 19:17–21** — "Then I saw an angel standing in the sun and with a loud voice he called to all the birds... Come gather for the great supper of God... And the rest were killed by the sword of him who was sitting on the horse and all the birds were gorged with their flesh." The eschatological battle-reversal of Revelation 19 is the ultimate form of the 3 Maccabees elephant-reversal pattern: the force assembled against the covenant-people is destroyed / the birds feast on the fallen army. The *sōtērias euōchia* = feast of deliverance of 3 Maccabees becomes the Marriage Supper of the Lamb and the eschatological feast of the birds. Both are reversals of maximum threat into total deliverance. Both are sealed with feasting.

### 3 Maccabees 7:6 — God as Father defending his children / the *pater* theology — CAE PRIMARY NODE

**7:6** — "But we very severely threatened them for these acts and in accordance with the clemency that we have toward all people — barely granted them their lives — and since we have come to realize that the God of heaven surely defends the Jews always taking their part as a Father does for his children (*hōs patēr hyper teknōn*) —"

***ton ouranion theon asphalōs hyperaspizein autōn patros dikēn aei***

= the God of heaven surely standing over them in defense as a father does for children — always.

The royal edict of Ptolemy in chapter 7 — his reversal decree — contains the book's most theologically charged phrase: the king's declaration that the God of heaven defends the Jews *as a father defends his children*. A pagan king, forced by three successive divine reversals, articulates the covenant-theology in its most intimate form: the God of Israel is a Father who defends his children.

The *pater* = Father language for God in the OT-deuterocanonical tradition is present but not dominant. In the Wisdom literature (Wisdom of Solomon 2:16–18 / Sirach 23:1, 4) and here it appears as the most intimate covenantal category. The pattern of the pagan king confessing the divine fatherhood parallels Addition E:15 of Esther (Artaxerxes declaring Israel *sons of the living God*): the empire that threatened to destroy the covenant-people is compelled by its own failure to articulate the covenant-theology it tried to negate.

**Deuteronomy 1:31** — "And in the wilderness where you have seen how the Lord your God carried you as a man carries his son all the way that you went until you came to this place." The Deuteronomic father-carrying-son image is the OT ground for the *hōs patēr hyper teknōn* formula of 3 Maccabees 7:6.

**Hosea 11:1** — "When Israel was a child I loved him and out of Egypt I called my son." The Hosea divine-fatherhood / son-of-God language for Israel is the prophetic ground. Matthew 2:15 cites Hosea 11:1 in reference to the flight to Egypt — the *son* = Israel / *son* = Jesus typological identification.

**Matthew 6:9** — "Our Father who art in heaven hallowed be thy name." Jesus's *Abba* prayer-form is the NT ultimate deployment of the fatherhood-theology that runs from Deuteronomy 1:31 through Hosea 11:1 through 3 Maccabees 7:6: the God who defended Israel *as a father defends his children* is addressed directly as Father by the Son.

**Romans 8:15** — "For you did not receive the spirit of slavery to fall back into fear but you have received the Spirit of adoption as sons by whom we cry Abba Father." Paul's *huiothesia* = son-placement and *Abba* cry is the NT expansion of the divine-fatherhood theology: what the pagan king Ptolemy was forced to acknowledge about Israel (*God defends them as a Father*) Paul declares as the present reality of all who have received the Spirit of adoption.

### 3 Maccabees 7:15–22 — crowned with flowers / the doxological departure / the great God accomplished wonderful things — CAE PRIMARY NODE

**7:15** — "Those who had held fast to God even to death and had received the full enjoyment of deliverance — departed from the city crowned with all manner of sweet-smelling flowers — joyfully and loudly — with thanksgiving and harmonious singing of praise — praising the God of their ancestors — the eternal savior of Israel."

**7:21–22** — "Every man received back all his belongings in accordance with the inventory — those who held any of their goods returning them with the greatest terror. The great God had perfectly accomplished wonderful and marvelous things to deliver them (*ta megala kai endoxa autou telesanta*). Blessed be the Deliverer of Israel forever and ever. Amen."

***ta megala kai endoxa autou telesanta... eulogētos ho rhysamenōs Israel eis tous aionas aiōnōn. Amēn.***

= having perfectly accomplished his great and glorious things... Blessed be the Deliverer of Israel unto the ages of ages. Amen.

The book's ending is the most doxological conclusion in the Maccabean corpus. The threefold pattern at the close:

**1. The crowned departure** (7:15): The survivors depart *crowned with sweet-smelling flowers* — the imagery is liturgical and festal. The crown (*stephanos*) of deliverance replaces the sackcloth of mourning. The floral crown is the anti-type of the sackcloth and ashes: the mourning-garments are stripped off / the festal garlands are put on. The departure is a procession of worship.

**2. The full restoration** (7:20–21): Every man receives back all his belongings — the enemy returns the plundered goods *with the greatest terror*. The restoration is total: nothing is lost / everything is returned. This is the Exodus-property-restoration pattern: Israel left Egypt with the wealth of the Egyptians (Exodus 12:35–36).

**3. The final doxology** (7:22): *ta megala kai endoxa autou telesanta* = having perfectly accomplished his great and glorious things — the doxology closes the narrative not with human achievement but with divine action. The great things are God's / perfectly accomplished / *telesanta* = completed / finished. The *eulogētos* = blessed / the *rhysamenōs* = deliverer / *eis tous aionas aiōnōn* = unto the ages of ages / *Amēn* — the formula is the standard LXX doxological close (cf. Tobit 13:18 / 1 Chronicles 29:10).

**Revelation 19:1–3** — "After this I heard what seemed to be the loud voice of a great multitude in heaven crying out: Hallelujah — salvation and glory and power belong to our God — for his judgments are true and just." The eschatological Hallelujah after the defeat of the great enemy is the ultimate *eulogētos ho rhysamenōs* = blessed be the Deliverer: the pattern that 3 Maccabees enacts on the historical level Revelation enacts on the cosmic level. Both close with the multitude praising the God who delivered / blessed be the Deliverer / forever and ever.

**Luke 1:68** — "Blessed be the Lord God of Israel — for he has visited and redeemed his people." Zechariah's Benedictus — *eulogētos Kyrios ho theos tou Israēl hoti epeskepsato kai epoiēsen lytrōsin* = blessed be the Lord God of Israel for he has visited and made a ransom-release — is the NT birth-narrative doxology that echoes the 3 Maccabees *eulogētos ho rhysamenōs Israel*. Both deploy the *eulogētos* blessing-formula / both identify God as the deliverer-redeemer of Israel / both close the reversal-narrative with praise. The 3 Maccabees formula is the deuterocanonical ground for the Benedictus form.

## STRUCTURAL NOTE — 3 MACCABEES AS THE TRIPLE REVERSAL NARRATIVE

3 Maccabees is the deuterocanon's most compressed demonstration of the reversal-theology. Where Esther uses one reversal (the Haman-plot reversed), where Judith uses one reversal (Holofernes defeated), 3 Maccabees uses three successive reversals of increasing intensity:

**First reversal** (1:16–2:22): Temple entry attempted → king struck down at the door. The reversal is immediate and personal — the king himself is the obstacle and the divine blow is instantaneous.

**Second reversal** (5:1–21): Mass execution by elephant scheduled → king given divine sleep / forgets / misses the appointed hour. The reversal operates through the most ordinary human mechanism — sleep — to frustrate the most extraordinary human violence.

**Third reversal** (6:1–21): Five hundred war elephants deployed → elephants turn on their own army. The reversal is militarily total — the weapon of mass destruction becomes the instrument of the enemy's own defeat.

The triple structure intensifies the core theology: **God does not need extraordinary resources to accomplish extraordinary reversals.** He can use a moment of unconsciousness / a direction-change in trained animals / a sudden collapse at a doorway. The maximum human force meets the minimum divine intervention — and the maximum force fails every time.

**The canonical feast sequence**: The 3 Maccabees feast (7:18–19) is the fourth in the LXX feast-after-reversal sequence:
1. **Passover** (Exodus 12) — eight days / death-of-firstborn reversed by blood / feast commanded
2. **Purim** (Esther 9) — two days / genocide reversed by providence / feast commanded
3. **Hanukkah** (1 Maccabees 4:59) — eight days / temple-defilement reversed by rededication / feast commanded
4. **3 Maccabees feast** (7:18–19) — seven days / elephant-annihilation reversed / feast established

Each feast is a covenant-memory: the reversal is not merely experienced once but institutionally remembered. The feast *institutionalizes the memory of divine reversal* — the liturgical calendar is built from remembered reversals.

The ultimate feast — the Marriage Supper of the Lamb (Revelation 19:7–9) — is the eschatological completion of this sequence: the final reversal of the final enemy / the final feast / blessed forever and ever.

\c 1
\p
\v 1 The subject that I am about to discuss is most philosophical — that is whether devout reason is sovereign over the passions (*ei autokratōr estin tōn pathōn ho eusebēs logismos*). So it is right for me to advise you to pay earnest attention to philosophy.
\v 2 For the subject is essential to everyone who is seeking knowledge — and in addition it includes the praise of the highest virtue — I mean of course rational judgment (*phronēsis*).
\v 3 If then it is evident that reason rules over those passions that hinder self-control — namely gluttony and lust —
\v 4 it is also clear that it masters the passions that hinder one from justice — such as malice — and those that stand in the way of courage — namely anger fear and pain.
\v 5 Some might ask why if reason rules the passions it does not control forgetfulness and ignorance.
\v 6 But that argument is ridiculous — for reason does not rule over its own passions but over those opposed to justice courage and self-control — and it is not for the purpose of destroying them but so that one does not give way to them.
\p
\v 7 I could prove to you from many and various examples that reason is dominant over the passions —
\v 8 but I can demonstrate it best from the noble bravery of those who died for the sake of virtue — Eleazar and the seven brothers and their mother.
\v 9 All of these by despising sufferings that bring death demonstrated that reason controls the passions.
\v 10 On this anniversary it is fitting for me to praise for their virtues those who willingly gave themselves to death for the sake of nobility.
\p
\v 14 Now reason is the mind that with sound logic prefers the life of wisdom.
\v 15 Wisdom next is the knowledge of divine and human matters and the causes of these.
\v 16 This in turn is education in the Law through which we learn divine matters reverently and human matters to our advantage.
\v 17 Now the kinds of wisdom are rational judgment self-control justice and courage.
\v 18 Rational judgment is supreme over all of these since by means of it reason rules over the passions.
\p
\v 26 Many and various are the passions — some belonging to pleasure and pain and others to desire and fear.
\v 27 In desire these are the passions most notable — greed ambition rivalry and contentiousness —
\v 28 in pleasure there are those of both body and soul.
\v 29 In pain there is suffering and mourning and sorrow.
\v 30 And in fear there is dread and cowardice.
\p
\v 31 But reason I maintain governs all these passions — through the cardinal virtues.
\v 32 Above all self-control governs the passions.
\v 33 Why else would our lawgiver Moses have forbidden the eating of pork or the flesh of certain birds — except that he knew that reason can rule the passions?
\v 34 For by means of his regulation of diet he educated our desires and by his regulation of touch he purified our pleasures.

\c 2
\p
\v 1 You can disprove this perhaps by saying that passion is not overcome by devout reason in every person.
\v 2 But I assert not only that reason controls passions but that it also controls desire — and not just the suffering that comes from within ourselves — but also that which comes from without.
\v 3 For it is for this reason that the temperate Joseph is praised — because by his reason he overcame sexual desire.
\p
\v 5 So it is that reason — I mean our father Jacob — blamelessly disregarded the emotion of the passions.
\v 6 For the affection for beauty and for children is of all the passions the most powerful — yet reason through devotion to God disdains even this.
\p
\v 14 For the Law says: You shall not covet your neighbor's wife or anything that belongs to your neighbor. In fact since the Law has told us not to covet — I could make the argument all the more that reason is able to control desires — just as it controls the passions that are obstacles to justice.
\p
\v 19 For these reasons I maintain then — not only for the sake of argument but for its persuasive power — that reason is properly sovereign over the passions.
\v 20 Since the passions of the soul prevent the attainment of virtue while reason overcomes them it follows that the wisest conduct is that in which reason is exercised according to the Law.

\c 3
\p
\v 1 Those who give heed to philosophy ought not to simply urge the mastery of the passions in accordance with reason because they profit therefrom — but also to urge it for the sake of piety — knowing that God is pleased by our piety.
\v 2 The fact that devout reason is sovereign over the passions is proved by many and diverse examples.
\p
\v 17 For just as if a sudden storm were to hurl the ship about or if the pilot were asleep or if the captain were not watching — then everything would be in disarray and overthrown — so also reason if it neglects its governance makes things worse.
\v 18 But the divine and holy Law — which governs everything together with divine wisdom — regulates human reason as if it were a ship on the waves — and acts as pilot to the psychē (*steuōn tēs psychēs*).

\c 4
\p
\v 1 As for the tyrant Antiochus — when he took from Onias the high priesthood and gave it to Onias's brother Jason — he displaced the law-abiding men from their ancestral way of life and in every way overturned the lawful conduct of the citizens.
\v 2 And to such an extent did he overthrow the constitution of the people that in the gymnasium directly under the citadel he established a training arena — and he abolished the service of the temple.
\p
\v 20 For such was the magnitude of their lawlessness that it led to the removal of the legitimate king from the priesthood and to the appointment of another — an impious man — in his place.
\p
\v 23 The great and most high God — who the whole time watches over all things and is a Father to the pious — heard the prayer and rescued Onias from the temple and placed him in the court of Seleucus.
\v 24 For he took Onias to safety and did not allow the temple to be defiled by the lawlessness of those who were determined to destroy it.
\p
\v 26 When he learned what had been decided he went to the king and said: Not all the Jews have defected — grant me the power and I myself will go and enforce the king's commands.
\v 27 Having gained his authority he went out and began to slaughter his own compatriots — coming against them with wild and savage cruelty.

\c 5
\p
\v 1 When he had arranged the spectacle and had summoned the people the tyrant Antiochus sat down with his counselors on a prominent raised platform and his armed soldiers stood in a threatening ring around him —
\v 2 and he ordered his guards to seize each and every Hebrew and to compel them to eat swine flesh and food offered to idols.
\v 3 If any were not willing to eat the defiled food they were to be broken on the wheel and killed.
\v 4 When many had been rounded up a man named Eleazar first among them — a priest — of distinguished family — trained in knowledge of the Law and well advanced in years — was brought before the king's tribunal.
\p
\v 5 When Antiochus saw him he said:
\v 6 Before I begin your torture old man I would advise you to save yourself by eating the swine flesh.
\v 7 I respect your age and your gray hairs — although in regard to the manner in which you have had them for so long a time and still persist in the religion of the Hebrews I do not think you are a philosopher.
\v 8 For when nature has granted it why should you abhor the pleasure that comes from eating swine flesh? It is foolish not to enjoy innocent pleasures and wrong to spurn nature's good gifts.
\p
\v 14 And he urged him further saying: Old man before the tortures begin save yourself by repenting.
\p
\v 16 Then the noble Eleazar was brought forward — and when he was asked if he would eat — he answered with great dignity in accordance with the Law:
\p
\v 17 We Antiochus who are persuaded that we live and die under God's Law do not have any authority to transgress his Law under any circumstances — in small things or in great.
\v 18 For not even if our monarch commanded us to eat the flesh of swine would we violate the Law.
\v 19 For not even if the pleasures were true pleasures and not passing ones would they be able to outweigh the Law's demands.
\v 20 But because we hold reason to be of overriding weight and of God-given authority we honor the truth of the Law with the obedience that we owe to God.
\v 21 Besides this — you might think that eating defiled food is a small thing — but we have sinned against God in no small thing if we eat even a small amount of swine flesh.
\v 22 Do not treat our reason with contempt — for though you may think that we do wrong to disobey you — the fact is that we hold the Law above your authority.
\p
\v 23 Then the tyrant urged him with threats to eat pork — but Eleazar answered: Do not suppose your threats can weaken our reasoning (*logismon*) — for our argument is stronger than your threats.
\v 24 Our reason (*logismos*) when educated by the Law is not overcome even by your violent coercion — and our mind (*dianoia*) is not persuaded by your supposed pleasures — for a vain pleasure is the cause of many vices.

\c 6
\p
\v 1 When he had spoken these words the guards seized the distinguished elder and beat him savagely.
\v 2 But Eleazar — even as he was being beaten and thought worthy of a noble death — turned his eyes upward to heaven and said while they flogged him:
\p
\v 3 You know O God of knowledge that though I might have been saved from death I am bearing terrible bodily pains — but in my soul (*psychē*) I am gladly enduring these things because of my reverence for you.
\p
\v 5 Even though our father Isaac could endure on the altar of sacrifice — yet he was not put to death. But Eleazar was being massacred and was becoming a saving cleansing (*katharsion*) through the blood.
\v 6 Consider it O divine Reason — if it had been appropriate for Eleazar to eat defiling food for a brief moment — only in order to be saved from a painful death — how much more would it have been right for the others to do the same — but they refused.
\v 9 Being entirely consumed by the agonies he said: To you O God we who know piety cling — knowing that you comfort the suffering.
\p
\v 12 But though he continued to bear his agony nobly and with great spirit he kept saying: Bear up my soul (*psychē*).
\v 13 Yet somehow through all his torment his reason (*logismos*) remained upright — even as his flesh (*sarkos*) was being ripped away in these sufferings and his body (*sōmatos*) was being consumed in the flames.
\v 14 O how wonderful it is to hold up under such sufferings — when reason (*logismos*) is master.
\p
\v 17 And the most admirable Eleazar in the midst of the fire cried out: O God you know that though I could have been saved I am dying in fiery torments for the sake of the Law.
\v 18 Be merciful to your people and let our punishment be a satisfaction on their behalf.
\v 19 Make my blood their purification and take my life in exchange for theirs (*antipsychon autōn labē tēn emēn psychēn*).
\p
\v 20 When he had said this the holy man died nobly in his torments — and by his reason (*logismō*) he resisted even to the point of death for the sake of the Law.
\v 21 Admittedly then devout reason is sovereign over the passions.
\v 22 For if the passions had been stronger than the reason we would have given them control.
\v 23 But now that reason has conquered the passions it deserves to be honored with the authority of the commander.
\v 24 And it is right to grant that reason has mastery when it endures even sufferings from without — for it would be absurd if reason could not master sufferings from within.

\c 7
\p
\v 1 Is it not true that the torments afflicting him were greater because his many limbs were on fire? And yet he governed his reason (*logismon*) and did not waver in his devoutness.
\v 4 Therefore being armed with the nobility of reason and having subdued the strong compulsion of the passions — Eleazar the priest — the old man — died.
\p
\v 8 For as our father Aaron armed with incense stood firm against the destroying angel and conquered it —
\v 9 so Eleazar the descendant of Aaron although the fire consumed him would not waver in his reason (*logismō*).
\p
\v 14 O blessed old age and venerable gray hair and a life in accordance with the Law that the faithful seal of death has perfected.
\v 15 If therefore an old man despised the torments even to death for the sake of piety — it is agreed that devout reason rules over the passions.
\v 16 But perhaps some might say: Not all men have power over the passions because not all men have wisdom.
\v 17 But those who keep in mind the sufferings for the sake of God exercise dominion over the passions and believe that they — like our father Abraham and like Isaac and Jacob — will not die to God but will live to God.
\v 18 Therefore the contradiction in terms among some that a man who has exercised reason and devoted himself to philosophy for the sake of the Law will die to God does not hold.
\v 19 For just as our patriarchs Abraham Isaac and Jacob did not die to God so too they who died for the sake of God die not to God but live to God — as Abraham Isaac and Jacob live and as all the patriarchs live to God.

\c 8
\p
\v 1 Since then some people raised the objection that not all men rule the passions — and since I have refuted them by showing that the religious exercised authority over the passions — I now pass on to demonstrate this from the case of the seven brothers.
\v 2 For just as the tyrant Antiochus while publicly torturing them urged them to eat defiling meat so also he was not able to make the courageous brothers change their reason.
\p
\v 5 Then the tyrant said to them: I respect you young men more than the older man and not only do I say this but I advise you not to be mad with the same madness as he was.
\v 6 Consider what a blessed thing it is to enjoy life and to behold the sweet light of the sun in the morning — and this you would not have if you refuse to obey me — you beautiful ones.
\v 7 If you make me your enemy by your lawless profession of the ancestral religion I will torture each of you with dreadful punishments through your torments.
\v 8 Therefore have pity on yourselves — for even I have compassion on your youth and beauty and feel for your tender age.
\p
\v 15 Then one of them speaking on behalf of all of them said:
\v 16 What are you intending to do tyrant? — or what do you plan to gain by questioning us? — We are prepared to die rather than transgress the commandments of our ancestors —
\v 17 for we are not ashamed to confess this.
\v 18 If the elders of the Hebrews have endured for the sake of piety after undergoing the wheel and rack — let no one be surprised if we who are young should suffer the same.
\v 19 Come then tyrant — if you test us you will learn that we are brothers who were born of the same father and educated in the same teaching and formed in the same virtue and trained in the same Law.
\v 20 We are ready to die for the ancestral law — and to proclaim false no one who commands us to transgress it.

\c 9
\p
\v 1 When they had said these words the tyrant was not only angered as by those who are disobedient but enraged as by those who are ungrateful.
\v 2 Then at his command the guards brought forward the eldest — and having torn off his tunic they tied his hands and arms with thongs on each side.
\p
\v 5 Then looking at him with a piercing gaze the tyrant said: Your father trained you for cowardice and stupidity in a religious matter — take the food and save yourself.
\v 6 But the noble youth replied: We have a father more noble than your tyrant king — the reason that educated us in the Law — from which we will not depart.
\v 7 What do you expect to gain from your interrogation of us? — For we are prepared to die rather than transgress the commandments of our ancestors.
\p
\v 8 Then as if by some higher power (*hypo tinos dynameōs theias*) the first brother endured the wheel and the rack without making any sound.
\v 9 When the tortures had been applied in an orderly sequence the tyrant thought that he could pull him away from his own reason (*logismou*) by threatening him with worse tortures.
\v 10 But the young man said to him with great confidence and courage: Do not think O tyrant that you will weaken our resolve (*logismon*) by torturing us — for it will be strengthened the more — the more you torment us.
\v 11 We through these sufferings and the patient endurance of them are the victors — while you are the conquered one — because of our humanity — and the cruel tyrant is defeated by our piety.
\p
\v 13 The courage of their godly tempers (*logismōn*) conquered the tyrants — and they held the mastery over them.
\p
\v 18 The second in line to be brought forward was the second brother — and having fastened his arms behind him and secured them with iron they took the skin of his head with the hair and tore it off — and he endured these sufferings with great composure.
\v 19 When the tyrant saw this he was moved to pity for him and thinking that the young man was beginning to be broken — he ordered the guards to loosen the bonds —
\v 20 but the youth said: I would not eat the defiling food even if you would release me. He persevered in his own reason (*logismō*).
\p
\v 21 Then when the third brother also was brought forward — he even willingly stretched forth his hands and heroically said:
\v 22 I possess hands and a body that my fathers gave me and for the sake of the Law I despise these — and I am the victor over your mad tyranny.

\c 10
\p
\v 1 When he too had endured a glorious death the fourth came forward saying:
\v 2 I will not delay nor will I fail in my zeal — for I will not give up until I die for the ancestral Law.
\v 3 As one who despises the pain of the torture with all his soul (*psychēn*) he joyfully said:
\p Come tyrant — you will not win over my reason (*logismon*) even by fire — by wild beasts — by the wheel.
\p
\v 4 By the heavens I swear and by the earth and by the water and by the fire and by the aeons of time — I will not give up my reason that is devoted to the Law.
\p
\v 9 But even in fire and amid great torments he endured nobly — unconquered — and did not waver in his devoutness.
\p
\v 15 When the fifth was brought forward he fixed his gaze on the tyrant and said:
\v 16 I will not hesitate — being master of my own passions — to die for the sake of virtue.
\p
\v 17 Come then — let your torments begin — but know that our reason (*logismos*) will not be bound by you.
\p
\v 19 Then he too underwent the same — and died still reasoning about the Law and holding it as the guide of his soul (*psychēs*).
\p
\v 20 After him the sixth was brought forward. When he was about to die he said:
\v 21 Do not go astray in vain tyrant — for I too am being tortured for the sake of virtue and for the sake of my reason (*logismon*) — just as my brothers before me.
\v 22 Do not think that you are unconquerable while you are fighting against piety — for our reason (*logismos*) conquers even in the depths of agony and in the midst of fire.

\c 11
\p
\v 1 As the sixth brother too was thrown into the cauldron and died there the seventh — the youngest of all — came forward.
\v 2 Although the tyrant had been put to shame by all the brothers yet even though he was filled with rage he still desired to torture the youngest — even more than the others.
\p
\v 3 Since the young man would not listen to him at all the king summoned the young man's mother and urged her to counsel her son to save himself.
\v 4 After he had earnestly urged her — she undertook to persuade her son.
\v 5 But she laughed at the tyranny and said within her mind (*logismō*) and said to her son in the ancestral language:
\p
\v 6 My son — have pity on me who carried you nine months in my womb and suckled you for three years and reared you and brought you to your present age.
\v 7 I beg you my child to look at the heaven and the earth and see all things in them and recognize that God made them out of non-existence (*ouk ex ontōn*) and that the human race has come to be the same way.
\v 8 Do not fear this torturer but being worthy of your brothers accept death — so that in the mercy of God I may receive you again together with your brothers.
\p
\v 12 But the youth said: Whom are you waiting for? I will not obey the king's command but I will obey the command of the Law which was given to our ancestors through Moses.
\v 13 But you who have devised all sorts of evil against the Hebrews will not escape the hands of God.
\p
\v 20 When the seventh too had been put to death — the mother looked on all of them in a serene manner — and saw them all dead around her — as though at a feast of honor — for the honor of the Law and the courage of her sons.
\v 21 And she sang praise to God — as she stood among them singing:
\p The children of Abraham are not conquered by the tyrant — for the seed of the patriarchs remains immortal (*athanatos*).

\c 12
\p
\v 1 But when the tyrant saw that he had been conquered by her piety and that her children had endured pain nobly and had died without being moved by his tortures — he gave orders for her too to be seized and to be tortured.
\p
\v 6 Then the mother leaped into the fire — in order that no one should touch her body.

\c 13
\p
\v 1 If therefore the seven brothers despised the sufferings even unto death the admitted conclusion is that devout reason (*eusebēs logismos*) is sovereign master of the passions.
\v 2 For if they had been ruled by their passions and had eaten defiling food we would say that they had been conquered by them.
\v 3 But in fact this was not so — but through the reason that is praised by God they prevailed over the passions.
\v 4 The supremacy of the mind is self-evident — for they were masters of the passions and pains.
\p
\v 12 And not only this but also because all of them while looking upon the same torments bore them together while still bound together by the bonds of piety.
\v 13 Consider how complex is a love between brothers — for nature and daily converse and the shared education in virtue have made it great.
\v 14 The fellowship they shared was more than a bond of affection — it was the shared training in the Law — their common covenant-life.

\c 14
\p
\v 1 Since then the seven brothers despised their sufferings and deaths they were proved to have the mastery over their passions.
\p
\v 6 Even the wild beasts do not show a greater love for their offspring than a mother feels for her children — especially when their children willingly go to their deaths for piety's sake.
\p
\v 11 But the devout mother who in no way yielded her reason (*logismon*) to any of the passions — with her heart (*kardia*) inflamed with piety — she put her reason above all else. She loved life but — because of her love for piety she chose to be a guardian of piety.

\c 15
\p
\v 1 O reason (*logismos*) of the children — more royal than kings and freer than free persons.
\v 2 O holy and harmonious concert of the seven brothers on behalf of piety!
\v 3 None of the seven youths proved coward or drew back from death — but all of them as if running on the road toward immortality hastened to death by torture.
\p
\v 11 O mother — who with your seven sons nullified the violence of the tyrant — frustrated his evil schemes and showed the courage of your faith.

\p

\p
\v 13 O woman — soldier of God for piety — elder and woman — you conquered even a tyrant through your endurance — and were found mightier in your deeds and words.
\p

\v 13 He spoke to you of the zeal of Phinehas and he taught you about Hananiah Azariah and Mishael in the fire.

## CRITICAL TEXTUAL NOTES — 4 MACCABEES

**Genre:** 4 Maccabees is a philosophical diatribe — a rhetorical demonstration-speech (*epideixis*) — not a narrative history. It retells the martyrdom stories of 2 Maccabees 6–7 (Eleazar / the seven brothers / their mother) as philosophical proof-texts for the thesis: *devout reason is sovereign over the passions* (*eusebēs logismos despotēs tōn pathōn*).

**Philosophical register:** The book deploys Stoic philosophical vocabulary — *logismos* (rational faculty) / *pathē* (passions) / *phronēsis* (prudence/wisdom) / the four cardinal virtues — but fills them with Jewish-covenantal content. The argument is: what the Stoics claim for reason-in-general, the Jewish martyrs demonstrate for *devout* reason specifically. The thesis is not Stoic but Torah-informed: the *logismos* that is sovereign is the *logismos* educated by the Law.

**Original language:** Greek. No Semitic original known. Probably composed in the first century BCE–CE, possibly in Alexandria or Antioch.

**The *antipsychos* theology:** The most doctrinally significant contribution of 4 Maccabees is the application of substitutionary / atoning language to the martyrs' deaths: 6:28–29 (Eleazar's prayer: *take my life in exchange for theirs / let my blood be their purification*) and 17:21–22 (the martyrs as *hilastērion* = atoning sacrifice / *antipsychos* = life-for-life ransom). This is the most explicit pre-NT statement of vicarious atoning death in the LXX.

## CAE NODES — 4 MACCABEES

### 4 Maccabees 1:1 / 13:1 — the thesis: *eusebēs logismos despotēs tōn pathōn* — CAE PRIMARY NODE

**1:1** — "The subject that I am about to discuss is most philosophical — that is whether devout reason is sovereign over the passions (*ei autokratōr estin tōn pathōn ho eusebēs logismos*)."

**13:1** — "If therefore the seven brothers despised the sufferings even unto death the admitted conclusion is that devout reason (*eusebēs logismos*) is sovereign master of the passions."

***eusebēs logismos despotēs estin tōn pathōn***

= devout reason is sovereign master of the passions.

The thesis of 4 Maccabees is stated at the opening and proven by the martyrdom narratives. The argument structure is philosophical-rhetorical: thesis → examples → conclusion. The distinctive move of the book is the qualifier *eusebēs* = devout / God-fearing attached to *logismos* = rational faculty. The Stoics argued that reason in general can master the passions; 4 Maccabees argues that reason *educated by the divine Law* specifically is the agent of this mastery.

The distinction matters for the GLE: the *logismos* is not autonomous — it is formed by *paideia* = education in the Torah. The rational faculty that masters the passions is the *kardia* and *dianoia* that have been shaped by the *nomos* = Law. The martyrs' ability to endure is not heroic willpower but covenant-formation: they were educated in the Law by their father (18:11–16) / their mother taught them in the ancestral language (7:21) / the Law is the *paidagōgos* = tutor that formed the *logismos*.

**Deuteronomy 6:4–7** — "Hear O Israel: the Lord our God the Lord is one... and these words that I command you today shall be on your heart (*kardia*)... You shall teach them diligently to your children." The Shema-command of heart-formation and children's education is the Torah-ground for the 4 Maccabees thesis: the *logismos* educated in the Law from childhood (18:11) is the *eusebēs logismos* that masters the passions in the moment of crisis.

**Romans 12:2** — "Do not be conformed to this world but be transformed by the renewal of your mind (*anakainōsei tou noos*) that by testing you may discern what is the will of God — what is good and acceptable and perfect." Paul's *nous* = mind transformed by renewal is the NT equivalent of the 4 Maccabees *eusebēs logismos* = devout reason educated by the Law: both describe the rational faculty shaped by divine instruction as the agent of proper moral discernment and resistance to conformity-pressure.

**Philippians 4:7** — "And the peace of God which surpasses all understanding will guard your hearts (*kardias*) and your minds (*noēmata*) in Christ Jesus." Paul's peace-of-God guarding the *kardia* and *noēmata* is the NT pneumatological form of the 4 Maccabees *eusebēs logismos*: both describe the divinely-formed inner faculty that holds firm under pressure. The 4 Maccabees *logismos* holds under physical torture; the Philippians *eirēnē* guards under anxiety. Both are forms of the covenant-mind.

### 4 Maccabees 5:16–24 — Eleazar's response to Antiochus / reason not overcome by coercion — CAE PRIMARY NODE

**5:17–20** — "We Antiochus who are persuaded that we live and die under God's Law do not have any authority to transgress his Law under any circumstances — in small things or in great... But because we hold reason to be of overriding weight and of God-given authority we honor the truth of the Law with the obedience that we owe to God."

**5:23–24** — "Do not suppose your threats can weaken our reasoning (*logismon*) — for our argument is stronger than your threats. Our reason (*logismos*) when educated by the Law is not overcome even by your violent coercion — and our mind (*dianoia*) is not persuaded by your supposed pleasures."

Eleazar's speech before Antiochus is the book's first philosophical demonstration. The argument:

1. **The Law is the authority under which we live and die** — Antiochus's authority is delegated and contingent; the Law's authority is absolute and constitutive. Antiochus can kill but cannot dissolve the covenant.

2. **The reason educated by the Law is God-given** (*theion logon*) — the *logismos* is not merely human willpower but a faculty formed by divine instruction. Its authority is derivative of the divine authority that formed it.

3. **The threats strengthen rather than weaken** — the coercion of pain and pleasure are the *pathē* = passions against which the *logismos* contends. Every application of torture is an opportunity for the *logismos* to demonstrate its sovereignty.

The philosophical precision here is significant: Eleazar is not making a martyr's emotional speech but a reasoned argument. He engages Antiochus's philosophy (the pleasure principle / the self-preservation instinct) with a counter-philosophy (reason educated by the Law is a higher principle than physical self-preservation).

**Daniel 3:16–18** — "Shadrach Meshach and Abednego answered the king: O Nebuchadnezzar we have no need to answer you in this matter. If this is so our God whom we serve is able to deliver us... But if not — be it known to you O king that we will not serve your gods." The three youths' *but if not* response to Nebuchadnezzar is the paradigmatic OT form of what Eleazar articulates philosophically: the Law's authority is not contingent on divine rescue. 4 Maccabees 18:13 explicitly cites the three youths as the founding example.

**Acts 5:29** — "We must obey God rather than men." Peter's statement before the Sanhedrin is the compressed NT form of the Eleazar argument: the divine authority (*theos*) supersedes the human authority (*anthrōpoi*) — the *logismos* educated by the divine Law recognizes the hierarchy of authorities.

**1 Corinthians 2:14–15** — "The natural person does not accept the things of the Spirit of God — for they are folly to him and he is not able to understand them because they are spiritually discerned. The spiritual person judges all things." Paul's *psychikos* / *pneumatikos* distinction maps onto the 4 Maccabees thesis: the *psychikos anthrōpos* who responds only to the passion-level (pleasure/pain) cannot understand the *logismos* formed by the divine Spirit / Law. The spiritual person — like Eleazar — judges by a higher principle than physical self-interest.

### 4 Maccabees 6:19 / 17:12–13 — the *antipsychos* / the *hilastērion* — CAE CRITICAL NODE

**6:19** — "Be merciful to your people and let our punishment be a satisfaction on their behalf. Make my blood their purification and take my life in exchange for theirs (*antipsychon autōn labē tēn emēn psychēn*)."

**17:12–13** — "And they having become as it were a ransom for the sin of our nation (*antipsychos gegonen hē hagia psychē*). And through the blood of those devout ones and their death as an atoning sacrifice (*hilastēriou*) divine Providence preserved Israel that previously had been mistreated."

***antipsychon autōn labē tēn emēn psychēn*** = take my life as a life-for-life ransom for theirs.

***dia tou haimatos tōn eusebōn ekeinou kai tou hilastēriou tou thanatou autōn*** = through the blood of those devout ones and the atoning sacrifice of their death.

The *antipsychos* = life-for-life / ransom-exchange theology and the *hilastērion* = atoning sacrifice / propitiatory application to the Maccabean martyrs are the most theologically significant passages in the entire deuterocanon for NT Christology. Two distinct but related concepts:

**1. *Antipsychos*** (6:19): Eleazar prays that his *psychē* = life-soul be taken *in exchange for* (*anti-*) the lives of the people. The *anti-* prefix carries the substitution force: one life given in place of another. This is the structural form of the *ransom* theology — not Eleazar dies alongside the people but in their place / on their behalf / so that they might be spared.

**2. *Hilastērion*** (17:13): The martyrs' deaths are described as a *hilastērion* = atoning sacrifice / propitiatory covering. The *hilastērion* is the LXX term for the *kapporet* = mercy seat / lid of the ark where blood was sprinkled on the Day of Atonement (Leviticus 16:14–15 / Exodus 25:17–22). To describe the martyrs' deaths as a *hilastērion* is to apply the Yom Kippur atonement-theology to their deaths: their blood is the covering that turns aside the divine wrath from the covenant-people.

The theological precision of 4 Maccabees here is extraordinary — it moves beyond general heroism-language into specifically cultic-sacrificial language. The martyrs' deaths are not merely admirable; they are *effective* in the way that the Temple sacrifices were effective: they accomplish something for the people that the people could not accomplish for themselves.

**Leviticus 16:14–16** — The Yom Kippur blood-sprinkling on the *hilastērion* / *kapporet* = mercy seat: the high priest's blood-act covers the sins of the people. The 4 Maccabees martyrs' deaths are described in this cultic vocabulary — their blood accomplishes what the Yom Kippur ritual accomplishes.

**Isaiah 53:10–12** — "When his soul (*psychē*) makes an offering for guilt... he bore the sin of many and makes intercession for the transgressors." The Servant's *psychē* as guilt-offering / the Servant's bearing of sin / the Servant's intercession — these are the prophetic ground for the *antipsychos* and *hilastērion* theology of 4 Maccabees. The martyrs' deaths are described in Servant-Song vocabulary.

**Romans 3:25** — "God put forward Christ as a propitiation (*hilastērion*) by his blood to be received by faith." Paul's application of the *hilastērion* to Christ's death is the NT fulfillment of the 4 Maccabees *hilastērion* application to the martyrs. The chain: Leviticus 16 (*kapporet* / *hilastērion* = mercy seat) → 4 Maccabees 17:13 (martyrs' deaths as *hilastērion*) → Romans 3:25 (Christ as *hilastērion*). Paul is deploying the same term in the same sacrificial-covering sense — but applying it to the death of the Son of God rather than the deaths of the Maccabean martyrs. The martyrs' *hilastērion* is the type; Christ's *hilastērion* is the antitype and fulfillment.

**Mark 10:45** — "For even the Son of Man came not to be served but to serve and to give his life as a ransom for many (*lytron anti pollōn*)." The *lytron anti* = ransom in place of / in exchange for is the NT verbal equivalent of the *antipsychos* = life-for-life ransom of 4 Maccabees 6:19. Both use the *anti-* substitution prefix: the one life is given *in exchange for* / *in place of* the many. The 4 Maccabees *antipsychos* is the deuterocanonical precedent for the Markan ransom-formula. The chain: Isaiah 53:10 → 4 Maccabees 6:19 (*antipsychon labē tēn emēn psychēn*) → Mark 10:45 (*lytron anti pollōn*) → Romans 3:25 (*hilastērion*).

**1 John 2:2** — "And he is the propitiation (*hilasmos*) for our sins and not for ours only but also for the sins of the whole world." The *hilasmos* = propitiation applied to Christ is the NT universal extension of the *hilastērion* / *hilasmos* language: what the Maccabean martyrs' deaths accomplished for Israel (4 Maccabees 17:13) Christ's death accomplishes for the whole world (1 John 2:2). The scope expands; the sacrificial-atonement structure is identical.

### 4 Maccabees 7:17–19 — live to God as Abraham Isaac and Jacob live to God — CAE PRIMARY NODE

**7:17–19** — "For those who keep in mind the sufferings for the sake of God exercise dominion over the passions and believe that they — like our father Abraham and like Isaac and Jacob — will not die to God but will live to God (*ou thnēskousin theō alla zōsin tō theō*). Therefore the contradiction in terms among some that a man who has exercised reason and devoted himself to philosophy for the sake of the Law will die to God does not hold."

***ou thnēskousin theō alla zōsin tō theō hōsper kai Abraam kai Isaak kai Iakōb kai pantes hoi patriarchai zōsin tō theō***

= they do not die to God but live to God — as indeed Abraham and Isaac and Jacob and all the patriarchs live to God.

The *zōsin tō theō* = live to God formula applied to the martyrs and the patriarchs is the 4 Maccabees resurrection-declaration. The argument is not merely that the martyrs will be resurrected in the future but that they *already* live to God — as the patriarchs already live to God. The present tense *zōsin* = live is deliberate: the life with God is not a future state contingent on resurrection but a present reality constituted by the covenant-relationship.

This is the most philosophically precise statement of the patriarchal-life-with-God theology in the LXX. The argument: if the covenant-relationship with God constitutes life / and if the covenant-relationship does not end at death / then the patriarchs and the martyrs live to God even now.

**Exodus 3:6** — "I am the God of your father the God of Abraham the God of Isaac and the God of Jacob." Jesus's exegesis of this verse in the resurrection-debate with the Sadducees (Matthew 22:32) is the NT deployment of the same argument: "He is not God of the dead but of the living — for all live to him (*pantes gar autō zōsin*)" (Luke 20:38). Jesus's *pantes gar autō zōsin* = for all live to him is the exact NT equivalent of the 4 Maccabees *zōsin tō theō* = live to God. The chain: 4 Maccabees 7:19 (*zōsin tō theō hōsper kai Abraam*) → Luke 20:38 (*pantes gar autō zōsin*). Jesus's resurrection-argument with the Sadducees is the NT exposition of what 4 Maccabees 7:17–19 establishes philosophically.

**Romans 6:10–11** — "For the death he died he died to sin once for all but the life he lives he lives to God (*zē tō theō*). So you also must consider yourselves dead to sin and alive to God (*zōntas tō theō*) in Christ Jesus." Paul's *zēn tō theō* = living to God is the NT covenantal appropriation of the *zōsin tō theō* formula: what the martyrs and patriarchs demonstrated by covenant-faithfulness in death / believers appropriate through union with Christ. The formula is identical; the ground shifts from covenant-Law-formation to union with the Risen One.

### 4 Maccabees 11:7 / 18:7 — *creatio ex nihilo* repeated / *ouk ex ontōn* — CAE NODE

**11:7** — "I beg you my child to look at the heaven and the earth and see all things in them and recognize that God made them out of non-existence (*ouk ex ontōn*) and that the human race has come to be the same way."

This is the 4 Maccabees repetition of the 2 Maccabees 7:28 *creatio ex nihilo* formula — word-for-word identical. The repetition across two different books in the Maccabean corpus is a canonical signature: the *ouk ex ontōn epoiēsen* = made from non-existence is the fixed formula for creation-from-nothing in the LXX. 4 Maccabees cites 2 Maccabees explicitly or has independent access to the same tradition.

The theological function is identical: the mother deploys *creatio ex nihilo* as the ground for resurrection-hope. If God created from nothing he can restore from death. The creation-ground of resurrection is the consistent Maccabean theological argument.

**Romans 4:17** — *ta mē onta hōs onta* = calling into existence what does not exist — Paul's formulation is the NT equivalent of the *ouk ex ontōn epoiēsen* formula. The chain through both Maccabean books is now confirmed: 2 Maccabees 7:28 = 4 Maccabees 11:7 → Romans 4:17 → Hebrews 11:3.

### 4 Maccabees 17:3–6 — the mother as cosmic pillar / honored before God like stars — CAE PRIMARY NODE

**17:3** — "Nobly set like a roof on the pillars of your children — you endured the earthquake of the tortures without swaying."

**17:5** — "Not so majestic stands the moon in heaven — with the stars around her — as you stand honored before God by leading your sons to piety — and setting them like stars in the sky."

**17:16** — "Now they stand before the divine throne and live through the blessed aeon."

The cosmic imagery applied to the mother and the seven brothers — roof / pillars / moon / stars / standing before the divine throne — is the book's doxological climax. The martyrs are not merely admirable humans who died well; they are now located in the divine presence (*before the divine throne* / *living the blessed aeon*). The spatial language moves from the torture-chamber to the heavenly court.

The **seven sons as stars** imagery is the 4 Maccabees deployment of the Daniel 12:3 promise: *those who turn many to righteousness like the stars forever and ever*. The seven martyrs are the constellation formed by their mother's covenant-formation — *she set them like stars in the sky*.

**Daniel 12:3** — "And those who are wise shall shine like the brightness of the sky above — and those who turn many to righteousness like the stars forever and ever." The stellar-immortality promise of Daniel is the prophetic ground for the 4 Maccabees star-imagery: the martyrs fulfill the Daniel promise — they shine like stars.

**Revelation 2:10** — "Be faithful unto death and I will give you the crown of life." The Revelation crown-promise to the martyr church of Smyrna echoes the 4 Maccabees *stephanos athanasias* = crown of incorruption (17:15): both address communities facing death for covenant-faithfulness / both promise life as the reward of faithfulness unto death.

**Matthew 13:43** — "Then the righteous will shine like the sun in the kingdom of their Father." Jesus's parable-conclusion deploys the same stellar-immortality imagery as Daniel 12:3 and 4 Maccabees 17:5: the righteous shine like stars / like the sun in the Father's kingdom. The 4 Maccabees martyrs are the historical pre-figuration of the Matthew 13 eschatological shining.

### 4 Maccabees 18:23 — *athanasian eis ton aiōnion zōēn* — CAE CRITICAL NODE

**18:23** — "O how blessed and most blessed are those who died to keep the Law — so that they received from God incorruptibility in everlasting life (*athanasian eis ton aiōnion zōēn*)."

***athanasian eis ton aiōnion zōēn***

= incorruption into everlasting life.

The book's final theological declaration is its most eschatologically explicit: the martyrs received *athanasia* = incorruption / immortality as a gift from God (*para tou theou*) and this incorruption is *eis ton aiōnion zōēn* = into the everlasting life. The formula is precise:
- *athanasia* = deathlessness / incorruption — the quality received
- *eis* = into — the directional/purposive preposition: the incorruption is for the sake of / directed toward
- *ton aiōnion zōēn* = the everlasting life — the sphere entered

The *aiōnios zōē* = everlasting life is the same formula that the NT uses as the primary term for the resurrection-life. The chain from 2 Maccabees 7:9 (*anastasōs aiōniou* = everlasting life-renewal) through 4 Maccabees 18:23 (*athanasian eis ton aiōnion zōēn*) to the NT is complete.

**John 3:16** — "For God so loved the world that he gave his only Son — that whoever believes in him should not perish but have everlasting life (*zōēn aiōnion*)." The Johannine *zōē aiōnios* is the NT fulfillment of the Maccabean *aiōnios zōē*: what the martyrs received through covenant-faithfulness unto death / all who believe in the Son receive through union with his death and resurrection. The scope of access changes (martyrdom → faith) but the substance of the gift (*aiōnios zōē*) is identical.

**1 Corinthians 15:53–54** — "For this perishable body must put on the imperishable and this mortal body must put on immortality (*athanasian*). When the perishable puts on the imperishable and the mortal puts on immortality (*athanasian*) then shall come to pass the saying that is written: Death is swallowed up in victory." Paul's *athanasia* = immortality that the resurrection-body puts on is precisely the *athanasia* that the Maccabean martyrs receive from God (4 Maccabees 18:23). The same vocabulary / the same concept / the same divine gift — but applied by Paul to all believers at the general resurrection rather than to the martyrs at death. The chain: 4 Maccabees 18:23 → 1 Corinthians 15:53–54.

## STRUCTURAL NOTE — 4 MACCABEES AS THE PHILOSOPHICAL CAPSTONE

4 Maccabees is the LXX deuterocanon's final theological statement — and its most intellectually ambitious. The book takes the raw material of 2 Maccabees 6–7 (the martyr-narratives) and processes them through a philosophical lens to extract their theological maximum.

**The argument's structure:**
1. **Thesis** (1:1): Devout reason is sovereign over the passions.
2. **Philosophical ground** (1:14–3:18): The four cardinal virtues / reason as governor of the soul.
3. **Proof from Eleazar** (5:1–7:23): The aged priest's reason conquers death-fear and pain.
4. **Proof from the seven brothers** (8:1–14:10): The coordinated martyrdom of seven young men.
5. **Proof from the mother** (14:11–17:6): The supreme instance — a mother watches seven sons die without breaking.
6. **Conclusion** (17:7–18:24): The martyrs as *hilastērion* / living to God / crowned with immortality.

**The theological synthesis 4 Maccabees achieves:**

Four major doctrines are stated at their clearest OT-deuterocanonical level here:

1. **Substitutionary atonement**: *antipsychos* (6:19) + *hilastērion* (17:13) — the martyr's death effective for the people's sins.
2. **Creatio ex nihilo**: *ouk ex ontōn* (11:7) — God made from nothing; resurrection from death is the same power.
3. **Life to God beyond death**: *zōsin tō theō* (7:19) — the patriarchs and martyrs live to God now.
4. **Immortality as divine gift**: *athanasian eis ton aiōnion zōēn* (18:23) — everlasting life received from God.

**The NT trajectory from 4 Maccabees:**
- *Hilastērion* → Romans 3:25 / 1 John 2:2
- *Antipsychos* → Mark 10:45 / Galatians 3:13
- *Zōsin tō theō* → Luke 20:38 / Romans 6:10–11
- *Eusebēs logismos* → Romans 12:2 / Philippians 4:7
- *Athanasian eis ton aiōnion zōēn* → John 3:16 / 1 Corinthians 15:53–54
- *Ouk ex ontōn* → Romans 4:17 / Hebrews 11:3
- Stars before the throne → Daniel 12:3 → Revelation 2:10 / Matthew 13:43

**The deuterocanonical corpus is now complete.** The arc from Tobit (the covenant household faithful in diaspora) through Judith (the God of the humble defeats the mighty) through Esther (hidden providence / *eis ton kairon touton*) through 1 Maccabees (the *bdelygma erēmōseōs* / the prophet-gap) through 2 Maccabees (resurrection / *creatio ex nihilo* / discipline-not-destruction) through 3 Maccabees (triple reversal / the feast of deliverance / God as Father) through 4 Maccabees (devout reason / the *hilastērion* / immortality) constitutes a sustained theological preparation for the NT.

The seven deuterocanonical narrative books form a unified theological argument:
- **Covenant faithfulness in diaspora** (Tobit / Esther): the *kardia* that remembers God when the Temple is inaccessible
- **Divine reversal through the weak** (Judith / 3 Maccabees): the God of the humble / *en cheiri gynaikos*
- **Historical faithfulness and its limits** (1 Maccabees): the rededicated altar / the prophet-gap / *eis ton kairon touton*
- **Resurrection and atonement** (2 Maccabees / 4 Maccabees): the body restored / the *hilastērion* applied / *athanasian eis ton aiōnion zōēn*

The entire sequence answers Nehemiah 9:36 (*we are slaves*) and prepares for Luke 4:18–21 (*today this Scripture is fulfilled*).

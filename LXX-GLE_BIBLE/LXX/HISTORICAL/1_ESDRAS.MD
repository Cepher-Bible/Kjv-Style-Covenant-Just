\v 1 Josiah kept the Passover to his Lord in Jerusalem and slaughtered the Passover lamb on the fourteenth day of the first month —
\v 2 having set the priests in their divisions arrayed in their vestments in the temple of the Lord.
\v 3 And he spoke to the Levites the temple servants of Israel that they should hallow themselves to the Lord by placing the holy ark of the Lord in the house that King Solomon the son of David had built —
\v 4 and said: You need no longer carry it on your shoulders. Now serve the Lord your God and attend to his people Israel and prepare yourselves by your fathers' houses and tribes according to the prescription of David king of Israel and the magnificence of his son Solomon.
\v 5 Standing in the temple according to the dignities of your fathers' houses the Levites who were before your brethren the sons of Israel —
\v 6 offer the Passover in order and prepare the sacrifices for your brethren and keep the Passover according to the commandment of the Lord which was given to Moses.
\v 7 And Josiah provided for the people who were present thirty thousand lambs and kids and three thousand calves — these were provided from the king's possessions as he promised to the people and the priests and the Levites.
\v 8 And Hilkiah and Zechariah and Esyellus the rulers of the temple gave to the priests for the Passover two thousand six hundred sheep and three hundred calves.
\v 9 And Jeconiah and Shemaiah and Nathaniel his brother and Hashabiah and Ociel and Joram captains over thousands gave to the Levites for the Passover five thousand sheep and seven hundred calves.
\p
\v 10 And when all this had been done the priests and the Levites stood in proper order having the unleavened bread according to their tribes —
\v 11 and according to the dignities of the fathers' houses before the people to offer to the Lord as it is written in the book of Moses. And thus they did in the morning.
\v 12 And they roasted the Passover lamb with fire as is proper — and the sacrifices they boiled in bronze vessels and caldrons and the good odor spread —
\v 13 and set them before all the people — and afterward they prepared for themselves and for their brethren the priests the sons of Aaron.
\v 14 For the priests offered the fat until night — and the Levites prepared for themselves and for the priests the sons of Aaron.
\v 15 The holy singers the sons of Asaph were at their post according to the appointment of David — namely Asaph Zechariah and Eddinus who was of the king's retinue.
\v 16 The gatekeepers were at each gate — no one needed to leave his daily duties for their brethren the Levites prepared things for them.
\v 17 So the things pertaining to the Lord's sacrifices were accomplished that day in keeping the Passover and offering sacrifices on the altar of the Lord according to the command of King Josiah.
\v 18 And the sons of Israel who were present at that time kept the Passover and the Feast of Unleavened Bread seven days.
\v 19 No Passover like it had been kept in Israel since the days of the prophet Samuel —
\v 20 and none of the kings of Israel had kept such a Passover as was kept by Josiah with the priests and the Levites and all Judah and Israel who were present with the inhabitants of Jerusalem.
\v 21 In the eighteenth year of the reign of Josiah this Passover was kept.
\p
\v 23 After all these acts of Josiah it happened that Pharaoh the king of Egypt came up to make war at Carchemish on the Euphrates and Josiah went out to meet him.
\v 24 But the king of Egypt sent word to him saying: What have I to do with you O king of Judah?
\v 25 I was not sent against you by the Lord God — for my war is at the Euphrates. And now the Lord is with me and the Lord is urging me on. Stand aside and do not oppose the Lord.
\v 26 However Josiah did not turn back to his chariot but undertook to fight him and did not heed the words of the prophet Jeremiah from the mouth of the Lord.
\v 27 He joined battle with him in the plain of Megiddo and the commanders came down against King Josiah.
\v 28 The king said to his servants: Take me away from the battle — for I am very weak. And immediately his servants took him out of the battle line.
\v 29 And he got into his second chariot — and after being brought back to Jerusalem he died and was buried in the tomb of his fathers.
\v 30 And in all Judah and Jerusalem they mourned for Josiah — and Jeremiah the prophet lamented for Josiah and the chief men with the women made lamentation for him to this day — and it was ordained that this should always be done throughout all the nation of Israel.
\v 31 These things are written in the book of the histories of the kings of Judah — and every deed of Josiah and his glory and his understanding of the law of the Lord — and what he had done before and what is now told — all is recorded in the book of the kings of Israel and Judah.
\p
\v 32 And the men of the nation took Jehoahaz the son of Josiah and made him king in Jerusalem in place of his father when he was twenty-three years old.
\v 33 And he reigned three months in Jerusalem — and the king of Egypt deposed him from reigning in Jerusalem —
\v 34 and imposed a tribute on the people of a hundred talents of silver and one talent of gold.
\v 35 And the king of Egypt made his brother Jehoiakim king of Judah and Jerusalem.
\v 36 And Jehoiakim bound the nobles — but Zarius his brother he seized and brought him out of Egypt.
\v 37 Jehoiakim was twenty-five years old when he became king of Judah and Jerusalem and he did what was evil in the sight of the Lord.
\v 38 Nebuchadnezzar king of Babylon came up against him and bound him with a chain of bronze and carried him off to Babylon.
\v 39 Nebuchadnezzar also took some of the holy vessels of the Lord and carried them away and stored them in his temple in Babylon.
\v 40 But the account of his acts his unclean ways and his impiety is recorded in the book of the chronicles of the kings.
\v 41 Jehoiachin his son became king in his place — when he was made king he was eighteen years old —
\v 42 and he reigned three months and ten days in Jerusalem and did what was evil in the sight of the Lord.
\v 43 After a year Nebuchadnezzar sent and brought him to Babylon together with the precious vessels of the house of the Lord —
\v 44 and made Zedekiah king of Judah and Jerusalem — Zedekiah was twenty-one years old. And he reigned eleven years.
\v 45 He too did what was evil in the sight of the Lord and did not heed the words of the prophet Jeremiah who spoke from the mouth of the Lord.
\v 46 And when King Nebuchadnezzar had made him swear by the name of the Lord he broke the oath and rebelled. And hardening his neck and his heart he transgressed the laws of the Lord the God of Israel.
\v 47 Even the leading men of the people and of the priests did many acts of sacrilege and lawlessness beyond all the unclean deeds of all the nations — and polluted the temple of the Lord which he had sanctified in Jerusalem.
\v 48 And the God of their fathers sent by his messenger to call them back because he had compassion on them and on his dwelling place.
\v 49 But they mocked his messengers and on whatever day the Lord spoke to them they scoffed at his prophets —
\v 50 until he was so angered at his people on account of their ungodly deeds that he commanded the kings of the Chaldeans to attack them.
\v 51 These slew their young men with the sword around their holy temple and did not spare young man or young woman old man or child — but the Lord gave them all into their hands.
\v 52 And they took all the holy vessels of the Lord — both great and small — the ark of God and the king's treasuries and carried them off to Babylon.
\v 53 And they burned the house of the Lord and broke down the walls of Jerusalem and burned its towers with fire —
\v 54 and they utterly destroyed all its glorious things and brought those who survived the sword back to Babylon —
\v 55 where they became slaves to him and to his sons until the Persians began to reign — in fulfillment of the word of the Lord by the mouth of Jeremiah:
\v 56 Until the land has enjoyed its Sabbaths — all the time that it lies desolate it shall keep Sabbath to complete seventy years.

\v 1 In the first year of Cyrus as king of the Persians — that the word of the Lord by the mouth of Jeremiah might be accomplished —
\v 2 the Lord stirred up the breath-of-life (*pneuma*) of Cyrus king of the Persians and he made a proclamation throughout all his kingdom and also put it in writing saying:
\v 3 Thus says Cyrus king of the Persians: The Lord of Israel the Most High Lord has made me king of the whole world —
\v 4 and he has commanded me to build him a house at Jerusalem which is in Judea.
\v 5 If therefore there is anyone among you who belongs to his people let his Lord be with him and let him go up to Jerusalem which is in Judea and build the house of the Lord of Israel — he is the Lord who dwells in Jerusalem.
\v 6 Let each man who lives in the various places be helped by the men of his place with gold and silver —
\v 7 with gifts and with horses and cattle together with the other things added as votive offerings for the temple of the Lord which is in Jerusalem.
\p
\v 8 Then the heads of families of the tribes of Judah and Benjamin rose up — with the priests and the Levites and all those whose breath-of-life (*pneuma*) the Lord had stirred to go up to build the house in Jerusalem for the Lord —
\v 9 and their neighbors helped them with everything — with silver and gold with horses and cattle and with very many free-will offerings from many whose hearts were moved.
\v 10 And King Cyrus brought out the holy vessels of the Lord which Nebuchadnezzar had carried away from Jerusalem and stored in his temple of idols —
\v 11 and Cyrus king of the Persians brought them out in the charge of Mithridates his treasurer —
\v 12 and by his count these were: a thousand gold cups a thousand silver cups twenty-nine silver censers thirty gold bowls two thousand four hundred and ten silver bowls and a thousand other vessels.
\v 13 All the vessels of gold and silver were five thousand four hundred and sixty-nine.
\v 14 All these were brought back by Sheshbazzar with the returning exiles from Babylon to Jerusalem.
\p
\v 15 But in the time of Artaxerxes king of the Persians Bishlam Mithridates Tabeel Rehum and Shimshai the scribe and the rest of their associates living in Samaria and in the other places wrote him a letter against those who were living in Judah and Jerusalem:
\p
\v 16 To King Artaxerxes our lord — your servants Rehum the recorder and Shimshai the scribe and the other members of their council in Samaria —
\v 17 greetings. Now be it known to our lord the king that the Jews who came up from you to us have come to Jerusalem that most seditious and wicked city —
\v 18 and are rebuilding the market places and repairing the walls and laying the foundations for a temple.
\v 19 Now if this city is rebuilt and the walls are finished they will not only refuse to pay tribute but will even resist kings.
\v 20 Since matters pertaining to the temple are now in progress we think it best not to neglect such a matter —
\v 21 but to speak to our lord the king in order that if it seems good to you inquiry may be made in the records of your fathers and you will find in the records what has been written about them and will understand that this city was a rebellious city troublesome to kings and to other cities and that the Jews were rebels and set up blockades in it from of old.
\v 22 Therefore we have made this known to you so that you may search the records and discover that this city is troublesome —
\v 23 so that this city may not be rebuilt and the wickedness of those people grow so that you may not be harmed.
\p
\v 25 Then in reply to Rehum the recorder Shimshai the scribe and their associates who were living in Samaria and the other places the king wrote:
\v 26 I have read the letter which you sent me. I gave orders to make inquiry and it was found that this city from ancient times has risen up against kings and the people in it have been given to rebellion and war —
\v 27 and that powerful kings have reigned in Jerusalem who have ruled and exacted tribute from Coele-syria and Phoenicia.
\v 28 Therefore I have now given orders to prevent these people from rebuilding the city and to take care that nothing further is done —
\v 29 and that these wicked men proceed no further to the annoyance of the kings.
\p
\v 30 Then when the letter from King Artaxerxes was read Rehum and Shimshai the scribe and their associates went in haste to Jerusalem with horsemen and a force of troops and began to hinder the builders. And the building of the temple in Jerusalem stopped until the second year of the reign of King Darius of the Persians.

\v 1 Now King Darius gave a great banquet for all who were under him and all his household and all the nobles of Persia and Media —
\v 2 and all the satraps and generals and governors who were under him from India to Ethiopia — in the one hundred and twenty-seven provinces.
\v 3 And when they had eaten and drunk and were satisfied they went home — and Darius the king went to his bedroom and slept but then woke up.
\p
\v 4 Then the three young men of the bodyguard who guarded the person of the king said to one another:
\v 5 Let each of us state what one thing is most powerful — and the one whose statement seems wisest Darius the king will give him great gifts and great honors of victory —
\v 6 such as being clothed in purple and drinking from gold vessels and sleeping on a gold bed and having a chariot with gold bridles and a linen turban and a necklace around his neck —
\v 7 and he shall sit second to Darius because of his wisdom and shall be called Kinsman of Darius.
\p
\v 8 Then each wrote his own statement and sealed it and put it under the pillow of King Darius —
\v 9 and said: When the king wakes up they will give him the writing and whichever of the three the king and the three nobles of Persia judge to have written the wisest statement — to him shall the victory be given as prescribed.
\p
\v 10 The first wrote: Wine is most powerful.
\v 11 The second wrote: The king is most powerful.
\v 12 The third wrote: Women are most powerful — but truth conquers all things.
\p
\v 13 When the king awoke he took the writing and it was read — and he sent and called all the nobles of Persia and Media and the satraps and generals and governors and prefects —
\v 14 and sat down in the council chamber and the writing was read before them.
\v 15 And he said: Call the young men and they shall explain their statements. So they were called and came in.
\v 16 And they said to them: Explain to us about what you have written.
\p
\v 17 Then the first who had spoken of the power of wine began and said:
\v 18 Gentlemen — how is wine the most powerful? It leads astray the minds of all men who drink it —
\v 19 it makes equal the mind of the king and the orphan the mind of the slave and the free man the mind of the poor man and the rich man.
\v 20 It turns every thought to festivity and mirth and forgets all sorrow and debt —
\v 21 and makes all hearts feel rich and forgets kings and satraps and makes everyone talk in millions.
\v 22 And when they drink they forget to be friendly with friends and brothers — and before long they draw their swords.
\v 23 And when they recover from the wine they do not remember what they have done.
\v 24 Gentlemen — is not wine the most powerful since it forces men to do these things?
\p When he had said this he stopped speaking.

\c 4
\p
\v 1 Then the second who had spoken of the power of the king began to speak:
\v 2 Gentlemen — are not men most powerful who dominate land and sea and all that is in them?
\v 3 But the king is more powerful — for he is their lord and master and whatever he says to them they obey.
\v 4 If he tells them to make war on one another they do it — and if he sends them out against the enemy they go and overcome mountains and walls and towers.
\v 5 They kill and are killed and do not disobey the king's command. If they win they bring everything to the king — whatever spoil they take and everything else.
\v 6 And those who do not serve in the army or go to war but farm the land — they too whenever they sow and reap bring some to the king — and they compel one another to pay tribute to the king.
\v 7 And yet he is but one man — if he says to kill they kill — if he says to release they release —
\v 8 if he says to attack they attack — if he says to lay waste they lay waste — if he says to build they build —
\v 9 if he says to cut down they cut down — if he says to plant they plant. All his people and his forces obey him.
\v 10 Furthermore — he reclines and he eats and drinks and he sleeps and they keep watch around him and they cannot go away and attend to their own affairs — nor do they disobey him.
\v 11 Gentlemen — how can the king not be most powerful since he is so obeyed?
\p And he stopped speaking.
\p
\v 13 Then the third — who had spoken of women and truth — this was Zerubbabel — began to speak:
\v 14 Gentlemen — is not the king great and are not men many and is not wine strong? Who then is it that rules them and has the mastery over them? Is it not women?
\v 15 Women gave birth to the king and to all the people who rule over sea and land.
\v 16 From women they came — and women raised the very men who plant the vineyards from which wine comes.
\v 17 Women make men's clothes — they bring men glory — men cannot exist without women.
\v 18 If men gather gold and silver and every beautiful thing and then see a woman lovely in appearance and beauty —
\v 19 they let all those things go and gape at her and with open mouths stare at her — and all prefer her to gold or silver or any beautiful thing.
\v 20 A man leaves his own father who brought him up and his own country and clings to his wife.
\v 21 With his wife he ends his days — he remembers neither his father nor his mother nor his country.
\v 22 And therefore you must realize that women rule over you. Do you not toil and labor and bring everything and give it to women?
\v 23 A man takes his sword and goes out to travel and rob and steal and to sail the sea and rivers —
\v 24 and he faces lions and he walks in darkness — and when he has stolen and robbed and plundered he brings it back to the woman he loves.
\v 25 A man loves his wife more than his father or his mother.
\v 26 Many men have lost their minds because of women and have become slaves because of them.
\v 27 Many have perished and stumbled and sinned because of women.
\v 28 And now do you not believe me? Is not the king great in his power? Do not all lands fear even to look at him?
\v 29 Yet I saw him and Apame the daughter of the illustrious Bartacus sitting at the right hand of the king —
\v 30 and she took the crown from the king's head and put it on her own head and slapped the king with her left hand —
\v 31 and at this the king gazed at her with mouth agape. If she smiled at him he laughed — if she was harsh with him he flattered her so that she might be reconciled to him.
\v 32 Gentlemen — why are not women most powerful since they do such things?
\p
\v 33 Then the king and the nobles looked at one another — and he began to speak about truth:
\v 34 Gentlemen — are not women strong? The earth is vast and the sky is high and the sun is swift in its course — for it sweeps around the circuit of the sky and returns to its place in a single day.
\v 35 Is he not great who does these things? But truth is great and stronger than all things.
\v 36 The whole earth calls upon truth and the sky blesses her — all works tremble and quake before her. With her there is no partiality or preference — she does what is just rather than all things that are unjust or wicked. All approve her deeds —
\v 37 and there is nothing wrong in her judgment. Hers is the strength and the kingdom and the power and the majesty of all ages. Blessed be the God of truth.
\p
\v 38 And he stopped speaking — and all the people then cried out and said: Truth is great and most powerful.
\p
\v 39 Then the king said to him: Ask what you wish — even more than was written — and we will give it to you because you have been found to be the wisest. You shall sit next to me and be called my Kinsman.
\v 40 Then he said to the king: Remember the vow that you made on the day when you became king — to rebuild Jerusalem —
\v 41 and to send back all the vessels that were taken from Jerusalem which Cyrus set apart when he vowed to destroy Babylon and vowed to send them back there.
\v 42 And you vowed to rebuild the temple which the Edomites burned when Judea was laid waste by the Chaldeans.
\v 43 And now this is what I ask and request of you my lord the king — this is the princely generosity that shall proceed from you: that you fulfill the vow to the King of heaven which you made with your own mouth.
\p
\v 44 Then King Darius rose and kissed him and wrote letters for him to all the treasurers and governors and generals and satraps that they should escort him and all who were going up with him to rebuild Jerusalem.
\v 45 And to all the governors in Coele-syria and Phoenicia he wrote letters and to those in Lebanon to bring cedar timbers from Lebanon to Jerusalem and to help him rebuild the city.
\v 46 And he wrote for all the Jews who were going up from the kingdom to Judea for the freedom of every slave as well as every debtor —
\v 47 and that no official should enter their gates and that all the territory that they would occupy should be free from tribute —
\v 48 and that the Idumeans should give up the villages of the Jews which they held.
\v 49 And for the building of the temple twenty talents a year were to be given until it was finished —
\v 50 and on the altar of burnt offering ten talents a year for additional offerings as they were directed —
\v 51 and for all who came from Babylon to build the city freedom until the city was finished.
\v 52 And for all the priests who came to minister provision and vestments were to be given — and the emoluments of the Levites were to be provided until the day when the house would be completed and Jerusalem built up.
\v 53 And for all the guards of the city he ordered that their payment and wages be given —
\v 54 and all the vessels that Cyrus had set apart from Babylon were to be sent back — and everything that Cyrus had commanded to be done so it was commanded to be done and to be sent to Jerusalem.
\p
\v 55 And when the young man Zerubbabel went out he lifted up his face to heaven toward Jerusalem and praised the King of heaven saying:
\v 56 From you comes the victory and from you comes wisdom and yours is the glory — and I am your servant.
\v 57 Blessed are you who have given me wisdom — and I give you thanks O Lord of our fathers.
\v 58 And he took the letters and went to Babylon and told all his relatives.
\v 59 And they praised the God of their fathers because he had given them freedom and leave —
\v 60 to go up and build Jerusalem and the temple that is called by his name — and they feasted with music and rejoicing for seven days.

\v 1 After this were chosen the heads of families to go up according to their tribes with their wives and sons and daughters and male and female servants and their cattle.
\v 2 And Darius sent with them a thousand cavalry to escort them to Jerusalem in safety with the music of drums and flutes —
\v 3 all their relatives joining in festivity — and he made them go up together with them.
\p
\v 4 These are the names of the men who went up according to their families from the tribe of Judah: from the sons of Phares Hananiah the son of Ezekias and Mosollamus the son of Zaraias and Zerubbabel —
\v 5 of the sons of Amos Seraiah the son of Ezekias — of the sons of Pahath-moab Esdrameleth and Jesua Moeth son of Josabad son of Jesua and Joab son of Zechariah son of Josabad.
\p
\v 40 The chief priests Jesua and Zerubbabel son of Salathiel and his brothers took their stand and celebrated the Feast of Tabernacles as it is written in the law of Moses — and the daily burnt offerings in the proper order —
\v 41 and after that the continual offerings and the offerings of the Sabbaths and the new moons and all the consecrated feasts to the Lord —
\v 42 and all the free-will offerings to the Lord as every man chose according to his ability.
\v 43 They began to offer burnt offerings to the Lord from the first day of the seventh month though the foundations of the Lord's temple had not yet been laid.
\v 44 And they gave money to the masons and carpenters and food and drink and carts to those from Sidon and Tyre to bring cedar logs from Lebanon and bring them by raft to the harbor of Joppa according to the order of Cyrus king of the Persians.
\p
\v 47 In the second year after coming to the temple of God in Jerusalem in the second month Zerubbabel the son of Salathiel and Jesua the son of Jozadak made a start together with their brethren the priests and the Levites and all who had come to Jerusalem from the exile.
\v 48 And they laid the foundation of the temple of God on the new moon of the second month in the second year after they came to Judea and Jerusalem.
\v 49 And they appointed the Levites who were twenty years old and upward to have charge of the work of the Lord.
\v 50 And Jesua and his sons and brothers and Kadmiel his brother and the sons of Jesua Emadabun and the sons of Joda the son of Iliadun with their sons and brothers — all the Levites — joined together as one in moving forward the work on the house of God.
\p
\v 51 So the builders built the temple of the Lord —
\v 52 and the priests in their vestments stood with musical instruments and trumpets and the Levites the sons of Asaph with cymbals —
\v 53 praising the Lord and blessing him according to the directions of David king of Israel.
\v 54 And they sang hymns giving thanks to the Lord: for his goodness and his glory are forever in all Israel.
\v 55 And all the people sounded trumpets and shouted with a great shout praising the Lord for the raising up of the house of the Lord.
\v 56 And some of the Levitical priests and heads of clans — old men who had seen the former house — came to the building of this one with weeping and great crying —
\v 57 while many came with trumpets and great joy so that the people could not hear the trumpets because of the crying of the people — for the multitude sounded so mightily that it was heard from afar.

\c 6
\p
\v 1 When Sisinnes the governor of Syria and Phoenicia and Sathrabuzanes and their associates came to them and said to them:
\v 2 By whose order are you building this house and this roof and finishing all the other things? And who are the builders who are carrying this out?
\v 3 Nevertheless the elders of the Jews found favor because the Lord had regard for the captivity —
\v 4 and they were not stopped from building until the matter could be reported to Darius and an answer returned about it.
\p
\v 8 In that letter was written the following: To King Darius — all peace. Let it be known to our lord the king —
\v 9 when we went to the country of Judea and entered the city of Jerusalem we found the elders of the Jews who were in the captivity building in the city of Jerusalem a great new house for the Lord of gold and silver cedar timbers —
\v 10 and with these works being done diligently and the work going forward in their hands with great splendor and care.
\v 11 Then we asked these elders saying: By whose authority do you build this house and lay the foundations of these structures?
\v 12 In order that we might inform you in writing who the leaders were we questioned them and asked them for a list of the names of those in charge.
\v 13 And in reply they said: We are the servants of the Lord who created the heaven and the earth.
\v 14 And the house was built many years ago by a great and strong king of Israel and was finished —
\v 15 but when our fathers sinned against the Lord of Israel who is in heaven and provoked him our lord handed them over to Nebuchadnezzar king of Babylon of the Chaldeans —
\v 16 who tore down the house and burned it and carried the people away captive to Babylon.
\v 17 But in the first year of Cyrus as king of Babylonia King Cyrus wrote that this house should be rebuilt —
\v 18 and the holy vessels of gold and silver that Nebuchadnezzar had taken from the house in Jerusalem and had placed in his own temple — Cyrus also gave back from his own temple in Babylon.
\v 19 And they were handed over to a man named Sheshbazzar whom he appointed as governor —
\v 20 and he ordered him to take back all these vessels and deposit them in the temple in Jerusalem and the temple of the Lord was to be rebuilt in its place.
\v 21 Then Sheshbazzar when he arrived here laid the foundations of the house of the Lord in Jerusalem — and since then until now the building is going on and has not yet been completed.
\v 22 So if it please the king let search be made in the royal archives of King Cyrus in Babylon — and if it is found that the building of the house of the Lord in Jerusalem was done with the consent of King Cyrus — and if it is the king's pleasure let him send us instructions about this.
\p
\v 27 Then King Darius ordered search to be made in the royal archives and in Babylon — and in the city of Ecbatana which is in the province of the Medes it was found:
\v 28 a roll in which it was recorded as follows: In the first year of Cyrus the king Cyrus ordered the building of the house of the Lord in Jerusalem where they sacrifice with perpetual fire —
\v 29 its height to be sixty cubits and its breadth sixty cubits — with three layers of unhewn stone and one layer of new native timber — the cost to be paid from the house of Cyrus the king —
\v 30 and also that the holy vessels of the house of the Lord both gold and silver which Nebuchadnezzar had taken from the house in Jerusalem and brought to Babylon should be restored to the house in Jerusalem and deposited where they had been.
\p
\v 31 Moreover King Darius ordered Sisinnes the governor of Syria and Phoenicia and Sathrabuzanes and their associates and those who were appointed as local rulers in Syria and Phoenicia to keep away from the place and permit Zerubbabel the servant of the Lord and governor of Judea and the elders of the Jews to rebuild that house of the Lord in its place.
\v 32 And I have commanded that it be built completely and that full effort be made to help those who have returned from the captivity of Judea until the house of the Lord is finished —
\v 33 and that from the tribute of Coele-syria and Phoenicia a share be given scrupulously to these men for sacrifices to the Lord — to Zerubbabel the governor — for bulls and rams and lambs —
\v 34 and likewise wheat salt wine and oil — to be given to them regularly every year without further question as the priests in Jerusalem may indicate —
\v 35 in order that libations may be offered to the Most High God for the king and his children and that prayer may be offered for their lives.
\p
\v 33 So all things were finished and sanctified — and on the same day there was a great celebration.

\v 1 And the priests and the Levites clothed in vestments according to their kindred groups were standing in the service of their father Aaron as the king of the Persians had commanded —
\v 2 and the sons of Israel who came up from the exile kept the Passover on the fourteenth day of the first month — when the priests and the Levites were sanctified together —
\v 3 for the Levites were all sanctified at the same time and they slaughtered the Passover lamb for all the sons of the exile and for their fellow priests and for themselves.
\v 4 The sons of Israel who had returned from exile — all those who had separated themselves from the abominations of the peoples of the land — they ate and sought the Lord the God of Israel.
\v 5 They celebrated the Feast of Unleavened Bread seven days rejoicing before the Lord —
\v 6 for he had turned the counsel of the king of Assyria toward them to strengthen their hands in the work of the Lord the God of Israel.

\c 8

## CRITICAL TEXTUAL NOTES — 1 ESDRAS

**Canonical status and relationship to Ezra-Nehemiah:** 1 Esdras is the LXX's independent Greek version of the Ezra material. It overlaps with 2 Paralipomenon 35–36, the entirety of the MT Ezra, and a portion of Nehemiah 8. It contains one unique section not found in the MT: the *Story of the Three Youths* (chapters 3–4), in which Zerubbabel debates before Darius and wins the right to rebuild Jerusalem by arguing that *truth is the strongest thing*. This section is the theological centerpiece of the book.

**The name:** In the LXX canonical order this is 1 Esdras. The MT parallel text (Ezra-Nehemiah) is rendered as 2 Esdras in the LXX tradition. Some Latin traditions call this book 3 Esdras.

**The Story of the Three Youths (chapters 3–4):** This narrative has no Hebrew or Aramaic parallel in the canonical MT. It is the book's unique contribution to the canon and is one of the most rhetorically structured passages in the deuterocanon. The three speeches — wine / the king / women and truth — build to the climax of Zerubbabel's truth-declaration (4:34–37). The *alētheia nikā* = truth conquers all is the book's theological thesis statement.

**Relationship to 2 Paralipomenon 36 / Ezra 1:** The Cyrus decree appears in identical form at the close of 2 Paralipomenon, the opening of Ezra, and the opening of 1 Esdras — signaling the canonical importance of the decree as the hinge-point between exile and restoration.

## CAE NODES — 1 ESDRAS

### 1 Esdras 1:1–21 — Josiah's Passover / the final covenant-faithful act — CAE NODE

**1:1** — "Josiah kept the Passover to his Lord in Jerusalem and slaughtered the Passover lamb on the fourteenth day of the first month."

**1:19–20** — "No Passover like it had been kept in Israel since the days of the prophet Samuel — and none of the kings of Israel had kept such a Passover as was kept by Josiah."

1 Esdras opens with Josiah's Passover — the same event that closes the Josiah narrative in 2 Paralipomenon 35. The book therefore begins at the last great covenant-faithful act before the exile: the greatest Passover since Samuel. The structural implication is theological: the exile is not the baseline; the restoration returns to the Passover-standard of Josiah.

**The Josiah-Passover → Ezra-Passover arc:** 1 Esdras 1 (Josiah's Passover) → 1 Esdras 7:1–6 (the returned exiles' Passover). The book brackets its narrative with two Passovers: one before the exile / one after the return. The exile is the interruption between two Passovers.

**1 Corinthians 5:7** — "For Christ our Passover lamb (*pascha*) has been sacrificed." Paul's *pascha* declaration is the NT fulfillment of the Josiah-Passover / Ezra-Passover arc: the Passover that Josiah restored and the returned exiles celebrated is fulfilled in the once-for-all sacrifice of the Lamb. The two historical Passovers are types of the definitive one.

---

### 1 Esdras 2:1–8 — the *pneuma* of Cyrus stirred / the people whose *pneuma* rose — CAE CRITICAL NODE

**2:2** — "the Lord stirred up the breath-of-life (*pneuma*) of Cyrus king of the Persians and he made a proclamation throughout all his kingdom."

**2:8** — "Then the heads of families of the tribes of Judah and Benjamin rose up — with the priests and the Levites and all those whose breath-of-life (*pneuma*) the Lord had stirred to go up to build the house in Jerusalem for the Lord."

***kai exēgeiren Kyrios to pneuma Kyrou... kai hōn exēgeiren Kyrios to pneuma autōn anabēnai***

Two parallel *pneuma*-stirrings in the same passage: the Lord stirs the *pneuma* of Cyrus (the pagan emperor) / and the Lord stirs the *pneuma* of those among the exiles who choose to go up. The same divine action operates in the pagan king and in the faithful remnant — both are movements of the *pneuma* that the Lord initiates.

The *pneuma*-stirring is the mechanism of both the decree and the response: Cyrus makes the proclamation because his *pneuma* is stirred / the exiles go up because their *pneuma* is stirred. The restoration is entirely a work of the divine *pneuma* operating in human agents at every level.

**Ezra 1:1, 5** — the parallel text: "the Lord stirred up the spirit of Cyrus... and the spirit of the heads of fathers' houses." The parallel confirms the double *pneuma*-stirring as a deliberate theological statement preserved in both texts.

**Acts 2:4** — "And they were all filled with the Holy Breath-of-life (*pneumatos hagiou*) and began to speak in other tongues as the breath-of-life gave them utterance." Pentecost is the definitive corporate *pneuma*-stirring: as the Lord stirred the *pneuma* of the exiles to go up and rebuild, the Lord pours the *pneuma* on all flesh (Acts 2:17 / Joel 2:28) to go out and proclaim. The Pentecost *pneuma*-filling is the eschatological fulfillment of the restoration-*pneuma* pattern.

**Ezekiel 37:1–14** — the valley of dry bones: "I will put my breath-of-life (*pneuma*) in you and you shall live and I will place you in your own land." The restoration from exile as a *pneuma*-resurrection (Ezekiel 37) is the prophetic ground for both the Cyrus-*pneuma* stirring and the Pentecost *pneuma*-pouring. The pattern: exile = death / *pneuma* stirred = life / return = resurrection.

---

### 1 Esdras 3:1–4:42 — the Story of the Three Youths / *alētheia* conquers — CAE PRIMARY NODE

**4:35–37** — "The whole earth calls upon truth and the sky blesses her — all works tremble and quake before her. With her there is no partiality or preference — she does what is just rather than all things that are unjust or wicked. All approve her deeds — and there is nothing wrong in her judgment. Hers is the strength and the kingdom and the power and the majesty of all ages. Blessed be the God of truth."

**4:38** — "And all the people then cried out and said: Truth is great and most powerful (*megalē hē alētheia kai ischyra*) ."

***megalē estin hē alētheia kai hyperischyei***

= great is the truth and it prevails / overcomes above all.

The Three Youths narrative is structured as an ascending competition of claims:
- Wine makes all equal / destroys distinctions
- The king rules by power and compulsion
- Women rule through desire and attachment
- **Truth rules because it cannot be corrupted or partial**

The climax of Zerubbabel's argument (4:34–37) is one of the most rhetorically concentrated wisdom-speeches in the entire canon. The *alētheia* = truth is:
- Uncorrupted (*ouk estin en autē adikon* = there is nothing wrong in her)
- Impartial (*ou diakrinei prosōpa* = she does not distinguish faces)
- Sovereign (*autēs hē ischys kai hē basileia* = hers is the strength and the kingdom)
- Eternal (*eis tous aiōnas* = for the ages)

The doxological close — *Blessed be the God of truth* — is the theological anchor: truth is not an abstract principle but the character of the living God.

**John 14:6** — "Jesus said to him: I am the way and the truth (*alētheia*) and the life." Jesus's *alētheia* self-declaration is the NT fulfillment of the 1 Esdras truth-argument: the *alētheia* that Zerubbabel demonstrates is strongest is not an abstraction but a Person. The *megalē hē alētheia* = great is the truth of 1 Esdras 4:41 is fulfilled in the *egō eimi hē alētheia* = I am the truth of John 14:6.

**John 18:37–38** — "Everyone who is of the truth hears my voice. Pilate said to him: What is truth?" The Pilate-Jesus exchange about truth during the passion narrative is the ironic NT echo of the Three Youths contest: Pilate asks what truth is while standing before the one who is truth. The *megalē hē alētheia* declared before Darius is present but unrecognized before Pilate.

**John 8:32** — "And you will know the truth and the truth will set you free." Jesus's truth-freedom declaration is the NT fulfillment of Zerubbabel's truth-argument: Zerubbabel argues for truth before Darius and is granted the freedom to rebuild Jerusalem — truth sets the exiles free to return. The *alētheia kai hē alētheia eleutherōsei hymas* = the truth and the truth will free you is the NT universalization of the 1 Esdras truth-liberation pattern.

**2 Esdras / Ezra 1:2–4** — Zerubbabel's victory in the truth-contest results in the Darius decree that parallels the Cyrus decree: the truth-argument produces the royal permission for the restoration. The narrative function of the Three Youths is to explain why Darius issues the restoration decree — truth wins, and the winning of truth is the covenant-restoration.

---

### 1 Esdras 4:43–57 — Zerubbabel's vow-appeal / the *diathēkē* with Darius — CAE NODE

**4:43** — "And now this is what I ask and request of you my lord the king — this is the princely generosity that shall proceed from you: that you fulfill the vow to the King of heaven which you made with your own mouth."

**4:56** — "From you comes the victory and from you comes wisdom and yours is the glory — and I am your servant."

***sou estin hē nikē kai sou hē sophia kai sou hē doxa***

= yours is the victory and yours is the wisdom and yours is the glory.

After winning the truth-contest Zerubbabel does not ask for personal honor. He asks for the restoration of Jerusalem and the fulfillment of the king's vow to the *Basilei tou ouranou* = King of heaven. His prayer of thanksgiving (4:55–57) is addressed to the Lord — not to Darius — attributing all victory, wisdom, and glory to God.

The structure is Davidic: as David's prayer (1 Paralipomenon 29:14) declared *all things are yours and from what is yours we have given to you* — Zerubbabel's thanksgiving declares *yours is the victory and the wisdom and the glory*. Both prayers are offered at the moment of receiving royal provision for the house of God.

**Matthew 6:13** (doxological close) — "For yours is the kingdom and the power and the glory forever." The Lord's Prayer doxology runs: 1 Paralipomenon 29:11 (David before the Temple offering) → 1 Esdras 4:56 (Zerubbabel before the restoration decree) → Matthew 6:13 (Jesus teaching the disciples). All three are doxological acknowledgments of divine sovereignty at the moment of covenant-provision.

---

### 1 Esdras 5:47–57 — the Temple foundations relaid / weeping and joy mixed — CAE PRIMARY NODE

**5:55–57** — "And they sang hymns giving thanks to the Lord: for his goodness and his glory are forever in all Israel. And all the people sounded trumpets and shouted with a great shout praising the Lord for the raising up of the house of the Lord. And some of the Levitical priests and heads of clans — old men who had seen the former house — came to the building of this one with weeping and great crying — while many came with trumpets and great joy so that the people could not hear the trumpets because of the crying of the people."

***kai ēn ho laos en salpinxin kai en shouts megals* = the people were with trumpets and great shouts praising — and also the old men weeping so that the voices could not be distinguished*

The mixed response at the foundation-laying — joy for those who never saw Solomon's Temple / weeping for those who remember its glory — is one of the most theologically significant moments in the restoration literature. The two sounds cannot be separated: the shout of praise and the cry of grief are simultaneous and indistinguishable.

**Haggai 2:3** — "Who is left among you who saw this house in its former glory? How does it look to you now? Is it not as nothing in your eyes?" Haggai's prophecy addresses the very grief that 1 Esdras 5:56 depicts: the old men weeping over the diminished glory of the second Temple.

**Haggai 2:9** — "The latter glory of this house shall be greater than the former says the Lord of hosts. And in this place I will give peace (*shalom*)." God's response to the weeping: the second Temple will surpass the first in glory — fulfilled in the one who said *something greater than the Temple is here* (Matt 12:6).

**Ezra 3:12–13** — the parallel account in Ezra preserves the same mixed-sound tradition. 1 Esdras and Ezra agree at this point: the restoration is not pure triumph — it carries the grief of what was lost alongside the joy of what is being rebuilt.

**Revelation 21:4** — "He will wipe away every tear from their eyes and death shall be no more — neither shall there be mourning nor crying nor pain anymore." The promise of Revelation 21 is the eschatological resolution of the 1 Esdras 5:56–57 mixed sound: the day when the weeping and the shouting no longer overlap / when the grief of exile is permanently answered by the joy of the New Jerusalem.

---

### 1 Esdras 8:70–89 — Ezra reads the Law / the tears and the joy — CAE PRIMARY NODE

**8:77–78** — "Then Ezra took up the book of the law before the multitude — for he occupied the place of honor in the sight of all. And when he opened the law they all stood upright."

**8:83–86** — "This day is holy to the Lord — do not mourn and do not weep. For they were all weeping as they listened to the law. Then he said to them: Go your way — eat the fat and drink the sweet and send portions to those who have none — for the day is holy to the Lord — and do not be sorrowful for the Lord will lift you up."

**8:88–89** — "Then they all went their way — to eat and drink and make merry and to give portions to those who had none and to make great rejoicing — because they were inspired by the words with which they had been instructed."

The 1 Esdras account of Ezra's reading of the Law corresponds to Nehemiah 8. The *all stood upright* when the book was opened is the posture of covenant-attentiveness: the same posture as the assembly at the Sinai covenant. The weeping at the Law — and the command not to weep but to feast — is the paradoxical covenantal moment: the Law reveals sin (tears) / the holy day demands joy (feast). Both responses are correct; the day determines which is appropriate.

**The *anepsychōthēsan* = were inspired / refreshed** (8:89) — the verb is *anapsychō* = to be refreshed / to have the breath restored (connected to *psychē* = soul/life). The Law-reading restores the *psychē* of the people — the word functions as a *pneuma*-breath that revives. The *anepsychōthēsan* is the 1 Esdras equivalent of Ezekiel 37's *pneuma* restoring the dry bones to life.

**Nehemiah 8:10** — the parallel: "Do not be grieved for the joy of the Lord is your strength." The Nehemiah formulation of the same command is the most compressed version: the joy of the Lord as *ischys* = strength. The feast commanded after the Law-reading is not a departure from the Law's seriousness but its proper response — the covenant is not primarily threat but gift.

**Luke 4:16–21** — Jesus stands in the synagogue, opens the scroll of Isaiah, reads, closes it, sits — and all eyes are fixed on him. The structure is the same as 1 Esdras 8:77–78: the scroll opened / the assembly standing / the attentive gaze. Jesus's *Today this Scripture has been fulfilled in your hearing* is the ultimate Ezra-moment: not weeping but announcement of the acceptable year of the Lord.

**Romans 10:17** — "So faith comes from hearing and hearing through the word of Christ." Paul's hearing-faith formula is the theological articulation of the 1 Esdras 8:89 *anepsychōthēsan* pattern: the word heard produces restoration / inspiration / new life in the hearer.

## STRUCTURAL NOTE — 1 ESDRAS AS THE BOOK OF THE RETURNING *PNEUMA*

1 Esdras is structured around three *pneuma*-movements:

**1. The *pneuma* of Cyrus stirred** (2:2): The exile ends because God moves the *pneuma* of a pagan emperor. The restoration is not Israel's achievement but God's sovereign act through an unexpected instrument.

**2. The *pneuma* of the returning exiles** (2:8): Those who go up are those whose *pneuma* God stirs — a remnant-by-divine-initiative, not by human decision alone.

**3. The *alētheia* as the ground of restoration** (4:35–38): The truth-argument of Zerubbabel is not merely rhetorical skill — it is the declaration that God's character (truth / uncorruptible / impartial / eternal) is the foundation on which the Temple is rebuilt. The Temple is built on truth because it is the house of the God of truth.

The book ends with the Law-reading and the *anepsychōthēsan* = the people were restored/inspired. The pattern: *pneuma* stirred → return → Temple foundations → Law read → *psychē* restored. The entire arc is the reversal of the exile: the exile was the departure of the *doxa* = glory / the restoration is the return of the word that restores the *psychē*.

**John 1:1, 14** — "In the beginning was the Word (*Logos*)... and the Word became flesh and tabernacled among us." The Logos who is *alētheia* (John 14:6) and who *tabernacled* (*eskēnōsen* = set up the *skēnē*) among us is the ultimate fulfillment of the 1 Esdras restoration pattern: the truth-word that Zerubbabel declared is great has become flesh and dwelt in the new Temple. The *megalē hē alētheia* of 1 Esdras 4:41 is the Person of John 14:6.

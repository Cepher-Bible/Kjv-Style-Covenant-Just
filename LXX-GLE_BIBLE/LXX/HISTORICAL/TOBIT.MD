\v 1 The book of the words of Tobit the son of Tobiel son of Ananiel son of Aduel son of Gabael son of Raphael son of Raguel of the seed of Asiel of the tribe of Naphtali —
\v 2 who in the days of Shalmaneser king of the Assyrians was taken captive from Thisbe which is to the south of Kedesh-naphtali in Galilee above Asher.
\p
\v 3 I Tobit walked in the ways of truth and righteousness all the days of my life and I performed many acts of charity to my brothers and to my people who had gone with me into the land of Assyria to Nineveh.
\v 4 When I was in my own country in the land of Israel while I was still a young man the whole tribe of my ancestor Naphtali deserted the house of David and Jerusalem — the city that had been chosen from among all the tribes of Israel for all the tribes to offer sacrifice — where the temple of the dwelling of the Most High had been consecrated and established for all generations forever.
\v 5 All my kindred and the house of Naphtali my ancestor sacrificed to the calf that Jeroboam the king of Israel had erected in Dan on all the mountains of Galilee.
\v 6 But I alone went often to Jerusalem for the feasts as it is written for all Israel by an everlasting ordinance — taking with me the firstfruits and the tithes of the produce and the firstlings and giving them to the priests the sons of Aaron at the altar.
\v 7 The tenth of all produce I would give to the sons of Levi who were serving at Jerusalem — the second tenth I would sell and go and spend the proceeds each year at Jerusalem —
\v 8 and the third tenth I would give to those to whom it was appropriate as Deborah my father's mother had instructed me — for my father had died and left me an orphan.
\v 9 When I came of age I took as my wife Anna a member of our own family and by her I had a son whom I named Tobias.
\p
\v 10 After I was carried away captive to Assyria and came as a captive to Nineveh all my kindred and my people ate the food of the Gentiles —
\v 11 but I kept myself from eating it because I remembered God with all my heart (*kardia*).
\v 12 The Most High gave me favor and good standing with Shalmaneser and I became his buyer of provisions.
\v 13 I used to go into Media and once left ten talents of silver with Gabael the brother of Gabrias at Rages in Media.
\p
\v 14 When Shalmaneser died and Sennacherib his son became king in his place the roads into Media became unsafe and I could no longer go there.
\v 16 In the days of Shalmaneser I performed many acts of charity to my kindred.
\v 17 I would give my bread to the hungry and clothing to the naked. If I saw any of my people dead and thrown out behind the wall of Nineveh I would bury him.
\v 18 I also buried any whom Sennacherib killed when he came fleeing from Judea during the days of the judgment that the King of heaven executed upon him on account of his blasphemies — for in his anger he killed many Israelites. But I would secretly bury their bodies and Tobias helped me.
\v 19 When Sennacherib looked for them he could not find them. Then one of the Ninevites went and informed the king about me — that I was burying them — so I hid myself. When I realized that he knew about me and was searching for me to put me to death I was afraid and ran away.
\v 20 Then all my property was confiscated and nothing was left to me except my wife Anna and my son Tobias.
\p
\v 21 But not forty days passed before two of Sennacherib's sons killed him and they fled into the mountains of Ararat. Then his son Esarhaddon reigned in his place — and he appointed Ahiqar the son of my brother Anael over all the accounts of his kingdom.
\v 22 Ahiqar then interceded for me and I returned to Nineveh. For Ahiqar had been cupbearer keeper of the signet ring steward and accountant under Sennacherib king of Assyria — and Esarhaddon reappointed him. He was my nephew and so a close relative.

\c 2
\p
\v 1 When I arrived home at my house my wife Anna and my son Tobias were restored to me. At the feast of Pentecost which is the sacred festival of the seven weeks I had a good dinner prepared and reclined to eat.
\v 2 When I saw the abundance of food I said to my son Tobias: Go and bring whatever poor person you may find from our people among the exiles in Nineveh who is mindful of God with his whole heart (*kardia*) — and I will wait for you.
\v 3 Tobias went to look for some poor person of our people. When he came back he said: Father — one of our people has been strangled and thrown into the marketplace.
\v 4 Before I tasted anything I sprang up and removed the body to a place of shelter until sunset.
\v 5 Then I returned and washed and ate my food in sorrow —
\v 6 remembering the prophecy of Amos as he said:
\q1 Your feasts shall be turned into mourning
\q2 and all your songs into lamentation.
\v 7 And I wept. When the sun had set I went and dug a grave and buried him.
\v 8 And my neighbors laughed at me and said: He is still not afraid. He was already hunted for this before and escaped and here he is again burying the dead.
\p
\v 9 That same night after I had buried him I returned and slept by the wall of the courtyard being uncovered because of the heat — and I did not know that there were sparrows in the wall above me — and the sparrows' droppings fell hot into my eyes and produced white films. I went to physicians but they could not help me. Ahiqar supported me for two years before he went to Elymais.
\v 10 Then my wife Anna earned money by women's work.
\v 11 She used to send what she made to the owners and they on their part would pay wages to her. One day — the seventh of Dystrus — when she cut off a piece she had woven and sent it to the owners they paid her whole wages in addition to a young goat for a meal.
\v 12 When the goat came into my house it began to bleat and I said to her: Where did this young goat come from? Is it stolen? Return it to its owners — for it is not right to eat anything stolen.
\v 13 But she said: It was given to me as a gift in addition to my wages. But I did not believe her and told her to return it to its owners. I became flushed with anger against her on account of this.
\v 14 Then she replied: And where are your acts of charity? Where are your righteous deeds? These things are known about you.

\c 3
\p
\v 1 Then I was deeply grieved and I wept and with grief I prayed:
\v 2 O Lord — you are righteous and all your deeds are just — all your ways are mercy (*eleos*) and truth — you judge the world.
\v 3 And now Lord remember me — call my iniquity to mind no more and do not record my sins or those of my ancestors. For we sinned against you —
\v 4 and disobeyed your commandments. So you gave us over to plunder exile and death to become a byword and a reproach and an object of scorn in all the nations among which you have scattered us.
\v 5 And now your many judgments are true in exacting penalty from me for my sins and those of my ancestors — because we did not keep your commandments and did not walk in truth before you.
\v 6 So now deal with me as seems best to you — command my breath-of-life (*pneuma*) to be taken from me so that I may depart and become dust — for it is better for me to die than to live because I have had to listen to undeserved reproaches and great is the grief within me.
\p Lord command that I be released from this affliction — let me go to the eternal abode — do not turn your face away from me.
\p
\v 7 On the same day at Ecbatana in Media it also happened that Sarah the daughter of Raguel was reproached by one of her father's maids —
\v 8 because she had been given to seven husbands and the evil demon Asmodeus had killed each of them before they had been with her as is customary for wives. The maid said to her: You are the one who kills your husbands — you have already had seven and have not borne the name of a single one of them.
\v 9 Why do you beat us? Because your husbands are dead? Go with them — may we never see a son or daughter of yours.
\p
\v 10 On that day Sarah was deeply grieved in breath-of-life (*psychē*) and she wept. When she had gone up to her father's upper room she intended to hang herself. But she thought it over and said:
\v 11 Never shall they reproach my father by saying to him: You had one beloved daughter and she hanged herself because of her distress. And I shall bring my father in his old age down to Hades in grief. It is better for me not to hang myself but to pray the Lord that I may die and no longer hear these reproaches in my life.
\v 12 At that same time with hands spread out toward the window she prayed:
\p Blessed are you — O merciful God — and blessed is your name forever — and let all your works bless you forever.
\v 13 And now to you I turn my face and lift up my eyes.
\v 14 Command that I be released from the earth and not have to hear such reproaches anymore.
\v 15 You know Lord that I am pure of any defilement with a man — and that I have not disgraced my name or my father's name in the land of my captivity. I am my father's only child — he has no other child to be his heir — and he has no close kinsman or other relative for whom I should keep myself as wife. Already seven husbands of mine have died. Why should I live? But if it does not please you to take my life then at least have regard for me and take pity on me and let me not hear these reproaches anymore.
\p
\v 16 The prayer of both was heard in the presence of the glory of God.
\v 17 And Raphael was sent to heal the two of them: to remove the white films from Tobit's eyes so that he might see God's light with his eyes — and to give Sarah the daughter of Raguel in marriage to Tobias the son of Tobit and to bind Asmodeus the evil demon because Tobias was entitled to have her.

\c 4
\p
\v 1 At that time Tobit remembered the money that he had left with Gabael at Rages in Media and he said to himself:
\v 2 I have asked for death — why do I not call my son Tobias so that I may explain to him about the money before I die?
\p
\v 3 So he called Tobias his son and when he came he said to him: When I die give me a proper burial. Honor your mother and do not abandon her all the days of her life — do whatever pleases her and do not grieve her in anything.
\v 4 Remember her — for she faced many dangers for you while you were in her womb. And when she dies bury her beside me in the same grave.
\v 5 Revere God all your days my son and refuse to sin or to transgress his commandments. Live uprightly all the days of your life and do not walk in the ways of wrongdoing —
\v 6 for those who act in accordance with truth will prosper in all their activities.
\v 7 Give alms from your possessions — do not let your eye be grudging when you give alms. Do not turn your face away from anyone who is poor and the face of God will not be turned away from you.
\v 8 If you have many possessions make your gift from them in proportion — if few do not be afraid to give according to the little you have.
\v 9 So you will be laying up a good treasure for yourself against the day of necessity.
\v 10 For almsgiving (*eleēmosynē*) delivers from death and keeps you from going into the darkness.
\v 11 Indeed almsgiving is a good offering in the sight of the Most High for all who practice it.
\v 12 Beware my son of every kind of fornication. First of all take a wife from among the descendants of your ancestors — do not marry a foreign woman who is not of your father's tribe — for we are the descendants of the prophets.
\p
\v 14 Pay fair wages to everyone who works for you and do not let the wages of those who work for you be held back overnight.
\v 15 Do not do to anyone what you yourself would hate.
\v 16 Give of your bread to those who are hungry and of your clothing to the naked. Give all your surplus as alms — and do not let your eye be grudging when you give alms.
\v 17 Place your bread on the grave of the righteous but give none to sinners.
\v 19 Bless the Lord God on every occasion — ask him that your ways may be made straight and that all your paths and plans may prosper. For no nation has wisdom but the Lord himself gives all good things — he humbles whomever he wishes and exalts whomever he wishes — so now my son remember my commandments and let them not be blotted out of your heart (*kardia*).

\c 5
\p
\v 1 Then Tobias answered his father Tobit: I will do everything that you have commanded me father —
\v 2 but how can I get the money from him since he does not know me and I do not know him? What evidence can I give him so that he will recognize and trust me and give me the money? Also I do not know the roads to Media or how to get there.
\v 3 Then Tobit answered his son Tobias: He gave me his note of hand and I gave him mine — I divided his in two and we each kept half — I put mine with the money. And it has been twenty years since I left this money with him. So now my son find yourself a trustworthy man to go with you and we will pay him wages until you return — and then get the money from Gabael.
\p
\v 4 So Tobias went out to look for a man and found Raphael the angel standing before him — though he did not know that he was an angel of God.
\v 5 Tobias said to him: Where do you come from young man? And he answered: From your kindred the Israelites — I came here to find work. And Tobias said to him: Do you know the way to Media?
\v 6 Yes — he answered — I have been there many times. I know all the roads and I have often stayed with our brother Gabael who lives at Rages in Media. It is a good two days' journey from Ecbatana to Rages — for it lies in a mountain region while Ecbatana is in the middle of the plain.
\v 7 Then Tobias said to him: Wait for me young man until I go in and tell my father — for I need you to make the journey with me and I will pay your wages.
\v 8 He replied: Very well — I will wait — but do not be long.
\p
\v 10 When Tobias went in and reported to his father Tobit the things he had said — Tobit said: Call him to come in so that I may find out about his family and tribe and whether he is trustworthy enough to go with you my son.
\v 11 So Tobias went out and called him and said: Young man — my father is calling for you. He went in and Tobit greeted him first.
\v 12 Raphael said: Many greetings to you. And Tobit replied: What joy is still left for me? Here I am — a blind man who cannot see the light of heaven but lies in darkness like the dead who no longer see the light. Although I am still alive I am among the dead. I hear people but I cannot see them. And the light of heaven has been taken from my eyes.
\p Raphael said to him: Take courage — the time is near for God to heal you. Take courage.
\p And Tobit said to him: My son Tobias wishes to go to Media — will you be able to go with him and guide him? I will pay your wages brother. He answered: I am able to go with him and I know all the roads. I have often gone to Media and have crossed all its plains and mountains and know all the roads.
\p
\v 17 But Anna his mother began to weep and said to Tobit: Why is it that you have sent our child away? Is he not the staff of our hands in going in and going out before us?
\v 18 Do not add money to money — but let it be a ransom for our child.
\v 19 For the life (*psychē*) that is given us by the Lord is enough for us.
\v 20 And Tobit said to her: Do not worry — our child will go safely and will return to us safely and your eyes will see him on the day when he returns safely to you.
\v 21 Do not worry — do not fear for them my sister — a good angel will accompany him — his journey will be successful and he will come back safe.

\c 6
\p
\v 1 Now as they were going along and had come to the Tigris river — and Tobias went down to wash his feet and a large fish leaped up from the water and tried to swallow his foot and he cried out.
\v 2 But the angel said to him: Catch the fish. So the young man seized the fish and drew it up onto the land.
\v 3 Then the angel said to him: Cut open the fish and take out its gall heart and liver. Keep them with you but throw away the intestines — for its gall heart and liver are useful as medicine.
\v 4 So after cutting open the fish the young man gathered together the gall heart and liver. Then he roasted and ate part of the fish and kept some to be salted.
\p
\v 6 Then the young man asked the angel: Brother Azariah — what medicinal value is there in the fish's heart and liver and in the gall?
\v 7 He replied: As for the fish's heart and liver — if you make a smoke from them before a man or a woman who is afflicted by a demon or an evil breath-of-life (*pneuma*) the trouble will flee and never return.
\v 8 As for the gall — anoint a person's eyes with it who has white films and blow on the films and the eyes will be healed.
\p
\v 10 When they came near to Ecbatana Raphael said to the young man: Brother Tobias. And he answered: Yes?
\v 11 Raphael said to him: Tonight we must stay with Raguel who is your relative. He has a daughter named Sarah.
\v 12 He has no male heir and no daughter except Sarah and you are her next of kin — the closest relative of her father's line. It is right that you should inherit her father's goods and it is proper that you should marry her. Her father is a sensible man and his daughter is admirable.
\v 13 Tobias said in response: Brother Azariah — I have heard that she has already been given in marriage seven times and that the bridegrooms have died in the bridal chamber. On the night they went into her the demon killed them. So now I am afraid that it may kill me too — for the demon loves her and perhaps I alone am the one destined for her. But if I die I will bring the lives of my father and mother down to their grave with grief over me — and they have no other son to bury them.
\v 14 Raphael said to him: Do you not remember your father's commands? He commanded you to take a wife from your father's family. So now listen to me brother — do not worry about the demon and take her. I know that tonight she will be given to you as your wife.
\v 15 And when you go into the bridal chamber take some of the fish's liver and heart and lay a little of it on the burning incense. An odor will be given off —
\v 16 the demon will smell it and flee and will never be seen near her again. Now when you are about to go to bed with her both of you should first stand up and pray — implore the Lord of heaven that mercy and deliverance be granted to you. Do not be afraid — for she was set apart for you before the world was made. You will save her and she will go with you. And I suppose that you will have children by her who will be like brothers to you. Do not worry.
\p
\v 17 When Tobias heard the words of Raphael and learned that she was his relative and of his father's lineage he loved her very much and his heart (*kardia*) was drawn to her.

\c 7
\p
\v 1 When they reached Ecbatana Tobias said: Brother Azariah take me straight to our brother Raguel's house. So he took him to Raguel's house where they found him sitting beside the courtyard door.
\v 2 They greeted him first and he replied: Many greetings brothers — welcome and good health to you. And he brought them into his house.
\p
\v 6 Then Raguel leaped up and kissed him and wept.
\v 7 He blessed him and said: You are the son of an honest and good man. But when he heard that Tobit had lost his sight he was stricken with grief and wept —
\v 8 and his wife Edna wept for him and their daughter Sarah also wept.
\p
\v 9 Then Raguel slaughtered a ram from the flock and they received them very warmly.
\p When they had bathed and washed and had reclined to dine Tobias said to Raphael: Brother Azariah — ask Raguel to give me my relative Sarah.
\v 10 But Raguel overheard it and said to the young man: Eat and drink and be merry tonight — for there is no one except you who should have my daughter Sarah — and indeed I myself have no right to give her to anyone other than you because you are my nearest relative. But let me explain the true situation to you my son.
\v 11 I have given her in marriage to seven husbands of our relatives and they all died on the night when they went in to her. So now my son eat and drink and the Lord will act on your behalf.
\v 12 Tobias said: I will neither eat nor drink here until you settle the matter and make a decision for me. And Raguel said: Very well — she is given to you in accordance with the decree of the book of Moses — and it has been decided from heaven that she be given to you. Take your relative — from now on you are her brother and she is your sister. She is given to you from today forever. The Lord of heaven guide you both tonight my son and grant you mercy and peace.
\p
\v 14 Then Raguel called his daughter Sarah and when she came to him he took her hand and gave her to Tobias and said: Take her to be your wife in accordance with the Law and the decree written in the book of Moses. Take her and bring her safely to your father. And the God of heaven prosper your journey with peace.
\v 15 Then he called her mother and told her to bring writing material — and he wrote out a contract and they set their seals to it.
\v 16 Then they began to eat and drink.
\p
\v 17 Raguel called his wife Edna and said to her: Sister prepare the other room and bring her there.
\v 18 So she went and made the bed in the room as he told her and brought Sarah there. She wept for her.
\p Then wiping away the tears she said to her:
\v 19 Take courage daughter — the Lord of heaven grant you joy in place of your sorrow. Take courage my daughter. And she went out.

\c 8
\p
\v 1 When they had finished eating and drinking they wanted to retire. So they took the young man and brought him into the bedroom.
\v 2 Then Tobias remembered the words of Raphael and took the fish's liver and heart out of the bag where he had them and put them on the embers of the incense.
\v 3 The smell of the fish repelled the demon and it fled to the remotest parts of Egypt — and Raphael followed it and bound it there hand and foot.
\p
\v 4 When the parents had gone out and shut the door of the room Tobias got up from the bed and said to Sarah: Sister rise and let us pray and implore our Lord that he grant us mercy and safety.
\v 5 So she got up and they began to pray and implore that they might be kept safe. Tobias began by saying:
\p Blessed are you O God of our ancestors — and blessed is your name in all generations forever.
\v 6 Let the heavens and the whole creation bless you forever.
\p You made Adam and gave him his wife Eve as a helper and support — and from them the human race has come.
\p You said: It is not good that the man should be alone — let us make a helper for him like himself.
\v 7 And now I am not taking this woman because of lust but with sincerity.
\p Grant that she and I may find mercy and grow old together.
\v 8 And she said with him: Amen.
\v 9 And they slept for the night.
\p
\v 10 But Raguel got up and went and dug a grave —
\v 11 thinking: Perhaps he too will die.
\v 12 Then Raguel went into his house and said to his wife Edna:
\v 13 Send one of the maids to see whether he is alive. If he is not I will bury him and no one will know.
\v 14 So the maid opened the door and went in and found them both asleep —
\v 15 and she came out and told them that he was alive and that nothing was wrong.
\p
\v 16 Then Raguel blessed God saying:
\p Blessed are you O God with every pure blessing — let them bless you forever.
\v 17 Blessed are you because you have had compassion on two only children. Show them mercy O Lord — and bring their lives to completion with health and happiness and mercy.
\v 18 And he told his servants to fill in the grave before dawn.

\c 9
\p
\v 1 Then Tobias called Raphael and said to him:
\v 2 Brother Azariah — take four servants and two camels with you and travel to Rages and go to Gabael's house and give him the note of hand and get the money and then bring him with you to the wedding celebration —
\v 3 for Raguel has sworn that I shall not leave. But my father is counting the days and if I delay much longer he will be very upset.
\v 4 So Raphael made the journey to Rages in Media and stayed with Gabael. He gave him his note of hand and informed him that Tobit's son Tobias had married and was inviting him to the wedding celebration.
\v 5 So Gabael got up and counted out to him the money bags with their seals and they loaded them on the camels.
\v 6 In the morning they both got up early and came to the wedding celebration — and Tobias blessed his wife.

\c 10
\p
\v 1 Meanwhile Tobit his father was counting each day — and when the days for the journey had passed and they had not arrived —
\v 2 Tobit said: Is it possible that he has been detained? Or that Gabael has died and there is no one to give him the money?
\v 3 And he was very sad.
\v 4 His wife Anna said: The boy has perished — he is no longer among the living. And she began to weep and mourn for her son saying:
\v 5 Woe to me my child — the light of my eyes — that I let you make this journey.
\p
\v 6 But Tobit said to her: Be quiet — do not worry — he is all right.
\v 7 But she said to him: Be quiet and stop trying to deceive me. My child has perished. And she went out every day to the road by which they had left. She ate nothing in the daytime and throughout the nights she never stopped mourning for her son Tobias until the fourteen days of the wedding celebration were ended.
\p
\v 11 So Tobias blessed Raguel and his wife Edna saying: I have been commanded by my father to honor you all the days of my life.
\v 12 And he took his wife Sarah and the half of all that Raguel possessed — male and female slaves cattle and sheep asses and camels clothing silver vessels and household furnishings — and set out with them.

\c 11
\p
\v 1 When they were getting close and coming near to Kaserin — which is opposite Nineveh —
\v 2 Raphael said: I know brother how your father's eyes will be opened. You should anoint his eyes with the fish gall — the medicine will make the white films shrink and peel off from his eyes — and your father will regain his sight and will see the light.
\p
\v 5 His mother Anna sat looking intently down the road by which her son would come.
\v 6 She caught sight of him coming and said to his father: Look — your son is coming and the man who went with him.
\v 7 Raphael said to Tobias before he reached his father: I know that his eyes will be opened.
\v 8 Go anoint his eyes with the gall. This medicine is sharp and will sting and will peel off the white films from his eyes and your father will see the light and will rejoice.
\v 9 Then Anna ran up to her son and threw her arms around him and said to him: I have seen you my child — now I can die. And they both wept.
\p
\v 10 Tobit got up and came stumbling out through the courtyard door. Tobias went up to him —
\v 11 with the fish gall in his hand and blew into his eyes. Supporting him he said: Take courage father.
\v 12 With this he applied the medicine and with both hands peeled off the white films from the corners of his eyes.
\v 13 Then Tobit saw his son and threw his arms around him —
\v 14 and he wept and said: I can see you my son — the light of my eyes. Blessed be God — blessed be his great name — and blessed be all his holy angels.
\p May his great name be upon us and blessed be all the angels throughout all the ages.
\v 15 For he afflicted me and now he has had compassion on me and I see my son Tobias. Go in my son in safety — and blessed be you — and welcome my daughter-in-law Sarah.
\p Blessed be God who brought you to me and blessed be your father Raguel and blessed be Sarah my daughter-in-law.
\p Blessed be God who brought you to me and blessed be your father and blessed be my son Tobias and blessed be Sarah my daughter-in-law. All the days of our lives God has blessed us and he will continue to bless us.
\p
\v 17 All his kindred who were in Nineveh came to rejoice with him and Ahiqar and his nephew Nadab came to him with rejoicing.

\c 12
\p
\v 1 When the wedding celebration was ended Tobit called his son Tobias and said to him: My son see to it that you give what is owed to the man who went with you and give him something extra.
\v 2 Tobias said: Father how much shall I pay him? It would do no harm to give him half of the possessions he brought back with me —
\v 3 for he has led me back safely — he healed my wife — he brought the money back — and he healed you. How much extra should I give him?
\v 4 Tobit said: He deserves half of everything we brought back.
\p
\v 5 So Tobias called Raphael and said: Take as your wages half of all that you brought back and farewell.
\p
\v 6 Then Raphael called the two of them privately and said to them: Bless God and give thanks to him — exalt him and give thanks to him in the presence of all the living for what he has done for you. It is good to bless God and to exalt his name — honoring the deeds of God with fitting honor. Do not be slow to give him thanks.
\v 7 A king's secret it is good to keep — but the works of God should be glorified and acknowledged. Do good and no evil will come upon you.
\p
\v 8 Prayer with fasting is good — and almsgiving (*eleēmosynē*) with righteousness is better than wealth with unrighteousness. It is better to give alms than to lay up gold.
\v 9 For almsgiving saves from death and purges away every sin. Those who give alms will enjoy a full life —
\v 10 but those who commit sin and do wrong are their own worst enemies.
\p
\v 11 I will now declare the whole truth to you and will conceal nothing from you. Already I have declared it to you when I said: It is good to keep the king's secret but to reveal the works of God in a worthy manner.
\v 12 And so when you — Tobit — and Sarah prayed it was I who brought and read the record of your prayer before the glory of the Lord — and likewise when you used to bury the dead.
\v 13 And when you did not hesitate to get up and leave your dinner to go and bury the dead neighbor of yours —
\v 14 I was sent to you to test you. And at the same time God sent me to heal you and Sarah your daughter-in-law.
\v 15 I am Raphael one of the seven angels who stand ready and enter before the glory of the Lord.
\p
\v 16 Then the two of them were shaken and fell face down — for they were afraid.
\v 17 But he said to them: Do not be afraid — peace be with you. Bless God forever.
\v 18 As for me — when I was with you I was not acting on my own will but by the will of God. Bless him each day — sing his praises.
\v 19 You thought you saw me eating and drinking with you — but I neither eat nor drink anything. What you saw was a vision.

## CRITICAL TEXTUAL NOTES — TOBIT

**The two recensions:** The LXX Tobit exists in two significantly different Greek recensions. GI (Vaticanus / Alexandrinus) is shorter; GII/Sinaiticus is longer and is the primary text for the GLE. The Sinaiticus recension is generally regarded as more original and is preserved in Rahlfs-Hanhart. The Qumran Aramaic and Hebrew fragments (4Q196–200) support the longer Sinaiticus text tradition.

**The Semitic substratum:** The Qumran fragments confirm that Tobit was composed in a Semitic language (Aramaic or Hebrew). The book has all the characteristics of a diaspora wisdom narrative — a Jewish family maintaining covenant faithfulness in Nineveh / the capital of the empire that destroyed the northern kingdom.

**Raphael's identity:** The self-revelation in 12:15 — "I am Raphael one of the seven angels who stand ready and enter before the glory of the Lord" — is the most explicit angelology in the deuterocanon prior to the apocalyptic literature. The seven angels before the divine throne are the *hepta angeloi* of Revelation 8:2.

**The demon Asmodeus:** The name is from the Avestan *Aēšma daēva* = demon of wrath. The binding of Asmodeus (8:3) is the only explicit demon-binding narrative in the LXX historical/narrative tradition. It anticipates the NT exorcism theology and the eschatological binding of Satan (Revelation 20).

## CAE NODES — TOBIT

### Tobit 1:3 / 1:11 — faithfulness in exile / *kardia* remembering God — CAE PRIMARY NODE

**1:3** — "I Tobit walked in the ways of truth and righteousness all the days of my life and I performed many acts of charity to my brothers and to my people who had gone with me into the land of Assyria to Nineveh."

**1:11** — "but I kept myself from eating it because I remembered God with all my heart (*kardia*)."

***hoti emnēsthēn tou theou en holē tē kardia mou*** = for I remembered God with my whole heart.

The opening self-description of Tobit is the covenant-faithfulness declaration of a man in diaspora. The three cardinal acts of Tobit's faithfulness: tithing correctly (1:6–8) / giving alms to the poor (1:16–17) / burying the dead (1:17–19). All three are covenant-obligations — the tithe / the *eleēmosynē* / the burial-of-the-dead (a particular expression of honor for the image of God in the human person).

The *kardia* remembering God with wholeness is the Shema-faithfulness of Deuteronomy 6:5 applied in the most adverse condition: captivity in Nineveh, the city of Israel's destroyer. The covenant is kept not in the Temple but in the heart — in the body-practices of tithe, alms, and burial that enact covenant-loyalty when cultic worship is unavailable.

**Daniel 1:8** — "But Daniel resolved that he would not defile himself with the king's food." Daniel in Babylon maintains dietary covenant-faithfulness exactly as Tobit maintains his (1:10–11). Both are diaspora narratives of the *kardia* that remembers God when the covenant-community's institutional structures are unavailable.

**Hebrews 13:2** — "Do not neglect to show hospitality to strangers (*philoxenian*) for thereby some have entertained angels unawares." The Hebrews hospitality-principle is directly activated by the Tobit narrative: Tobit/Tobias receives Raphael as a traveling companion without knowing he is an angel. The *philoxenia* = love-of-strangers that the book models is the social expression of the *kardia* that remembers God.

---

### Tobit 3:1–6 / 3:10–15 — the double prayer / both heard simultaneously — CAE PRIMARY NODE

**3:2** — "O Lord — you are righteous and all your deeds are just — all your ways are mercy (*eleos*) and truth — you judge the world."

**3:6** — "So now deal with me as seems best to you — command my breath-of-life (*pneuma*) to be taken from me so that I may depart and become dust — for it is better for me to die than to live."

**3:16–17** — "The prayer of both was heard in the presence of the glory of God. And Raphael was sent to heal the two of them."

***kai eisēkousthē hē proseuche amphoterōn enōpion tēs doxēs tou megalou Raphael***

= and the prayer of both was heard before the glory — and Raphael the great was sent.

The structural theology of 3:16–17 is one of the most remarkable moments in the deuterocanon: two prayers are offered simultaneously in different cities (Nineveh / Ecbatana) — one by a blind man asking for death, one by a tormented woman asking for death — and both are heard at the same moment before the divine glory, and one angelic mission addresses both simultaneously.

The theology declares:
1. **Both prayers are heard** — suffering from different sources (blindness / demonic oppression) is equally attended to by God.
2. **The answer is not what was asked** — both asked for death; the answer is healing and marriage.
3. **The divine response is coordinated** — the answer to Tobit's blindness and the answer to Sarah's torment are one mission. God's economy is not two separate responses but one integrated act of providence.

The *enōpion tēs doxēs* = before the glory is the throne-room language: the prayers are received at the divine throne (cf. Revelation 8:3–4 — the angel offers incense and the prayers of the saints rise before God). Raphael explicitly confirms in 12:12: "when you prayed... it was I who brought and read the record of your prayer before the glory of the Lord."

**Matthew 6:6** — "But when you pray go into your room and shut the door and pray to your Father who is in secret. And your Father who sees in secret will reward you." Jesus's prayer-in-secret teaching is the NT equivalent of the Tobit theology: the prayers of Tobit and Sarah are made in private (Tobit in his room / Sarah in her upper room with hands spread at the window) and are heard in the divine presence. The *en tō kryptō* = in secret of Matthew 6:6 is precisely where both Tobit's and Sarah's prayers are offered.

**Revelation 8:3–4** — "And another angel came and stood at the altar with a golden censer — and he was given much incense to offer with the prayers of all the saints on the golden altar before the throne. And the smoke of the incense with the prayers of the saints rose before God from the hand of the angel." The Revelation incense-altar scene is the eschatological form of the Tobit 3:16–17 / 12:12 pattern: an angel (Raphael / the Revelation angel) brings the prayers of the saints before the divine throne. The theology of angelic intercession of Tobit is the OT ground for the Revelation liturgical vision.

### Tobit 4:7–11 — the theology of *eleēmosynē* / almsgiving as covenant act — CAE PRIMARY NODE

**4:7** — "Give alms from your possessions — do not let your eye be grudging when you give alms. Do not turn your face away from anyone who is poor and the face of God will not be turned away from you."

**4:10** — "For almsgiving (*eleēmosynē*) delivers from death and keeps you from going into the darkness."

**4:15** — "Do not do to anyone what you yourself would hate."

***hoti eleēmosynē ek thanatou ryetai kai ouk eā eiselthein eis to skotos***

= for almsgiving delivers from death and does not permit entry into darkness.

The *eleēmosynē* = almsgiving theology of Tobit 4 is the most concentrated OT-deuterocanonical statement of the covenant-function of giving to the poor. The *eleēmosynē* is:
- **Proportionate** (4:8 — give according to your means)
- **Non-grudging** (4:7 — do not let your eye be evil)
- **Life-saving** (4:10 — delivers from death)
- **Face-preserving** (4:7 — God's face toward you = your face toward the poor)

The *panim* = face theology: the face you show to the poor is the face God shows to you. The covenant-symmetry is exact. This is the wisdom-register application of Proverbs 19:17 (*whoever is generous to the poor lends to the Lord*).

**The Golden Rule precursor** (4:15): *do not do to anyone what you yourself would hate* is the negative formulation of the Golden Rule — predating the positive NT formulations. The Tobit version is the earliest explicit formulation of this principle in the Jewish tradition.

**Matthew 7:12** — "So whatever you wish that others would do to you do also to them — for this is the Law and the Prophets." Jesus's positive Golden Rule is the NT fulfillment of Tobit 4:15: the negative formulation (do not do what you hate) is transformed into the positive (do what you wish done to you). Jesus explicitly grounds it in *the Law and the Prophets* — the Tobit tradition is the deuterocanonical expression of that grounding.

**Matthew 6:19–21** — "Do not lay up for yourselves treasures on earth... but lay up for yourselves treasures in heaven." Jesus's treasure-in-heaven teaching is the NT equivalent of Tobit 4:9: "So you will be laying up a good treasure for yourself against the day of necessity." Both texts use *thēsauros* = treasure language for almsgiving as eschatological investment.

**Acts 10:4** — "And he (Cornelius) stared at him in terror and said: What is it Lord? And he said to him: Your prayers and your alms have ascended as a memorial before God." The angel's declaration to Cornelius — that his prayers and alms have ascended before God — is the NT enactment of the Tobit *eleēmosynē* theology: Raphael tells Tobit (12:12) that his alms were presented before the divine glory; the angel tells Cornelius the same. The *eleēmosynē*-before-God theology of Tobit 4 is the OT ground for the Cornelius episode.

### Tobit 6:16–17 — Raphael's instruction / *do not be afraid for she was set apart for you before the world was made* — CAE PRIMARY NODE

**6:16** — "Now when you are about to go to bed with her both of you should first stand up and pray — implore the Lord of heaven that mercy and deliverance be granted to you. Do not be afraid — for she was set apart for you before the world was made."

***mē phobou — hoti soi autē ēn hētoimasmenē apo tou aiōnos***

= do not be afraid — for she was prepared for you from eternity / from before the age.

Raphael's declaration that Sarah was *set apart for Tobias before the world was made* is the most explicit statement of divine pre-arrangement of marriage / covenant-partnership in the OT-deuterocanonical tradition. The *apo tou aiōnos* = from eternity / from before the age is the Tobit equivalent of the *pro katabolēs kosmou* = before the foundation of the world language in the NT.

The theology: Tobias's anxiety about the demon / the seven dead husbands / his own death is met not with reassurance about outcomes but with a declaration about origins — *she was prepared for you from eternity*. The fear is answered not with information about the future but with information about the eternal purpose that precedes the present. The foundation precedes the fear.

**Ephesians 1:4** — "Even as he chose us in him before the foundation of the world (*pro katabolēs kosmou*) that we should be holy and blameless before him." Paul's pre-foundation election language is the NT expansion of the Tobit *apo tou aiōnos* theology: what Raphael declares about Tobias and Sarah (chosen for each other from eternity) Paul declares about the church (chosen in Christ before the foundation). The marriage-covenant prepared from eternity is the type of the covenant-union of Christ and the church.

**John 17:24** — "Father I desire that they also whom you have given me may be with me where I am to see my glory that you have given me because you loved me before the foundation of the world (*pro katabolēs kosmou*)." Jesus's high-priestly prayer grounds the disciples' union with him in the love that existed before the world — the same eternal-preparation language as Tobit 6:16.

### Tobit 8:4–8 — the bridal chamber prayer / *not because of lust but with sincerity* — CAE PRIMARY NODE

**8:5–7** — "Tobias began by saying: Blessed are you O God of our ancestors — and blessed is your name in all generations forever. Let the heavens and the whole creation bless you forever. You made Adam and gave him his wife Eve as a helper and support — and from them the human race has come. You said: It is not good that the man should be alone — let us make a helper for him like himself. And now I am not taking this woman because of lust but with sincerity. Grant that she and I may find mercy and grow old together."

***kai nyn Kyrie ouk apo porneias egō lambánō tēn adelphēn tauten all' ep' alētheias***

= and now Lord I am not taking this sister because of fornication but in truth.

The bridal-chamber prayer of Tobias is one of the most theologically significant texts in the deuterocanon. It is the only prayer in the OT/deuterocanon offered in the marriage bed — and its structure is covenant-theology:

1. **The blessing** (8:5–6): The prayer begins with a doxology — the marriage enters the covenant-relationship through blessing God, not through personal gratification.

2. **The creation-ground** (8:6): The prayer appeals directly to Genesis 2:18 (*it is not good that man should be alone*) and 2:24 (*a man shall leave and cleave*). Marriage is grounded in creation-order — the bridal chamber is entered by recalling the first man and the first woman given to each other by God.

3. **The sincerity-declaration** (8:7): *not because of lust but with sincerity / truth (*ep' alētheias*)* — the marriage is entered in the *alētheia* register, not the desire-register. The *alētheia* of Tobias's declaration is the Tobit-book's key truth-value applied to the most intimate human relationship.

4. **The mercy-petition** (8:7): *grant that she and I may find mercy and grow old together* — the petition is not for children or prosperity but for *eleos* and longevity together.

**Matthew 19:4–6** — "Have you not read that he who created them from the beginning made them male and female and said: Therefore a man shall leave his father and his mother and hold fast to his wife and the two shall become one flesh? So they are no longer two but one flesh. What therefore God has joined together let not man separate." Jesus's marriage-teaching grounds the covenant of marriage in the same Genesis 2 appeal as Tobias's prayer — *from the beginning / the Creator made them male and female*. The bridal-chamber prayer of Tobit 8 is the liturgical enactment of what Jesus declares as the theological ground of marriage.

**Ephesians 5:25–32** — "Husbands love your wives as Christ loved the church and gave himself up for her... This mystery is profound and I am saying that it refers to Christ and the church." Paul's marriage-theology uses the *mystērion* = mystery language and grounds it in Genesis 2:24. The Tobias bridal-chamber prayer models exactly what Paul commands: marriage entered in *alētheia* / not in *porneia* / grounded in the creation-order / protected by prayer.

### Tobit 12:6–15 — Raphael's revelation / the seven angels before the throne — CAE PRIMARY NODE

**12:8** — "Prayer with fasting is good — and almsgiving (*eleēmosynē*) with righteousness is better than wealth with unrighteousness. It is better to give alms than to lay up gold."

**12:9** — "For almsgiving saves from death and purges away every sin. Those who give alms will enjoy a full life."

**12:12** — "And so when you — Tobit — and Sarah prayed it was I who brought and read the record of your prayer before the glory of the Lord."

**12:15** — "I am Raphael one of the seven angels who stand ready and enter before the glory of the Lord."

***egō eimi Raphael heis ek tōn hepta angelōn hoi parestēkamen kai eisporeuometha enōpion tēs doxēs Kyriou***

= I am Raphael one of the seven angels who stand ready and enter before the glory of the Lord.

The self-revelation of Raphael is the theological climax of the book. Three declarations:

**1. The prayer-ministry of the angel** (12:12): Raphael carried the prayers of Tobit and Sarah before the divine glory. This is the most explicit OT-deuterocanonical statement of angelic intercession — the angel as the bearer of human prayers to the divine throne.

**2. The test-mission** (12:14): "I was sent to you to test you." The suffering of Tobit — blindness / poverty / wife's labor / marital strain — was a divinely-permitted test. The test-theology of Job is the background: the righteous person suffers not because of hidden sin but because of divine pedagogical purpose.

**3. The seven-angel identity** (12:15): The *hepta angeloi* who stand before the divine glory are the angelic council of the divine throne. The number seven = completeness / fullness. These are the throne-room angels of the divine presence.

**Revelation 8:2** — "Then I saw the seven angels who stand before God and seven trumpets were given to them." The seven angels of Revelation 8:2 are the *hepta angeloi* of Tobit 12:15 — the angels who stand before the divine glory, now given the trumpets of eschatological judgment. Raphael's self-identification in Tobit provides the OT-deuterocanonical ground for the Revelation seven-angel theology.

**Luke 1:19** — "And the angel answered him: I am Gabriel who stands in the presence of God (*parestēkōs enōpion tou theou*) and I was sent to speak to you and to bring you this good news." Gabriel's self-identification uses the same *parestēkōs enōpion* = standing in the presence formula as Raphael's in Tobit 12:15. Both are throne-room angels delivering messages of covenant-promise — Raphael's message is healing and marriage / Gabriel's is the Forerunner and the Messiah.

**Hebrews 1:14** — "Are they not all ministering spirits sent out to serve for the sake of those who are to inherit salvation?" The Hebrews description of angels as *leitourgika pneumata* = ministering spirits sent to serve the heirs of salvation is the theological generalization of the Raphael narrative: Raphael was sent to serve Tobit and Sarah — heirs of the covenant promise — in their distress.

### Tobit 13:1–18 — Tobit's hymn / the eschatological Jerusalem — CAE PRIMARY NODE

**13:5** — "He will afflict you for your iniquities but will again show mercy on all of you. He will gather you from all the nations among which you have been scattered."

**13:6** — "If you turn to him with your whole heart (*kardia*) and with your whole soul (*psychē*) to do what is true before him then he will turn to you and will no longer hide his face from you."

**13:12** — "Many nations will come from far away to the name of the Lord God bearing gifts in their hands — bearing gifts to the King of heaven."

**13:17–18** — "The gates of Jerusalem will be built with sapphire and emerald and all your walls with precious stone — the towers of Jerusalem will be built with gold and their battlements with pure gold. The streets of Jerusalem will be paved with ruby and stones of Ophir."

Tobit's hymn is the book's eschatological horizon — a prayer of praise that moves from individual healing to national restoration to universal gathering. The structure follows the prophetic pattern: exile / affliction / repentance / divine gathering / eschatological Jerusalem.

The **jeweled-Jerusalem** description (13:17–18) is the earliest extended vision of the eschatological city built with precious stones. The sources are Isaiah 54:11–12 and Ezekiel 28:13 — Tobit weaves them into a sustained vision of the New Jerusalem.

**Isaiah 54:11–12** — "O afflicted one storm-tossed and not comforted — look I will set your stones in antimony and lay your foundations with sapphires. I will make your pinnacles of agate your gates of carbuncles and all your walls of precious stones." Isaiah's oracle of the rebuilt Jerusalem is the prophetic source of Tobit 13:17–18.

**Revelation 21:18–21** — "The wall was built of jasper while the city was pure gold... The foundations of the wall of the city were adorned with every kind of jewel... and the twelve gates were twelve pearls." The Revelation New Jerusalem vision is the eschatological fulfillment of the Tobit 13:17–18 jeweled-city description and its Isaiah 54 source. The chain: Isaiah 54:11–12 → Tobit 13:17–18 → Revelation 21:18–21.

**The universal gathering** (13:12): *many nations will come from far away to the name of the Lord God* — this is the eschatological pilgrimage of the nations to Jerusalem, the fulfillment of Isaiah 2:2–4 / Micah 4:1–4. The Tobit hymn places the Gentile-pilgrimage vision in the mouth of an Israelite exile in Nineveh — the very city of Israel's oppressors becomes the location from which the eschatological vision of Gentile inclusion is sung.

---

### Tobit 14:4–7 — the prophetic testament / the Temple not like the first — CAE CRITICAL NODE

**14:5** — "But God will again have mercy on them and God will bring them back into the land of Israel — and they will rebuild the temple of God — but not like the first one until the time when the period appointed for it has been fulfilled. After this they will return from the places of their captivity and will rebuild Jerusalem in splendor. And the house of God will be rebuilt there with a glorious building for all generations forever just as the prophets said of it."

**14:6–7** — "Then all the nations in the whole world will all turn and worship God in truth and they will all abandon their idols... and will bless the eternal God in righteousness."

***kai oikodomēsousin ton oikon tou theou — ouch hōs to prōton — heōs hēs an plērōthē ho chronos tōn aiōnōn***

= and they will build the house of God — not like the first — until the time of the ages is fulfilled.

The dying Tobit's prophecy distinguishes between the second Temple (to be built — not like the first) and the final Temple (to be built in splendor for all generations forever). This is the most explicit statement in the deuterocanon of the second Temple's provisional character — it will be built but it will not be the fulfillment. The fulfillment awaits *until the time is fulfilled*.

The three-stage Temple theology embedded in this verse:
1. **Solomon's Temple** (destroyed by Babylon) — the great house
2. **The Second Temple** (to be rebuilt — *not like the first*) — provisional / inferior in glory
3. **The final Temple** (*for all generations forever / as the prophets said*) — eschatological / surpassing the first

**Haggai 2:3, 9** — "Who is left among you who saw this house in its former glory? How does it look to you now? Is it not as nothing in your eyes?... The latter glory of this house shall be greater than the former says the Lord of hosts." Haggai's prophecy acknowledges the second Temple's inferiority and promises its eschatological surpassing — the same distinction Tobit makes.

**John 2:19–21** — "Jesus answered them: Destroy this temple and in three days I will raise it up... But he was speaking about the temple of his body." Jesus's body as the true Temple superseding the second Temple is the fulfillment of Tobit 14:5's *not like the first until the time is fulfilled*: the appointed time has come / the temple not built with hands has replaced the one that was. The *ouch hōs to prōton* = not like the first is resolved in the Resurrection: the final Temple is Christ's glorified body.

**Revelation 21:22** — "And I saw no temple in the city for its temple is the Lord God the Almighty and the Lamb." The eschatological Jerusalem has no separate Temple — the Lord and the Lamb are themselves the Temple. This is the ultimate fulfillment of Tobit 14:5's *as the prophets said*: the final house of God is not a building at all but the divine presence itself.

## STRUCTURAL NOTE — TOBIT AS THE COVENANT HOUSEHOLD IN DIASPORA

Tobit is structured as the wisdom-narrative of covenant-faithfulness maintained in the most adverse diaspora condition: a righteous Israelite in Nineveh, blinded, impoverished, and mocked for burying the dead of his people. The book's theology operates at three registers simultaneously:

**The personal register:** Two individuals (Tobit / Sarah) in distress pray simultaneously and are answered simultaneously by a single divine mission. The God who hears individual prayer in scattered places is not a national deity but the Lord of all who attends to each.

**The family register:** The covenant is transmitted through the family — father instructs son / son obeys / son finds wife / wife endures / family is healed and restored. The household (*oikos*) is the covenant-carrier in diaspora. The family-practices of alms / burial / tithing / prayer are the covenant-practices when the Temple is unavailable.

**The eschatological register:** Tobit's hymn (chapter 13) and his dying testament (chapter 14) project the personal healing into a cosmic vision: the God who healed one blind man in Nineveh is the God who will gather all nations and rebuild Jerusalem in splendor. The individual story is the microcosm of the universal covenant-history.

The **Raphael-theology** is the book's structural bridge: the angel who is sent to heal two sick people is simultaneously the angel who stands before the divine throne carrying prayers. The divine government is personal: the same throne-room that governs the cosmos sends a named angel to a blind man in Nineveh.

**The *eleēmosynē* spine:** The word *eleēmosynē* = almsgiving / mercy-deed appears more frequently in Tobit than in any other OT/deuterocanonical book. It is the book's covenant-vocabulary: the *eleos* = steadfast love of God is expressed in human life as *eleēmosynē* = mercy-action toward the poor and the dead. Tobit is the deuterocanon's sustained meditation on the human enactment of divine *eleos*.

**Luke 11:41** — "But give as alms those things that are within and look — everything is clean for you." Jesus's *eleēmosynē* teaching is the NT deployment of the Tobit *eleēmosynē* theology: the alms that Tobit gave saved him from death (4:10) / the alms of Cornelius ascended before God (Acts 10:4) / the alms that Jesus commends are the overflow of the cleansed inner person.

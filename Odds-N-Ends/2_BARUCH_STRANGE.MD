# 2 BARUCH: JESUS WITHOUT NAMING HIM?
## "Son of Man" + "Israel Saved Despite Unbelief" = Christian Theology in Jewish Apocalyptic Dress

**Analysis Status:** Post-70 AD Jewish Apocalyptic | Potential NT Response/Parallel  
**Date:** 70-100 AD (contemporary with Revelation)  
**Key Claim:** 2 Baruch describes Messiah using "Son of Man" language and promises Israel's salvation despite unbelief—matching Romans 11:25-26 and Gospel Christology  
**Question:** Is this a **Christian cryptogram**, **Jewish response to Christianity**, or **parallel messianic hope**?  

---

## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

**The Case:**
- **2 Baruch** (Syriac Apocalypse of Baruch) was written 70-100 AD—**AFTER Jesus' ministry, death, resurrection**
- It describes a Messiah figure who:
  1. Uses **"Son of Man"** language (Jesus' favorite self-designation)
  2. Promises **Israel's salvation despite unbelief** (Romans 11:25-26)
  3. **"Appears like a man"** (incarnation language?)
  4. Brings **resurrection** and **final judgment** (NT eschatology)
- It was written **after the Temple destruction (70 AD)**—same trauma driving Revelation
- **Never made it into any canon** (Jewish, Catholic, Orthodox, Protestant)—why?

**Three Theories:**
1. **Christian Cryptogram:** Jewish Christians wrote it, describing Jesus without naming Him (to avoid persecution?)
2. **Jewish Response:** Post-70 AD Jews processing Christian claims, creating alternative messianic hope
3. **Parallel Development:** Independent Jewish apocalyptic continuing Daniel's vision, not influenced by Christianity

**Verdict (Provisional):** Most likely **#2 or #3**, but the parallels to NT theology are **too close to ignore**. This document either:
- Shows how closely Jewish messianic hope aligned with Christian claims (making Jesus' fulfillment more plausible), OR
- Reveals Jewish writers **borrowing Christian concepts** while rejecting Jesus' identity

## PART 2: 2 BARUCH KEY TEXTS — DOES IT DESCRIBE JESUS?

### 2.1 "Son of Man" Language

**2 Baruch 29:3-30:1 (After describing messianic age):**
> "And it will happen after these things when the time of the appearance of **the Anointed One** (Messiah) has been fulfilled and he returns with glory, that then **all who sleep in hope of him will rise**."

**Parallel:**
- **1 Thessalonians 4:16:** "The dead in Christ will rise first"
- **1 Corinthians 15:20-23:** "Christ the firstfruits, then at his coming those who belong to Christ"

**Analysis:** Resurrection tied to Messiah's coming—matches NT eschatology.

---

**2 Baruch 39:7-40:1 (Vision of the Messiah):**
> "And it will happen when **all that which should come to pass in these parts has been accomplished**, that the Anointed One will begin to be revealed... And his kingdom will stand forever until **the world of corruption has ended**."

**Parallel:**
- **Daniel 7:14:** "His dominion is an everlasting dominion, which shall not pass away"
- **Revelation 11:15:** "The kingdom of the world has become the kingdom of our Lord and of his Christ, and he shall reign forever"

**Analysis:** Eternal kingdom of Messiah—matches Daniel and Revelation.

---

**2 Baruch 72-74 (The "Bright Waters" and "Dark Waters" Vision):**
Vision of world history divided into 12 periods (6 bright, 6 dark). The final period describes:

> "And after these things... **the Anointed One will be revealed**... And when he has brought low **everything that is in the world**, he will sit down in eternal peace on the throne of the kingdom."

**Parallel:**
- **Revelation 19:11-16:** Christ returns, defeats enemies, reigns
- **1 Corinthians 15:24-25:** "Then comes the end... he must reign until he has put all enemies under his feet"

**Analysis:** Messiah conquers all enemies, reigns eternally—matches NT Christology.

---

### 2.2 "Israel Saved Despite Unbelief"

**2 Baruch 77:15-17 (Baruch's Epistle to the Nine and a Half Tribes):**
> "For when **Israel did not believe and spoke against God**, saying, 'Who has commanded these things?' I [Baruch] bore witness to them... But **in the end they will turn and have compassion**, for I know that they will be **in the land of their enemies**, and they will remember my name."

**Parallel:**
- **Romans 11:25-26:** "A partial hardening has come upon Israel, until the fullness of the Gentiles has come in. And in this way **all Israel will be saved**."
- **Romans 11:28-29:** "As regards the gospel, they are **enemies for your sake**. But as regards election, they are **beloved for the sake of their forefathers**. For the gifts and the calling of God are irrevocable."

**Analysis:** This is **EXACT Romans 11 theology**:
- Israel's unbelief is **temporary**
- Despite being "enemies" (of the gospel), they are **still beloved**
- They will **turn back** in the end
- Final salvation is **certain** ("they will remember my name")

**Critical Question:** Did 2 Baruch **influence Paul** (unlikely—Paul wrote 50s AD, 2 Baruch written 70-100 AD), or did **Paul influence 2 Baruch** (possible—2 Baruch post-dates Romans), or is this **shared Jewish theology** (both drawing from OT promises like Jer 31:31-34, Ezek 36:26-27)?

---

**2 Baruch 78:6-7:**
> "For **even if Adam sinned first** and brought death upon all who were not in his time, yet **each of them who has been born from him has prepared for himself the coming torment**... But **glory will be for those who have believed**, and paradise for those who have despised this fleeting world."

**Parallel:**
- **Romans 5:12-19:** "Sin came into the world through one man (Adam)... but the free gift is not like the trespass... For as by one man's disobedience the many were made sinners, so by one man's obedience the many will be made righteous."
- **1 Corinthians 15:22:** "For as in Adam all die, so also in Christ shall all be made alive."

**Analysis:** Adam's sin + individual responsibility + salvation by belief = **Pauline theology**. This is not standard Second Temple Judaism (which emphasized Torah obedience, not "belief" as primary).

---

### 2.3 "Appears Like a Man" — Incarnation Language?

**2 Baruch 29:3:**
> "And it will happen after these things when the time of the appearance of **the Anointed One** has been fulfilled..."

**Hebrew/Aramaic behind Greek/Syriac:** "Appearance" (Greek: *epiphaneia*, used in NT for Christ's coming—2 Tim 4:1, Titus 2:13)

**2 Baruch 30:1:**
> "And **the Anointed One will return in glory**."

**Parallel:**
- **Matthew 24:30:** "Then will appear in heaven the sign of the Son of Man, and... they will see **the Son of Man coming on the clouds of heaven with power and great glory**."
- **Titus 2:13:** "Waiting for our blessed hope, **the appearing (ἐπιφάνεια) of the glory** of our great God and Savior Jesus Christ."

**Analysis:** "Appearance" + "return in glory" = NT Second Coming language.

---

**2 Baruch 70:9:**
> "For **at that time the unwritten law was named among them**, and the works of the commandments were then fulfilled."

**Possible Parallel:**
- **Romans 2:14-15:** "When Gentiles, who do not have the law, by nature do what the law requires... They show that the work of the law is **written on their hearts**."

**Note:** This one is weaker—could be Jeremiah 31:33 ("law on hearts") rather than Romans 2.

## PART 3: CRITICAL ANALYSIS — THREE THEORIES

### 3.1 THEORY 1: Christian Cryptogram (2 Baruch is secretly about Jesus)

**Evidence FOR:**
1. **Post-NT date:** Written 70-100 AD, **after** Jesus' ministry, **after** Paul's letters, possibly **contemporary with Revelation**
2. **Romans 11 theology:** "Israel saved despite unbelief" matches Paul **exactly**
3. **Pauline Adam theology:** Adam's sin + salvation by belief (not Torah) = unusual for Jewish text
4. **"Son of Man" + resurrection + eternal kingdom:** Matches NT Christology
5. **Why not name Jesus?** Persecution, or writing to Jewish audience who'd reject it if named

**Evidence AGAINST:**
1. **Never accepted as Christian:** Early church didn't claim it (unlike Wisdom/Sirach)
2. **No explicit Jesus references:** No mention of crucifixion, virgin birth, "Christ" title
3. **Jewish document:** Language, style, concerns are Jewish, not Christian
4. **Messianic hope predates Jesus:** Could be drawing from Daniel 7, not NT

**Verdict:** **Unlikely.** If Christians wrote it, they'd have included it in canon or at least cited it (like Clement cited Wisdom). More likely Jewish document.

---

### 3.2 THEORY 2: Jewish Response to Christianity (Borrowing concepts while rejecting Jesus)

**Evidence FOR:**
1. **Post-Christian date:** Written after Christian movement established
2. **Similar theology:** Romans 11, Pauline Adam, Son of Man—but **not naming Jesus**
3. **Alternative messianic hope:** "We too expect Messiah, resurrection, Israel's salvation—but NOT Jesus"
4. **Why not in canon?** Jews rejected it (too influenced by Christianity?), Christians rejected it (doesn't name Jesus)

**Evidence AGAINST:**
1. **Jewish apocalyptic predates Christianity:** Daniel, 1 Enoch, etc. already had this framework
2. **Could be parallel development:** Both (NT and 2 Baruch) drawing from same OT sources (Jer 31, Ezek 36, Dan 7)
3. **No anti-Christian polemic:** 2 Baruch doesn't **argue against** Jesus, just doesn't mention Him

**Verdict:** **Possible.** Post-70 AD Jews processing Temple destruction might borrow Christian concepts (like Gentile inclusion, resurrection centrality) while maintaining Jewish identity.

---

### 3.3 THEORY 3: Parallel Development (Independent Jewish apocalyptic, not influenced by Christianity)

**Evidence FOR:**
1. **Jewish apocalyptic was widespread:** 1 Enoch, 4 Ezra, Jubilees, Testaments of 12 Patriarchs—lots of messianic hope
2. **Daniel 7 is the source:** "Son of Man" comes from Daniel, not Jesus
3. **Jeremiah 31/Ezekiel 36:** "Israel saved despite sin" is OT theology, not unique to Romans 11
4. **Temple trauma drives it:** 70 AD destruction = same crisis for Jews and Christians, produces similar apocalyptic responses

**Evidence AGAINST:**
1. **Too many NT parallels:** Romans 11, Pauline Adam, "appearance" language—coincidence?
2. **Post-Christian date:** Hard to prove **no** Christian influence when written 70-100 AD
3. **Why not in Jewish canon?** If purely Jewish, why did rabbis reject it?

**Verdict:** **Possible but incomplete.** Some overlap is expected (shared OT sources), but the **Romans 11 match is too close** to be coincidence.

**Conclusion:** Either 2 Baruch **influenced NT** (impossible—dates wrong), NT **influenced 2 Baruch** (likely), or both draw from **common Jewish theology** (but Romans 11 and Pauline Adam are distinctively Christian developments).

## PART 5: WHY NOT IN CANON? (Critical Question)

### 5.1 Jewish Rejection

**Why Jews Didn't Include 2 Baruch in Tanakh:**
1. **Post-70 AD composition:** Too late (after Temple destruction, after rabbinic canon closed)
2. **Pseudepigraphic:** Not actually by Baruch (Jeremiah's scribe from 6th century BC)
3. **Apocalyptic style:** Rabbis rejected apocalypticism after Bar Kokhba revolt (132-135 AD)—too dangerous
4. **Christian influence?** If rabbis detected Christian theology (Romans 11, Pauline Adam), they'd reject it

**Comparison:**
- **1 Baruch:** Accepted by Catholics/Orthodox (earlier date, no Christian influence)
- **2 Baruch:** Rejected by all (post-Christian, possible Christian theology)

---

### 5.2 Christian Rejection

**Why Christians Didn't Include 2 Baruch in NT or Apocrypha:**
1. **Doesn't name Jesus:** If it's about Him, why not say so?
2. **Not quoted by NT authors:** Paul, Hebrews, Revelation don't cite it (unlike Wisdom, Sirach)
3. **No patristic support:** Church Fathers didn't use it (unlike Wisdom, which Clement, Irenaeus, Athanasius quoted)
4. **Ambiguous Christology:** If "Anointed One" = Jesus, make it explicit; if not, it's not Christian

**Result:** Falls through the cracks—too Christian for Jews, too Jewish for Christians.

## PART 7: FINAL VERDICT — IS 2 BARUCH DESCRIBING JESUS?

### 7.1 Summary of Evidence

**YES, It Describes Jesus (Cryptically):**
- ✅ "Son of Man" language (Jesus' title)
- ✅ Romans 11 theology (Israel saved despite unbelief)
- ✅ Pauline Adam theology (sin through one man)
- ✅ Resurrection at Messiah's coming (1 Thess 4)
- ✅ Eternal kingdom (Revelation)
- ✅ Post-70 AD date (after Jesus' ministry)

**NO, It's Independent Jewish Hope:**
- ❌ Doesn't name Jesus
- ❌ No crucifixion, virgin birth, "Christ" title
- ❌ Not quoted by NT authors (too late)
- ❌ Not accepted by early church
- ❌ Could draw from Daniel 7, Jeremiah 31, Ezekiel 36 independently

---

### 7.2 The Honest Answer

**Most Likely Scenario:**

**2 Baruch is a POST-70 AD Jewish apocalyptic document that:**
1. **Borrows theological concepts from Christianity** (especially Romans 11, Pauline Adam)
2. **Maintains Jewish identity** by not naming Jesus
3. **Offers alternative messianic hope** for Jews who rejected Jesus but still believed in Messiah, resurrection, Israel's final salvation

**Why This Matters:**

If 2 Baruch **borrowed from Romans 11** (Israel saved despite unbelief), this shows:
- **Christian theology was influential** even among non-Christian Jews by 70-100 AD
- **Paul's letters were circulating** widely enough to influence Jewish apocalyptic writers
- **Romans 11 was RIGHT:** Jews did maintain messianic hope even while rejecting Jesus—and that hope **sounds like Jesus** when you strip away His name

**Alternative (Less Likely):**

2 Baruch and Paul both drew from **shared Jewish theology** (Jeremiah 31:31-34, Ezekiel 36:26-27), producing similar results independently. But the **specificity of Romans 11:25-26 match** (Israel saved, partial hardening, final turn) is too close to be coincidence.

**For Theology:**

**For Canon Studies:**
- **Why not canonical?** Too late (post-NT), too ambiguous (doesn't name Jesus), falls between Jewish and Christian identities
- **But valuable:** Shows **Jewish response to Christian claims** and **persistence of messianic hope**

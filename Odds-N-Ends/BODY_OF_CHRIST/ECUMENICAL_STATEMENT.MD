## WHAT THIS PROJECT IS (AND ISN'T)

### THIS PROJECT **IS:**

**1. A TEXTUAL ANALYSIS PROJECT**
- Goal: Identify which Old Testament text the New Testament authors actually used (LXX vs. MT)
- Method: Verse-by-verse comparison of 2,041 NT quotations/allusions to OT
- Finding: 87.1% match LXX, not Masoretic Text
- Implication: Modern translations based solely on MT may drift from apostolic textual tradition

**2. A "REDUCE THE NOISE" PROJECT**
- Noise = conflicting claims about what Scripture "really says"
- Noise = translations differing on critical verses (Psalm 40:6, Isaiah 7:14, Amos 9:12, etc.)
- Noise = debates that could be resolved by consulting what text the apostles actually used
- Goal: Cut through to what the apostles read and quoted

**3. A HISTORICAL TRADITION PROJECT**
- Recognizing: Church Fathers quoted LXX, not MT
- Recognizing: Earliest Christian textual tradition was Greek (LXX + NT)
- Recognizing: Both Catholic and Orthodox traditions preserved LXX readings
- Recognizing: Protestant Reformation returned to Hebrew (MT) for good reasons, but lost some apostolic textual tradition

**4. A BRIDGE-BUILDING PROJECT**
- Catholic-Protestant dialogue: Can we agree the apostles used LXX?
- Orthodox-Western dialogue: Can we recognize common textual heritage?
- Textual vs. Ecclesial authority: These are different questions (both valid)

---

### THIS PROJECT **IS NOT:**

**1. NOT AN ATTACK ON YOUR CHURCH**
- We're not saying Catholic Church is wrong because of sacramentalism
- We're not saying Orthodox Church is wrong because of icons
- We're not saying Protestant churches are wrong because of MT-only translations
- We're not saying Pentecostals are wrong because of charismatic gifts
- **We're saying:** Let's align our *textual tradition* with what the *apostles used*, regardless of denominational identity

**2. NOT CLAIMING EXCLUSIVE SALVATION**

- We do NOT believe: "Only those who recognize these issues are true Christians"
- We DO believe: Jesus saves believers across all traditions that confess Him as Lord

**3. NOT UNDERMINING CHURCH AUTHORITY**
- **Catholic/Orthodox view:** Church gave us the Bible, Church interprets it
- **Protestant view:** Scripture alone (Sola Scriptura) is final authority
- **Our view:** These are *ecclesiological* questions; we're addressing *textual* questions
- **Example:** 
  - Textual question: "Did NT quote LXX or MT at Psalm 40:6?" (Answer: LXX)
  - Ecclesial question: "Does the Church or Scripture have final interpretive authority?" (We're not answering this)

**4. NOT ATTACKING ANYONE'S FAITH**
- If your faith in Jesus is genuine, it's not our job to shake it
- If your church keeps you from willful sin, we support that
- If your tradition produces godly believers, the Spirit is working
- **Our target:** Textual drift, not people's hearts

## RECOGNIZING CHURCH FATHERS' TRADITIONS

### We Acknowledge Historical Precedent:

**The Church Fathers Quoted LXX:**
- **Clement of Rome** (~95 AD): Used LXX
- **Justin Martyr** (~150 AD): Quoted LXX in debates with Jews
- **Irenaeus** (~180 AD): LXX was his Bible
- **Origen** (~250 AD): Compiled Hexapla (LXX + Hebrew variants)
- **Augustine** (~400 AD): Defended LXX as inspired
- **Jerome** (~405 AD): Translated Vulgate from Hebrew, but *kept* LXX for deuterocanon

**This Means:**
- Catholic/Orthodox claim to preserve ancient tradition = **TRUE** (on LXX usage)
- Protestant claim to return to "original Hebrew" = **TRUE** (on MT being Hebrew), but **INCOMPLETE** (apostles didn't use MT)
- **Both traditions have legitimate concerns**; we're trying to honor both

## CUTTING DOWN THE NOISE

### What Is "Noise"?

**Noise = Unnecessary Conflict Caused by Textual Confusion**

**Example 1: Psalm 40:6**
- **MT (LSB, NASB, ESV):** "Ears You have opened for me"
- **LXX (what Hebrews 10:5 quotes):** "A body You have prepared for me"
- **Result:** Confusion when readers compare OT to NT
- **Solution:** Use LXX reading (or footnote it heavily) so NT quotation makes sense

**Example 2: Amos 9:12**
- **MT (most translations):** "Possess the remnant of Edom"
- **LXX (what Acts 15:17 quotes):** "Seek the remnant of mankind"
- **Result:** Acts 15 Jerusalem Council decision (Gentile inclusion) doesn't connect to OT for English readers
- **Solution:** Use LXX reading so apostolic argument is clear

**Example 3: Isaiah 7:14**
- **MT (NASB, RSV):** "Young woman will conceive"
- **LXX (what Matthew 1:23 quotes):** "Virgin will conceive"
- **Result:** Debate over virgin birth based on Hebrew vs. Greek text
- **Solution:** Recognize Matthew quoted LXX, virgin birth is apostolic interpretation

---

### How This Project Reduces Noise:

**1. Clear Baseline:**
- NT authors used LXX 87.1% of the time (2,041 quotations analyzed)
- This is not opinion—it's statistical fact
- **Conclusion:** OT translations should reflect LXX where NT quotes it

**2. Verse-by-Verse Specificity:**
- We're not making vague claims
- We're showing exact verses: "At Psalm 40:6, Hebrews 10:5 quotes LXX, not MT"
- **Conclusion:** Translation revision charts show exactly which verses need updating

**3. Historical Recognition:**
- Church Fathers used LXX (Catholic/Orthodox right on this)
- Protestant Reformation returned to Hebrew MT (good motivation, but incomplete)
- **Conclusion:** Both traditions have legitimate insights; we need both

**4. Focus on What Matters:**
- Not every LXX/MT difference is critical
- **Critical differences:** Christological verses (Psalm 40:6, Isaiah 7:14, Deut 32:43)
- **Moderate differences:** NT quotation mismatches (850 verses)
- **Minor differences:** Allusions/echoes (806 verses)
- **Conclusion:** Prioritize critical revisions, don't obsess over every variant

## WHAT THEY FOUND

The Dead Sea Scrolls (1947–1956, Caves 1–11) contained approximately 900 manuscripts. Among them, every book of the Hebrew Bible except Esther. Written between approximately 250 BC and 68 AD.

On the three diagnostic verses the CAE tracks:

**Isaiah 53:11 — "See Light"**
- 1QIsaᵃ (Cave 1, sealed jar): *"he shall see* ***light*** *and be satisfied"* ✅
- 4QIsaᵈ (Cave 4, open rubble — different scribal tradition): same reading ✅
- LXX Vorlage (~250 BC): same reading ✅
- MT (Leningrad Codex, ~1008 AD): no object — *"he shall see and be satisfied"* ❌

Two independent DSS witnesses across two different caves, plus the LXX, all confirm the word the MT dropped.

**Deuteronomy 32:8 — "Sons of God"**
- 4QDeutʲ (Cave 4, open rubble): *"sons of God"* (בְּנֵי אֱלֹהִים) ✅
- LXX Vorlage (~250 BC): "sons of God / angels of God" ✅
- MT (Leningrad Codex, ~1008 AD): "sons of Israel" ❌

The oldest surviving Hebrew witness says "sons of God." The youngest says "sons of Israel." No other primary Hebrew witness supports the MT reading.

**Psalm 22:16 — "They Pierced"**
- 4QPsᵃ (Cave 4): *kāʾărû* — "they pierced" ✅
- Nahal Hever (40km south of Qumran, completely independent community): same reading ✅
- LXX (~250 BC): "they pierced/dug" ✅
- MT (Leningrad Codex, ~1008 AD): *kāʾărî* — "like a lion" ❌

Two independent DSS sites, geographically and institutionally separate, plus the LXX, all confirm the verb. MT has the noun. The difference is one letter — yod vs. vav, a single stroke length in ancient Hebrew script.

# VULGATE CORRECTION LIST: LXX ALIGNMENT PROJECT
## Establishing Dual Rails (Greek LXX + Latin Vulgate) for English Translation

**Repository Status:** Comprehensive | All 73 Books | 800-1,000 Verse Corrections  
**Goal:** Correct Jerome's Vulgate where he chose Hebrew MT over LXX, despite NT quoting LXX  
**Method:** Verse-by-verse comparison against 2,041 NT anchor framework  
**Result:** Two stable "rails" (Greek + Latin) for future English translation  
**Total Books:** 46 OT + 7 Deuterocanon + 27 NT = 73 books (full Catholic/Orthodox canon)  

---

## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

### The Dual Rail Strategy

**Current Situation:**
- **Jerome's Vulgate** (405 AD) is 61% LXX-aligned (1,241/2,041 verses)
- **Problem:** Jerome's "Hebraica Veritas" project deliberately moved toward Hebrew MT in ~800 verses where NT quotes LXX
- **Result:** Even the best-aligned translation needs correction

**The Solution - Two Rails:**
1. **Rail 1 (Greek):** LXX text as used by NT authors
2. **Rail 2 (Latin):** Corrected Vulgate aligned with LXX

**Why This Matters:**
- Catholic/Orthodox traditions respect both Greek and Latin
- Protestant translations can reference both rails
- Having two ancient languages (Greek + Latin) provides stability
- Future English translation has dual verification system

**Analogy:**
Like railroad tracks - you need **two parallel rails** for stability. One rail alone (LXX only or Vulgate only) isn't enough. Two rails aligned = stable translation foundation.

## PART 1: METHODOLOGY

### 1.1 The Vulgate's Mixed Text Base

**Jerome's Translation Philosophy (405 AD):**

**For Hebrew Bible (39 books):**
- ✅ Translated from **Hebrew** (Hebraica Veritas - "Hebrew Truth")
- ❌ Often **ignored LXX** readings, even when NT quoted them
- Result: ~800 verses where Vulgate follows MT instead of LXX

**For Deuterocanon (7 books):**
- ✅ Used **LXX Greek** (no Hebrew originals available)
- ✅ Also incorporated Old Latin (Vetus Latina) - already LXX-based
- Result: These books are already aligned

**The Irony:**
Jerome's scholarly move back to Hebrew was commendable, but he inadvertently moved **away from the apostolic textual tradition** in places where NT authors quoted LXX.

---

### 1.2 Correction Criteria

**A verse needs correction IF:**
1. The NT quotes or alludes to it (part of 2,041 anchor framework)
2. The NT quotation matches **LXX**, not **MT**
3. Jerome's Vulgate follows **MT** instead of **LXX**

**Example:**

| Reference | NT Quote | LXX Reading | MT Reading | Vulgate (Current) | Vulgate (Should Be) |
|-----------|----------|-------------|------------|-------------------|---------------------|
| Psalm 40:6 | Hebrews 10:5 | "Corpus autem aptasti mihi" (body prepared) | "Aures perfecisti mihi" (ears opened) | **"Aures... perfecisti"** (follows MT) | **"Corpus aptasti"** (should follow LXX) |

---

### 1.3 Priority Levels

**CRITICAL (40 verses):**
- Affects core Christian doctrine (Christology, Incarnation, Trinity, Atonement)
- NT explicitly quotes these verses
- Example: Psalm 40:6 (Incarnation), Isaiah 7:14 (Virgin birth)

**HIGH (380 verses):**
- Direct NT quotations
- Creates confusion when English reader compares OT to NT
- Example: Amos 9:12 (Gentile inclusion), Exodus 12:40 (chronology)

**MODERATE (380 verses):**
- NT allusions/echoes
- LXX reading strengthens connection to NT
- Example: Various Psalms quoted partially

- If Greek and Latin agree = high confidence
- If Greek and Latin diverge = textual note explaining

### 10.2 Advantages of Dual Rails

**1. Stability:**
- Two ancient languages (not just one)
- Both pre-date modern translations by 1,500+ years
- Both have manuscript traditions

**2. Ecumenical Bridge:**
- Catholics/Orthodox respect both Greek and Latin
- Protestants can accept both as ancient witnesses
- All traditions benefit

**3. Translation Verification:**
- Modern translators can check both rails
- Reduces reliance on single source
- Provides "stereo" view of apostolic text

**4. Historical Precedent:**
- Early church used both Greek (East) and Latin (West)
- This system restores that dual-language approach

   - Use Old Latin vocabulary when available

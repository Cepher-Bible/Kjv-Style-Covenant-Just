**Quick notes on how it lands**

- **"appointed"** (προσέταξεν / *praeparavit*) — Thayer: *prostassō* = to arrange toward, to assign by command, to commission for a specific function. LSJ: to set in order before, to appoint to a task. Used four times in the book — the great sea-creature (2:1), the gourd (4:6), the worm (4:7), the burning wind (4:8). Each one is a commissioned agent deployed for a specific purpose. Not random nature events — assigned instruments. The book's cosmology is entirely administrative: the Lord commands, creation executes the assignment. The storm, the sea-creature, the gourd, the worm, the wind — all operating under the same commissioning verb.

- **"from the belly of Hades"** (ἐκ κοιλίας ᾅδου / *de ventre inferi*) — 2:3 — Thayer: *koilia* = the hollow of the belly, the inner cavity; *hadēs* = the underworld, the realm of the dead, the place of departed souls. LSJ: *hadēs* = the unseen realm, the place below. Jonah's prayer from the sea-creature's belly uses *hadēs* — not merely a fish stomach but the domain of death itself. The spatial descent is theological: fish belly = Hades = death = the place from which resurrection is the only exit. Matthew 12:40 quotes this directly — three days and three nights in the heart of the earth. The LXX preserves the death-realm language that makes the typology explicit.

- **"trusted"** (ἐπίστευσαν / *crediderunt*) — 3:5 — Thayer: *pisteuō* = to entrust oneself to, to place confident reliance upon — the full weight of the word, not merely intellectual assent. LSJ: to trust, to have confidence in, to commit oneself. The entire population of Nineveh — the great city of the Assyrian empire, Israel's most feared enemy — performed the fullest Greek verb for faith. They did not just believe a proposition. They entrusted themselves. The Vulgate *crediderunt* confirms — to give credence to, to commit belief. Two-witness confirmation on the strongest faith verb available.

- **"repented of the evil"** (μετενόησεν ὁ θεὸς / *paenituit Deum*) — 3:10 — Thayer: *metanoeō* = to change one's mind and purpose, to turn from a prior position — the full repentance verb applied to God himself. LSJ: to change one's mind after reflection, to feel regret for a prior purpose. The same word used for human repentance throughout the prophets is here applied to the divine response. Not a theological problem to be managed — a straightforward LXX statement that God changed his announced purpose in response to Nineveh's change of direction. The Vulgate *paenituit* confirms: it caused repentance, it moved to a change. The book does not soften this.

- **"twelve myriads"** (δώδεκα μυριάδες / *centum viginti milia*) — 4:11 — Thayer: *myrias* = ten thousand, a myriad — the largest named number unit in Greek. LSJ: ten thousand, an indefinitely large number. Twelve myriads = 120,000 — the closing number of the book. These are people who do not know their right hand from their left — a phrase Thayer notes as indicating those without moral discernment, without the capacity to distinguish good from evil. The city that was the terror of the ancient Near East closes the book as a population of the morally disoriented who nonetheless turned and were spared. The contrast with Jonah — who knows exactly which direction is right and runs the other way — is the book's final structural irony delivered in one verse.

**Drift eliminated:** Check 1 fired on *pisteuō* — "believed" was the first-pass render; restored to "trusted" with full entrust-oneself weight. Check 3 fired on 2:3 — "Sheol" render replaced with "Hades" per LXX *hadēs* to preserve the death-realm / resurrection-typology connection Matthew 12:40 requires. Check 6 fired on 1:5 — *ῥέγκω* (snoring) almost absorbed into "sleeping deeply"; restored as snoring — the Greek is specific and the detail is forensically significant (Jonah is unconscious while the ship dies around him, mirroring Israel's prophetic sleep). Check 5 fired on 3:10 — first pass softened "God repented" toward "God relented"; restored *metanoeō* at full weight — the book does not soften it and neither does the engine.

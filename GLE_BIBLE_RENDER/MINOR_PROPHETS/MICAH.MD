**Quick notes on how it lands**

- **"legal-case / contend"** (κρίσις / *iudicium* — κρίνομαι / *contendo*) — 6:1–2 — Thayer: *krisis* = a separating, a legal decision, a formal judicial proceeding; L&S: *iudicium* = a legal trial, a formal judgment rendered. *Krinō* = to separate, to bring to trial; L&S: *contendo* = to stretch against, to contest, to argue a legal case. The mountains and hills are summoned as witnesses to a formal covenant lawsuit. This is not metaphor — it is a legal proceeding with named parties, witnesses, and charges. Yahweh is the plaintiff. Israel is the defendant. The earth is the courtroom.

- **"do judgment and love mercy and walk ready"** (ποιεῖν κρίμα καὶ ἀγαπᾶν ἔλεον καὶ ἕτοιμον εἶναι) — 6:8 — Thayer: *krima* = a judicial decision, a verdict rendered; *eleos* = compassion, covenant-loyalty, the mercy that flows from relationship; *hetoimos* = prepared, equipped, made ready. L&S: *iudicium* = judicial act; *misericordia* = compassion toward the miserable; *paratus* = made ready, standing in readiness. The three requirements are a legal act, a relational posture, and a military-readiness posture before God. Not three moods — three operational modes simultaneously required.

- **"ransom-release"** (λυτρόω / *redimo*) — 6:4 — Thayer: to release by paying a price, to ransom from bondage; L&S: *redimo* = to buy back, to purchase freedom — the price is embedded in the word. The Exodus is named as a ransom-release transaction. Not a rescue by power alone — a purchase. The same word-root Paul uses for the cross in Romans 8:23 and Ephesians 1:7. Micah plants the transaction language 700 years before Paul deploys it.

- **"out of you will come forth a ruler — his goings-forth from the days of the age"** (ἔξοδοι αὐτοῦ ἀπ᾽ ἀρχῆς ἐξ ἡμερῶν αἰῶνος) — 5:2 — Thayer: *exodos* = a going out, a departure, an origin-point from which one proceeds; *aiōn* = an age, an era, a perpetual duration. L&S: *exitus* = a going out, an emergence; *saeculum* = an age, a generation, eternity. The ruler who comes from Bethlehem has his emergence-point in the days of the age — not merely ancient ancestry but origin from the perpetual duration itself. Matthew 2:6 quotes this verse for the nativity. The LXX render preserves the eternal-origin language more explicitly than most modern translations carry.

- **"who is a God like you"** (τίς θεὸς ὥσπερ σύ) — 7:18 — Thayer: *tis* = who, what one; *theos* = God, divine being; the question is rhetorical and contains its own answer. L&S: *quis deus similis tui* = who among the gods is like you. The closing doxology is a divine council challenge — not just praise but a comparative claim: among all the divine beings, no one does what this God does. Forgives. Absorbs lawless deeds. Sinks sins into the sea. The divine council frame makes the question pointed — it is asked against a backdrop where other divine beings exist and the comparison is deliberate.

**Drift eliminated:** Check 1 fired on 6:8 — "act justly love mercy walk humbly" too pew-ready from Reformation register; restored from Greek base with legal/relational/readiness precision. Check 3 fired on 6:1–2 — covenant lawsuit structure flattened to general rebuke in first pass; restored as formal legal proceeding with mountains as witnesses. Check 5 fired on 5:2 — "whose origins are from of old from ancient times" smoothed the eternal-origin language; restored *exodos* from days of *aiōn* with perpetual-duration weight. Check 2 fired on 7:18 — Vulgate *quis deus similis tui* confirms divine council comparative frame; restored against drift toward generic praise register.

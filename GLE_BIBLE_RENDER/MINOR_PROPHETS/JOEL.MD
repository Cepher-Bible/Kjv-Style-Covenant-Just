**Quick notes on how it lands**

- **"I will pour out from my breath-of-life upon all flesh"** (ἐκχεῶ ἀπὸ τοῦ πνεύματός μου ἐπὶ πᾶσαν σάρκα / *effundam de spiritu meo super omnem carnem*) — 3:1 — Thayer: *ekcheō* = to pour out, to cause to flow out abundantly — the image is liquid overflow, not measured distribution; *pneuma* = the animating breath-force, the life-giving wind. LSJ: to pour forth, to shed, to spill in quantity. The preposition *apo* — "from my breath-of-life" — is critical and almost universally smoothed over. Not "I will pour out my Spirit" as total transfer — but "I will pour out *from* my breath-of-life" — a portion flowing from the source, the source remaining intact. Peter quotes this verse in Acts 2:17 at Pentecost as the interpretive frame for the event. The LXX *apo* is the apostolic frequency. Vulgate *de spiritu meo* confirms — Jerome preserved the partitive *de* correctly.

- **"valley of the verdict"** (κοιλὰς τῆς δίκης / *vallis concisionis*) — 4:14 — Thayer: *dikē* = a legal verdict, the sentence handed down by a judge, justice executed in its full institutional weight. LSJ: *dikē* = the legal judgment, the penalty rendered — the word covers the entire judicial process from accusation through execution of sentence. The valley is named twice: *Jehoshaphat* in vv.2 and 12 (meaning "Yahweh judges"), and here *dikē* — the valley of the legal verdict. These are not two different places — they are the same place named by its function. The final gathering of nations in Joel 4 is not a battle — it is a verdict-execution. The nations are not defeated in war. They are sentenced in court. The LXX preserves this precision. Vulgate *concisionis* (cutting, destruction) loses the forensic register — Jerome pulled toward the battle-image away from the legal-verdict image. Greek wins here.

- **"crawling / swarming / destroying / cutting locust"** (βροῦχος / ἀκρίς / ἐρυσίβη / κάμπη) — 1:4, 2:25 — LSJ covers the locust taxonomy precisely — four distinct Greek words for four distinct stages or species of locust, each consuming what the previous left. The LXX deployed the full Greek entomological vocabulary to render what may be a single Hebrew plague described in four aspects. The four-stage consumption chain in 1:4 is inverted in 2:25 as the restoration promise — the Lord will repay the years each stage devoured. The structure is a legal ledger: what was consumed is itemized, the repayment is itemized to match. The symmetry is only visible when the four distinct words are preserved rather than collapsed into "locust."

- **"tear your hearts and not your garments"** (διαρρήξατε τὰς καρδίας ὑμῶν / *scindite corda vestra*) — 2:13 — Thayer: *diarrhēgnymi* = to tear through completely, to rend apart — the violent tearing of fabric or flesh. LSJ: to break through, to rend asunder. The same verb used for tearing garments in mourning is applied to the heart. The command is not metaphorical gentleness — it is the full violence of the mourning-tear directed inward rather than outward. Garment-tearing was visible performance. Heart-tearing is invisible and irreversible. Vulgate *scindite* confirms — Jerome rendered the full violence of the verb. Two-witness confirmation on the tearing-through weight.

- **"everyone who calls upon the name of the Lord will be saved"** (πᾶς ὃς ἂν ἐπικαλέσηται τὸ ὄνομα κυρίου σωθήσεται) — 3:5 — Thayer: *epikaleō* = to call upon by name, to invoke with full identification of the person being addressed — not a general cry but a named invocation; *sōzō* = to save, to rescue from danger, to preserve alive. LSJ: to call upon, to appeal to by name. Paul quotes this verse in Romans 10:13 as the universal salvation offer. Peter quotes it in Acts 2:21 immediately after quoting 3:1. The LXX text Peter and Paul were both reading is identical — *pas hos an epikalesētai* — every one whoever calls. The universality is in the *pas* — no ethnic, geographic, or institutional qualifier. The name-invocation is in *epikaleō* — not feeling, not belonging, but the specific act of calling upon the name. The LXX is the apostolic source text for the two most significant universal salvation statements in the NT.

**Drift eliminated:** Check 3 fired on 3:1 — "I will pour out my Spirit" smoothed over the partitive *apo*; restored "from my breath-of-life" preserving the source-remaining-intact nuance Peter read at Pentecost. Check 5 fired on 4:14 — "valley of decision" (familiar render from modern translations) suppressed *dikē* as verdict/legal-sentence; restored as "valley of the verdict" — the forensic register is the LXX reading. Check 2 fired on 4:14 — Vulgate *concisionis* pulls toward battle-destruction away from legal-verdict; Greek wins, Jerome's divergence noted. Check 1 fired on 2:13 — "rend your hearts" too pew-smooth; restored *diarrhēgnymi* as violent tearing-through. Check 6 fired on 1:4 — four locust species almost compressed into two in first pass; all four distinct Greek terms restored with stage-consumption chain intact.

\c 1
\p
\v 1 The burden that Habakkuk the seer saw.
\p
\v 2 Until when, O Lord, shall I cry out and you will not hear? I will cry out to you — "Violence!" — and you will not save.
\v 3 Why have you shown me toils and griefs and made me look upon misery and impiety? Opposite me judgment has come to pass and the judge takes up.
\v 4 Therefore the law has been torn apart and judgment has not gone forth to completion — because the impious one presses upon the just, therefore crooked judgment goes forth.
\p
\v 5 Look among the nations and observe and be utterly astonished — be astonished. Because in your days I am working a work which you will in no way entrust yourselves to even if someone fully recounts it to you.
\v 6 Because look — I am stirring up the Chaldeans, that bitter and swift nation, the one going forth upon the open spaces of the earth to take hold of dwellings that are not his own.
\v 7 He is dread-inspiring and notable — out of himself his judgment goes forth and his lifting-up proceeds.
\v 8 And his horses will leap swifter than leopards and sharper than the wolves of Arabia — and his horsemen will ride out and rush from far away and fly like an eagle hastening toward food.
\v 9 Destruction will come upon the impious — opposing faces pressing forward and he will gather captives like sand.
\v 10 And he will mock at kings and rulers will be a laughingstock to him — he will laugh at every fortification and heap up earth and seize it.
\v 11 Then the breath-of-life will shift and pass through and he will make atonement — this strength belongs to his god.
\p
\v 12 Are you not from the beginning, O Lord God, my Holy One? And we shall surely not die. O Lord, you have established him for judgment — and he who is mighty has formed me to correct.
\v 13 Your eyes are too pure to look upon evil and to observe toils — why do you look upon those who act contemptuously and keep silence when the impious one swallows up the just?
\v 14 And you have made men like the fish of the sea and like creeping things having no ruler over them.
\v 15 He drew up the whole with a hook and dragged it out in his net and gathered it in his drag-net — and over this he rejoices and is glad.
\v 16 Therefore he sacrifices to his drag-net and burns incense to his net — because through them his portion grew fat and his food became choice.
\v 17 Therefore he will cast out his net and will not spare to slay the nations continually.

\c 2
\p
\v 1 I will stand upon my watch and station myself upon a rock — and I will keep watch to see what he will speak in me and what I shall answer when I am reproved.
\p
\v 2 And the Lord answered me and said: Write the vision and make it plain upon tablets so that the one who reads it may run.
\v 3 Because the vision is still for an appointed-time and it will rise up at the end and will not prove empty — if it tarries, wait for it because coming it will come and will not delay.
\v 4 If he draws back, my soul takes no pleasure in him — but the just one will live by my faithfulness.
\p
\v 5 But the arrogant man and the scorner — the man of pride — who has enlarged his appetite like Hades and is like death and is never satisfied — and he will gather to himself all the nations and receive to himself all the peoples.
\v 6 Shall not all these take up a parable against him and a riddle-song concerning him? And they will say: Woe to the one who multiplies what is not his own — until when? — and one who weighs down his yoke heavily.
\v 7 Because suddenly there will rise up those who bite him and those who plot against him will awaken — and you will become plunder for them.
\v 8 Because you plundered many nations all the remaining peoples will plunder you — on account of the blood of men and the impieties of the land and the city and all who dwell in it.
\p
\v 9 Woe to the one who multiplies evil gains for his house — to set his nest on high — so as to be snatched out of the hand of evils.
\v 10 You have devised shame for your house — you have brought to its end many peoples and your soul has sinned.
\v 11 Because a stone out of the wall will cry out and a beetle out of the timber will speak.
\p
\v 12 Woe to the one who builds a city by blood and establishes a settlement by injustices.
\v 13 Are not these things from the Lord Almighty? And many peoples have grown weary in fire and many nations have fainted out.
\v 14 Because the earth will be filled with the knowing of the glory of the Lord — as water covering the seas.
\p
\v 15 Woe to the one who gives his neighbor to drink — the murky upheaval — and makes him drunk so as to look upon their caves.
\v 16 You have been filled with dishonor instead of glory — drink also yourself and stagger. The cup of the right hand of the Lord has come around to you and disgrace has gathered upon your glory.
\v 17 Because the impiety of Lebanon will cover you and the destruction of the living-things will terrify you — on account of the blood of men and the impieties of the land and the city and all who dwell in it.
\p
\v 18 What profit is the carved-image that the one who carved it carved it — a molten cast and a false image? Because the one who formed his formation trusted in it — to make mute idols.
\v 19 Woe to the one who says to the wood: Awaken — and to the stone: Rise up. And it is a phantasm — this is cast in gold and silver and there is no breath-of-life in it at all.
\v 20 But the Lord is in his holy temple — let all the earth be in awe before his face.

\c 3
\p
\v 1 A prayer of Habakkuk the seer concerning ignorances.
\p
\v 2 O Lord, I have heard your report and I was in awe — O Lord, I contemplated your works and was utterly astonished. In the midst of two living-things you will be made known — as the years draw near you will be recognized — as the appointed-time arrives you will be made visible — when my soul is troubled in wrath you will remember mercy.
\p
\v 3 God will come from Teman and the Holy One from the shaded mountain. His majesty covered the heavens and the earth was full of his praise.
\v 4 And his radiance will be like light — horns in his hands. And he placed the mighty-love of his strength.
\v 5 Before his face goes the Word and it goes out into the open-fields at his feet.
\v 6 He stood and the earth was shaken — he looked and the nations were dissolved. The mountains were shattered with force — the everlasting hills melted. The ways of the age are his.
\v 7 Against toils I saw the tents of the Ethiopians — the tent-curtains of the land of Midian will be shaken.
\p
\v 8 Were you angry at the rivers, O Lord? Or was your wrath against the rivers — or your fury against the sea? Because you will mount upon your horses and your riding is deliverance.
\v 9 Straining you will bend your bow at the scepters — says the Lord. The rivers of the earth will be split apart.
\v 10 The mountains saw you and were in pain — the torrent of water passed through — the deep gave its voice, the height of its phantasm.
\v 11 The sun was lifted up and the moon stood in its order — at the light of your arrows they will go and at the radiance of the flashing of your weapons.
\v 12 In threatening you will diminish the earth — in wrath you will bring down the nations.
\v 13 You went out for the deliverance of your people — to save your anointed-ones. You hurled death upon the heads of the lawless — you raised up bonds unto the neck.
\v 14 You cut through in amazement the heads of the powerful ones — they will be shaken in her — they opened their teeth like a poor man eating in secret.
\v 15 And you drove your horses into the sea — stirring up many waters.
\p
\v 16 I kept watch and my belly was shaken — at the sound of the prayer of my lips trembling entered into my bones — and below me my strength was shaken. I will rest in the day of my affliction — to go up to the people of my sojourning.
\p
\v 17 Because the fig tree will not bear fruit and there will be no produce on the vines — the work of the olive will lie and the fields will produce no food — the flock was cut off from the feeding-place and there are no cattle in the stalls —
\v 18 yet I will rejoice in the Lord and be glad in God my Savior.
\v 19 The Lord God is my strength — and he will set my feet as of a doe and upon the high places he leads me to conquer — with his song.

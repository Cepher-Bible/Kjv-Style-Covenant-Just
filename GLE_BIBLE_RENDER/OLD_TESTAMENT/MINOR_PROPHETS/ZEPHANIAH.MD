**Quick notes on how it lands**

- **"has prepared his slaughter-feast and has consecrated his invited-guests"** (ἡτοίμακεν τὴν θυσίαν αὐτοῦ / ἡγίακεν τοὺς κεκλημένους αὐτοῦ) — 1:7 — Thayer: *thysia* = a slaughter-offering, a sacrifice — specifically the animal killed and presented; *hagiazō* = to consecrate, to set apart as holy, to make sacred for a specific function; *kaleō* = to call, to summon by name — the invited ones are those specifically called and named. LSJ: *thysia* = a sacrifice, the act of slaughter in the ritual sense; *hagiazō* = to hallow, to consecrate to sacred use. The Lord has prepared a slaughter-feast — not a banquet of blessing but a ritual killing — and has consecrated the guests who will attend it. The guests are consecrated as participants in the slaughter, not as recipients of hospitality. The nations summoned to the day of the Lord are not being welcomed — they are being set apart as the sacrifice. Vulgate *sanctificavit vocatos suos* confirms — Jerome read the consecration-of-the-called register correctly. Two-witness confirmation on the dark inversion.

- **"thickening upon their dregs"** (τοὺς παγέντας ἐπὶ τὰ κατάλοιπα αὐτῶν / *qui coagulati sunt super feces suas*) — 1:12 — Thayer: *pēgnymi* = to fix, to fasten, to cause to congeal — the image is liquid that has stopped moving and begun to solidify; *kataloipa* = the remainder, the leavings, the dregs at the bottom. LSJ: *pēgnymi* = to make solid, to congeal, to fix in place — used for wine that has settled and thickened on its sediment. Vulgate *coagulati super feces* = curdled upon their dregs — L&S: *coagulo* = to cause to curdle, to thicken. Two-witness confirmation: wine that has not been poured or stirred — sitting so long on its sediment that it has begun to congeal. The image is not merely complacency but a physical process of settling and thickening into immovability. These are men who have sat so long undisturbed in their prosperity that they have solidified — they no longer move, no longer pour, no longer flow. The Lord searches them out with lamps precisely because they have become inert.

- **"a warrior who saves"** (ὁ κύριος ὁ θεός σου ἐν σοί / δυνατὸς σώσει σε) — 3:17 — Thayer: *dynatos* = powerful, mighty, one having force and ability — the word of capability and strength; *sōzō* = to save, to rescue, to preserve from destruction. LSJ: *dynatos* = capable, strong, the one who has the power to accomplish — used for warriors, rulers, those with actual force. The Lord in the midst of his people is *dynatos* — a force-capable one who saves. Not a gentle presence of comfort — a warrior with the actual capability to rescue. The verse then fires three times: he rejoices over you with gladness, he renews you in his love, he exults over you with a shout of joy. The warrior who just saved you is also the one shouting for joy over you. The combat register and the exultation register fire simultaneously.

- **"I will turn to the peoples a tongue into her generation"** (μεταστρέψω ἐπὶ λαοὺς γλῶσσαν εἰς γενεὰν αὐτῆς) — 3:9 — Thayer: *metastrephō* = to turn around, to transform, to convert into something else — a reversal of direction; *glōssa* = tongue, language, the instrument of speech and the people group identified by it. LSJ: *metastrephō* = to turn about, to change the direction or character of; *glōssa* = tongue, dialect, a people defined by their language. The Lord will transform the tongues of the peoples — turning the scattered language-groups toward a single calling-upon of his name. This is the reversal of Babel: at Babel the one language was scattered into many tongues and the peoples were dispersed — here the many tongues are turned toward one purpose and gathered under one yoke. The structural arc from Genesis 11 to Zephaniah 3 is explicit in the LXX vocabulary.

- **"O the conspicuous and ransomed city — the dove"** (ὦ ἡ ἐπιφανὴς καὶ ἀπολελυτρωμένη / ἡ πόλις ἡ περιστερά) — 3:1 — Thayer: *epiphanēs* = conspicuous, notable, brought to light — from *epiphainō* = to shine upon, to appear openly; *apolytrōō* = to ransom-release, to free by payment of price — the transaction verb; *peristera* = a dove, the bird of peace and of the breath-of-life descending. LSJ: *epiphanēs* = appearing openly, manifest, illustrious; *apolytrōō* = to release for ransom, to free by buying back. The LXX opening of chapter 3 is a concentrated indictment — the city that was conspicuous, that had been ransomed-released, the dove-city — immediately followed by: she did not hear, she did not receive correction, she did not trust. The one who had been bought back refused her ransom. The dove-city became the city of roaring lions and Arabia-wolves. The gap between v.1 and v.2 is the entire tragedy of Jerusalem compressed into two verses.

**Drift eliminated:** Check 5 fired on 1:7 — "consecrated his invited guests" almost softened to "hallowed his called ones" losing the dark-inversion of guests-as-sacrifice; restored at full weight — the consecration is for slaughter not for welcome. Check 1 fired on 1:12 — "settled on their dregs" too familiar from modern translations; restored as "thickening upon their dregs" with *pēgnymi* congealing-solidification weight. Check 3 fired on 3:17 — "mighty to save" pew-register from Handel/Isaiah smoothed *dynatos* to devotional warmth; restored as warrior-with-actual-force-capability. Check 4 fired on 3:1 — "O rebellious and defiled" standard modern render almost replaced the LXX conspicuous-and-ransomed-dove reading; LXX base held — the indictment in the LXX is more precise than the MT-based renders because it names what the city had been before naming what she became. Check 6 fired on 3:9 — Babel-reversal structure of *metastrephō* + *glōssa* almost lost in compression; both words restored with the Genesis 11 reversal architecture noted.

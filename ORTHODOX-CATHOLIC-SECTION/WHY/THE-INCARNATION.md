**## Biblical Justification for the Incarnation — Full God, Full Man**  
**The Covenant Continuity of God With Us: From Promise to Presence**

**Purpose of this Document**  
This is the central, unifying pivot of the Covenant Toolkit. It traces the single, unbroken promise of **God-with-us** from Genesis 3:15 to consummation, fulfilled when the eternal Son becomes fully God and fully Man in one Person (hypostatic union). This reality enables every previous pillar: images of the God-Man, sacraments as His presence, the Heart Law as God dwelling within, Mary as Theotokos, signs/seals as visible reminders of Covenant-Favor, and the seven sacraments as visible seals of the invisible grace (Covenant-Favor).

**CRITICAL HONESTY DISCLAIMER**  
The precise formulation “one Person, two natures, without confusion, without change, without division, without separation” is the Chalcedonian Definition (451 AD). The **biblical reality** itself — the eternal Son is fully God and fully Man in one Person — is explicit and repeated in Scripture. We defend the biblical truth; the Chalcedonian language is the Church’s faithful summary to protect that truth.

**What this document defends:**  
- The Incarnation as full God and full Man, the decisive turning point of all covenant history.  

**What this document explicitly does NOT defend:**  
- Any view that divides Christ into two persons (Nestorianism) or confuses the natures (Eutychianism).  
- Any practice that uses the Incarnation to justify idolatry or superstition.

## INTRODUCTION: The God-With-Us Story Begins with God’s Promise

**The covenant story begins with the Promise of God With Us.**

**Genesis 3:15 (Protoevangelium):**  
> "And I will put enmity between you and the woman, and between your offspring and hers; he will crush your head, and you will strike his heel."

**The Seed of the woman will be the one who defeats the serpent.** This promise unfolds as God repeatedly draws near to His people, always pointing to the day when He will dwell among us in the flesh.

**The honor given to the Incarnation does not create a new mediator; it fulfills the promise that God Himself would come.**

**Isaiah 7:14 & 9:6:**  
> "The virgin will conceive and give birth to a son, and will call him Immanuel… For to us a child is born… and he will be called Wonderful Counselor, Mighty God, Everlasting Father, Prince of Peace."

**John 1:14 (The Fulfillment):**  
> "The Word became flesh and made his dwelling among us. We have seen his glory, the glory of the one and only Son, who came from the Father, full of grace (Covenant-Favor) and truth."

**The issue is never the humanity of Christ itself but covenant relationship.** The Incarnation guards the truth that God truly took flesh without ceasing to be God.

## PART ONE: THE OLD COVENANT PATTERN — God Prepares the Way for God With Us

**If the Incarnation meant “God cannot become man,” God contradicts Himself by repeatedly appearing in human form and promising to dwell among His people.**

### A. Theophanies — God Appearing in Human Form (Genesis 18; Exodus 3; Joshua 5:13-15)

The Lord appears to Abraham as a man, eats with him, and speaks face to face. The Angel of the Lord (often identified as the pre-incarnate Son) appears to Moses in the burning bush and to Joshua as the Commander of the Lord’s army.

### B. The Tabernacle & Temple as Temporary Dwelling (Exodus 25:8; 1 Kings 8:27)

God commands a tabernacle so He may “dwell among them.” The glory fills the temple, yet Solomon acknowledges that even the highest heaven cannot contain God. These are shadows of the greater reality when God will dwell among us in the flesh.

**Expanded Table: Legitimate vs Illegitimate Views of God’s Nearness**

| Aspect                  | Legitimate View (Old Covenant)                  | Illegitimate View                              |
|-------------------------|------------------------------------------------|-----------------------------------------------|
| Origin                  | God promises to dwell among His people         | Human invention of gods in human form         |
| Function                | Points to the coming Incarnation               | Becomes idolatry (worship of creation)        |
| Posture of Heart        | Awe and faith in God’s promise                 | Fear of the divine or denial of the divine    |
| Theological Role        | Signpost to God With Us                        | Replacement of the true God                   |
| New Covenant Parallel   | Shadows → Full Incarnation                     | Gnosticism or modalism                        |

**Governing Safeguard:** Appearances of God are legitimate signposts; they become Nehushtan when treated as the full reality or denied as impossible.

### C. The Prophetic Promise of Immanuel (Isaiah 7:14; 9:6; Ezekiel 37:27; Zechariah 2:10-11)

A virgin will bear Immanuel (“God with us”). The child will be called Mighty God. God promises, “I will live with them and walk among them.”

---

## PART TWO: THE PROPHETIC PROMISE — Internalization, Not Abolition

**Ezekiel 37:27; Zechariah 2:10-11.** God will live with His people and walk among them. The promise moves from external tabernacle to the day when God Himself will tabernacle in human flesh. This is escalation: the promised presence becomes personal and permanent.

**Reasoning Context:** The prophets never say “God cannot become man.” They say He will dwell among us in a new and greater way.

---

## PART THREE: THE FULFILLMENT IN CHRIST — The Decisive Turning Point

**Reasoning Context:** The invisible God becomes visible. The eternal Son takes human nature without ceasing to be God.

### A. The Word Became Flesh — Full God and Full Man
**John 1:1,14:**  
> "In the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God, and the Word was God… The Word became flesh and made his dwelling among us."

**Philippians 2:5-8:**  
> "Who, being in very nature God, did not consider equality with God something to be used to his own advantage; rather, he made himself nothing by taking the very nature of a servant, being made in human likeness."

**Colossians 2:9:**  
> "For in Christ all the fullness of the Deity lives in bodily form."

**Hebrews 1:3; 2:14-17:** The Son is the exact representation of God’s being and shared in our humanity fully.

**The hypostatic union** (one Person, two natures) is the biblical reality Scripture presents: the eternal Son is fully God (John 1:1; Col 2:9) and fully Man (Heb 2:17; 1 Tim 2:5), without confusion or division. Only the God-Man can represent us to God and God to us.

### B. Christianity Is Irreducibly Embodied in the God-Man

| Element            | Reality in the Incarnation           | Theological Significance                     |
|--------------------|--------------------------------------|----------------------------------------------|
| Seed Promise       | Genesis 3:15 fulfilled               | The woman’s Seed is God Himself              |
| Tabernacle         | John 1:14 (“tabernacled” among us)   | God dwells among us in human flesh           |
| Temple             | John 2:19-21 (His body is the temple)| The true Temple is the body of the God-Man   |
| Image              | Colossians 1:15                      | The invisible God is now visible in Christ   |
| Heart Law          | Ezekiel 36 fulfilled in us           | God dwells in us by the indwelling Spirit (Spirit-Breath) |

**Matter is not competition with spirit; it is the chosen vehicle for the eternal Son to become fully Man while remaining fully God.**

### C. The Bronze Serpent Typology Parallel: The Lifted-Up One

Just as the serpent pointed forward, the Incarnation is the ultimate sign: God lifted up in human flesh for our healing (John 3:14-15).

### D. Addressing the Core Objections

**“How can God become man?” (Arianism, modern skepticism):** John 1:1 and Colossians 2:9 are explicit. The eternal Son is fully God and takes full humanity without ceasing to be God.

**“The Incarnation makes images of Christ permissible”:** Yes — once God has taken a real human nature, depicting that nature confesses the Incarnation, not idolatry.

**“Full God and full Man is impossible”:** The Bible shows both full deity and full humanity in one Person (John 1:1,14; Phil 2:5-8; Col 2:9; Heb 1:3; 2:14-17). Chalcedon faithfully summarizes this biblical truth.

**Practical Abuse:** Where the humanity of Christ is denied or the divinity is downplayed, the Incarnation is distorted.

## PART SIX: THE CONTROLLING PRINCIPLE — Safeguards Against Heresy & Neglect

### A. The Distinctions That Matter
**Worship belongs to the Trinity alone.**  
**Honor of the humanity of Christ and His mother is permissible without divinization.**

### B. The Functional Tests
**Test 1:** Does this increase faith in Christ as fully God and fully Man?  
**Test 2:** Is the heart trusting the Spirit (Spirit-Breath) or a distorted view of Christ?  
**Test 3: Nehushtan Threshold** — The moment the humanity is treated as less than God or the divinity as less than human.  
**Test 4: Supplication Test** — Does prayer flow from union with the God-Man?  
**Test 5: Covenant-Favor Test** — Are you resting in the grace (Covenant-Favor) of the Incarnation?  
**Test 6: The Pharisee Test** — Does it make you merciful or judgmental?  
**Test 7: The Fruit Test** — Does it produce the fruit of the Spirit (Spirit-Breath)?
